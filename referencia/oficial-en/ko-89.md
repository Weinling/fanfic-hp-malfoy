The first thing that greeted Carina was the sensation of something flowing into her mouth. Only then did she realize she was thirsty—and that she needed to drink more of whatever this was. The liquid didn't go down easily, so she worked it with her tongue like a small animal nursing, coaxing it past her parched throat. Like water seeping into cracked earth, it spread through her throat, into her stomach, and along her veins until it had soaked through every part of her.

Some time later, Carina Malfoy opened her eyes. There was something hard in her mouth. She traced its shape with her tongue and teeth—round, hollowed out, capable of holding liquid. A spoon.

Someone was feeding her, one careful spoonful at a time. With extraordinary tenderness.

It was only natural to want to see who. Carina turned her head slowly, and found herself looking into the eyes of a boy holding a spoon.

She called his name in a cracked voice.

"Har… ry?"

"You're awake."

Harry smiled. Beside him, Professor Slughorn stood holding a potion vial, his expression characteristically sour.

Still not fully oriented, Carina lay there for a moment in a daze before gathering herself and pushing upright. However many days she'd been lying there, her body had gone rigid. Every time she moved her limbs, her muscles ached in protest—as though lodging a formal complaint.

Still, Carina was alive. That was remarkable in itself. Just before she'd lost consciousness, the poison had been so agonizing…

"Ugh—wait, just—"

She forced herself upright through the pain, a low groan escaping her. Her platinum hair hung limp and dull, but she had no attention to spare for that. Panic seized her, and she grabbed Harry's arm.

"Wait, wait. The Dark Mark—Harry, the snake—He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named—"

She was murmuring in confusion and fear when Harry set down the spoon and wrapped an arm around her shoulders. He held her against his chest as she trembled in uneven intervals, and whispered close to her ear.

"It's all right, Carina. It's over."

The trembling stopped. Harry's voice was cold like deep lake water—and it carried some quality that stilled her, smoothed her out. Her ragged breathing began to ease, and reason returned like cold water poured over her head.

Carina steadied herself, then looked at Harry with eyes still full of questions. The look demanded an explanation.

Harry read it and answered.

"Your poison had an antidote. I worked it out too late—it nearly went badly—but fortunately I found it and got it into you in time. You barely kept your life."

"Wait, then—"

The reason Carina had drunk the poison was to suppress the Dark Mark on her wrist. But now that she was conscious, the snake within the Mark would not stay quiet.

She moved to push up her sleeve. With Harry's arm still around her, it wasn't easy.

"Oh—the tattoo on your wrist, that?"

Harry understood before she could manage it. He drew back and calmly folded her sleeve up, layer by careful layer. As the fabric fell away, there was the ghastly skull—and a snake lying utterly still, as though dead.

Carina's eyes went wide. The snake that had watched her, monitored her—it was doing nothing, even with Harry right there. Showing this Mark to anyone else was unambiguously betrayal, the kind you couldn't argue your way out of—

Then Harry raised his wand and spoke.

"That's rather rude, you know. We had a falling-out for a bit, yes—but getting a delinquent tattoo without my consent? On this pretty wrist, no less."

He brought his wand to her wrist. In that instant, Carina felt something strange.

It was exactly like the moment Voldemort had given her the Dark Mark. That tingling sensation, as though a channel had opened in her wrist. Back then, Voldemort had pushed a snake through that channel. She had shuddered at the wrongness of it every time she thought of it.

But Harry, remarkably, was pulling the snake back out.

The tattoo dissolved like ink washed away by rain, streaming toward his wand. The snake went with it. It writhed a few times as though resisting, but couldn't fight the current. Hissing all the while, it was drawn inexorably into the wand.

"Ha… hah…"

When the last trace vanished, Carina exhaled—a breathless, exhilarated sound. With the magical snake extracted from inside her, she felt a clarity she had no words for. She was free now. Free from that magic, from that contract.

Harry, for his part, looked entirely unmoved, as though he'd done nothing of consequence.

"The information you gave us let us find all of his Horcruxes. And then the opportunity to kill him presented itself. So we decided to turn your plan against him—used Polyjuice to disguise someone as you, followed Snape into their stronghold. We struck from inside and out simultaneously, and they collapsed without putting up much resistance…"

Now that calm had returned, Carina finally had the space to look at Harry properly. His face was as composed as ever, but beneath it sat deep exhaustion—and something else. A darkness she couldn't quite name.

The easy confidence she knew in him was gone. Something had been taken out of him. What had happened?

"There were losses. Professor Snape and Professor Dumbledore both died… But through their sacrifice, we were finally able to defeat He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, and most of his followers have been arrested and sent to Azkaban."

Was it the losses? But was that truly the source of the darkness in his face?

No—there was something else. Something more fundamental.

Voldemort was gone. So how had Harry removed a Dark Mark that had persisted even after Voldemort's death?

"Your father… will stand as a key witness at trial. His release on bail was agreed in exchange for information about the Death Eaters. He'll be back at the Manor before long."

"Re—really?"

That was good news. The news that her father would be out of prison and back with his family pushed the swelling questions aside, at least for a moment. Harry smiled faintly at her unguarded joy.

That afternoon, in the Great Hall, the atmosphere pressed down like a great stone weight. Students who hadn't yet heard the full story whispered among themselves, eyes drawn to the empty seats at the staff table.

Most conspicuous of all was Dumbledore's chair at the head. The seat where he should have been sitting—absurd hat and all, laughing his unhurried laugh—stood empty. The professors on either side of it were pressing handkerchiefs to their eyes every ten seconds.

Even the most oblivious students understood that something had happened. The Slytherin table was no different.

"Dumbledore must be dead!"

Pansy Parkinson said it with barely concealed delight.

"He must have killed him—who else could have killed Dumbledore?"

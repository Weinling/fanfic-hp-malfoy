The Lestrange family vault held countless treasures. Gold coins rolled across the floor of a chamber the size of a modest room, and every inch was crammed with gilded goblets, silver armour, dragon hide, and crystal vials of potion.

Finding the golden badger cup among all of it was, in itself, straightforward enough.

Provided one was sufficiently prepared to touch scorching metal.

Sirius, gloved hands braced, exhaled and seized the golden cup. The instant he did, it blazed a furious red and began multiplying—dozens of identical cups spreading in every direction.

He grimaced. Lupin called out a warning.

"Don't lose the real one, Sirius!"

"I know! Bloody hell, it's burning straight through the dragonhide!"

While Sirius struggled, Lupin raised his voice—loud enough to carry to the goblin waiting outside.

"Magnificent gold, this! Madam, you truly mean to let us take some of it?"

Lupin was playing the part of a fugitive who had followed Bellatrix for the money. It wasn't a particularly convincing performance, but it worked well enough on Tonks.

At the word Madam, Tonks squirmed and answered in a simpering voice.

"Yes, hee... please, help yourselves..."

"Er... Madam?"

"Did you call for me, darling?"

"No, wait—"

Sirius shook his head and dropped Hufflepuff's Cup into the enchanted pouch. He grabbed a few extra coins for appearances, then turned to the other two, who were generating an entirely different kind of atmosphere inside the vault.

"I'm giving you the house-elf for the evening, so save the rest for when we're back at the manor."

"What on earth are you saying, Sirius!"

"Thank you..."

"Thank you? Tonks, what are you—"

Regardless, once the cup was secured, the three of them strolled out of the vault as though nothing had happened. Mardnak closed the vault door with a deferential bow and asked,

"Mrs. Lestrange. Is something the matter? It appears the anti-theft enchantment was triggered."

"I told you already. I promised these two I'd let them get their hands on some gold!"

Time to be Bellatrix again. Tonks let out a grating cackle.

"And I kept my promise. They touched the gold without knowing a thing—watched their hands and feet blister right up! Like turkeys in an oven! And still they wouldn't let go!"

An explanation for the anti-theft enchantment. That was precisely why she had brought Lupin and Sirius along.

Dumbledore had known the Gringotts anti-theft spells in extraordinary—and inexplicable—detail, and had helpfully explained that if the enchantment triggered, the goblins would grow suspicious. They needed a plausible reason for why someone had touched gold that didn't belong to them.

Tonks had proposed using Bellatrix's own nature against her.

The plan was to make it appear that the anti-theft magic had been triggered by Bellatrix's sadism—that the foreign visitors had touched the gold on their own, and she had let them suffer for it.

But Mardnak's manner was subtly different from when he had admitted them. The goblin studied Tonks sidelong and probed carefully.

"I—I mean no disrespect to Madam, but we made enquiries, and the Ministry has informed us that... there have been no escapes from Azkaban."

Tonks and Mardnak locked eyes. Tonks stared down at the goblin with Bellatrix's cold face, and the goblin—though his gaze flickered to the wand and a tremor ran through him—did not look away.

The Ministry would be run ragged with the Triwizard Tournament right now. They had miscalculated in assuming Gringotts could reach the Ministry directly even at a time like this.

Sirius and Lupin moved their hands toward their wands.

Tonks snapped irritably,

"Do I really have to spell it out for you? What happened today, of all days? Why today—the day the Ministry is gutted empty because of the Triwizard Tournament—did I choose to leave that wretched place and come here, before attending to a rather more glorious appointment? Do I need to hold your hand and explain it to you?"

"No, that's not—"

Tonks raised her wand. Beyond the passage, she could see several armed goblins flinch.

They're suspicious. That much is clear. To avoid suspicion, she had to act like Bellatrix. But what would Bellatrix actually do in a situation like this?

Easy enough to know. Considerably harder to replicate.

Tonks knew that Bellatrix was a master of the Cruciatus Curse. She tormented others with it whenever the mood struck her, and she preferred driving her enemies to madness through endless suffering rather than simply finishing them off. Mercifully vile.

But right now, to avoid suspicion, Tonks had to cast an Unforgivable. She forced her voice steady and raised her wand.

"And why, exactly—should I have to answer to the likes of you? How dare a lesser creature presume to—*Crucio*—!"

At that moment, Lupin let a handful of gold coins and a necklace slip from his hands—nearly hurling them to the floor. The gold struck the stone with a jarring clatter. Every goblin in the passage instinctively snapped their head toward the sound.

Tonks seized the moment and lowered her wand, shouting,

"You idiot! Do you have any idea what that is?!"

"S-sorry. I'm so sorry."

Lupin ducked his head and scrambled to gather the coins, then stared at them with a slack, vacant expression. It was painfully obvious acting—but fortunately, goblins had little interest in the cultural habits of humans.

Tonks jabbed her wand into Lupin's chest.

"Drop anything again and I'll stretch that tongue of yours out and trim it piece by piece!"

"Madam, please forgive me. I've never held gold like this before—my hands were trembling..."

Tonks looked at Lupin, head bowed before her.

The quiet attentiveness Lupin showed at moments like this always reached her somewhere warm. He was always mindful of her, always adjusting himself so she could do what she needed to do.

Tonks, who could change her appearance at will, was in many ways acutely sensitive. But when she was with Lupin, she could simply be herself.

She smiled faintly and whispered,

"Thank you."

Then she spun on her heel and swept her wand in a wide arc, firing spell after spell. Red beams ricocheted off the walls and ceiling of the passage. Goblins screamed and threw their arms over their heads. Shattered stone dust rained down from the walls and ceiling.

Tonks bellowed,

"Get out of my sight, you filthy little creatures! Every last one of you will answer for this when the Dark Lord returns!"

The goblins waiting around the corner fled in a scramble. The only one who remained was Mardnak, who had escorted them in.
Tonks turned to the thoroughly cowed goblin.

"Say one word to me on the way out. Just one. I'll pull that tongue out by the root and cut it into pieces."

"But of course, Madam! Not a word from me!"

Mardnak attended them with a newly heightened servility. The cart ascended toward the surface in perfect silence. Tonks did not speak again until Mardnak had opened the doors for them.

The goblins still watched with uncertain eyes—but in the end, not one of them moved to stop her.

The moment the three of them stepped out of Gringotts, they Disapparated to 12 Grimmauld Place.

Between number eleven and number thirteen on Grimmauld Place, there was a very slight gap. The Muggles who lived on the square had long since written it off as a minor administrative error, and referred to the narrow sliver between eleven and thirteen as number twelve with a vague, dismissive shrug.

Remarkably, they were correct. They would never know it.

A space imperceptible to Muggle eyes. Within it stood an ancient wizarding house, fortified by layer upon layer of protective enchantments.

The moment Tonks, Lupin, and Sirius arrived, the gap in space opened. When a witch or wizard who knew of number twelve's existence drew near, the house revealed itself—like a folded piece of paper unfolding, or a closed drawer sliding open. One step forward and a door thrust itself into view; two steps more and a broad, solid outer wall materialized around it.

By the time they reached the entrance, the grand brick townhouse stood before them as though it had always been there, utterly unperturbed.

"Right, in you go."

Sirius ushered Tonks and Lupin inside. Lupin glanced back.

"And you?"

"I need to deliver the cup to Dumbledore. And since the Triwizard Tournament's on, I might as well go and see how Harry's getting on."

Sirius called for Kreacher and ordered him to go to Hogwarts. Once Kreacher had vanished, Sirius departed as well, seen off by Lupin and an enthusiastic Tonks, and made his way to Hogwarts.

What Lupin and Tonks got up to alone was really none of his concern.

—

At that same hour, Harry was fighting a Blast-Ended Skrewt in the maze.

The Skrewt's weakness was its underbelly—the one part of it not protected by its armoured shell. A curse had to land precisely between the segments to do any real damage.

There were two ways to manage it: levitate the Skrewt, or slide underneath it himself.

Someone else might have preferred a more elegant, arcane solution. Harry preferred the simplest option available.

"Stop messing about and move, Skrewt. Honestly, if it weren't for Hagrid, you'd have been tied to a rock and sunk to the bottom of the lake long ago."

Kreeeeeeee!

It probably hadn't heard the provocation. Skrewts had no ears.

Which meant the reason it snapped its claws and lunged at Harry was simply instinct.

Getting caught in those claws was not something Harry intended to allow, so he threw himself sideways and rolled clear.

Crunch. Crunch. Every time the claws drove into the ground, the turf erupted as though a giant had taken a shovel to it. The Skrewt's armoured pincers were impressively efficient at tearing up a lawn.

Though, being a blood-sucking creature, they were presumably even more effective against living things.

"Next year's Quidditch pitch is going to be in a sorry state."

Harry muttered this and ducked sharply. A massive claw swept over his head and plunged into the hedge wall. Branches and leaves snapped apart in handfuls. When the Skrewt wrenched its claw free, a tangle of vines came with it.

The gouge in the maze wall was already filling in—new shoots pushing through from within, sealing the gap.

Why did a creature like this even need to exist? Harry watched the trail of destruction and muttered,

"...Skrewt. You know I'm fond of you."

Crunch.

The Skrewt snapped its claw once—severing a thick vine in the process—and charged again. This time it changed tactics: instead of striking directly, it drove both claws into the ground on either side of Harry, boxing him in. Harry rolled to escape and found no way out.

With Harry pinned between its claws, the Skrewt reared up. It meant to crush him. Its pale grey underbelly was exposed, the red suckers pointing straight down at him.

Just before it came down, Harry raised his wand and aimed at the pale grey belly.

"*Impedimenta!*"

The Impediment Jinx struck squarely between the abdominal segments. The Skrewt let out an unearthly shriek and collapsed, all strength gone from its limbs.

Harry, still trapped between the claws, couldn't clear the radius in time and was buried under it. He thrashed his wand from beneath.

"*Wingardium Leviosa!*"

The Skrewt's body floated upward. Harry rolled out from under it. His hair and robes were caked with dirt and leaves, and his leg—where the suckers had punctured him—was bleeding freely.

He'd won, but the cost was steep. Harry bound the wound tightly with a Bandaging Spell, then limped onward in the direction he'd been heading.

At the next junction, he turned right—and a carnivorous magical plant lunged at him, iron teeth gaping. He slashed its bud with a Diffindo and it recoiled with a shudder.

Two blocks further, a bog appeared with stepping stones across it. Harry tossed a stick experimentally; the third stone coiled around it and dragged it under. He crossed by testing every stone before he trusted his weight to it.

Picking through trap after trap for several more minutes, he finally reached a wide passage—broad and straight, stretching ahead of him.

The mental map he'd been drawing had done its work. From the moment this main corridor appeared, the path to the Cup was a single unbroken line. Harry limped down the royal road toward the Triwizard Cup.

The end of the maze. The view opening up ahead. Harry stepped forward, one pace at a time, just crossing the threshold into the chamber where the Cup stood—

Cedric Diggory appeared from the side. He had been walking with his eyes fixed on the Cup, but he sensed Harry's presence and spun around.

"...Harry."

"Cedric. Good to see you."

Harry raised his wandless hand in greeting. Cedric's gaze moved between the Cup and Harry. The Cup was right there—but Harry had had more than enough time to cast a Jinx if he'd wanted to.

Harry said,

"I mean it, even knowing you're the competition. Whatever they put in this maze—how many creatures did they actually release in here?"

"My thoughts exactly."

"What about Viktor Krum? Fleur Delacour?"

Harry and Cedric faced each other, circling slowly clockwise. Neither lowered their guard.

"Delacour was taken down by an Acromantula. The giant spider got its venom into her leg... Fortunately the rescue team arrived in time. Spider venom is paralytic, apparently—not lethal."

"Krum?"
"I found him collapsed, scorched all over. Probably a Blast-Ended Skrewt."

"So it's just the two of us left, Cedric."

Thud. Harry's footsteps stopped. Cedric halted at the same moment.

They faced each other. Both tightened their grip on their wands. The tendons stood out across their knuckles.

The maze built into the Quidditch pitch was utterly still. No insects, no birds, nothing natural—a purely artificial magical construction, built for the Tournament alone. Nothing here but walls of vine that regrew no matter how many times they were cut, and the enchanted barrier wrapped around them.

The spectators above were watching with rapt attention, but Harry and Cedric couldn't see them.

"There's one Cup and two of us. We have to settle this."

"...How do you want to do it, Harry?"

"Two people, one Cup. Isn't it obvious?"

Harry raised his wand straight up to eye level. Cedric flinched at the movement—but Harry showed no sign of attacking.

...Not yet.

"We duel for it."

"A duel—but Harry, you're still a fourth-year. You haven't learned Nonverbal Magic yet, or advanced Shield Charms. This isn't fair."

"There's no other way, is there? If you want to go easy on me, throw your wand down and step back. I'll take the Cup and tell everyone you made a tactical retreat."

"...I can't do that. You're right—a duel is the only answer."

Cedric said it and raised his wand to match Harry's. They stood like mirror images of each other, then bowed simultaneously, as though by prior agreement.

And simultaneously stepped back, levelling their wands at each other. Eyes locked, each holding the other in their line of fire. The Cup stood tall between them in the taut silence, looking down at them both.

"Count to three and we start. You count, Cedric."

"All right. One, two... three!"

Both thrust their wands forward at once.

"*Impedimenta!*"

"*Stupefy!*"

The spells collided in midair and ricocheted apart. Harry and Cedric circled counter-clockwise, firing in rapid succession. Flashes of light sought each other out, missed, grazed. Their wands poured out spell after spell.

Cedric barely dodged an Impediment Jinx and stumbled—just for a moment. Harry moved to cast a Disarming Spell, but something instinctive made him throw himself to the ground instead. Cedric had been feigning the stumble; beneath his robes, his wand was already trained on Harry.

A Stunning Spell scorched past Harry's ear. He felt the heat against his cheek as he swung his wand and shouted,

"*Relashio!*"

He wasn't aiming at Cedric—he was aiming at the ground beneath Cedric's feet. The turf exploded, throwing up a wall of dirt and debris that blinded him. Harry immediately followed with a Banishing Charm, hurling the clods and dust straight at Cedric. Cedric stumbled and went down.

Harry pressed the advantage without hesitation, striding forward and firing a Rope-Binding Jinx followed by a Paralysis Hex. Cedric threw up a Shield Charm and barely held—but he had no room to counter.

Harry had him cornered. He moved in to finish it.

"*Tarantel*—"

At that moment, the bandage wrapped around Harry's leg—loosened by the violence of the fight—slid free. Blood welled up from the Skrewt wound in a sudden rush.

The strength going out of you is not simply a matter of willpower or resolve. When the only pressure keeping a bleeding wound closed is suddenly gone, the body responds accordingly. Harry's leg buckled. There was nothing left in it to hold him up.

His leg gave way, and Harry pitched forward.

Cedric countered a beat too late.

"*Expelliarmus!*"

Harry's wand flew from his hand. Cedric caught it with a Seeker's reflexes. Harry's eyes snapped wide—he started to push himself up—then let the tension drain from his body and dropped flat on his back.

He said, in a voice worn thin with exhaustion,

"You win, Cedric."

Cedric stood over him, breathing hard, staring at the wand in his hand.

"...No. That was genuinely brilliant. If your leg had held, I'd have lost."

"That's part of it too."

Cedric crossed to Harry without being asked and helped him up. Harry leaned on him and managed to get upright against the wall.

Cedric clapped him on the shoulder.

"Harry, you should be proud. You're the youngest Champion here, and you outpaced everyone. If you hadn't been hurt—or if you'd run into fewer dangerous creatures in the maze—you'd have won this."

"You talk too much, Cedric. Go pick up the Cup. You're the winner."

Harry pushed him toward it.

Cedric glanced back at Harry one last time, reluctance written plainly on his face, then walked toward the Cup. Harry watched in silence.

What a perfect picture.

Injured but unbroken, he had fought his way through the maze. He had come face to face with another Champion at the threshold of victory, duelled him fairly, and lost after a desperate, hard-fought battle.

Moving enough to bring the whole crowd to its feet. Beautiful, in its way.

Exactly as Harry had planned it.

Defeat wins more allies than victory.

He had pretended to lose his way, wandering into a few extra traps to sell the performance. Failing outright and being dragged out would have been too undignified, so he had fought through everything with everything he had.

He had confirmed the moment Cedric turned onto the path leading to the Cup, then timed his own arrival accordingly. A chance encounter with Cedric Diggory—a duel—a loss.

Hufflepuff had been openly hostile to Harry from the start. He had taken the glory that should have been Cedric's.

But Harry had no use for glory, or for a thousand Galleons. What Harry needed were allies. People who would stand with him next year against Slytherin, against the Death Eaters.

Hufflepuff would be on his side now.

Cedric Diggory reached out toward the Triwizard Cup. He glanced back at Harry one final time—but did not pull his hand away.

He closed his fingers around the Cup.

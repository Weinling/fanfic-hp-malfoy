Words carry weight—that much is certain.

Simply wrapping something in language and releasing it into the open air had made Harry's heart feel light enough to float. Secrets kept are suffocating; secrets spoken bring relief. There was a reason people had always been warned to guard their tongues. With one burden lifted, even his attachment to life seemed to have diminished, if only slightly. The only unease that remained was the sense that whatever weight he'd shed had been transferred directly onto her.

Harry reached up and touched Carina's face. Her expression was rigid, but her skin was soft—warm in a way that didn't match the frozen atmosphere around her. She always showed two things at once, this girl. Like the only living thing moving through a world of still life. Something stirred in him without warning, and before he knew it, his hands had begun to draw her close.

But what stopped him was nothing more than a single year.

If he reached out carelessly, pulled those slender shoulders toward him, pressed his lips to hers—

If he squandered her brilliant years on a moment's selfishness—

If he filled a burial urn with memories that would soon be buried underground—

What a cruel thing that would be to do to Carina.

Harry took hold of her shoulders and gently pushed her back. Her face receded by that same distance. Into the space left between them, he placed cold words.

"This is a secret known only to Ron, Hermione, and a few others who were there. Now you're included. Professor Moody and Professor Lupin said they'd look for a way. But if there truly isn't one, then there's no choice but to… before my birthday comes…."

"Before?"

Moody and Lupin had said they would find a way—but everyone knew it was a promise with no foundation beneath it. Even Dumbledore, who had known every truth, had done nothing more than spend his own life pushing Riddle's soul beneath the surface. Could Moody and Lupin really succeed where he had not?

The ending was already written. They were only delaying it for as long as delay was possible. Harry answered with a heaviness he couldn't conceal.

"They're going to kill me."

"Nonsense."

Carina dismissed it without hesitation. Harry had been about to reassure her, but something in her posture and bearing made him pause.

She wasn't trying to deny reality. She was denying his conclusion. Not fleeing from a truth she couldn't bear to face—but genuinely, methodically, condemning the flaw in his reasoning.

"That's not worth a second thought. Die? Be killed? By whom, and on what authority?"

"Carina, you saw my memory. If I don't die—"

"Riddle's soul will emerge. So what?"

She held his gaze and shot back without blinking.

"You think stepping forward will solve everything on its own. You speak as though offering your life will restore peace to the world. You really are a Gryffindor. Arrogant, self-willed, and hopelessly optimistic."

"But if I don't, Riddle's soul will—"

"Shut up, Harry. I'm going to teach you how a Slytherin thinks. Listen carefully."

Could Carina actually find a way? Harry knew she was brilliant, that she carried knowledge of obscure and secret magic—but he didn't believe she could solve this. Still, watching her work her mind furiously on his behalf would be its own kind of comfort. He waited for her to speak.

At last, she did.

"Kill Riddle before your birthday arrives. Separate that soul from yourself and reclaim your own, whole and intact. If you can't manage that, then kill him the moment he takes over your body. That way only Riddle's soul dies, and yours may reclaim what's yours."

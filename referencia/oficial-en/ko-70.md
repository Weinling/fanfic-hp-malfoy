Something strange was happening at Hogwarts.

The Quibbler—banned from sale, out of print for months—had begun turning up all over the castle. Its latest issue. Children who picked it up thinking it was some kind of prank were left speechless by what they found inside.

The Azkaban breakout. A suspicious death. And a rumoured affair between Umbridge and Snape. Explosive content that read as though it had been aimed squarely at Hogwarts students.

The children devoured it breathlessly, turning pages with mounting anticipation. And then—

This magazine is a sample. Please look forward to the full version, on sale this Hogsmeade weekend!

Price: 2 Sickles.

All proceeds will go toward freeing Snape from his bachelor existence.

The wizards of Hogwarts, having been subjected to a highly sophisticated Muggle sales technique, shuddered at its cruelty. Then they set aside two Sickles for Hogsmeade weekend.

Word spread quietly but surely, until it seemed as though no one would be staying behind at school that day. Students filed toward Hogsmeade with two Sickles apiece in their pockets.

The point of sale was printed on the back of the sample. Upon reaching each marked location, the next meeting point would appear on the reverse—a treasure hunt, essentially, and the students followed the map in that spirit.

One boy, more furtive than the rest, combed through Hogsmeade in search of The Quibbler. He followed the map through several rendezvous points before the final marker led him to the Hog's Head.

Heart thudding, he pushed open the door. The smell of something unidentifiable hit him first, then the dim light falling across dust-caked windowpanes, the general disorder of the place. Robed figures sat around wooden tables, drinking or talking amongst themselves. The atmosphere was chaotic enough that someone selling contraband magazines would barely register.

At one table, Ron Weasley sat surrounded by a towering stack of books, wearing an expression of grave authority. As the boy approached, Ron looked up.

"The Quibbler?"

"Yeah."

"How many copies?"

"Five."

"Sorry, but it's two per person. Demand's high."

"I promised to send some to my parents and my uncle. Can't you make an exception?"

"Sorry, rules are rules."

"I'll pay a Galleon."

"—Though every rule has its exceptions, of course. Hmm. What do you think?"

The boy produced the gold coin. Ron, visibly swayed, turned to look at Hermione Granger. Hermione answered without hesitation, her expression firm.

"We can't change the rules. If we sell five copies to everyone, we'll run out immediately. Did you come with anyone? Ask a friend to buy the extra copies for you."

"Ah, well, the thing is—"

The boy trailed off awkwardly. Hermione studied his face, then said:

"Wait. I recognise you. You're from Slytherin, aren't you?"

The boy startled. Ron, who had been eyeing the Galleon, snapped his head up and lunged to his feet, grabbing the boy by the collar.

"What? Slytherin? You little—you're one of Umbridge's lot!"

"No, I'm not!"

"Don't give me that. You came here to hand this over to Umbridge or Malfoy, didn't you! I'll show you—"

"Ron, wait!"

Hermione stepped in to restrain him. Ron snarled.

"Don't stop me, Hermione! People like this need to be taught a lesson—"

"That boy is a third-year Slytherin, and he's not in the Inquisitorial Squad. He's not going to report us to Umbridge or Malfoy."

"What are you on about? He's a Slytherin! They're all the same!"

"Even if that were true, we're distributing this because we want the truth to reach people, Ron. We can't afford to turn customers away."

"But—"

"The moment we started distributing The Quibbler, Umbridge was always going to find out eventually. It's only a matter of when."

Hermione cut the argument short, picked up three copies, and pressed them into the boy's hands. He stared at her, bewildered.

"Right. I don't know who you are, but if you want five copies, take them. All I ask is that you don't tell Umbridge about The Quibbler—at least not today. Understood?"

The boy nodded, then turned and bolted before she could change her mind. Ron glared after him.

"Hermione! How could you just give those to him? Harry trusted us with this!"

"Ron, honestly—why are you like this? A Slytherin student came here alone, without going to Umbridge or the Inquisitorial Squad first. That means he could be on our side! You should be a little kinder to someone like that!"

"Kinder? I don't owe Slytherins any kindness."

"Ron! The Sorting Hat said it—we need to stand together against the threat, all of us. Even Slytherins!"

"Slytherins don't see it that way. Even if some of them agreed with the idea of unity, they'd still be more united with each other than with anyone else."

The history of pureblood families ran deep. Those who had preserved their bloodlines by rejecting Muggles possessed a kind of solidarity that ordinary people simply didn't share. While the other houses had spent years competing and cooperating with one another, Slytherin alone had always maintained its own insular, self-contained world.

Ron said flatly, "Before the day's out, this new issue of The Quibbler will be circulating through the Slytherin dormitory. I'd bet a Galleon on it."

One copy for his mother. One for his father. One for his uncle. One for himself.

The boy ran through the list in his head, then closed his eyes and picked up the fifth copy. He had bought it with Carina in mind.

Carina Malfoy was the backbone of Slytherin—as prefect, she looked out for the other students, smoothed things over, made herself useful. He himself had benefited from her help more than once.

Knowing all that and not giving her a copy felt, to him, like a betrayal.
"Um, this—"

Hermione Granger had asked him not to tell Umbridge. She hadn't said anything about not showing it to Carina. So this wasn't breaking his promise.

He found Crabbe and asked him to leave the neatly wrapped magazine on Carina's bed.

Carina picked up The Quibbler from her bed. The cover, cluttered with its chaotic design, listed the articles inside. One of them made her let out a short, derisive laugh.

"Umbridge and Professor Snape... now that's what I call viral."

But that article was gossip—bait, nothing more. The one that actually mattered was the piece on Igor Karkaroff. Carina snorted and flipped through the pages.

[This publication has warned repeatedly, since our previous issue, about Igor Karkaroff. He is powerful, brutal, and above all, contemptible. Harry Potter and Dumbledore, who were among the first to recognise his danger, issued repeated warnings to the Ministry of Magic and contributed articles to this very publication.

Yet rather than heed these warnings, the Ministry saw fit to ban us on the grounds of incitement to falsehood. Many reasonable people were baffled by this decision, but the Ministry pressed ahead with characteristic foolishness.

Now, however, you know that this publication has spoken only the truth. Igor Karkaroff broke into Azkaban and freed those who were once followers of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named.

But why? Why would Karkaroff—who once sold out his own comrades—turn around and break those same comrades out of prison? Even the most dim-witted wizard alive would have grasped by now that this affair is connected to He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named...]

The same argument as before. Karkaroff was dangerous, the Ministry needed to act. Last time, it had been entertaining enough—until the Ministry intervened and reduced it to gossip.

But the situation had shifted. With the Azkaban breakout fresh in everyone's minds and public opinion turning cold, The Quibbler was no longer just a tabloid. It had become the foremost voice of criticism against a Ministry that had proven itself incompetent.

Left unchecked, this would become a problem for Carina too. But still.

"Hmph. How adorable."

Carina scoffed. The content was thorough enough to be interesting, even with the fabrications woven in—but interesting was all it was.

"Did you really think you could change anything this way, Potter? All you've done is tighten the noose around your own necks."

Harry didn't understand how entrenched power worked. Those who held it couldn't change course overnight. Even when they knew they were wrong, the weight of years spent denying it dragged them back—they became prisoners of their own past judgements, condemned to repeat the same mistakes. They could deny anything in the world except their former selves. That was the nature of politics and power. Carina understood this.

"...Still, I can't let this circulate freely. There's no real chance of it, but if the Ministry decides to post guards at the Department of Mysteries, that would be inconvenient."

She would have to bring this to Umbridge. The Quibbler had been published in defiance of the ban—it was an illegal publication, and the Ministry would be well within its rights to arrest whoever was selling or editing it.

From Harry's perspective, this was a bold gambit. But if you were going to play boldly, you had to expect the Ministry to respond in kind.

Carina felt a small, sharp thrill. Who else at Hogwarts had been working this hard, this effectively, from the shadows? Not even Voldemort or the Death Eaters could deny her usefulness.

A little more effort, and perhaps—even if they noticed the diary was gone—they might let it pass...

Carina smiled to herself, the smile of someone who had already won, and turned to another article out of idle curiosity.

[Umbridge's ideal type revealed... hooked nose, cold eyes. Surely not?]

It was so absurd that even Carina couldn't suppress a few involuntary snorts. She had no doubt this article would send both Snape and Umbridge into a fury.

Remarkable talent, really. One article, and he'd managed to torment both of them simultaneously. Harry remained, as ever, mercilessly vicious toward the people he disliked.

Carina made a mental note to avoid the subject of Umbridge anywhere near Snape for the foreseeable future, and turned the page.

[Muggle Studies Column: What is Surgery—The Muggle Method of Healing?

For those who cannot fathom it, the simplest analogy is this: Muggle surgery is rather like repairing a broken clock.

To fix a broken clock, one must first take it apart. Then, peering inside, one straightens the misaligned gears, reattaches the broken hands, and applies oil where needed. That is the proper method of repair.

In precisely the same fashion, Muggles heal the body by slicing open the skin with a knife, physically manipulating bones back into place, and injecting blood directly using a syringe. They then sew the wound shut with a large needle and thread—and have the audacity to call this a cure!

Many readers will be sceptical. And yet every word of this is true.

Muggles apply this barbaric method to a wide variety of ailments and conditions. One Muggle-born witch, whose parents are dentists, told us: 'Muggles have no way to regrow lost teeth. Instead, they drill a model tooth directly into the gum as a replacement.' She then admitted that she herself had sought magical assistance to reduce the size of her front teeth.

One can only despair at such savagery. Muggle doctors—they are less healers than mechanics of the human body.

As anyone who has ever had a clock repaired in this fashion knows, a body patched together this way will begin to creak again before long. The underlying problem has not been solved; it has merely been held together temporarily. This tells us everything we need to know about the short-sightedness of Muggles...]

Carina snorted after reading it.

Outrageous content—though admittedly interesting. Well, for Muggles who couldn't use magic, this was presumably the best available option for fixing a broken body.

She turned the page.

[...What gives us cause for concern, however, is the news that a healer at St Mungo's Hospital has been attempting something similar. It has come to light that certain healers have been recommending 'surgery' to patients in place of established, proven treatments—a revelation that has shocked many.

'Developing new treatments requires a degree of experimentation. We simply tried it on a voluntary basis, with patients whose lives were not in immediate danger.'

The healer offered this brazen response, but given the recent death of Broderick Bode—who had been admitted to St Mungo's Hospital—it is difficult to see how they can escape public censure...]

Carina stopped.

She had recognised a name.

Broderick Bode?

She knew who he was. An Unspeakable, employed in the Department of Mysteries.

Some time ago, Carina had passed along information to her father—information she had gleaned from something Umbridge had muttered to herself—in order to help him learn what he needed about the Department of Mysteries. Bode's name had been part of what she'd shared.

Admitted to St Mungo's Hospital, and then...

Carina was not stupid. She had given her father the information. Broderick Bode had ended up in St Mungo's Hospital.

The conclusion was obvious enough. Her father had been involved. He was endlessly gentle with his family, but for Voldemort's sake he was capable of anything—he was a Death Eater, and he had never pretended otherwise.

...He's dead?

It was nothing. Carina told herself this firmly. Her father had been given a mission, and to carry it out he'd needed information. Bode would hardly have volunteered it willingly, so some form of coercion had been used—enough to put him in hospital.

That much, Carina had always known.
I did what I was supposed to do.

She had contributed meaningfully. Because she had identified Broderick Bode, Lucius Malfoy had obtained a crucial piece of intelligence about the prophecy.

The prophecy in the Department of Mysteries can only be retrieved by the person it concerns.

Without that knowledge, the Death Eaters might have lost several of their own trying to take it by force. Lucius had been delighted, and had praised her warmly.

...He had not, however, told her what had become of Broderick Bode in the process.

"Hmph."

Carina closed the magazine. Trivial news, and yet it had unsettled her for no good reason. She shouldn't be dwelling on it.

Broderick Bode had not been killed by Carina. She had simply done her part; the Death Eaters had done the rest. His death was not something she needed to feel guilty about.

No—this was war, after all. People dying was a common occurrence in wartime, wasn't it? She tried to think it through that way, tried to press the discomfort down and cover it over. Like closing a book.

But.

"Ugh—"

The heart was on the inside. It couldn't be closed like a book. It was hers. No matter how hard Carina tried to look away, her sharp mind forced her faculties awake and held the truth directly in front of her eyes.

"...He's... dead."

The prophecy in the Department of Mysteries can only be retrieved by the person it concerns.

How had they known that without any casualties? The answer was obvious, wasn't it.

Someone had been sacrificed in their place. Offered up as tribute to the Dark Lord, their blood and flesh used to make his throne a little more secure.

"...Because I told them?"

Of course—even without Carina, they would eventually have found someone else.

But.

Choosing this side meant... ultimately, enabling someone's death... no, not merely enabling—it meant that someone had to die for it to work at all—

"—Ugh!"

She couldn't hold it back. Carina lurched to her feet and ran for the bathroom. She grabbed the door handle as though clinging to it, wrenched the door open, braced her hands against the toilet, and brought up whatever was rising from inside her.

She had half-expected something black and foul to come out. What came was only clear stomach acid. But even that felt revolting to her—disgusting beyond bearing.

It wasn't the bile that disgusted her. It was herself.

"Hh—"

She couldn't bring herself to look in the mirror.

She had been through a great deal, and through all of it she had held her head up. Pride in her family, confidence in herself—those had been the twin pillars that kept her standing.

But now.

She couldn't even look at herself.

"I... for the Malfoys..."

She had worked to prevent her father's death. Her brother's death. For a Hogwarts student, she had worked hard—she had done well, she'd thought. No one could deny what she had accomplished.

But what did any of it mean? If she had to kill innocent people, watch their names appear in the Daily Prophet, read the grief and hatred left behind in those who remained—

Was she supposed to keep living like that, eyes averted from her own guilt, telling herself she hadn't done the killing herself?

Could Carina Malfoy endure that?

"For the family..."

Carina shook her head. Perhaps it would get easier, in time. Perhaps she could tell herself that she would grow accustomed to it, and endure the guilt until it faded.

But only those who could survive the present had any right to speak of the future. If she broke—if she crumbled—then the future was nothing but an empty promise that would never come.

She didn't know if she could hold on. There were too many people she would have to kill. Harry. Ron. Hermione. Students from Gryffindor, Hufflepuff, Ravenclaw. The entire Weasley family. The professors of Hogwarts.

People who had stood against her at every turn, who would stand against her again, and who she would one day have to kill.

Again, Carina bent over the toilet. There was nothing left in her stomach. Only clear liquid came.

"I—I can't... I can't do this..."

Moody had been right. Carina had known nothing. Not what war truly was. Not how commonplace death could become.

Perhaps—just perhaps—

Perhaps Carina had been on the wrong side from the very beginning.

"This isn't... the time for this..."

She straightened unsteadily.

She had to bring The Quibbler to Umbridge. She had to get the paper that catalogued her sins out of her sight, and at the same time prepare for the next step.

But... if she could still turn back, even now.

A thread of hope, thin as a hair, caught the light. What if she held onto this magazine and said nothing? What if she turned away, right now? Would that be all right? Could she still undo it?

Carina deferred the decision. Until her heart had made up its mind, she would not tell Umbridge anything about The Quibbler.

In that moment, she had offered the Dark Lord the smallest, most passive act of defiance.

And still, she could not bring herself to look in the mirror.

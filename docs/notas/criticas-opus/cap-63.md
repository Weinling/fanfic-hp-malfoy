# Crítica externa: extra *Bed Rest* y capítulo 63, *The Spoon*

*Lectura a ciegas, seguidos. Continuidad con grep. Notas: extra, 8,5; capítulo 63, 10.*

## 1. Veredicto

El 63 es lo mejor del libro: la escena de Bode es casi insoportable sin una sola imagen gráfica, y la mano de Harry en la barbilla, con *She smiled at him*, es el mejor final que tiene esta historia. El extra es felicidad tonta de verdad, y puesto delante multiplica el 63 (cama junto a la ventana, lluvia, alguien que cuida a alguien). Solo hay que corregir detalles: una expresión imposible en boca de Lyra, un chiste de resfriado que no funciona y una memoria sin plantar.

**Extra:**

- **¿Felicidad tonta?** Sí: el vapor por las orejas, *I bean it, Botter*, *You’re doing it with your face!*, leerle el grosor de los calderos. No pasa nada, y es lo que se pedía.
- **¿Hace reír?** Sí. *Goyle got it from a pasty*, *I see the bed is empty*, *She’s horrible when she’s ill*, *held anything so disgusting*, y el *Bagnold* final en voz baja.
- **¿Respeta las reglas?** Los gestos sí: la mano como en las Tres Escobas, la capa, la tarta de Goyle. El presagio, no del todo: *Nobody was going to try to kill him until June* (Extra:73) es un chiste, pero es el único aviso del extra, y sobra.

**Capítulo 63:**

- **Bellatrix.** Da miedo y tiene voz propia: es una niña feliz. *There were tears of pure delight in her eyes* es lo opuesto a Voldemort, y por eso funciona. *She had very few teeth left at the back* y *It’s in the wrist!* son muy buenos.
- **La lección del pavo real.** No sobra: enseña que el sitio sigue ahí (*exactly the size of her hand*). No es gráfica: *its feathers stood out, all of them, all at once* basta. *She had always wondered what they thought about* es la línea más helada del libro. No se pasa.
- **La segunda pérdida.** Se nota sin explicarse. *That was two.* Pero pesaría más con una memoria que el lector ya conozca (corrección 3).
- **Abraxas y la cuchara.** Preparan perfectamente: *You’ve got good hands*, *Never go and look*. Y ella va y mira.
- **Bode.** Es tan fuerte como debe, y no se pasa de gráfica en nada. *It was like looking through the front windows of a house, and seeing straight out at the back*; *They were a sort of hazel* (paga 61:47); *He takes it very nicely from the young ladies*; *for about as long as it would take to say a name*; la hermana y *He was a very polite man*; los once. Cada una de estas cosas podría ser el clímax, y se suman sin estorbarse.
- **El lavabo.** No se pasa. El vómito dura una frase; lo que importa es el ritual de después (*She washed her right hand again*). La mano de Narcissa en el carruaje es de lo mejor del capítulo.
- **El final.** Funciona, y se entiende que actúa: *she took hold of the corners of her mouth. It went up.* La barbilla limpiada y el *There* repiten al Healer y a ella con Abraxas y Bode, sin decirlo. No toquéis nada.
- **¿Se alarga?** No. 3.693 palabras. Alice podría parecer una digresión, pero retrasa a Bode y carga a Narcissa.

**Avisos.** Nada sexual. Tortura animal y Bode, no gráficos. Solo un matiz: 63:23, *trailing one long dirty finger across her back*, de una adulta sobre una menor, puede leerse con un tinte que no hace falta (corrección 4).

## 4. Rastro de IA

Casi nada en las escenas fuertes, donde la frase corta es de propósito. **La advertencia del 62 se ha cumplido:** van tres capítulos con *She… She… She…* como ritmo por defecto. En Bode y en el lavabo es la escena. En el resto se está volviendo monótono, y el entumecimiento de Lyra perderá efecto si todo suena igual. Mantened 63:111-131 y 63:145; soltad 63:39 y 63:69 (corrección 6). En el 64, frases largas: que se note cuando vuelva lo corto.

La fórmula *She looked at X. She looked at Y.* vuelve en 63:91 y 63:169. *very*: la mayoría en boca de la Healer van bien; los del narrador sobran en 63:13, :31, :71, :149.

## 5. Correcciones listas para aplicar

**1. El enchufe.** 63:161: “He looked as though somebody had plugged him in.” → “He looked as though somebody had lit him.”

**2. El presagio y los chistes del extra.**

- Extra:23: “Go away,” said Malfoy. It came out as *Go away*, with all the consonants filed off. The left ear whistled. “I bean it, Botter.” → “Ged oud,” said Malfoy. All the consonants had been filed off. The left ear whistled. “I bean it, Botter.”
- Extra:45: “He did the births and deaths in Mrs Skower’s voice.” → “He did the births and deaths in Professor Binns’s voice.”
- Extra:73: “There was nothing in the world that needed doing. Nobody was going to try to kill him until June.” → “There was nothing in the world that needed doing.”

**3. Una memoria que ya tengamos.** `cap-09:149`: el primer mensaje oculto de su padre, cuando cumplió ocho años. Es el origen de todo lo de los huecos, y el lector lo conoce desde el 9. (Decisión del autor: enseñárselo a Voldemort es un riesgo que ella correría a sabiendas, y eso también suma.)

63:39: “the summer she was six, and the lawn in the early morning, and her father’s hands holding the broom steady, and his voice saying *Look up, my treasure. Don’t look at the ground, look up.* She carried it in and set it in the sun.” →

> the morning she was eight, and a letter about something else, with a gap in the second line, and her father’s face coming round the door, not saying anything, while she held it up to the light; and his voice saying *Well done, my treasure. Now find the next one.* She carried it in and set it in the sun.

63:41: “He picked the morning up, and looked under it.” → “He picked the letter up, and looked under it.”

63:53: “there was the lawn. There was the dew on it. It had been early. His hands had been on the broom, she could have told anybody that; she knew what he had said.” →

> there was the letter. There was the gap in it. It had been her birthday. There had been a treasure at the end, she could have told anybody that; she knew what he had said.

**4. Bellatrix.** 63:23: “She walked round Lyra, trailing one long dirty finger across her back.” → “She walked round Lyra, and rapped her on the shoulder blade with one long dirty finger.”

**5. *very* del narrador.**

- 63:13: “very fast” → “fast”.
- 63:31: “It made very little noise.” → “It made little noise.”
- 63:71: “she knew very well who had the keeping of it now” → “she knew who had the keeping of it now”.
- 63:149: “very upright” → “upright”.

**6. Soltar la frase donde no es la escena.**

- 63:39: “She went down the length of the boards. It was a long way. She put the room in order as she walked.” → “It was a long way down the boards, and she put the room in order as she walked.”
- 63:69: “She took up the bowl. It was soup. It seemed to be all that they gave him. She sat down on the stool, and put the napkin under his chin, and she dipped the spoon, and held it out.” → “The bowl was soup; it seemed to be all they gave him. She sat down with it on the stool, and put the napkin under his chin, and dipped the spoon, and held it out.”

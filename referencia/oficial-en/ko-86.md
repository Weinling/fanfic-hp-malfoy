Harry Potter had gathered every potion Carina left behind. Unfortunately, there was no poison remaining—she had already used it all.

Even if there had been poison, it would have been difficult to deploy. Poison was easy for the target to detect, and given the sheer number of Death Eaters, incapacitating one or two through poisoning would have accomplished very little.

So Harry had taken the other potions instead.

Colorless, tasteless, odorless. A potion that could extract the truth from any person with just three drops.

Veritaserum.

At Harry's request, Narcissa Malfoy had slipped Veritaserum into the drinks prepared for the Death Eaters.

Of course, it was a truth potion—not a paralytic. Drinking it wouldn't send someone rolling helplessly across the floor. There was a side effect of dazed disorientation, not unlike intoxication, but as the long history of wine and alcohol demonstrated, people tended to dismiss such minor symptoms without a second thought.

Narcissa had added the Veritaserum to the wine, which was precisely why those who drank it were slow to notice anything amiss.

By the time they realized, it was already too late.

"Hey, you! What is your master's name!"

Fred shouted at the Death Eater crouched behind a pillar.

It was a baffling question, and there was no obligation to answer. The Death Eaters sneered at it and began preparing lethal curses in response.

But the Veritaserum in their stomachs had other ideas.

"Volde— gkh!"

"The D-Dark Lord, ah, ah. Vol, Voldemort—!"

The Death Eaters who spoke Voldemort's name went white as sheets, their faces contorting as though invisible hands were closing around their throats, and they crumpled one after another. Thanks to that, several of the Order of the Phoenix members were able to bypass entire skirmishes entirely.

Stepping over the fallen bodies, Fred said with undisguised delight:

"Wow. Bringing someone down with a single question. I feel like a grand sorcerer from a fairy tale!"

"Harry, what exactly did you do?"

"Nothing. I simply helped them be a little more honest."

Harry shrugged.

"It's just that He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, in his obsession with his own mystique, put a curse on his own name. His followers bear the brunt of it more than anyone. Idiotic, really. What was he planning to do if someone fed them Veritaserum and forced them to say it?"

"You're probably the only person in the world who would ever think of that."

"Where did the Veritaserum come from?"

"Carina's doing."

She hadn't left it for this purpose, but it had served one well enough. Harry said it briefly and moved on.

The manor was divided across four floors, and Voldemort's quarters were at the very top. Carina's room was on the third floor—already deep in the heart of the building, which meant there were few obstacles between there and the uppermost level.

By now, Dumbledore and the Aurors he'd brought would be making their way through the front entrance. The Death Eaters on the first and second floors would have their attention fixed in that direction. All that remained was to push through to the fourth floor.

That said, complacency was not an option. Their group was elite but small. If they were surrounded, they would be isolated—and that would be dangerous.

Fred and George stopped at a door and turned back.

"Right then—as planned, we'll hold this door. Getting flanked from behind would be bad."

"Transforming barricade. We've been saving this for exactly this moment."

They reached into a large sack and pulled out a set of toothed clamps, which they wedged into the door frame. The door seemed to stretch and yawn—and then sharp steel teeth began sprouting along its edges. Any Death Eater careless enough to approach would find themselves caught in those steel jaws like a bear trap.

The Weasley twins pressed on, pulling item after item from their pockets: Peruvian Instant Darkness Powder, a slippery cleaning solution that sent anyone who stepped on it skidding, protective cloaks, protective gloves, and more. A full arsenal of tricks designed to confuse and obstruct.

Once they'd finished their preparations, the twins called out:

"Go on ahead! We'll hold the rear!"

"And when you defeat You-Know-Who, put in a good word for our joke shop!"

"Sorry. I'll be calling Carina's name at that moment. I'll try if there's time left over."

The Weasley twins cackled and waved them off. The rest of the group pressed forward, leaving them behind. Molly Weasley glanced back at her sons several times with barely concealed anxiety—but with Dumbledore entering from the ground floor, staying behind might actually be the safer position.

Of course, no one could say what would happen. This was a battlefield, and no one could guarantee anyone's safety. All they could do was make the best choices available to them.

Ron and Hermione, armed with the gadgets and protective gear the twins had handed out, stayed close to Harry's side as they advanced. Ron looked down at the heavy sack of tricks and said:

"I honestly didn't think my brothers would give us any of this before a fight. They haven't given me a single thing for free all year. But I suppose blood is blood—they'll hand things over when lives are on the line."

Ron looked genuinely moved. Hermione gave him a withering look.

"They're planning to bill us for it afterward."

"Don't say things like that right before a battle—"

"And you shouldn't be talking nonsense right before a battle either. Review your Shield Charms. Have you mastered nonverbal spells? When two wizards of equal skill face each other, nonverbal casting is absolutely essential—"

"Do I really have to listen to your lectures even now?"

They both fell silent and walked on. The long corridor ended, and the next door drew steadily closer. With it, Ron and Hermione's strides grew shorter and shorter.

Tension, anticipation, and the dread of not knowing what lay beyond that door—it had made them both go quiet. They approached it carefully.

While Mad-Eye scanned the other side of the door with his magical eye, Ron looked at Hermione and drew a long breath.

"Hermione, I'm only saying this now because there won't be another chance… When this fight is over—"

"Everyone down!"

At that moment, Moody shoved Ron and Hermione aside and bellowed. An instant later, a thunderous crash rang out and the door was blown clean off its hinges. Screams erupted around them, and several people who hadn't managed to get clear were pinned beneath the door, groaning.

Footsteps followed. A young man with swept-back blond hair and a face drained of all expression—Bartemius Crouch—stood in the doorway, spine straight, wand raised. Behind him, a cluster of tense Death Eaters waited.

Bartemius spoke.

"How did you get this far? Dumbledore should still be on the ground floor."

"Curious, are you?"

The fallen door rose into the air, spinning furiously, and hurtled toward the Death Eaters. They scrambled for cover—but Bartemius himself didn't move. He simply flicked his wand.
Crack.

The chandelier hanging from the ceiling erupted outward like a branch caught in a growth charm. Its steel frame lengthened, the glass ornaments at its tips sharpening into jagged points, and it drove straight through the wooden door.

Bartemius looked past where the door had been. Through the darkness, Moody emerged, his prosthetic leg clunking with each step.

Moody twisted his scarred face into a sneer.

"I'll tell you. It means that even with Dumbledore occupied on the ground floor—he has enough to spare a separate force to come for you."

Bartemius shook his head slowly.

"I don't understand… You'd go to face the Dark Lord without Dumbledore? Do you have a death wish? Or have you simply grown overconfident in your old age, Mad-Eye?"

"We have our reasons—reasons you could never anticipate. Bartemius Crouch Junior."

Bartemius's dark brows twitched.

"You remembered me, Mad-Eye Moody."

"I remember everyone I've ever arrested… You've changed considerably. Back then you were a wild, weeping child begging for your life."

"Time has passed. I've grown stronger. I'm not what I was."

"Yes. Time has passed." Moody's voice dropped. "I've grown old. I'm not what I was either."

Moody muttered the words and then drove his prosthetic leg hard into the floor. The shockwave radiated outward, cracking the walls and splitting the floorboards. Wind howled through the shattered windows. The sea breeze rolling in from beyond the cliffs whipped both men's stiff blond hair into disarray.

In the same instant, as though by agreement, both raised their wands and leveled them at each other. Fire erupted from the tips.

"*Avada Kedavra!*"

"*Stupefy!*"

The beams collided. Something detonated in midair, and the shockwave swept the room. People threw spells into the fray or dove behind cover.

Moody deflected the violet curse and roared:

"Leave this to me! Remus—take the children and go!"

"Understood! Come on, everyone! Molly, you too!"

Harry, Ron, and Hermione nodded and moved toward the door on the far side. Bartemius's eyes went wide. He swung his wand.

"Don't you dare—!"

At his command, the chandelier—now monstrous—groaned and swiveled. It lashed out with its many arms toward Harry, like the Whomping Willow of Hogwarts given form in steel. The difference was that its branches were iron, and its leaves were razor-edged shards of glass. Being struck by one would not merely sting.

The spear-like arms came rushing at Harry. The sound of them tearing through the air was enough to raise goosebumps. Harry raised his wand quickly, narrowed his focus, and traced a small, tight circle.

"*Engorgio!*"

The chandelier's already-elongated arms grew even larger—

But only on the right side.

The arms that had been hurtling toward Harry wrenched violently to the right and swept past him. The chandelier, robbed of its direction, plowed into the wall. Glass shattered and scattered. Steel rods drove into the brickwork.

Crack. Sparks flew from the stone. The impact was tremendous—one entire wall of the manor shuddered, and the whole building seemed to groan in agony. Stone and glass and steel, once unified, were now torn apart, broken and twisted into dust.

"*Locomotor Mortis!*"

Hermione quickly bound the chandelier's arms together with a Leg-Locker Curse. Bartemius lost control of it for a moment. In that window, Ron had already sprinted ahead and thrown the far door wide open.

"Let's go! Hurry, Harry!"

Bartemius reached out toward Harry's retreating figure.

Then Moody's thunderous voice filled the room.

"How rude. Am I really so easy to ignore!"

Mad-Eye's spell came screaming toward Bartemius—powerful enough that the floorboards trembled from the shockwave alone. Sensing the danger, Bartemius threw every Shield Charm he had at it.

The mysterious silver beam carried extraordinary force. It shattered the shield and sent the chandelier flying. One of Bartemius's arms was caught in the chandelier's trajectory and bent at a grotesque angle.

But Bartemius showed no sign of pain. He frowned slightly, as though mildly inconvenienced, and raised his wand again.

Moody said:

"Not even a flinch. Doesn't it hurt?"

"…After nearly ten years of living under the Imperius Curse, my body no longer feels like my own. It simply feels like something that doesn't move quite right."

Bartemius said it without inflection, then glanced briefly down the corridor where Harry had disappeared.

"It doesn't matter where they go. They're only hastening their own deaths. They have some talent, I'll grant them that… but that paltry skill won't be enough to stop him."

"And yet you underestimate Harry. He is the Chosen One—the only real chance of stopping Voldemort. And tonight, that man meets his end."

"…You've grown talkative in your old age. Perhaps it's your time as well. Allow me to help."

"Locking up the same man in Azkaban twice is a rare experience, even for me…"

"That won't happen. One of us will be dead before then!"

Both raised their wands and clashed again. A bolt of lightning struck the darkness-shrouded manor.

The fourth floor—the top—was not far now. The skirmishes along the way had whittled their number down, and it was only Lupin, Molly, Harry, Hermione, and Ron making for the staircase. Most of the Death Eaters had gone out to fight; by Voldemort's nature, there would be no one standing guard over him. The most powerful wizard in the world had no need of an escort.

All Harry had to do was get there. While the joker held the enemy king at bay, their own king would seize the battlefield entirely. That was the plan they had committed to, and they had held to it in escorting Harry this far.

Then Harry caught a smell. A suffocating, deeply unpleasant sensation—the feeling of death closing in around him, something he had experienced once before.

"Watch out!"

He stamped hard on the staircase and spun around instantly. A translucent barrier bloomed from the tip of his wand.

"*Crucio!*"

"*Protego!*"

The Cruciatus Curse struck the Shield Charm and ricocheted away. He had successfully deflected the ambush—but Harry ground his teeth and glared upward.

A gaunt, sharp-featured witch was descending from the bell tower. She couldn't have been waiting there for them, which meant she had most likely flown in from outside the manor and tracked them down.
Harry snarled her name.

"Bellatrix Lestrange."

"Still alive, Potter? You must have done a convincing job of fooling Carina."

Bellatrix strode in and said:

"What are you doing here? Surely you're not planning to face the Dark Lord on your own."

"And what if we are?"

Harry said it flatly. Bellatrix doubled over with laughter.

"Kahahaha! You? Face him? Potter, just because a prophecy mentions your name, you think you can stand against him? Are you sure about this? Your friend Sirius isn't here to save you this time!"

A vein rose at Harry's temple. The fury that surged through him was violent enough to make his blood run hot—but he didn't let it show. Not because of any superhuman restraint, but because he knew that if he lost his temper, Bellatrix would only enjoy it more.

Instead, Harry answered with a cool, cutting sneer.

"Right. You killed my godfather, Sirius. So I'll return the favor—I'll kill your beloved Lord Voldemort and show you the result. I'm rather curious what expression you'll make."

The provocation landed. The grating laughter cut off abruptly. Bellatrix fixed Harry with a gaze full of cold fury.

"You have a sharp tongue… Let's see how long that lasts. A half-blood, and a blood traitor. Let's see if you're still talking like that when I'm torturing your friends in front of you!"

Without warning, her wand flashed. Harry only realized she had cast a nonverbal spell after the flash had already happened. He was too slow to recognize it, too slow to dodge. The curse was almost upon him. But—

"Don't you dare!"

Molly Weasley swung her wand with fierce force. Wind surged in its wake and drove Bellatrix's curse crashing down the staircase. Bellatrix's eyes went wide, and she tensed as she found herself face to face with the short, stout witch who had stepped into her path.

Molly Weasley cried out:

"You will never lay a hand on my children!"

"Your children? Kahahaha, this just got more entertaining. Torturing family is so much more satisfying than torturing friends."

Bellatrix raised her wand again. Both women moved at the same instant.

In the blink of an eye, five exchanges of spells flew between them. The banister tore away from the staircase. A chunk of stone punched through a window and flew out into the night. Both women staggered back a step, pale-faced, from each other's spells. A thin line of blood ran from the corner of Bellatrix's mouth; Molly was limping, her leg clearly injured.

Ron raised his wand in alarm and stepped forward.

"Mum! Let me help!"

"You take Harry and go! Leave this woman to me!"

"But—!"

At that moment, a dark shape appeared from the far end of the corridor. Everyone in the room went rigid, watching the intruder's face.

The darkness parted, and Narcissa Malfoy stepped forward, her face pale as chalk. She surveyed the scene slowly, as though taking stock of the situation.

Bellatrix's face lit up when she recognized who it was.

"Cissy. Perfect timing! Help me with these—!"

But Bellatrix couldn't finish the sentence. She flinched and swung her wand instinctively—the spell Narcissa had fired deflected off it and struck the ceiling. Broken stone rained down behind Bellatrix.

Bellatrix stared at Narcissa with an expression of pure disbelief.

"Ci—Cissy?"

"Bella. Stand down. Now."

"You're—you're betraying him? The Dark Lord? You?"

"I'm not betraying anyone. I want to save my daughter."

Bellatrix seemed unable to make sense of it. Then her eyes found Harry's face, and understanding flooded in—and her expression twisted into something demonic.

"You didn't fail to kill Harry Potter! It was a lie from the start! You were working with him all along! You and Snape, all of you!"

Narcissa's voice trembled with fear, but the tip of the wand in her hand did not waver by a fraction.

"More than that. That boy was prepared to die… This was the only way."

"You'll all die for it! Anyone who defies the Dark Lord will be the first to fall!"

"That is exactly why I am here! Stand down, Bella!"

Two mothers raised their voices and their wands. Bellatrix screamed back at them with a ferocity that was equal parts rage and something that sounded almost like grief.

Harry moved to join the fight—but just then, Lupin broke the enchantment on the door and wrenched it open.

"Harry, leave this to the others and go! Stopping He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named is more urgent! If he appears from behind, no one will be able to hold him! Go!"

"But—"

"It's all right. Ron and Hermione will stay! Ron, Hermione—hold this position and stop anyone who tries to follow!"

Ron and Hermione shifted their weight anxiously, reluctant to let Harry go alone. But the Dark Lord could only be faced by Harry, and Lupin would keep him safe until then. Right now, having more people crowd in would only create more danger.

In the end, Ron gave a single nod and raised his hand toward Harry.

"Win. No matter what."

Hermione added:

"Harry. Use your nonverbal spells well. Keep your mind defended with Occlumency, and—"

"That's enough—go! If you list everything out, he'll have time to take a nap and wake up refreshed before you're done!"

Harry laughed and waved back at them. Even in this final moment, his friends had reminded him of what made ordinary life worth fighting for—and sharpened his resolve that he had to win, so that ordinary life could continue.

The heavy sound of the door closing behind him fell like a full stop. The manor seemed to go quiet with it. The fighting below no longer mattered. What mattered now was only this: could Harry hold Voldemort long enough for Dumbledore to arrive? That was the sole question. One corridor remained. Beyond it, he was waiting.

Harry ran after Lupin down the gallery leading to the final room. He tightened his grip on his wand, set his stance to kick the door open—

The heavy door swung open on its own, as though it had been expecting him.

And beyond it, he saw the headless corpse of a serpent—and Snape, collapsed on the floor, gasping for breath.

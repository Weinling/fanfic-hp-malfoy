Sirius stood before the fireplace and tossed in a handful of Floo Powder. The flames roared to life in brilliant green. Without a moment's hesitation, he thrust his head into the fire and called out.

"12 Grimmauld Place!"

Much to Snape's misfortune, the fire did not see fit to burn Sirius's face. His head traveled through the Floo Network and emerged in the fireplace of a wizarding manor.

12 Grimmauld Place was the Black family estate, owned by Sirius. The Black family had long since struck Sirius from their family tree, but a series of unfortunate incidents had left him the last surviving member of the line—and the rightful owner of the house.

Sirius himself had no great fondness for the inheritance, but a wizarding manor protected by powerful enchantments was an extraordinarily useful thing, and Dumbledore had made the building the headquarters of the Order of the Phoenix.

Not that there were more than two people in the house at the moment.

Sirius's face appeared in the fireplace at 12 Grimmauld Place. As arranged, Lupin was waiting by the hearth.

"Remus! Good to see you!"

"I'm well enough. Never mind me—what about Harry? Is he safe?"

"Safe might be a stretch, but one thing's certain: the Triwizard Tournament hasn't managed to hurt him. Harry took the top score in both the first and second tasks with absolutely brilliant thinking!"

"That is remarkable. I always knew he was talented, but to distinguish himself so thoroughly—"

"And do you know what he did in the second task? He flew his broomstick straight into Snape's office and walked out with Gillyweed. You should have seen Snape's face. It was a sight to behold."

Sirius delivered this with enormous satisfaction, but Lupin, who had spent the better part of last year in Snape's company, could not quite bring himself to laugh.

"Doing something like that. The Snape I saw last year didn't seem the sort to tolerate it at all. You may need to protect Harry from him."

"Well, what can Snape do about it? As long as Mad-Eye's there, he won't dare put a foot wrong."

The two exchanged the usual small talk. Sirius spoke mostly of Harry—what he'd gotten up to, how much he resembled James.

Watching him carry on, he was every inch the proud parent. The only difference was that he felt no shame whatsoever about Harry's misdeeds. The more dangerous the prank, the more proudly he recounted it. Lupin couldn't decide whether Sirius simply lacked the instincts of a godfather, or whether being a godfather was precisely what gave him license to speak that way.

Perhaps Sirius hadn't changed at all, and Lupin had simply become too dull an adult.

Lupin smiled faintly and got to the point.

"Right. You said Moody's back from his assignment? When will he be returning to the house?"

"Probably not before the Tournament ends."

"What? Why?"

Sirius looked genuinely puzzled by Lupin's urgency.

"Because he still hasn't identified whoever's been targeting Harry. Sorry, but I'll have to leave the next mission's preparations entirely in your hands. I'm counting on you."

"Wait. You mean I have to stay in this house? Alone with Tonks?"

"What's the problem with—oh."

The meaning clicked, and Sirius's face broke into the roguish grin of his Hogwarts days—charming and conspiratorial in equal measure.

"Ha. What are you on about? I know you. You're not the type to make a move on a woman just because you happen to be in the same house. If you were, you'd have dated half the girls at Hogwarts. What are you so worried about? Have you gone and fallen for Tonks?"

"That's absurd. The age difference alone—"

"How much of a difference can there really be?"

"Tonks is your cousin's daughter. She was four years old when I graduated from Hogwarts."

"She's an adult now. Age, background, family, being a werewolf—you've always let those things weigh on you far too much."

Sirius shrugged. Lupin couldn't see the gesture through the flames, but the sentiment came through perfectly clearly.

He wasn't the least bit troubled by Lupin's concerns. He was simply enjoying himself.

"Besides, if you did want to pursue Tonks, I wouldn't stop you for a second. Turning into a beast once a month is a rather thin reason to keep women at arm's length. You ought to know you're remarkably decent, even by ordinary standards. Even when you've transformed, you—"

"Sirius."

"I've been looking forward to Defense Against the Dark Arts class so much lately."

Barnette Crabbe said cheerfully.

"Mad-Eye Moody going on leave was the best Christmas gift ever! Because of that, we got to meet such a young, handsome professor—Padfoot!"

"I'll grant you young and handsome, but I still don't understand why you're so fond of that layabout."

Carina gave her a reproving look. Sirius Black was, as Crabbe said, young and handsome, and he did have a sense of humor—but if she was being honest, his teaching ability fell short of even Professor Lupin's.

He carried on as though he were a student himself, and rather than imparting knowledge, he tended to share tips for pranks and tricks for doing well on exams.

Carina agreed that he was an improvement over Mad-Eye Moody, but she didn't particularly care for him. Even accounting for the fact that he was Harry's godfather.

"His flippant manner and his casual attitude toward lessons don't sit well with me. On top of that, he makes no secret of the fact that he'd rather not be teaching at all."

"Oh, come on. A face like that solves most of those problems. Sure, he's a bit prickly, but still."

"A bit? He may not be as bad as Professor Moody, but he doesn't like Slytherins."

"To put it another way, he doesn't seem particularly fond of Slytherins, but he's nowhere near as bad as Moody! Malfoy, would you actually prefer Padfoot to treat us as badly as Moody did?"

"...No, that's not what I'm saying."

But Carina knew why Sirius didn't openly discriminate against Slytherins. It was because of her.

Carina was Harry's girlfriend, and Sirius cared about that considerably.

Though he was a Black—a pureblood family—and seemed to harbor a deep-seated contempt for Slytherin, that contempt never quite surfaced, presumably out of consideration for his godson's girlfriend. The irony was that this ambivalence read as 'brooding charm' to the girls in the class.

Of course, now that Moody was back, things would probably change. Carina gave a quiet snort.

"I wonder if that's still the case now."

"Hmm?"

There's something that exists between people in a relationship. A kind of unspoken understanding that passes between them through atmosphere and glances alone, without a single word being said.

Since that day, Harry and Carina had not met even once.

Moody was real. Whoever had put Harry's name in the Goblet of Fire was still out there, watching them from the shadows.

The situation surrounding them had reverted to what it was before Christmas.

That Harry and Carina's relationship had not reverted to what it was before Christmas was due, simply, to lingering feeling.

"Brace yourself, Crabbe. There may be some rather devastating news in store for you today."

"What's that supposed to mean? You're scaring me. When you say it like that, it really feels like something's coming..."

Crabbe grumbled as she pushed open the door to the Defense Against the Dark Arts classroom. Then immediately closed it again.

She turned to look at Carina with the stiff, mechanical motion of a broken doll.

"M-Malfoy. H-how did you know."

"Crabbe! Closing that door won't do you any good!"

A gravelly voice rang out from inside the classroom. Crabbe let out a small whimper and gripped the door handle tight.

"That's right. I'm back from leave. My eye is still very much in working order, I'm afraid. I can see right through that door, so stop clutching the handle and get in here!"

Crabbe opened the door with trembling hands and swallowed hard as she looked toward the front of the room. There sat Mad-Eye Moody, wearing an expression of profound displeasure.

The Slytherin students sat in their seats looking as though the world had ended. They exchanged glances with one another, silently lamenting when Moody had returned and why Dumbledore had seen fit to send him away for a mere three months.

Moody's magical eye couldn't read minds—but no magical eye was needed to read the thoughts written plainly on their faces.

"None of you seem particularly pleased to see me back... Padfoot must have done quite a decent job, it seems?"

Crabbe, oblivious to the danger, began nodding before catching herself and slapping a hand over her own mouth. Moody observed the reaction and turned his head, raising his voice.

"Yes. He's pureblood like the rest of you, with a charming face and a free-spirited nature... You may have already grown attached to him."

When Moody put it into plain words, quite a few students gave small, reluctant nods. Several of them were clearly hoping that this confirmation might shock Moody enough to send him back on leave.

But when Moody slammed his fist down on the desk, those soap-bubble hopes burst in an instant.

Moody barked fiercely.

"What you feel about it is no concern of mine! But you still haven't grasped the fundamental point of my class... I have told you, repeatedly, to remain vigilant! Did any of you think to look into who Padfoot was, or how he came to be teaching Defense Against the Dark Arts in my place? Were you really willing to learn from someone whose identity you didn't even know?"

No one had thought to investigate Padfoot. They had all been too busy celebrating Moody's absence, and the one student who might have taken the initiative—Carina—already knew who Sirius was.

While everyone sat in silence, Moody produced a copy of last year's Daily Prophet. On the front page, a gaunt, dark-haired man with a beard glared out with wild eyes.

Around this time last year, the news of Sirius Black's escape had dominated the front page week after week, so everyone recognized the man in the photograph. Beyond that, a few sharp-eyed students noticed that he bore a striking resemblance to Padfoot.

After a moment of simple deduction, they arrived at one conclusion.

"W-wait... Sirius Black?"

"Padfoot's true identity was a murderer?"

"Hold on. Wasn't Sirius Black his most devoted follower? Didn't he betray Harry Potter's parents?"

"What's going on? How did this happen?"

Murderer or professor. Ally or enemy.

The Slytherin students floundered, unable to reach any firm conclusion. Truth and fiction were so thoroughly tangled together that it was impossible to tell one from the other.

Moody waited until the confusion had spread sufficiently, and when the chatter began to die down, he called out sharply.

"What a spectacle. What a spectacle! You spent three months in the company of a wanted criminal without even knowing it. If he'd had a mind to, he could have killed half of you!"

Confusion and shame, fear and anger. A range of emotions played out across the students' faces.

Had it not been for the formidable presence of Mad-Eye Moody pressing down on them, it all would have erupted then and there—that was how unsettled they were.

"He appeared on the front page of the Daily Prophet as a wanted criminal less than a year ago! And you couldn't recognize him just because he'd put on decent clothes and had a shave. You wouldn't even need a disguise to slip in among you lot... just change your outfit and you'd never be spotted! And you call yourselves Slytherins?"

"We only accepted him because we were told he was a professor appointed by Hogwarts!"

Gray Goyle shot back indignantly.

"It's Hogwarts' job to appoint trustworthy professors!"

"Don't leave your judgment to others! Hogwarts? Last year it was a werewolf, the year before that an imbecile, and before that... After four years of being taken in like this, have you learned nothing? A Flobberworm has better learning capacity than you lot! If you want to have a sharp tongue, you ought to at least have sharper instincts than everyone else!"

But even the spirited Goyle was nothing more than a child in front of Mad-Eye. No matter how pointed her words, the fact remained that she too had failed to see through Sirius's identity.

Having silenced Goyle, Moody raised a finger.

"Of course, Sirius Black is not a murderer. He was falsely accused! His name was cleared at the end of last year and the warrant for his arrest was cancelled... But not one of you tried to find out who Padfoot was, or why he'd been imprisoned on false charges in the first place! Complacent—utterly complacent! You wouldn't even need an Imperius Curse or Polyjuice Potion to infiltrate your ranks... just act friendly, and you'll open your hearts all on your own!"

"B-but we were barely born when all that happened..."

Crabbe ventured haltingly.

It was an argument that missed Moody's point by some distance, but for some reason Moody fell silent and fixed Crabbe with a long stare.

Crabbe, frightened as she was, pressed on in her own defense.

"Sirius Black was imprisoned about twelve years ago... We were just children then, we didn't know anything. Everything we know about Sirius Black, everything about what happened—we only learned it from the Daily Prophet last year. How were we supposed to know the full story? How were we supposed to recognize his face?"

"...So it has nothing to do with you whatsoever?"

"Th-that's not what I mean. It's just that the past is something we never lived through. It came to us like a story from a fairy tale..."

Crabbe trailed off uncertainly. It was the kind of excuse a child makes to protect herself.

But it seemed to land on Moody with unexpected weight.

For just a moment, Carina had the impression that Moody had suddenly grown very old.

His actual age must have been considerable. He had been a friend of Dumbledore's, and even when he retired over a decade ago, he had already been among the most senior Aurors of his generation.

But Moody's ferocity and his razor-edged manner made him seem vigorous—young, almost, or at least gave that impression. Perhaps he was not a man sustained by life at all, but by the venom that had accumulated inside him over the years.

"Akin to a fairy tale, you say... something distant and long ago..."

But when something drained that venom from him, what remained was only a wounded, aging, retired Auror.

Moody spoke in a tired voice.
"That complacency... it can cost you an eye. A leg. Scars that never fade... Heh. But you lot can still read small print without squinting, and running comes easier than walking at your age... Yes. You're right, Crabbe."

Moody sank wearily into his chair. He pressed at the corner of his eye—the ordinary one, not the magical one—and spoke with an air of profound exhaustion.

"Sirius Black will be working at Hogwarts for the remainder of the Triwizard Tournament as my assistant and liaison. If any of you want his help, you're welcome to seek him out at any time."

A few small cheers broke out among the students. Daphne Greengrass and Millicent Bulstrode looked as though they intended to go find him the moment class was over.

Who Sirius Black was, or whose side he was on—none of that seemed to matter much to them.

One could call it naïve, or one could call it innocent. Someone who disliked children might dismiss it as simple-mindedness.

But there was no arguing with the fact that they were young.

Moody smiled faintly. It was not the smile of a man burning on venom, but the smile of an old man looking at children—a smile that held years rather than spite.

With a face older than Carina had ever seen it, Moody spoke.

"I'll give you an assignment for next week... Summarize everything you covered with Padfoot and write it up on one sheet of parchment. That's all. I'm tired today, so we'll stop here for now... But starting next week, we return to normal lessons, so be prepared!"

Even though class had ended early, the students packed their bags in a state of despair at Moody's return. The most despairing of all was Crabbe, who was so aggrieved that she resorted to complaining to Carina, who had foreseen the whole thing.

"Malfoy... how did you know Moody was coming back?"

Carina answered with her customary air of superiority.

"The same way I always know things. My own methods."

"That's so unfair. You should have warned me!"

"And if I had warned you, would you have felt any better? If anything, knowing in advance would only have given you more time to be miserable. I was showing you mercy, Crabbe."

"That's... actually true."

Crabbe bowed her head gloomily.

Since class had ended early, they made their way back to the Slytherin common room. There was an assignment, but fortunately it was a review of previously covered material—if they worked alongside someone like Crabbe or Greengrass, who had taken careful notes during Padfoot's lectures, it wouldn't be too difficult.

Goyle, walking alongside Crabbe and Carina, remarked after a moment.

"Malfoy. Come to think of it, you haven't been seeing much of Potter lately, have you?"

"Haven't I?"

Carina's expression was perfectly calm, entirely unbothered. She tilted her gaze upward for a moment as though considering the question, then found a satisfactory answer and gave a small clap of her hands.

"We were seeing each other too often. It's time to dial it back a little. Even the best company loses its appeal if you overindulge, doesn't it? I have no desire to let what's fresh become familiar, and what's familiar become stale."

Goyle replied with a puzzled look.

"...That's a theory I've never heard before in my life."

"That's because Malfoy's in a relationship. She's different from you, still single. She's already a step ahead of the rest of us."

"Shut it, Crabbe. I'm choosing not to date anyone."

After class, the natural thing was to head back to the dormitory. Whatever else was happening, the dormitory was where you went first to think.

No plans. Nothing to do. There was the assignment, but Carina was not the sort to pour genuine effort into something like that. Straining herself for the sake of a few extra marks was, in her estimation, beneath her.

She had time to spare, and more than enough ability. She ought to have felt at ease.

But the empty space in Carina's chest did not feel like ease. It felt like absence.

Back when she and Harry were nothing to each other.

The three of them used to walk together and talk. When the day was done, they went back to their dormitories, and sometimes stopped by the library to look for books.

Carina had been at ease then. She was certain of it.

The circumstances were exactly the same now. So why did everything feel so hollow?

Spring was warm, but the wind was cold. Carina pulled her collar close and walked toward the dormitory.

An owl swept into the headmaster's office. Fawkes trilled softly, drawing Dumbledore's attention. Dumbledore stroked Fawkes's wing and turned to welcome the intruding owl.

Attached to its leg was a small parcel containing a single letter and a crystal vial. It was a letter from Remus Lupin, whom Dumbledore had dispatched in secret.

The letter contained a brief explanation of what the shimmering, starlight-like powder inside the vial was.

Andromeda Tonks. Sister of Bellatrix Lestrange, daughter of the House of Black—a witch who had been struck from the family tree for marrying Ted Tonks, a Muggle-born.

She was also an informant who held a crucial piece of the puzzle.

Dumbledore went to the Pensieve and released the memory. Silver particles swirled like a galaxy, forming a hazy image. A prophecy in reverse—the shadow of a star reflected in water, replaying the traces of what had once existed in the world.

Dumbledore stepped into the Pensieve alongside Harry, and together they watched the memory.

A memory holds more than simple information.

The expressions a face makes carry dozens of emotions. The direction of a gaze reveals where feeling points; whether a body is open or closed tells you how much of oneself is being offered to another. A trembling voice conveys the intensity of emotion, and the words that recur most often reveal what a person thinks about when no one is watching.

"Andromeda! You could be so much more glorious! You were born with such extraordinary gifts—why do you insist on making such a foolish choice?"

Bellatrix Lestrange.

Voldemort's most devoted servant—a fanatic who had gone on worshipping him even after his fall.

A woman who placed Voldemort's commands above her own life. If there was anyone she still held dear, it was her sister.

Toward Andromeda in particular—a sister who held different beliefs—she felt both resentment and something close to grief, simultaneously.

"Look at these treasures. He gave them to me!"

"I have no interest in your treasures, sister."

Andromeda resembled Bellatrix, yet was entirely unlike her.

The narrow face, the sharp, irritable eyes—anyone would recognize them as sisters at a glance.

And yet they would be puzzled. How could two sisters, raised in the same household, wear such utterly different expressions?

"Leave them alone. Why do you feel the need to go out of your way to torment them? Their disappearance won't make us any more special."

"No—we are special! More special than any of them. We have his favor! These treasures I've shown you are symbols of his favor! If you change your mind even now, you could earn his favor too!"

"Friends matter more to me than any of that."

Where Bellatrix was stained with fanaticism and hatred, Andromeda was composed. She looked at her sister with something like pity and tried to reason with her.

But the two of them were too different. They were beyond understanding each other through words alone.

They quarreled, and in the end, the rift between them became irreparable. After that day, Andromeda severed all ties with Bellatrix completely.

Throughout, Dumbledore watched Bellatrix in silence.

What her face held was affection and worry for her sister—but beneath that, something more urgent, more elated. She had been trying to persuade Andromeda. Through envy. Through the stirring of jealousy.

She believed the whole world adored Voldemort, and she had no doubt that her own sister Andromeda felt the same. The price of fanaticism.

Bellatrix had boasted of Voldemort's favor—and among the gifts that favor had bestowed upon her was a Horcrux he had entrusted to her keeping.

Hufflepuff's Cup.

"So that's it."

"Yes. It took quite some time... but we've found it at last."

Finding Slytherin's Locket in the Black family home had been a stroke of good fortune.

Dumbledore had traced the locket's provenance and discovered that it had once belonged to a collector named Hepzibah Smith.

He had also established that among her collection was Hufflepuff's Cup, and had been tracking its whereabouts ever since.

The Triwizard Tournament and the Goblet of Fire had cost them some time, but Dumbledore and Harry had at last succeeded in locating the Horcrux.

"I think we may allow ourselves a small celebration. Let us return..."

Dumbledore and Harry stepped back into the headmaster's office. The moment his feet touched the floor, Harry spoke with sharp, barely contained hostility.

"Let's go destroy that soul fragment right now."

"Patience, Harry. There is still much to sort out."
Dumbledore restrained Harry, who was already moving to act. Harry waited, impatient, for Dumbledore to continue.

"As expected, it seems Voldemort entrusted a Horcrux to each of his most faithful servants. The Lestranges... Bellatrix and Rodolphus. They were the last known holders of Hufflepuff's Cup."

"Good. Where are they? We need to find them and interrogate them immediately."

"Oh, I'm afraid that won't be possible, Harry."

He said the words, but Harry could tell that Dumbledore was not particularly troubled by the fact.

"They are currently imprisoned in Azkaban. The Lestranges declared they would sooner be locked away than deny him... and so it came to pass."

"Wait, Professor. They're in Azkaban?"

"Yes."

"Then do we have to break into Azkaban?"

Dumbledore smiled at Harry's audacious reply.

"You've had Sirius nearby for so long that you may have lost your sense of scale, Harry. Azkaban is not a place to be taken lightly. You are subjected to a thorough search upon entry, stripped of your wand, and left entirely helpless. A witch or wizard imprisoned there is rendered completely powerless. If Bellatrix valued that treasure as dearly as she did, she would never have brought it with her."

"All the better, then. The one person who knows where it is happens to be sitting there, helpless."

Harry gripped his wand tightly, as though he might set off at any moment.

"I'll go to this Bellatrix woman and find out where Hufflepuff's Cup is. Bring a vial of Veritaserum along."

"The idea has merit, Harry. But it isn't quite as straightforward as that."

Dumbledore shook his head.

"Azkaban does not grant visits easily. Least of all for the purpose of interrogation. The Ministry, having already handed down life sentences, would never permit an outsider to conduct a private interrogation."

"But you could manage it, couldn't you, Professor?"

Harry's tone carried a hint of a challenge.

"Slipping into Azkaban without anyone knowing, administering Veritaserum, extracting the information. That's within your capabilities, isn't it?"

"Oh, certainly it is."

In a sense, Harry was urging Dumbledore to break the law—but rather than reproach him, Dumbledore simply smiled with his usual warmth.

"You seem impatient. Is it because of Miss Malfoy?"

"...More or less."

It may be difficult to define a person in just a few words—but that rule does not apply to Harry Potter.

Harry's motivations were simple. His first love, his friends, his allies. So long as none of them came to harm, he was pleasant and harmless to everyone else.

Utterly cold-headed and rational, willing to use any means necessary to achieve his ends—yet because his ultimate goal was love, he could never truly go astray. He was, in every sense, Voldemort's mirror image and his opposite.

"However impatient you are, you know I won't take that approach, don't you?"

"I know. However powerful magic may be as a tool, it mustn't be used freely in defiance of the rules. You practice what you preach, Professor—true to form as an educator. Even when the other side is doing every manner of terrible thing..."

Harry's tone was sardonic, but a moment later he let out a breath and muttered.

"I'm sorry. I've been on edge lately and my mouth is running away with me."

"Oh, Harry. Not at all. You're quite right. I am a thoroughly old-fashioned and frustrating sort of person."

Even in the face of rudeness, Dumbledore simply laughed it off with his characteristic ease. He laced his fingers together and spoke.

"But an old, old-fashioned creature must remain exactly that. It may seem stifling, but it is far better than keeping a spark of youth and passion burning in some corner of one's heart. An old man out of step with the times who strains to keep up with them is nothing short of a disaster for those younger than himself."

"Judging by the robes you wear, Professor, I'd say you're living quite youthfully as it is."

"The finest compliment I've received all year. Thank you. In truth, I may not know much about many things, but I've always believed my sense of fashion, at least, has never fallen behind."

Which presumably explained the floral-print pyjamas he occasionally wore, the six-foot-long knitted socks, and the resplendent violet cloak embroidered with glittering constellations. His fashion sense was, without question, something approaching catastrophic.

"Well, in any case, we can return to that subject another time—I have no intention of taking that approach. Setting aside the matter of breaking the rules, doing so risks alerting Lord Voldemort that something is amiss. If it became known that we have a rather thorough understanding of his Horcruxes, Miss Malfoy—who provided the lead that brought us here—would be placed in considerable danger, wouldn't she."

"I suppose so. But if not that straightforward method, how else are we supposed to find out where the Horcrux is?"

"Harry. Where there is an easy method, there is always a harder one. Come now—let's think it through."

Dumbledore occasionally asked Harry for his opinion in this way.

Harry was never quite sure whether Dumbledore genuinely didn't know the answer and wanted to think it through together, or whether he had already seen the whole thing from beginning to end and was simply testing Harry.

Either way, the exercise itself was not without value, and so Harry pushed his mind hard, trying to work out the answer.

"...Does the Lestrange family have any relatives?"

"Most pureblood families are related to one another in some fashion. But I don't believe Bellatrix would have entrusted that object to anyone else. She took enormous pride in Lord Voldemort's favor."

"If she wouldn't entrust it to anyone, then she'd want it somewhere safe—somewhere it could stay hidden from all eyes even in her absence. A secret location, or a vault of some kind."

"A secret location, or a vault. A very good observation. Now then—where might that be?"

"I've never been to their estate, so I couldn't say. The only vault I know of is one."

It felt almost too obvious, and Harry hesitated for a moment before Dumbledore gestured for him to continue.

"Gringotts. If it were me, I'd put it in a bank."

"A reasonable assumption."

Dumbledore gave a firm nod and showed Harry the letter. Written in what looked like a hasty scrawl, Remus Lupin's letter contained his best guess as to the whereabouts of Hufflepuff's Cup.

'Bellatrix Lestrange had no interest in money or material wealth. To her, his gifts would have been something closer to trophies. No one builds a dungeon to hide their trophies. We believe she kept it somewhere with rigorous security, yet somewhere she could take it out and look at it whenever she wished... Most likely Gringotts, in the Lestrange family vault.'

Now that they knew where it was, going to retrieve and destroy it presented no real obstacle. Gringotts was undeniably well-secured, but no fortress was without its gaps.

The defending side was always forced to play the harder game.

Harry closed his eyes.

"That makes three."

He didn't know how many pieces Voldemort had split his soul into, but it certainly wouldn't be double digits. Voldemort simply didn't have that many cherished possessions capable of housing a soul.

Of the four Hogwarts founders' relics—Slytherin's Locket had already been destroyed, and Gryffindor's Sword had not been a Horcrux. Once Hufflepuff's Cup was destroyed, the only remaining relic would be Ravenclaw's Diadem.

"If we find Ravenclaw's Diadem... that should be all the Horcruxes accounted for, shouldn't it?"

"Most likely, yes. Lord Voldemort had a deep attachment to Hogwarts, and nothing short of a founder's relic would be worthy of housing his soul... I find it difficult to imagine he had any other possessions he cared for deeply enough."

"Good."

Harry rose from his seat.

"Let me know when you move on Gringotts. I'll be ready."

"Harry. You have the Triwizard Tournament keeping you occupied. None of us want you participating in a robbery—even if it is in service of defeating Lord Voldemort. Leave this one to someone better suited for it."

"Nothing I like more than having someone else do the work for me. Please make it as soon as possible."

Every time a Horcrux was destroyed, Harry earned the right to draw a little closer to her.

When all of Voldemort's Horcruxes were gone and he could finally deliver the last death—then Harry could truly be with Carina.

Harry had dozens of reasons to kill Voldemort. But the greatest reason had always been just one.

Harry left the headmaster's office.

He unfolded the Marauder's Map. Carina was in her dormitory. It seemed she had no intention of coming out today.

The happy time they had shared felt like a dream. The moment you woke, cold reality came flooding in. A today no different from yesterday greeted him with dry indifference.

Nothing had changed at all—and because of that, Harry could not bring himself to face Carina.

Harry had deceived her. He had driven Moody away to put her at ease, then slipped through the crack that had briefly opened in her heart.

But now that Moody had been revealed as genuine, Harry felt exactly like a great fraud. Like Gilderoy Lockhart, blithely insisting everything would be fine without knowing a thing—no better than a braggart who made promises he had no intention of keeping.

'But not anymore. Once Hufflepuff's Cup is destroyed. And once I find information on Ravenclaw's Diadem.'

He would confess to her again.

By then, Voldemort would have no time to spare worrying about what he had already lost.

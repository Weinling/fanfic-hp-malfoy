[I'm sorry, Carina. It seems I won't be able to obtain Veritaserum after all.

Thanks to your efforts, I've been able to move about with a fair degree of freedom. But a potion like Veritaserum—one that carries the risk of illegal use—is simply too difficult to acquire. On top of that, the Ministry has been consuming more of it lately, so there's no surplus to be had.

Might you be able to obtain some at Hogwarts? In my day, the Potions professors always kept at least one vial on hand. There were no shortage of stories about a few drops finding their way into a student's drink when homework went undone.

As it happens, Professor Slughorn is at Hogwarts now. He was teaching Potions when I attended as well. If you speak to him, I expect he could spare a vial.

You work so hard. It is thanks to your efforts that our family has been spared from harm. I am rooting for you.

P.S. Though it needn't be Harry Potter, specifically.]

Carina let out a small, involuntary laugh at that last line. Then, just as quickly, the weight of the problems stacked before her settled back in, and she exhaled a short sigh.

Veritaserum, brewed by Slughorn. Carina had stolen it before Potions class some time ago.

She had taken only what she needed of most potions, storing the rest away—but the Veritaserum she had taken in its entirety, replacing it with water. Colourless, tasteless, odourless: no one would notice the switch, and no one would be the worse for it, she had reasoned.

But the Veritaserum she had pilfered was used up entirely when Harry had slipped into the seaside villa. Neither Slughorn nor Carina had any left.

"I should have saved a little…."

It wasn't as though there were no way to obtain more. Harry—who had, by some strange turn of events, inherited Snape's estate—would surely be able to procure it if she asked. That much was certain.

The problem was whether Harry would see through her plan.

"…There's no other way."

Better than making the journey to Nurmengard and returning empty-handed. Carina turned and made her way toward Snape's office.

Books fluttered open without a hand to touch them. Pages turned of their own accord, stopping precisely where they were needed. Harry sat surrounded by dozens of volumes and thousands of sheets of parchment suspended in mid-air, utterly still, lost in thought.

Even with the power and knowledge he had absorbed, he could not comprehend all of this at once. On his own, it would have taken a lifetime. But there was a single thread running through everything Snape had gathered—a unifying theme—and following that thread was something Harry could manage alone.

After several days with barely any sleep, poring over every page, Harry finally understood what Snape had been researching.

He murmured, hollow with the realisation.

"Sacrifice…."

Ancient magic. From a time before the discipline had been properly codified—when wands were merely one of many instruments, when sorcery and spellwork had not yet been distinguished from one another. A form of magic born in that primordial era.

A binding that exacted a price, and in doing so, produced a powerful effect upon whoever met the conditions.

The moment he grasped what it was, Harry could only laugh bitterly.

"Devoted to the end…."

Snape's reason for studying this magic was simple enough.

It was the last magic Lily Potter had cast before she died—Harry's mother. And it was her remnant, still flowing in Harry's blood.

Magic with no practical use, demanding a price too terrible to contemplate… and Snape had spent his life studying it anyway, unable to let go.

Harry flicked his wand. The books and parchment that had been arrayed around him like a folding screen fell into neat rows and settled onto the desk. A tap of the wand against the surface, and a thick leather cover yawned open, beginning to swallow the books and documents one by one. Thousands of pages in total—and yet the leather binding consumed them all, orderly and unhurried.

When the last page had disappeared, Harry exhaled slowly.

"I have a rough understanding. What the magic is. How it works. But…."

Would it actually unfold the way the theory described? Could Harry do it? Did he have the resolve? What if he failed?

Could he convince Carina?

Harry was not ready. Not now—not when the desire to live burned this fiercely inside him. In this state, he could never go through with it.

What if he erased the memory of that night? Could he steel himself again, find his resolve anew? But the will to live was like a flame—removing the fuse now would do nothing to dispel the heat already filling him.

No. That wasn't the only reason.

Harry didn't want to erase that memory.

A night watched over by moonlight. She had been spread across the bed like a work of art—something so extraordinary he had been afraid to touch her, as though to do so would be a kind of desecration.

But human beings are creatures of desire, and desire had overcome reverence. Harry had opened Pandora's box that night. He had drunk the red liquid from the grail.

All of it, exactly as Carina had intended. Harry had become someone who could not bring himself to die. In a way, perhaps that too was a kind of sacrifice.

"Haa."

He was mid-sigh when the door swung open.

Harry glanced toward it, caught sight of that beautiful face, and let out another hollow laugh.

Carina Malfoy must have taken up Legilimency. There was no other explanation for how she managed to appear every time he thought of her, as if to test him.

She looked as though she'd stepped out for a casual stroll—no jumper, just a shirt—and she was walking into the room as though she owned it. Harry watched her and said:

"Someone who's usually so particular about manners—why is it you never knock?"

"Don't be ridiculous. We're past the point of needing to knock."

Carina closed the door without a sound, then reached behind her and locked it.

Whether she'd done it deliberately or without thinking, the effect was the same: in that brief moment, a sealed room for two had been created. Harry attempted a moment of meditation to steady himself.

Wait. Why am I the one who has to hold back?

Why should he restrain himself? It had happened once. There was no reason it couldn't happen again. And this room—Snape's room, technically, but still—was about as private a space as one could ask for.

Harry exhaled softly and ran his tongue over his lips.

"I heard the things in this room were left to you. Is there something here—"

Carina was stating her business as she approached the table. Harry had been watching for his moment, and when she drew close without her guard up, he moved—quick and deliberate. He caught her by the waist and pulled her down onto his lap, wrapping both arms around her from behind. Before she could so much as gasp, Carina found herself thoroughly trapped.

She was his quarry now. Settled on his lap, held fast by both arms, he was just beginning to consider where to start—

Carina turned her head just slightly and met his eyes. As if she'd expected exactly this. She continued speaking without missing a beat.

"…You have Veritaserum, I'm told. Give it to me."

Perhaps it was Harry who had been hunted all along. He smiled faintly. From the moment she'd walked through that door, everything had been within her calculations. He felt vaguely as though he'd been manipulated—and yet, oddly, he didn't mind. He could stand to be manipulated like this a few more times.

He tightened his hold and asked:

"What do you need it for?"
"…Can't you just give it to me?"

Harry nearly nodded on instinct. Whatever she wanted Veritaserum for, she was clearly willing to deploy a little charm to get it. For almost any other potion, he'd have handed it over without a second thought. The only reason he hesitated was that this was Veritaserum—and it had come from Snape.

Then something shifted in Harry's expression.

Veritaserum. If she couldn't tell him what she needed it for, this was no ordinary matter. She was planning something, somewhere he didn't know about.

The smile faded. His voice dropped a register, edged with something close to irritation.

"Is it something you can't tell me?"

"…A little."

"You said we're past the point of needing to knock."

"That's not quite the same thing. The heart may still require a knock."

"Really. I thought we'd laid everything bare that night."

Tap. Harry's fingers brushed across the buttons of her shirt. They slipped open one by one, as if by enchantment, and the hem fell slightly apart to reveal a smooth, slender waist.

He traced his fingers along it and murmured:

"If there's still something you're hiding from me, I'm a little hurt. Is it more secret than what happened that night?"

The back of Carina's neck flushed. Her breathing had quickened, her temperature risen slightly—she didn't answer. Harry couldn't see her expression from this angle, but he could imagine the colour of her face well enough.

"I don't want to say. I'm afraid you'll be angry with me."

She probably meant that it was something Harry would object to. But there weren't many things Carina could be planning that he'd truly object to.

Unless—Harry asked:

"Is it dangerous?"

"I don't want to answer that."

Which was answer enough.

Dangerous. Dangerous, was it. Harry let out a short, derisive breath. A different kind of heat from desire rose in his head. When he spoke, his voice carried a sharp edge.

"My beloved."

Tap. Tap. Tap.

His fingertips moved up the front of her white shirt, parting it as they went. Each time his fingers found a button, it came undone as though by magic.

"It wasn't enough that she drank poison in front of me—now she wants to go sticking her head into something dangerous again."

Tap.

The last button gave way. All that remained was the tie. Harry slipped his fingers beneath the knot and drew it slowly to the side. Even the tie surrendered without resistance, falling loose. Everything that had held the two sides of the shirt together was now undone.

The shirt hung from her shoulders by nothing more than habit. One small tug from Harry and it would cease to function as clothing at all.

He toyed with the fabric with unmistakable intent.

"What should I do about this? Should I leave you senseless again, the way I did then? If I keep you from standing on your own two feet, you won't be able to go anywhere dangerous, will you?"

He was holding her from behind, so he couldn't see her face—but the flush spreading up the back of her neck and the slight rise in her temperature told him she was close to breaking. He let his hand trail down her back in a slow, deliberate stroke, and Carina shuddered violently. If she wouldn't tell him, he intended to keep this up until she did. Eventually she'd give in.

"Then will you tell me everything? Anything I ask?"

But Harry's prediction was wrong. He'd expected a little pressure to be enough—instead, Carina, still held fast in his arms, answered him point by point.

"What you inherited from Professor Snape's office. Why you're always in here. What you've found. What it means." She paused. "Can you tell me all of that? Honestly. Everything."

Honestly—yes, he could. He'd have to, eventually.

But if he told her, she would worry. No matter how many times he assured her it would be fine, it would unsettle her. He was certain of it.

Harry considered explaining, then gave up. He didn't want her to know until he had a complete answer.

"…I could. But I'm afraid you'd be upset."

"Same here. If you won't tell me, I won't tell you either."

You won't, so neither will I. Harry stared at her, momentarily lost for words.

How could she possibly call that the same thing? Harry was carrying Voldemort's soul inside him. His magic and his very self were unstable, dangerous—death was a better outcome than losing himself by degrees. At least then the world wouldn't be plunged back into darkness.

Carina, by contrast, was whole. Her body, her soul—still radiant, still beautiful. If Harry weren't in the picture, she could live a perfectly graceful life. Why should she have to take on any risk at all?

Harry pressed the point.

"What if I don't give you the Veritaserum? What then?"

"Then I'll go without it. I'll most likely be making the trip for nothing. And I'll be taking on the risk regardless."

"Wow. Is that a threat?"

"Depending on how you choose to receive it—it could be."

"Ha. So that's how it is."

Harry reached for his wand on the table and extended his hand. A cabinet swung open across the room, and a small vial of clear liquid came sailing through the air toward them.

Carina reached out to take it.

"If you're going to put up walls like that, I can't just hand over something this valuable."

Harry pulled her tight against him—tight enough that she couldn't break free no matter how she tried. With her arms pinned, Carina watched the approaching vial in bewilderment as it drifted closer and came to a stop directly before her eyes.

She startled and looked back at Harry. He gave a single, confirming nod.

"Open your mouth, Carina."

"Harry. You—"

"Three drops. Just three drops, and I'll give you the whole vial."

Carina studied his expression—asking with her eyes whether he was serious, whether he truly meant to dose her with Veritaserum. But when she saw the anger in his face, she let her shoulders fall and closed her eyes.

"…Do as you like."

Harry's finger tapped lightly at the corner of her lips. He slipped it between them, found her tongue, and pressed down gently. The smooth warmth of it seemed to curl around his finger.
He tilted her chin up slightly. Carina closed her eyes in resignation and parted her lips.

The floating vial tilted. Drop. Drop. Drop. Three tasteless, odourless beads of liquid seeped onto the back of her tongue.

"Don't spit it out."

Harry's finger moved through her mouth, thorough and unhurried—a strict sentinel, sealing her lips to ensure the liquid couldn't be expelled, waiting until it had been fully absorbed.

When enough time had passed, he withdrew his finger, slick with saliva. Carina's hazy gaze followed his fingertips. He held them before her eyes, the wet gleam catching the light.

"Now speak only the truth, Carina."

"Mmh… what…."

"Do you like me?"

At Harry's question, Carina nodded immediately. The words followed a beat behind.

"…I do."

"Do you love me?"

She nodded again. This time, no words came. Harry smiled faintly and bit the shell of her ear. A low, breathless sound rippled through the room.

"How much?"

Carina answered between unsteady breaths.

"Quite… a lot…."

"This dangerous thing you want to do—it's for my sake, isn't it?"

The nod this time was so small that he would have missed it entirely if he hadn't been this close.

Harry gathered her against him again. He'd only shifted his hold—but the sound she made was close to a sob.

The shirt had slipped further. His hands were now free to reach anywhere. Completely defenceless, Carina breathed in shallow gasps, waiting for whatever he chose to do.

Harry covered her face with one hand. His index and middle fingers rested over her eyes; his ring finger gauged the rhythm of her quickened breath; his little finger lay across her lips.

He traced the shape of her face as though committing it to memory, and whispered against her ear:

"What would you like me to do?"

Carina answered at once.

"Live."

It was the kind of honesty that scraped the inside of the heart bare. Harry fell silent. In the quiet, Carina turned her face into his hand and whispered:

"I hate it when someone dies…. Especially if it's you. Even imagining it makes me feel sick."

With every ounce of feeling she had, Carina spoke to him. Like a plea. Like a vow.

"Don't die. Not ever. I won't let you. Whatever it takes. I promise."

They say the one who falls in love first is the one who loses. By that measure, Harry had never once won against Carina. He had fallen for her first—and now he was falling again.

He had held the advantage. He had kept her trapped in his arms, teasing her. She had been the one left dishevelled.

But live was the word she had spoken first.

And in the end, Harry could only follow where she led.

Because the one who had been undone from the very beginning—the prisoner whose heart had been taken long ago—was Harry.

"All right. If that's what you want—I'll give you the Veritaserum."

What she intended to use it for, who she meant to use it on—he would hand it over without asking.

Harry smiled, wry and defeated, and let his shoulders drop.

"There. I lose. I never stood a chance. Is there anything else you need?"

"Yes…."

Carina drew out the word and pulled his hand toward her. Before he quite understood what was happening, Harry's arm was pressed against her chest—against the soft curve of it, barely covered by a single layer of fabric beneath the open shirt.

Harry went still. Carina took several slow, deep breaths in that position. Her chest rose and fell with each one. Harry sat rigid, as though he'd forgotten how to move, listening to nothing but the sound of her breathing.

"Touch me."

Caught off guard, Harry swallowed and asked:

"What—where—how?"

"However you like. Wherever. However."

Carina drew his hand close and whispered:

"Let me feel that you're alive right now. And that you'll keep being here with me."

Dishevelled clothes. Unsteady breath. Eyes that looked up at him with a faint glimmer of tears, aching and earnest.

The brakes had broken long ago. There was no stopping now. Like Carina, Harry decided to stop fighting what he wanted.

Then he paused, tilting his head.

Wait. Something's off…. That was water I gave her, wasn't it?

Harry reached out and turned the Veritaserum vial upside down. It was still sealed—not a single drop had escaped.

The liquid he had let fall onto Carina's tongue, under the pretence of Veritaserum, had been water. He had cast Aguamenti and let the drops fall as though they were the real thing—hoping she might speak honestly if she believed she'd taken the potion. But something wasn't adding up.

In that case, Carina's startlingly candid responses were…

Either a placebo effect. Or she knew, and played along.

If the former, there was no reason to point it out and embarrass her. If the latter, pretending not to know was probably what this moment called for.

Harry made the rational choice. Rather than revealing that what he'd slipped past her lips had been nothing but water—he decided to taste those lips instead.

The sun had not yet set. Through the window, a red dusk was creeping toward the lake, watching over them. Long, slanted rays of crimson light fell across the room at an angle.

But even the long night that followed felt short to the two of them. Time had always been the one thing they never had enough of.

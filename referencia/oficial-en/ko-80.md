To a child, the world is a collection of second persons.

The self and the other. And the line drawn between them. That is the entirety of society as a child sees it. To a child, society is a web of lines radiating outward from the self.

That is why children are guileless. They are the center of their world, unbothered by appearances, and so they collide with you honestly, emotion first.

And yet children are sensitive. Their world is not a sturdy thing—so when those around them change significantly, it is, to a child, no different from the whole world changing entirely.

"…Sis."

In the span of barely a year, Draco had become a quiet, watchful child. Old habits die hard—when excited, he still looked ready to bolt in any direction—but lately, there had been precious little to excite him.

There had been a time when Carina had wished, more than anything, for Draco to grow up quickly. Now she missed the insufferable little troublemaker.

A well-behaved child who watched his step was far less pleasant to look at than a guileless, insolent brat.

"Draco. It's late. What have you been doing in the common room all this time?"

"Just… reading a bit."

"Working hard at your studies. Good. You should. If you want to become someone worthwhile, you have to study."

Platitudes really are useful things. They can be deployed at any moment, in any situation. Even when you have nothing real to offer, they let you keep a conversation alive—which is probably why people use them even as they cringe at their own triteness.

The fact that she no longer despised triteness—perhaps that meant Carina had grown up a little, too.

"Is, is there anything I can do to help?"

Draco stumbled over the words, visibly anxious.

Everyone in the manor had tried not to worry him. They all tiptoed around Draco, careful to preserve whatever innocence he had left.

But consideration, at its core, is distance—keeping things from colliding. A child grows uneasy when his cries find no echo. At first Draco had acted out more deliberately, screaming and causing trouble, but as the family around him only withdrew further, he eventually taught himself not to reach out at all.

He had grown up.

"I know defensive spells, and I know every secret corner of Hogwarts inside and out. If you're in trouble—I can sort it out!"

The sentiment was endearing beyond measure. But a second-year's defensive spells would only go so far, and however well he knew Hogwarts, he could hardly know it better than Carina, who had spent five years there.

Carina smiled and met his eyes.

"I'm fine. I know enough on my own. Surely I haven't spent all this time here only to know less than you."

"But you didn't even know about the Room of Requirement."

"The Room of Requirement?"

Reassured by the fact that his sister didn't know, Draco puffed out his chest with pride.

"That's right. The place where that club—Dumbledore's Army—used to meet last year! It's supposed to be a secret, but I'll make an exception and tell you!"

Remarkably, Draco's information turned out to be genuinely useful. After hearing his explanation, Carina made her way up to the seventh floor and found the Room of Requirement.

She walked back and forth three times past the great tapestry on the opposite wall, concentrating desperately on the need for a place to hide something. A small, secretive door appeared, and Carina stepped inside.

The room was packed dense with objects. Ancient books of indeterminate age, magical ingredients that looked dangerous at a glance. Half-finished potions and various tools. Empty bottles that had clearly been smuggled in to drink away from the professors' eyes. Silver platters thick with dust, and various contraband items rattling their teeth from inside iron cages.

It seemed as though every object every Hogwarts student had ever wished to conceal had found its way here. In any case, it was exactly the right place for Carina.

To hide a tree, plant it in a forest. In a place crammed with every manner of junk, nothing Carina brought here would stand out.

"This is it. From now on, all of our work will be done here."

Carina had brought Crabbe and Goyle with her. They stood with their mouths hanging open, overwhelmed by the sheer scale of the space and the staggering quantity of clutter.

"Good lord. All of this is just lost property that nobody's claimed?"

"If no one comes looking, that's what it amounts to."

"Right then. So I can help myself to a bit of it?"

Goyle said this and reached toward a tiara resting atop a large bust. Carina startled and grabbed his hand before he could touch it.

Goyle scowled and snapped at her.

"What are you doing, Malfoy!"

"What on earth are you doing? What if you touch something carelessly and end up cursed?"

Goyle flinched at her words. His eyes slid uneasily toward the tiara.

"C-cursed? Surely not. Something that beautiful couldn't possibly be—"

"All the more reason to be careful, precisely because it's beautiful. Don't you know the saying? There is nothing more foolish than reaching for treasure left sitting alone in an unfamiliar place."

"Ugh."

Goyle seemed to come to his senses and yanked his hand back quickly. His eyes, however, were still full of longing.

"Just look at this jeweled crown. I've never seen anything so beautiful in my life. That silver filigree, those pale blue stones…"

As Goyle refused to look away, Crabbe gave him a sharp nudge.

"Goyle. You're drooling."

"Oh."

"And rein in the greed. Even if you took it now, you couldn't exactly wear it around. Let's call it a spoil of war—once Malfoy completes the mission, we'll come back for it properly. After we've learned how to lift the curse."

"…Crabbe, that's actually sensible for once."

"Excuse me! I always say sensible things!"

Goyle swallowed his longing and fell in behind Carina. She searched her memory as she navigated the room—rounding a corner where books reached the ceiling, passing a rack of battered old broomsticks, stopping when she spotted a bust with blinking eyes, and turning sharply.

"Here. This is the Vanishing Cabinet. The thing we'll need to repair."

Carina rapped her knuckles against a cylindrical wardrobe just large enough for a person to step inside.

"If my assumptions are correct, the matching cabinet is in Borgin and Burkes. If we can repair this one perfectly, it will become a portal connecting Knockturn Alley to Hogwarts."

"Then we just fix it, don't we? Why are we wasting time?"

Goyle immediately drew his wand and leveled it at the Vanishing Cabinet.

"*Reparo!*"

But the spell bounced off the cabinet and struck a large chandelier overhead. The gem-like shards of glass scattered across the floor flew upward and reattached themselves to the chandelier, which promptly began to gleam and sparkle, fully restored.

"Did you really think I hadn't already tried a Repairing Charm, Goyle?"

Carina's reproach was met with a sullen lowering of his wand.

"The Vanishing Cabinet has highly advanced enchantments on it. An ordinary spell won't fix it. It will require rather specific materials and magic. Quite a lot of time and expense, I expect."

Carina placed the potions she had taken from the Potions classroom into a nearby cabinet. She cast a simple locking charm, then turned on her heel.

"This will be our top priority for the year. If you need money, or potions, or anything else—tell me. I'll provide it."

"What about asking Professor Snape for help? He'd help us, wouldn't he!"

Crabbe said it as though the idea had just struck him as brilliant. Carina, who had already settled the matter in her own mind, shook her head firmly.

"No. I still can't trust Professor Snape—and beyond that, this is something I need to accomplish entirely on my own."

She was immovable.

"But he could probably sort it out quite easily…"

Crabbe's voice trailed off with regret, but what could she say?

That was precisely why Carina could not accept Snape's help.

The days that followed were grueling and relentless.

N.E.W.T.-level coursework was staggeringly complex and vast in scope. Students who had sailed through their O.W.L.s with 'Outstanding' marks found themselves screaming at the sight of five-scroll assignments that arrived daily without mercy. Somehow the number of subjects had decreased while the workload had multiplied, and students became convinced that someone had cast a Confundus Charm on their timetables.
Of course, there was no magic involved in what was, in the Muggle world, a perfectly ordinary phenomenon. The students lamented their perfectly un-enchanted schedules and trudged toward the library with the resigned air of cattle being led to slaughter.

Without exaggeration, Carina was roughly twice as busy as any of them. She kept up with her coursework, searched for a way to repair the cabinet, and on top of all that, turned over in her mind the question of how to carry out the mission.

She divided her time into ten-minute increments and pushed through. She squeezed in her prefect duties and Quidditch practice wherever she could, and whenever a gap appeared, she slipped into the Room of Requirement to work on the cabinet.

It was slow going.

Assignments devoured her time, and the cabinet that refused to be fixed wore down her resolve. Prefect duties stripped away her breathing room, and Quidditch practice gnawed through her stamina.

And the weight of the mission itself hovered over her like a Dementor, undulating, shaving away at her soul.

Kill Harry Potter or Dumbledore. A merciless assignment with no room for sentiment.

'If I could kill anyone, it would only ever be Harry.'

Harry might not notice the poison she slipped him at all—he might drink it down with that easy, open laugh of his. Or she could lure him close, wind her arms around his neck as if to kiss him, and let the poison pass between them. She could ambush him from behind. If she had the will to kill him, she could.

But Carina did not want Harry to die. The attachment she hadn't managed to discard, the affection she hadn't managed to sever—it still clung to her, still tormented her.

Even now it was like this. If she watched Harry die before her eyes… it would be Carina herself who didn't survive it.

'If I have to kill someone, then Dumbledore…'

That was even more impossible. How? The most powerful wizard of three eras, a man even the Dark Lord had never managed to defeat—how was a sixteen-year-old girl supposed to kill him? Poison? A curse?

And even if such a poison or curse existed, how would she ever get close enough to use it on Dumbledore?

'Not through ordinary means. Something more meticulous, something that exploits a gap, something that no decent, ordinary person would ever think to imagine…'

Snakes circle in her mind. A stronger, more vicious snake swallows a smaller, more moderate one and grows fat on it. They kill and tear at each other, and the last snake left alive is a hideous, grotesque thing.

Carina carved away at her own morality, her humanity, her soul—and from the wreckage, she built a plan to kill someone.

Time, stamina, ease, will, and soul. Everything that made up Carina was being worn away. Her weight, already with nowhere left to fall, had dropped further still, until her body felt as though it had shrunk in on itself; her complexion, already corpse-pale, had not a trace of color left.

The word 'haggard' might have been coined for her alone.

Even so, Carina kept up appearances. She sacrificed what little sleep she had to brush her platinum hair until it gleamed, and forced her heavy eyes wide open, maintaining a composed smile at all times.

She had to project at least this much ease—otherwise those who watched her for any sign of weakness would think her easy prey.

Of course, she couldn't hide it entirely.

"You're pushing yourself."

Carina was making her way back to the dormitory common room when she startled at a figure that had appeared directly in front of her. She looked up to find Ginny Weasley watching her with an expression of undisguised displeasure.

She must have been more exhausted than she realized. She hadn't noticed Ginny until she was practically on top of her. Carina rubbed the corner of her eye and regarded Ginny through bleary eyes.

Fifth year now, was it. Ginny had grown noticeably, and there was a studied casualness to her appearance that she was clearly trying to pass off as effortless. Her red hair gave her an energetic quality, and her sharp features held a richness of expression that Carina rarely found in herself.

Ginny held out a square envelope.

"An invitation from Professor Slughorn."

"An invitation?"

"He wants to invite you to one of his parties."

Carina had heard rumors in passing. Apparently Professor Slughorn had gathered a select group of students he had his eye on and formed a sort of club. Colloquially known as the 'Slug Club.'

The name was not merely unfashionable—it was actively appalling.

Even Carina, who had a genuine fondness for this sort of 'exclusive' gathering, had heard the name and felt a flicker of relief that she would never be invited. That was how bad it was.

With that in mind, Carina looked at the envelope with genuine puzzlement.

"Why is this being given to me?"

"Professor Slughorn invited you. He'd like you to come to the next Slug Club meeting."

It made even less sense. Professor Slughorn had been conspicuously ignoring Carina. For him to suddenly reverse course and extend an invitation—?

And given his inclinations, the Slug Club would be full of students from the opposing camp.

'Even so… I should go.'

Setting that aside, now that the invitation was actually in her hands, Carina decided she would attend. She thought that being in close proximity to Professor Slughorn might present an opportunity to take something useful.

She tucked the invitation away, then voiced the question that had surfaced immediately after.

"Why you?"

"Why am I the one delivering it? I volunteered. Professor Slughorn was looking for someone to pass it along to you, so—"

"No. Why are you in this club?"

The implication was clear enough beneath the surface: I can understand my being there, but what business do you have? The Weasley family had neither notable connections nor influence. Ginny caught the meaning and ground her teeth.

"…Harry's been busy lately. You may not know that."

"My, there's really no need to update me on his affairs."

"There is every need! If you have even the slightest care for Harry—Sirius died, and you have no idea how hard that hit him, how much he suffered—!"

Ginny's voice rose. Carina answered with cool indifference.

"You really are still so young. Isn't it about time you understood?"

Tired and worn as she was, there was always one last reserve of contempt left in her. That was Carina Malfoy. She narrowed her eyes and looked down at the girl who was one year her junior with a smile of unhurried disdain.

"When will you come to understand what position means? Are you really still this naive?"

"You're the naive one! I know. I've been watching long enough to see it. You and Harry still have feelings for each other!"

The fact that no immediate rebuttal came was because it was true. And also because it was too trivial a matter to bother addressing.

Carina, drained of energy, raised one hand slowly and propped up her chin. She tilted her head slightly and looked at the girl in front of her.

"You genuinely don't understand what position means, do you. Feelings—fine. Let's say they exist. Does that matter? Would he hand over all of your intelligence to me because he has feelings for me? Would I sell out my own people because I have feelings left for him?"

"…What?"

"Feelings are just feelings. Something you can ignore when you need to, or erase entirely. Please—I won't fault you for being young, but don't hold me to your standards."

Ginny's mouth opened and closed. She had apparently still been thinking of Carina as a rival in love. How terribly young of her.

Regrettably, Carina was not something as warm and uncomplicated as a romantic rival. She was an enemy. An enemy of the Order of the Phoenix, of the Weasley family, and of Harry Potter.

"You… are you really going to betray Harry? You're going to stand with them?"

"Betray? I don't know what you mean by that. You seem to think that love requires you to throw everything away."

Carina exhaled briefly and waved a tired hand.

"The trust between us, from the very beginning… it was never that kind, Ginny Weasley. Simply—very simply—we happened to be of like mind for a while."

Ginny's eyes hardened by degrees. In place of jealousy or envy, pure hostility filled her.

"Even so, this is Hogwarts. If you think you can use Harry's feelings to do something here… I'll make sure you join your father so he won't be lonely."

Carina met the clumsy provocation with a smile.

"Very well. Do your best while I'm occupied, little sister of a friend."

Carina brushed past Ginny. The exchange had been sharp as crossed blades, and when it was over, only silence filled the corridor.

Several days passed, and the night of the Slug Club party arrived. Knowing it would be an unpleasant affair, Carina attended anyway.

What she hadn't anticipated was regretting it within minutes of walking through the door.

Professor Slughorn was standing before Cho Chang, practically spraying spittle as he exclaimed.

"Oh! This lovely young lady here is Cho Chang. Everyone knows her, of course? She was the sweetheart of Cedric Diggory, the Triwizard champion!"

Cho's face flushed. Slughorn smiled with satisfaction and raised his glass.
"Not having seen young Diggory myself is my greatest regret, I must say. I should have accepted Dumbledore's offer a year sooner! And at such a young age, already serving as an aide to the Minister for Magic! Tell me, are you still in touch with young Diggory these days?"

"His letters have been… a bit less frequent lately…"

"Dear me. But surely you exchange news now and then? I hear young Diggory has recently been meeting with the Ministers of other countries' Ministries alongside the Department of International Magical Cooperation. Did he not mention where he was going?"

"I—really? Cedric doesn't talk to me much about work…"

Cho shrank slightly as she spoke. Slughorn gave a sympathetic sigh.

"When two people in a relationship are a year apart, there's always a period like this. You both get busy and drift a little. But it will be fine. Once the busy spell passes and he looks back, you'll find your way close again."

His words were reassuring enough, but his interest in Cho Chang seemed to have dimmed somewhat. His attention shifted, and he made his way toward Blaise Zabini with an expression of genuine delight.

"Ah! Blaise! How are you finding this gathering? Not quite up to the grand balls at your estate, I imagine, but still!"

Blaise Zabini, who also served as Slytherin prefect, had a mother who had been married and widowed seven times, and wielded considerable influence on the strength of the enormous fortune inherited from seven fathers.

Carina gave a quiet, contemptuous exhale. The gathering was transparent to the point of embarrassment. Everyone here had either fame or influential connections. Even Carina herself, however much she stood apart from the others. The only exception, perhaps, was Ginny.

In any case, just as his interest in Cho had just visibly cooled, Slughorn seemed to have little interest in Carina either. She had connections, so he had apparently thought to invite her once and see. Naturally, Slughorn paid Carina no attention whatsoever, entirely occupied with shining a light on the evening's true guest of honor.

"Ah, Harry Potter! You really are… remarkable. Lily, James… and yet after that terrible night, you survived. The battle at the Ministry, the night all of London shook… you faced him then, too, and survived."

"Well, I'm standing here, so I must have managed."

"There are a great many rumors surrounding you these days. The Chosen One. That you possess some power beyond that of an ordinary wizard…"

"I wouldn't say I have any particular power beyond the ordinary, but I am The Chosen One, yes. He Who Must Not Be Named chose me, after all. He came in person to bestow his blessing not long after I was born. Where else in the world would you find someone who received such an extravagant welcome?"

It was impossible to tell whether he was being sardonic or genuinely cheerful. Half the room smiled; the other half looked vaguely worried. Even Slughorn seemed unsettled by Harry's breezy manner. Carina, standing a step removed from the center of things, raised a hand to cover her mouth and laughed quietly. She had known Harry long enough to recognize when he was being cutting. Unlike Slughorn, Harry clearly didn't think much of the man.

At that moment, Blaise let his expression convey that he had just heard something absurd.

"Heh. The Chosen One. What a ridiculous—"

"Come now. We're all chosen ones here. No need to be embarrassed about it. That goes for you too, Blaise. Your mother changed fathers more times than anyone can count—and the one person she chose to keep by her side through all of it was you, wasn't it?"

Blaise's face cycled rapidly through several shades of red and purple. But you can't spit on a smiling face, and Harry kept right on grinning.

"All right, all right! How long are we going to stand around talking about family? You might be enjoying yourselves, but those of us with painful childhood memories—me and Blaise—are getting rather depressed over here."

Loud laughter rippled through the room. Harry was unmistakably steering the gathering now, drawing everyone toward him. Carina slipped quietly to the edge of the room and began to take stock of Professor Slughorn's quarters.

The room was considerably larger than any other professor's—likely the result of an Extension Charm. Gold and crimson hangings draped the ceiling and walls, and a cabinet displayed crystal glasses and vials of colorful potions arranged at precise intervals, clearly intended to impress.

What were those potions? Could Carina get to them?

She turned the question over briefly, then smiled with a trace of bitterness. What was she, a common thief? Planning to steal things from the moment she walked in the door. How far had she fallen, to be thinking like this. She was in the middle of that grim self-reproach when—

"Hello there. You must be the famous Malfoy."

A boy built like a bear had apparently walked right up to her without her noticing. She really must have been exhausted. Carina steadied herself and gave a small nod.

"Well! Pleasure to meet you. I'm Cormac McLaggen. Fifth year, Gryffindor, and the future Quidditch captain."

"Future?"

"Once Harry Potter graduates, who else is going to be captain? It'll be me, the next Keeper! It's generally the Keeper or the Seeker who takes on the captaincy, after all—and I've got the broader field of vision!"

Cormac announced this with supreme confidence. Carina gave a faint, amused smile.

"If you're from Gryffindor, you'd be better off not approaching me."

"Oh, all that. I couldn't care less, honestly. What does it matter who your parents are, or whether they're in Azkaban? What matters is right now."

Cormac seemed to have taken her smile as encouragement. He pressed on with enthusiasm.

"And my uncle Tiberius is on good terms with Minister Rufus Scrimgeour himself. Who knows—I might be useful to you."

Whether to call this naïve or simply presumptuous, she wasn't sure.

In another time, she might have laughed in his face. But right now, this brash, unguarded confidence—this complete ignorance of how the world actually worked—struck her as rather appealing.

Probably because of a vague, formless longing for something she had lost.

"I'm not particularly interested in the kind of help you're describing."

"You're hard to please."

"If indifference counts as being hard to please, then every woman in the world is hard to please. Never mind that—show me around this place. That's something I might actually find worthwhile."

As Carina had expected, Cormac seemed to have spent a fair amount of time in Slughorn's company. Likely because he had a prominent relative and an ego that demanded an audience.

Had Carina herself looked exactly like this, once? She smiled wryly and let the thoroughly animated Cormac lead her around Professor Slughorn's quarters.

"There's not much of interest here."

"Right? Just letters from old students and pointless decorations. All completely useless."

The valuable potions and ingredients were locked safely away in the cabinet. Breaking into this room to take them would be next to impossible.

The realization that she had come here for nothing drained the last of her energy. The noise of the party and the mingled smells pressed in on her. Her head swam. Carina swayed slightly with the dizziness.

"Hey, you all right?"

"…Mm. Could you get me something to drink?"

Cormac McLaggen returned quickly with two glasses of mead. Carina sank onto a sofa and sipped at hers. Cormac dropped into the seat beside her without invitation and launched into a monologue.

"Oh, right—you played Quidditch too, didn't you? Then you'd know something about defensive formations?"

The sweet liquid settling into her body made her limbs go heavy. She told herself not to let her guard down, but her body had reached its limit, and the soft sofa and the gentle warmth of the drink were more than she could resist.

She let Cormac's lecture on Quidditch drift in one ear and out the other, nodding off where she sat. Before long, Carina had fallen into a deep sleep.

"—the point being that Seekers all think they're the main character and ignore the formation entirely. If Seekers understood the concept of a flying formation, the team could take a much stronger position in the match…"

Cormac trailed off when he realized Carina had fallen asleep. His pride stung a little—she had dozed off right in the middle of his explanation—but one look at her face, serene and softly breathing like an angel, and he decided to forgive her.

Cormac prided himself on his high standards, but by any measure, objective or otherwise, Carina was well above them. And the way the tension drained from around her eyes when she slept, leaving her looking utterly, disarmingly innocent—he found that particularly appealing.

He was so absorbed in watching her that he was slow to notice someone approaching. He looked up to find Harry Potter staring down at him as though he were a personal enemy.

"Move, Cormac."

A Dementor descending from the sky could not have lowered the temperature further. Cormac felt the hair on the back of his neck stand up.

What remained of his pride made a brief attempt to hold him in place.

"Wh—what? Harry. You two broke up."

"I said move."

Cormac scrambled to his feet in a panic. Pride or no pride, anyone who had spent nearly five years in the same dormitory as Harry Potter could read his anger well enough. This was a moment for turning around and walking away without looking back.

Without sparing a glance for the retreating Cormac, Harry looked down at the sleeping Carina.

She was someone who could never sleep properly outside. And yet here she was, curled deep into the sofa, completely under. It was pitiable and yet she looked so utterly at peace that he couldn't bring himself to disturb her.

Across the room, Blaise was spinning in circles as though dancing. The result of Harry's earlier efforts to draw everyone's attention away. He probably had about a minute.

Harry raised his wand without a word. The sofa rose smoothly into the air and drifted slowly toward the corner of the room. With the party this loud, hardly anyone noticed.

He set the sofa down in a quiet corner, draped his jacket carefully over her, and turned back toward Professor Slughorn.

An old professor who had taught Potions since the days of Tom Riddle, who had been at Hogwarts since before Harry was born. And the only living witness who might know something about Horcruxes.

Harry needed to win his trust, and from it, extract the memory of that time.

Harry was about to make his way back toward the crowd when he found himself meeting Professor Slughorn's eyes directly. The old professor gave a slow, knowing nod.

"So that's why the Felix Felicis… hm."

It seemed that Slughorn's reasons for inviting Carina had, at least in part, something to do with Harry after all.

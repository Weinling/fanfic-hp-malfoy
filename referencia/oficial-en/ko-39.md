After the Christmas Ball, Professor Moody was nowhere to be seen. Professor Dumbledore informed the students:

'Professor Moody is away on an extended assignment of a sensitive nature. In the meantime, someone capable and promising will serve as temporary Defence Against the Dark Arts instructor.'

No one missed the way Snape's face twitched with barely contained anticipation.

'Now then—until Professor Moody's return, your temporary Defence Against the Dark Arts professor will be... Padfoot!'

That expression did not last long before it collapsed entirely.

Clean-shaven and impeccably dressed, Sirius was the sort of man who belonged in a fashion spread. Most of Hogwarts had no idea that this striking figure was the same Sirius Black who had escaped Azkaban the previous year. Those who did know also knew he had already been pardoned—and so they simply smiled.

Snape, of course, did not smile at all.

Where Professor Moody's lessons had been professional and intense, Padfoot's were wildly popular. He was handsome, irreverent, and effortlessly charming. His instruction lacked Moody's technical rigour, but he wove humour into every spell until even the most bewildering concepts became clear. Fred and George, who had been so deeply impressed by Mad-Eye Moody, fell completely under Padfoot's spell—particularly once they learned he had helped create the Marauder's Map, after which they treated him with something approaching reverence. Padfoot, for his part, moved among the students as though he were one of them.

Snape regarded Padfoot with undisguised contempt, but no one found that strange. It was common knowledge that Snape coveted the Defence Against the Dark Arts post, and the contrast between cheerful Padfoot and brooding Snape was as stark as oil and water.

Carina Malfoy, however, noticed that the tension between them ran deeper than that.

"Is something going on between Professor Snape and Sirius Black?"

"Hm? Why do you ask?"

Harry looked at her. Carina spoke with an uncharacteristic note of concern.

"Professor Snape seems genuinely unsettled. He clearly can't stand that Sirius Black has been made a professor."

"So my plan worked."

"What?"

"I suggested it. Sirius wanted to stay at Hogwarts to protect me—I told him he might as well take on the temporary Defence post while he was at it."

Harry said it with the expression of a boy who had just pulled off a particularly satisfying prank. Evidently, he still couldn't bear to see Snape get anything he wanted.

"Snape's been in a state since day one. Leave it alone."

"'In a state.' You really do say whatever you like about him, Harry. He's Head of Slytherin House. Show some respect."

"Respect? For someone like that?"

"Show it anyway. You'd be annoyed if I spoke badly about Professor McGonagall, wouldn't you?"

Harry let out a low groan at the argument he couldn't counter. He answered with a look of profound reluctance.

"...Professor Snape and Sirius were in the same year at Hogwarts."

"Were they? Professor Snape was in Slytherin, and Sirius Black was—"

"Gryffindor. Apparently they've been at each other's throats since then. It's... quite something."

"They fought a lot?"

"Well, 'they'... and 'fought'... are both terms that leave a bit of room for debate."

Harry trailed off with a look of mild discomfort and shrugged.

"Not really my concern, though. Sirius is my godfather and my father was my father—does that make it my problem? That's between the two of them. What's strange is that he takes it out on me. Don't you think?"

"That part, at least, is fair. Your own conduct aside."

"I genuinely don't understand why he keeps coming after me."

Harry shrugged again, looking entirely sincere in his bafflement. Carina stared at him.

"Before you ask that question, Harry, perhaps try reflecting on your own behaviour."

"I don't retaliate unless I'm provoked first. You know that. Honestly, Snape fired the first shot."

Carina had nothing to say to that. She had been among those who had piled on when Snape spent their first year making Harry's life miserable. Dumbledore's intervention had eventually put a stop to it—but the fact remained that Carina had played her part in shaping who Harry had become.

"Same goes for you. I always return what I'm given."

"I—what exactly did I give you?"

"Something rather memorable, the first time we met. Whether you remember it or not."

He said it, then pressed his lips to her hair. Carina's face went scarlet. It was as though his lips were a switch—the moment they made contact, her body stopped obeying her entirely.

She steadied her breathing and spoke.

"The first time we met—Diagon Alley? What did I do?"

"So you do remember."

"How could I forget—"

Harry's face drew closer. Carina, momentarily lost in the memory, caught herself just in time and shoved him away with both hands.

"You've gotten awfully bold lately! Did you really think the mood was just going to fall into your lap like that?"

"Tch. What a waste."

"You've been taking me far too lightly, Harry. If you want anything from me, you'd better earn the atmosphere first."

"Your Highness. Your command is my solemn duty."

Carina shot him a withering look. He still walked that infuriating line—and the moment she showed the slightest displeasure, he pivoted to flattery with uncanny precision. How had it come to this? She looked away and changed the subject.

"Smooth talker. Right—have you found Professor Moody yet?"

Harry's expression darkened. It was not a topic he particularly wanted to raise in front of her.

"We have so little time together as it is. Does it have to be this?"

"Tell me. I need to not be worried."

"In that case, I suppose I have no choice. The short answer is: not yet."

Harry walked her through the recent developments. Since his escape, Moody had left no trace whatsoever, and Professor Dumbledore had maintained his silence. He also mentioned that Sirius would continue teaching Defence Against the Dark Arts until the real Moody returned, then added quietly:

"What I can't work out is this—if he wanted to attack me, he should have come to the bottom of the lake. So what was he actually after?"

"What he was actually after..."

Carina murmured, her voice going flat. Harry read her expression and asked:

"Does something come to mind?"

"No. Nothing. And that's exactly what's strange. That Moody—what was he trying to do?"

"Oh, the usual, I'd imagine. Kill me, like so many others."

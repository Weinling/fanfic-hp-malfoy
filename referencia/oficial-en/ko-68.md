Within the D.A. meetings, there existed a smaller, more clandestine gathering: Diggory's Arm.

Two people led this organisation. Fred Weasley and George Weasley. Hogwarts' foremost troublemakers had assembled their members in secret—recruiting only the most diligent from Hufflepuff, the most daring from Gryffindor, and the most peculiar from Ravenclaw. This group was moving toward a goal that was, by any measure, thoroughly subversive and audacious.

That goal was a Howler printing press.

It was an enterprise grand enough to make Gutenberg slap his own forehead in disbelief.

"Neville! Is the printing done?"

"Y-yes!"

"Then hand it all over! We'll enchant them into Howlers!"

The Room of Requirement answers to all manner of needs. When Fred and George wished for a room capable of printing letters, it provided an antiquated press that looked as though it hadn't been used in over a century, along with every variety of pen and ink imaginable.

There, Fred and George set their plan into motion.

"Howlers can be copied!"

Fred crowed with delight. He cast a Sonorous charm on a letter with his wand, then slid it sideways, where Luna Lovegood received it and sealed it with meticulous care.

Luna was remarkably capable for someone who gave the impression of being perpetually elsewhere. Her father was, however dubious his publication, the editor of a magazine, and Luna possessed a thorough working knowledge of the printing trade.

Luna paused in her folding and turned to look at Neville, who was struggling with the press, and said:

"Neville. If you operate the press like that, you'll attract Tromkacks."

"T-Tromkacks?"

"Yes. Tromkacks. They're black, elongated insects that eat ink and then impersonate letters on the page. Sometimes they wriggle about and change words, causing no end of trouble for editors. They're the demons of the publishing world."

Luna stated this with complete composure. Neville nodded along for a moment, then thought it over and ventured, with a look of cautious scepticism:

"Er... couldn't that just be a misprint?"

"It's the Tromkacks."

"B-but..."

"My father doesn't make mistakes mixing up his a's and e's. It's the Tromkacks."

"R-right! Of course! It's the Tromkacks!"

Neville nodded hastily to cut her off. He was fairly certain it was simply a typo, but contradicting her would have amounted to insulting her father.

"S-so. How do you stop Tromkacks from appearing?"

"You must be careful not to get confused when writing letters with your wand. It can help to say each letter aloud as you write it, one at a time."

"I really do think it's just a typo..."

"Tromkacks."

"...R-right. It's the Tromkacks."

Luna maintained her position without a shadow of doubt. Neville, who had never been particularly assertive to begin with, was swept along by her conviction and nodded. Luna looked at him with evident satisfaction, then took hold of his wand and began to guide him directly.

"You're forming the plates with a spell. You have to be careful here... vowels are easy to muddle. If you don't pronounce them precisely, the Tromkacks will appear..."

"It's really just a mispronunciation, not—ah, all right..."

Meanwhile, Fred and George were channelling every ounce of their creativity into the Howlers. Ones that exploded with a bang, ones that released a foul stench, ones that bellowed while playing background music—the twins poured their singular inventiveness into each envelope.

Lee Jordan, who had been assigned the task of packaging the letters, said with a look of deep satisfaction:

"At this volume, we'll easily surpass the total number of Howlers the Ministry received in an entire year!"

Fred and George nodded in agreement. A prank that reached beyond Hogwarts to the Ministry itself—this was a moment that would go down in the annals of mischief-making history.

"Take that, Ministry. This is our revenge for sending us the great stinking bomb that is Umbridge!"

"Oi, Fred. Don't compare her to a stink bomb. At least a stink bomb is actually fun."

Hundreds of Howlers. Enchanted ones, at that.

The Ministry would know despair today, faced with a righteous deluge.

The Department for the Misuse of Muggle Artefacts, where Mr Weasley worked, was naturally excluded. Fred and George weren't complete ingrates.

Clank, clank. The printing press rattled away noisily. While Fred and George wore the grins of born troublemakers, the enchanted machine churned out letter after letter, each bearing the same message, utterly indifferent to the chaos it was about to cause.

From Carina's perspective, it was advantageous for Umbridge and Cornelius Fudge to remain in power as long as possible. So long as those who refused to believe—and refused to want to believe—in Voldemort's return held the reins, preparations against him and his followers would be delayed.

Beyond that, she needed to cultivate allies within the Ministry to support her father's covert operation to search the Department of Mysteries.

Her work beneath Umbridge as a member of the Inquisitorial Squad was entirely a matter of necessity.

Though, well—even setting that aside.

Wielding borrowed power was, it had to be said, rather enjoyable.

"Hufflepuff. Five points."

Carina said it in passing as she moved by a student—a boy who had been caught in the act of defacing a notice board and was now feigning innocence. He reacted with vehemence.

"W-why? I haven't done anything!"

"You haven't done anything 'yet.' That's precisely why it's only five points. Had you actually defaced the board, it wouldn't end at a deduction."

"Defaced? That's slander!"

"*Accio.*"

Carina flicked her wand, and a 'peel-anything scraper' that had been sitting behind him clattered to the floor. She looked down at him with cold eyes as he scrambled.

"If you have any further excuses, feel free. I'll hear them out, however pathetic they may be."

"Tch... Umbridge's dog...!"

The boy ground his teeth and pushed past her. Carina answered with a derisive snort. Those who couldn't even manage to be a dog weren't worth her breath.

They would all grovel beneath her eventually. Whether the Ministry stood or fell—if Voldemort rose to reign as the Dark Lord amid the chaos, Carina would reign at Hogwarts in kind.

...Provided, of course, that her father executed his part flawlessly.

It was then that she noticed someone sprinting furiously down the corridor. Carina had been about to issue a casual reprimand when she caught sight of the face and stopped short.

"Draco?"

Something quite extraordinary happened.

Draco—who had always seemed perfectly capable of getting along without his sister—threw himself at Carina of his own accord. Had they been at the manor, she would have looked around for an April Fool's prank. Sensing immediately that this was no ordinary matter, Carina asked urgently:

"What's happened, Draco? Has someone been bothering you?"

Draco shook his head without a word. But the fact that he didn't deny it aloud made it easy enough to infer that something of that nature had indeed occurred.
"Tell me, Draco Malfoy. Tell me what someone did and who it was. I will use every means at my disposal to make them pay for it."

"It's... it's just..."

Draco faltered. Carina waited calmly for him to continue. Outwardly calm—but inwardly, she was seething.

Everything she had sacrificed to play the villain, and someone had dared to touch her brother within these walls. She would not let it pass lightly. Anyone who harmed her family, or showed even the slightest inclination to do so, needed to be made to understand what hell felt like.

Whether Draco knew the depth of her resolve or not, he hesitated for a long moment before speaking with careful reluctance.

"People are saying awful things about you...."

Draco's words left Carina puzzled. It was difficult to quickly make sense of why her insufferably arrogant little brother was looking so miserable.

"Don't get the wrong idea! I'm not saying you're not an idiot! B-but! You're less of an idiot than the bigger idiots. You're capable enough to run the Inquisitorial Squad and take points from other students! But they keep calling you—they keep calling you Umbridge's dog, and they're not even fit to be dogs themselves, the stupid—"

But turning the words over a few times, the tender meaning beneath them became clear. He was upset—upset that his sister, who had taken on the role of villain, was being pointed at and condemned by everyone.

She had prepared herself for this. She had steeled herself and resolved to endure it. And yet the pure, uncomplicated worry her little brother was showing her made her chest ache. The thread of family that bound them seized her heart and would not let go.

A cord she could neither loosen nor sever. Carina forced down the tightness rising in her throat and spoke to Draco, still held within her arms.

"Draco. Whether you're willing to admit it or not, I am strong. I am a top student, a prefect, and a member of the Inquisitorial Squad."

"...Yeah."

Carina pressed her palm firmly against Draco's forehead. Her hand met his brow with a sharp, satisfying—if not particularly loud—smack. Draco's eyes went wide and he reached up to touch the spot.

"So stop worrying about me. I am not so weak that a little brat like you needs to fret on my behalf."

"Ugh...!"

She had resolved to stand as Harry Potter's enemy and fight. For her family.

Which meant Draco wearing that worried expression over her current circumstances was precisely what she did not want.

"...Do whatever you like."

Draco said it curtly and turned his back on her. For a brief moment, his small shoulders looked impossibly distant—as though she might never be able to reach them again.

Carina found herself extending a hand without thinking, but caught herself and pulled it back. What good would it do to show weakness in front of a first-year?

Right now, Carina needed to use her situation and the power she held. Leaning on anyone was nothing but a vulnerability.

She reached into her pocket and withdrew a piece of paper. Sent to her in secret by her father, it contained the contents of tomorrow's Daily Prophet forecast.

Mass breakout from Azkaban.

Ministry suspects Igor Karkaroff as accomplice in the escape...

So the Dark Lord had finally succeeded in bringing the Dementors over to his side. He had promised them war—countless prisoners, an endless supply of emotions to feed upon—and in exchange, secured their full and willing cooperation.

This breakout was the opening shot. The Death Eaters who had remained loyal to the Dark Lord until the very moment of their imprisonment—the most brutal and devoted among his followers—had been set free by the Dementors.

Among them was Carina's aunt, Bellatrix Lestrange...

"Good..."

She should have been glad. For the Dark Lord, this was an enormous boon.

But Carina could not bring herself to feel glad. Not entirely.

The Ministry was in greater difficulty than it had ever been.

Anonymous Howlers came pouring in, addressed to Umbridge and the Ministry alike. Somehow, they all seemed to have been written on the same paper with the same ink—and yet they arrived at the desks of Ministry employees one after another, voicing their grievances in no uncertain terms.

'The Hogwarts High Inquisitor is a monstrous figure who wields arbitrary power without restraint... The undersigned cannot express sufficient outrage at this state of affairs...'

'The Educational Decrees are null and void! Null and void!'

'You lot ought to be deep-fried in troll bogeys! School is not a place for politics!'

The Minister for Magic declared that all correspondence from Hogwarts would be refused, but the members of Diggory's Arm simply routed their owls through Hogsmeade or their own homes. The disguise was so seamless that no one could tell a letter was a Howler until they opened it.

Just as the Ministry was shuddering under this onslaught, news of the mass Azkaban breakout arrived, and the Howlers sent by ordinary witches and wizards added to the din.

What on earth is the state of that prison's management? Have you any sense of urgency whatsoever? Letters of complaint and condemnation rained down on the Ministry day after day, making it impossible to think.

And then, seeping in from somewhere, a small rumour began to circulate quietly through wizarding society.

That they were truly aiming for the return of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named.

"Last night, I dreamed that he was pleased."

Snape's office. The cabinet held sealed vials of completed potions alongside their raw ingredients; the desk was as cluttered as its owner's appearance; the space was busy and entirely devoid of any sense of habitation.

It was much the same as when Harry had barged in on a broomstick the year before. The only differences were a gap on one side of the cabinet where something had been removed, and a single pane of glass that had been replaced with a new one.

Snape seemed to notice Harry's gaze and narrowed his eyes, but the past was the past. Harry had resolved not to be held captive by minor incidents that were already behind him.

Harry sat down on the worn sofa—clearly unaccustomed to receiving guests—and folded his fingers together with deliberate calm.

"Bellatrix Lestrange, Rodolphus Lestrange, Antonin Dolohov... the loyalists who devoted themselves to him right up until the moment they were imprisoned. They've finally been freed."

"And what does that have to do with the present situation, Potter?"

Snape said it in his most sepulchral tone. Harry smiled pleasantly and replied:

"Remarkable Occlumency, Professor Snape. I genuinely cannot tell whether you're pleased or grieved by this news."

Harry was learning the art of mental defence from him. Occlumency—the skill of protecting oneself from a magic that reads thoughts and penetrates the mind.

Dumbledore had insisted that Harry needed this skill above all else, and had declared that he would share nothing further until Harry had mastered Occlumency. Harry resented it, but he had no choice but to learn from Snape.

Of course, even as he learned, Harry did not trust Snape entirely.

"Dumbledore is quite something himself. Can't have his mind read, keeps an antidote to Veritaserum on his person at all times—what exactly did he see in a former Death Eater that made him take you in, Professor?"

"Are you interrogating me?"

"Call it a question. Isn't it a teacher's job to satisfy a student's curiosity?"

Harry said it lightly; Snape dismissed it with equal ease. He spoke with a face set like stone.

"I cannot express sufficient despair at the fact that the student receiving my instruction is such an utter blockhead, Potter."

"A lack of aptitude, perhaps. Whether that's the teacher's aptitude or the student's, I couldn't say."

"Do you know why Dumbledore told you to learn Occlumency? So that the Dark Lord's memories and thoughts don't seep into you—as they did in last night's dream. But our hero Harry Potter, rather than defending his mind, has flung it wide open and welcomed the Dark Lord in."

"It wasn't only him I let in, was it? A certain professor who's supposed to be teaching me defence keeps forcing his way into my head under the guise of practical training, so naturally my walls have crumbled and I've been leaving the door open."

"No ambition whatsoever. Remarkable insolence, too. I say without reservation that you are the worst student I have ever had. It is a source of unending lament that there are no more points left to take from you..."

Snape said this while fixing Harry with his black eyes. Harry scowled and held his gaze.

Under ordinary circumstances, Harry would never have been cowed by Snape.
But here, during Occlumency practice—in those moments, Harry could not help but feel diminished in his presence.

"...Regardless, I was asked by Dumbledore, and so I will teach you to the best of my ability. Until you have mastered Occlumency. Today... yes. How are things with Carina Malfoy, in our house? She seems to be having a difficult time of late."

Harry's face crumpled without restraint.

The magic Snape used to teach him was Legilimens—the spell that read memories. He made a habit of deliberately targeting Harry's most private and tender recollections.

Memories that were never meant to be shared. Memories that belonged entirely to the two of them. Every time Snape attempted to pry into them, Harry resisted with everything he had, and had even succeeded a few times—but they were victories that left him feeling raw.

Snape knew precisely when and how many times Harry and Carina had met. He probably understood the nature of their relationship better than anyone except Harry and Carina themselves.

Harry ground his teeth and stared him down.

"If you're curious, come in and read it yourself, Professor Snape."

There was only one reason Harry hadn't refused these lessons.

To prevent someone far more dangerous than Snape from reading his thoughts, he needed to master Occlumency while he had the chance.

If he ever came face to face with Voldemort, and Voldemort read everything Harry felt for Carina—Harry would be in danger, yes, but so would Carina.

"I'm not sure this counts as proper teaching, but I'll block you this time."

Harry issued the challenge. Snape's lips twisted into something resembling a smile.

"I sincerely hope you do. I take no particular pleasure in viewing your happy memories either... I'll count to three. One, two, three. Legilimens!"

Those black eyes bored into Harry. Harry clenched his jaw and pushed back against the magic drilling into his mind like an awl.

Harry was with Carina. She was glaring at him, then turned sharply away.

Meetings, longing. Their bodies had touched; even their hearts had touched—and yet the circumstances they were given kept tearing them apart.

Carina had turned her back, and Harry had reached out. He wrapped his arms around those small shoulders that looked as though they might vanish at any moment.

Don't let him see.

Harry gritted his teeth. This was a secret, a memory, a vulnerability. The more it was exposed, the more it would wound both him and Carina.

To hold her again... I have to protect my own mind.

Occlumency. This was the bare minimum required to be worthy of approaching Carina. To go to her without the ability to keep secrets would be no different from killing her with his own hands.

Block him, Harry Potter. Don't let that gloomy, pitying Slytherin pry any further!

Harry summoned his will and drove out the magic eroding him. A burst of emotion, ignited in an instant, expelled Snape's magic from his body. In that same moment, Harry's consciousness shot forward like a striking snake and was pulled into the depths of Snape's black eyes.

Snape staggered. Suddenly, someone else's memory came flooding into Harry. A slight boy struggling to mount a broomstick, while a red-haired girl watched him and laughed—not mockingly, not cruelly, but with pure, unguarded delight. The boy was mortified by his own gracelessness, and yet her laughter saved him somehow...

"Enough!"

Harry was thrown backwards as though he'd taken a blow to the chest. His body struck the shelf mounted on the wall. Pain flared where his back hit the edge, but Harry was smiling—the smile of someone who had actually done it.

"...Very well, Potter. Your skill has improved somewhat, I'll grant you that."

What had just flashed through him was unmistakably Snape's memory. When casting Legilimens, even the caster had to lower his own mental defences—so when Harry successfully defended himself, Snape's memory had surged back in reverse.

And though he couldn't be certain, the tenderness he had glimpsed in that memory...

Harry smiled slowly and said:

"So even Professor Snape had a first love. What sort of remarkable woman could have satisfied someone as exacting as you? I'd rather like to see her face."

Harry had expected Snape to bristle. People with walls as strong as his tended to react sharply when something inside was exposed.

But to his surprise, Snape showed nothing of the sort. His expression was something Harry couldn't quite name—as though he wanted to be angry but couldn't quite bring himself to it.

Instead, Snape looked at Harry and said:

"You are a Gryffindor, and Carina is a Slytherin."

Harry replied:

"That's common knowledge. Why bring it up now? Was your first love a Gryffindor, by any chance?"

Snape's lips moved as though he were about to say something, then he looked into Harry's eyes and composed himself again.

"Beyond the simple matter of house affiliation, your positions are diametrically opposed. More so than anything that came before... Can you hold onto your feelings, even in circumstances like these?"

"What's this suddenly about? Did glimpsing that memory stir up some old nostalgia?"

"Answer me, Potter. Can you love her, no matter what happens?"

Snape's face was utterly serious. Harry had been about to needle him as usual, but the gravity of his expression made him stop.

For reasons Harry couldn't quite explain, Snape was being entirely sincere—more so than Harry would ever have thought him capable of. He could ignore it, or mock it, but doing so would yield nothing beyond a cheap satisfaction.

Harry squared his shoulders and said:

"Of course. I love her right now, in this very moment."

"Now, perhaps. But as time passes? If something terrible happens? If you betray her, or she betrays you?"

Snape's black eyes met Harry's green ones.

"Can you hold onto that feeling—always?"

Harry bristled.

Fifth year. Five years spent in Gryffindor, and Harry had not forgotten Carina for a single moment. Not when they were at each other's throats, not when they had confirmed their feelings and been together, and not now—now, when they understood each other's circumstances and had turned their backs on one another. Even now, Carina was in Harry's heart.

Whatever Snape's past held, had it lasted as long as this? Surely not—if it had, he wouldn't be alone now.

"Don't worry about me, Professor. If it weren't for that bloody Voldemort, Carina's surname would already be Potter."

Harry would stake anything on it. That much was undeniable. If things had been different, Harry wouldn't have held back.

A world without Voldemort? A world where Harry could look toward the future without a shadow of dread and build the life he wanted?

He would have spent months, or years if necessary, circling Carina—wearing her down gradually, patiently. Carina was not particularly good at saying no. Push hard enough, and then, when the smallest crack appeared, lean in close and whisper.

She might push him away a few times at first, too proud to be honest. But someday, as had happened once before—if they found themselves alone together in a bathtub full of rainbow-coloured bubbles. If she happened to slip and fall into the water, and Harry caught her, and they were left facing each other, soaking wet.

He wouldn't have held back then.

But right now, all of that was a luxury. He couldn't build something new on ground that was shaking, on footing that might give way at any moment.

If he killed Voldemort—if he killed him, and Harry reclaimed his future entirely—then he could have that dream.

"Words are easy."

Snape remained disagreeable to the last. Just as Harry's face was beginning to twist, Snape raised his wand again and said:

"You've rested long enough. Let's go again..."

Cowardly as ever, Snape had shattered Harry's composure before launching his next attack. Harry clenched his jaw and fought to hold his mind together.

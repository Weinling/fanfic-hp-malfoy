The next morning, the back of Cedric's hand bore a fresh scar: I will respect my professors. Yet even after a punishment that bordered on torture, the light in Cedric's eyes had not gone out. He stood in the middle of the Great Hall, placard in hand, just as before.

Umbridge glared at him as though she would have liked nothing better than to cast a Cruciatus Curse, but so long as Dumbledore was watching, she never would.

When it went on for several days, she stopped coming to breakfast altogether.

"Cedric! You've won!"

"Now sit down and eat something!"

But Cedric, with characteristic diligence, kept holding his sign. This was a protest, he said, and stopping halfway would only diminish its meaning. So he continued.

His example seemed to leave a genuine impression on the other students. The way they treated Umbridge shifted considerably.

"Professor Umbridge, I have a question."

Seamus raised his hand.

"On page seventy-eight, in the section on countering curses—could you explain which counter-spells are considered valid?"

"...!"

"Sorry? I was only asking about what's written in the textbook. Is there a problem? You seem rather sensitive to the word counter."

The next morning, blood ran freely down the back of Seamus's hand as well—but the students kept emphasizing the word counter, defiantly, as though daring her to stop them. Punishments and threats proved useless. The word appeared constantly in the textbooks. Children began reading their books with genuine enthusiasm, hunting for every instance of it.

When the number of defiant students far exceeded her capacity to deal with them, Umbridge finally let it go for a time. The students erupted in cheers, savoring the sweetness of victory. The balance of power seemed to have shifted entirely.

Then, without anyone noticing, a new decree was posted. Students gathered as Filch pinned the notice in the entrance corridor.

Educational Decree Number Twenty-Eight.

The High Inquisitor shall have the power to strip or confer upon students certain privileges. Such privileges shall include the authority to oversee and discipline fellow students, and the right to issue detentions and deduct house points.

"...What?"

Under ordinary circumstances, the students might have laughed it off as more pointless posturing. But when they read what followed, the color drained from their faces.

Cedric Diggory. On grounds of insubordinate conduct, failure to comply with instructions, and dereliction of duty in student oversight, his status as Head Boy is hereby revoked.

Furthermore, a temporary 'Special Inquisitorial Squad' is hereby established, empowered to deduct house points, assign detentions, and impose disciplinary measures upon students.

Hogwarts High Inquisitor, Dolores Umbridge.

"She's stripping him of his Head Boy badge?"

"That's outrageous. On whose authority?"

"Even a High Inquisitor can't just do whatever she likes!"

"Actually, she can."

Carina pushed through the crowd, walking with unhurried purpose. No one told the students to move aside—they simply did, parting to let her through.

She turned to face them all. Pinned below her prefect badge was a small silver T. Behind her, Pansy and Montague and a handful of other Slytherins sauntered up with matching self-satisfaction.

"The Inquisitorial Squad. Appointed by Professor Umbridge herself. From now on, we'll be managing you in place of the Head Boy who's been removed from his post. Oh—"

Carina pointed, one finger at a time, at the students who had just been voicing their complaints. They flinched and looked at one another.

"You, you, and you. Five points each. Reason: questioning Ministry directives. If you'd like to express dissatisfaction in future, I suggest burying your face in a pillow somewhere no one can hear you."

"What? Hold on—I'm a prefect! Even you can't just dock points whenever you feel like it!"

"As of now, I can. We're the Inquisitorial Squad. If you don't believe me, go and check for yourself."

Carina swept past them, her long hair trailing behind her. The students stared at one another with blank expressions, then looked—half in disbelief—at the hourglasses standing in the entrance hall.

Gryffindor's, Hufflepuff's, and Ravenclaw's were nearly empty. What little sand remained was trickling upward in reverse.

Only Slytherin's hourglass held its level. The students stared at the near-empty columns with hollow eyes.

D.A. meeting. The Room of Requirement. Before the self-directed Defence practice could begin, the frustration spilled out.

"This is tyranny! No matter how far she pushes things, she can't just strip a Head Boy of his position on a whim!"

Ernie Macmillan was furious. Hufflepuff had produced its first Head Boy in eight years, and they had placed enormous hopes in Cedric—their Triwizard Champion. The other Hufflepuffs had worked hard to give their Head Boy the finest year possible, and their house points had been leading the standings because of it.

And—though it was somewhat unfortunate for Gryffindor—with Harry Potter stripped of his Seeker status, Hufflepuff's chances of winning the Quidditch Cup had risen dramatically.

But now.

"Head Boy stripped, Quidditch players banned... this is insane! Professor Dumbledore would never allow it!"

"But it's a Ministry order! Even Professor Dumbledore can't do anything!"

Everything had unraveled at once. Their points were gone, their Quidditch eligibility revoked. With both their Seeker and captain gone, Hufflepuff's Quidditch season was in crisis too.

"Did we push too hard? Should we never have provoked Umbridge?"

"Don't be ridiculous. To just roll over for someone like her..."

"We have exams coming! We can't afford to waste time on this!"

A collective crisis arrives when outside pressure reaches the breaking point. Whether they would draw closer and resist together, or fracture and scatter—that depended entirely on what they chose to do next.

It was no easy thing. They represented the majority of Hogwarts, but the students had been divided by house for too long. Without a unifying centre, coming together was difficult.

"We need to fight back harder. If we keep retreating, we'll be backed off the edge of a cliff!"

"This was supposed to be a study group for Defence Against the Dark Arts! Why are we fighting Umbridge?"

"This club isn't even officially sanctioned to begin with!"

"So you're going to report us? Like Slytherin—Umbridge's lapdogs?"

"That's not what I said!"

It was chaos. Everyone was talking over everyone else, and the emotional rifts were visibly deepening. Left unchecked, they would soon be turning the spells they'd learned in D.A. on each other.

Someone needed to intervene—but Harry knew it couldn't be him. Rather than stepping in to separate them, he reached for his Enchanted Galleon and called for someone else.

That was when it happened.

"Take that back!"

Draco shot to his feet.

"Umbridge's lapdogs? Slytherin, with its proud traditions, is using her—not the other way around! We don't get pushed around like you lot!"

Under normal circumstances, the hot-headed outburst might have passed without incident. But at a moment like this, it was incendiary. Zacharias Smith of Hufflepuff fixed Draco with a hard stare.

"...And what exactly do you call what the Slytherins are doing, if not running errands for her? Cedric lost his Head Boy badge because of you lot—because of Carina Malfoy."

"Why is this my sister's fault?! You lot couldn't read the room and went too far—that's on you! Even without her, you'd have been knocked down! You brought this on yourselves!"

That might have been true. In fact, it simply was true.

But truth can sometimes land like a blow, and it has a way of igniting the very anger it describes.

A prickling hostility crawled across Draco's skin. Children are perceptive. They read the malice in a glance instinctively, without meaning to.

Dozens of eyes turned cold. Draco, suddenly very aware of how outnumbered he was, began to back away.

"I—I'm not wrong..."

"No. You're not."
Harry stepped forward, resting a hand briefly on Draco's head as he moved past him. Draco ducked behind Harry's back, looking rattled.

"This is the result of choices we made. Blaming someone else for it won't change anything. Instead of arguing about who's responsible, why don't we think about what we do next?"

"You're one to talk, Potter."

Zacharias still had a grievance with Harry, apparently. He bristled.

"None of this would have happened if you hadn't dragged Cedric into it!"

"Cedric didn't do this for me. He chose it for everyone at Hogwarts."

"Ha! You used him! Cedric had no reason to fight Umbridge—"

"Zacharias!"

At that moment, the door opened and Cedric walked in—he'd come running the moment Harry's Galleon had summoned him. Every head in the room turned.

Cedric looked around at them all and spoke.

"I imagine this has been a shock for everyone. But don't lose sight of what we're actually here for. I knew something like this might happen. If you're going to stand up as Head Boy, you have to be willing to put the position on the line. And I think it was worth it."

Good. With Cedric—the most natural rallying point, and the one who had suffered most—back at the centre, the group would pull together again. Harry exhaled quietly and turned to look at Draco, still hovering behind him.

"You missed your chance, Draco. If you'd attacked me just now, you'd have had me completely."

"I—I didn't because it would've been cowardly!"

Draco snapped, indignant. Harry chose not to enumerate the number of times Draco had attacked him from behind.

"The mood's gone sideways. I don't think we'll get any practice done tonight. Head back to your dormitories—I'll walk you out..."

But if Harry walked Draco back, it might raise suspicions. He looked around.

Hermione looked after Draco well enough, but she was Muggle-born. She might end up in an unnecessary confrontation with Draco or the other Slytherins.

Harry looked at Ginny.

"Ginny—would you mind walking Draco back to the Slytherin common room? Please."

Ginny was pureblood and more than capable of holding her own. She didn't dislike Draco, either.

Ginny nodded.

"Fine. Does this count as a favour?"

"Between us?"

"Precisely because we're close, we keep careful accounts. You're buying me a Butterbeer on the next Hogsmeade visit."

"I'd buy you one without any debt attached."

"Is that a promise?"

Ginny flashed Harry a grin and turned to Draco.

"Right. Let's go, Draco."

Draco gave a single nod and fell into step behind her without a word. When Hermione smiled and waved at him, he lifted his hand in a small, almost reluctant wave back.

Harry smiled inwardly. There was something quietly ironic about the fact that the person Draco liked best in D.A. was a Muggle-born, and the second best was a blood traitor.

Cedric's speech was drawing to a close.

"...But I'm not going to stop. You can't win anything by running away."

"We hear you, Cedric."

"We're not giving up!"

The students nodded, steeling themselves. But Cho Chang—Cedric's girlfriend—looked at him with eyes full of worry.

"Cedric... can't you stop now? You're not even Head Boy anymore..."

"I can't, Cho. You know that."

Cedric tried to reason with her, much as he had before. But this time, Cho held firm.

"You've already received the harshest punishment a student can get, Cedric! If you keep going—! You could end up in Azkaban!"

"That's not going to happen."

"It's not impossible! They're doing this at Hogwarts, with Professor Dumbledore right here! What are they going to do when there's no one to stop them—! Hic—"

Cho shook her head, unable to finish the sentence. Cedric looked down at her for a moment, then pulled her firmly against his shoulder.

"Cho. They stripped me of the title, but I'm still Head Boy. I have a responsibility to represent the students."

"...And so?"

"If I stop here, I'm just a dog that tucked its tail and ran. I can't let that be what I am."

"...Even when I'm asking you like this—*hic*—you're still going to keep going...?"

"Please understand."

Cedric may have thought that was enough. Relationships had always come easily to him—his looks and reputation had a way of smoothing things over.

But even Hogwarts' most celebrated champion was not immune to certain kinds of difficulty.

"...Is the title more important to you than I am?"

"What?"

Cho said it through tears. Cedric, who had navigated many a hard moment with please understand, found himself genuinely at a loss when confronted with the real thing.

"How could you think that? They're completely different."

"*Hic*... No, they're not. My worrying about you matters less than the Head Boy position..."

"That—that's not it! But for everyone's sake—"

"What does that matter... when you're the one getting hurt. I—I just... hic..."

Harry offered Cedric a silent moment of sympathy, then quietly rose and gestured to the room.

That's all for tonight. Dismissed.

The others nodded without a word and got to their feet. They filed out in small clusters, leaving Cedric and Cho to their own world.

In the seventh-floor corridor, the students exchanged a few murmured words before beginning to drift toward their respective dormitories—when footsteps approached from the far end of the hall.

"And what might you all be doing up here? Hufflepuff, Gryffindor—and Potter."

Carina Malfoy was walking down the corridor. The silver T badge on her chest caught the torchlight.

The students panicked and clustered in front of the Room of Requirement. The door had already vanished, but a group of students huddled like penguins against a blank stretch of wall was suspicious enough to invite questions.

Harry stepped forward.

"What brings you up here? Looking for me?"

Carina didn't answer. She scanned the corridor. But the door was gone, and the students gathered there had no obvious connection to one another—she couldn't piece together what lay behind them.

"I believe I made it clear that clubs of three or more meeting regularly are prohibited, Potter. Don't tell me you hit your head hard enough to forget."

"This isn't a club—it's a chance gathering, Carina. We were commiserating about the new Educational Decree."

Harry lied smoothly and gestured to the others. They glanced once between Harry and Carina, then made themselves scarce with impressive speed.
It was transparently suspicious. There was simply no proof.

Carina frowned.

"What exactly were you doing up here?"

"I'll tell you—if you can offer me a small amusement in return."

Harry smiled, easy and unrepentant. Carina's expression soured.

"Never mind. It'll be something pointless."

"Or maybe something you could use to put me in a corner?"

"...Even if I asked, you wouldn't tell me. I'd rather find out myself than let you lead me around."

She turned sharply and walked away. Harry fell into step behind her, unhurried.

"So what is it, really? You looked like you were searching for someone."

"Mind your own business and go back to your dormitory. One more gathering like that and you'll be looking at a detention."

"Ah, a detention? That does sound unpleasant. Though I'd probably be fine even if you came at me with something sharp."

"Useless drivel. Five points from Gryffindor. Now get out of my sight."

Carina seemed intent on shaking him off. Whatever had brought her up here, it wasn't him.

A Slytherin had no reason to climb from the dungeons all the way to the seventh floor for a routine patrol. She was looking for something—or someone.

Unless—

Harry said it lightly, as though it had just occurred to him.

"Are you looking for your brother?"

Carina stopped dead. She spun around and seized his arm. Her eyes were blazing.

"...You haven't been doing anything to Draco, have you?"

"Why do you ask?"

Carina's mouth worked silently.

Draco's behaviour had been strange lately. He disappeared somewhere regularly, gone for two or three hours at a stretch, and when he came back he would climb into bed looking oddly drained and exhausted. Once or twice she might have ignored it, but the pattern had repeated enough times that she'd taken notice.

That was as far as she'd gotten. She'd been too busy since taking charge of the Inquisitorial Squad to look into it properly.

And following her own brother around felt a bit much.

"Anyway. You haven't, have you?"

"He's the one who keeps coming at me—I haven't done anything. If I had, do you think he'd have walked back to his room on his own two feet?"

Carina shot him a sharp look, but she couldn't bring herself to deny it. She knew Draco despised Harry; and if Harry had done something, word would have reached her one way or another.

Then what was behind Draco's odd behaviour? She felt the unease but had no choice but to nod.

Still, she couldn't quite let it go. She lowered her voice and fixed Harry with a warning look.

"That had better be the truth. If you so much as touch a hair on Draco's head, I won't forgive you."

"Forgive? Carina. You've got the wrong word."

Harry smiled. Carina, who had been on the receiving end of that smile more times than she cared to count, raised her guard on instinct. He took a step closer.

"Your little brother—the one who's the spitting image of you at that age—comes at me every time he sees me. Shouldn't you be thanking me for letting it go? Even I get a bit irritated when someone keeps hexing me from behind."

"...He's only ten."

"Do I look like someone who discriminates by age? I was fighting fifth-years when I was in first year. Now that I am a fifth-year, you think I can't handle a first-year?"

Harry had no intention of fighting Draco, of course. They were both in D.A., for one thing. He'd also grown too old to brawl with small children. And, if he was honest, he'd developed a quiet fondness for the boy.

Still—he was teaching Draco magic and keeping an eye on him. Surely that entitled him to something in return. He talked himself into it.

"Naturally," Harry said, "my approach might shift a little depending on yours."

"You're despicable."

"Now, now—aggrieved? I've never caused Draco the slightest harm. Given how many times he's launched himself at me, I'd say I've been remarkably generous."

"It won't happen again. I'll speak to him firmly. Just leave him alone."

"Fine by me—but what about you? Draco doesn't exactly strike me as the type who takes instruction well."

It was an accurate observation, and Carina couldn't refute it. She bit her lip. Harry said, with easy warmth:

"It's all right. I'm here. If you're that worried about your brother—do me a small favour. Just a small one. And I'll make sure he's looked after."

Harry drew closer. One hand settled naturally around her slender shoulder; he met her upturned, defiant gaze and held it, still smiling.

With his other hand, he reached up and touched the badge on her chest, turning it lightly between his fingers. The silver T swayed against her soft sweater.

"As you've been so keen to remind me—we're enemies, aren't we? Enemies don't help each other for nothing."

He let the badge go. It swung back and clinked against her prefect badge with a small metallic sound. His hand moved toward her chin.

Then Carina knocked it away—hard. The crack of it echoed in the corridor. Harry flinched. In the same instant, Carina had her wand out and drove the tip into his solar plexus. Harry let out a short, pained sound and stumbled back.

Carina pressed him all the way to the wall and spoke in a low, precise voice.

"Don't flatter yourself, Harry Potter. I am the Slytherin prefect and head of the Inquisitorial Squad. You are nothing but an ordinary student. If I choose to, I can make your life—and the lives of everyone around you—absolutely miserable."

Harry, winded, drew a short breath.

"Is that a threat?"

"Is it? It's exactly what you were doing."

Carina drove her wand in harder. Harry raised both hands. She gave a short, contemptuous exhale and turned away.

"Ha. Siblings, both as bad as each other."

"Speak when you know what you're talking about. Potter. Next time, I won't stop at a warning."

"Understood. Then consider this payment in full for looking after Draco."

The moment the words left his mouth, Harry wrapped his arms around her from behind. It was so quiet and unhurried that Carina didn't notice until his arms were already around her waist.

She started, wand rising to aim backward—

"Is this not a fair price? I'm not doing anything. Just borrowing your back for a moment."

Carina felt the press of a nose against the top of her head. He was definitely rubbing his face into her hair.

The indignity of having someone loom over her from behind was one thing—but the sudden, mortifying thought that she hadn't washed her hair yet was quite another. What kind of psychology was that, exactly?

The heart was a strange thing. Carina found herself wishing the Department of Mysteries would spend less time on prophecy and more time studying it. The future, at least, revealed itself eventually. This feeling, she suspected, would remain a mystery indefinitely.

She slid her wand back into its holster at her thigh, let out a small sigh, and replied.

"I'll overlook your presumptuous behaviour. Don't mistake this for anything else. It's entirely for Draco's sake."

"I know. I prefer it this way too."

There was something not entirely unpleasant about having something solid and warm at her back. Perhaps that was why wizards were so fond of cloaks.

Carina decided she would allow it for exactly ten minutes. Provided, of course, that no one walked past.

# Crítica externa: capítulo 64, *The Date*

*Lectura a ciegas. Continuidad con grep. Nota: 9,5.*

## 1. Veredicto

Es el capítulo más tierno y más triste desde el 43, y paga casi todo lo que ese capítulo dejó abierto: las patatas, la sal, el pañuelo. El final da miedo por lo razonable que suena: Harry repite el *keep you safe* del 39 con sus propias palabras y sin darse cuenta. Falta un solo paso lógico, y es de los que suben el miedo: acaba de contarle la fecha a quien cree que Voldemort puede leer.

**Lo que preguntáis:**

- **¿La felicidad de las patatas es de verdad?** Sí, y sin presagio. *That was all it was. He wanted that understood* y *It’s a good chip* son exactos. Cobra 43:33 (*They had never gone out for chips*) sin citarlo, y que lo haya buscado Hermione paga las entradas del 51.
- **¿Sirius es Sirius?** Sí: *a very happy cow*, *Nice is for people with something to fall back on*, *Does she hit you?*
- **«Pass the salt, Prongs».** Lo cobra muy bien. Dentro de la risa, con *embarrass your children at weddings* justo antes, y sin que él se oiga. *Fifty-nine days* y *There you go* no necesitan nada más.
- **Harry llorando frente al 51.** Funciona. *It was not like the time with Hermione* y *She did not turn her back… or go and rub anything out* convierten una delicadeza en otra opuesta. Ser mirado es lo que no había tenido.
- **El pañuelo.** Paga el 47 entero, y *Blow* es perfecto: lo menos romántico posible.
- **Lyra desde fuera.** Se lee muy bien: *extremely clean*, *Everything about her had been put away*, *in the right place*. Harry lo nota y no lo entiende. Solo sobra el guiño de 64:45 (incoherencia 1).
- **«We shall not have that».** Pesa, y sobre todo porque le devuelve la vida a ella: *somebody who was at home*. Tras el 63, tener una guerra propia es la primera buena noticia.
- **El final.** Creíble y aterrador. *It was just common sense… what you would do for anybody that you wanted to keep safe* es la frase del capítulo. La cadena de frases cortas funciona como un hombre convenciéndose.
- **¿Las frases largas?** Funcionan. Media de 11,4 palabras, con cinco de más de 50, y el inicio *She/He* cae a 32 por 1.000. 64:5-7 y 64:21 fluyen; no se atascan. El 64:79, plano a propósito, es lo que dice que es.

**Nada sexual ni sensual.**

## 4. Rastro de IA

Muy poco. Es un capítulo de cobros, con muchos ecos, pero casi todos sin explicar. Dos detalles: *two small hard spots of colour* (64:103) es la fórmula de Granger en 58:59, y en Lyra suena a plantilla; y *as one wipes a child’s* (64:97) es la voz de Lyra en narración de Harry.

## 5. Correcciones listas para aplicar

**1. El guiño.** 64:45: “Harry thought about it. He thought about a small cold hand with a spoon in it, for some reason. He could not have said why. He had a bad habit of seeing things.” →

> Harry thought about it. He thought about a girl on a terrace, with a spoon in her hand.

**2. El paso que falta.** 64:121, tras “If she didn’t know a thing, then nobody could get it out of her.”:

> He had told her the date. He had told her an hour ago, on this rug, with his glasses off. He looked at that, and then he stopped looking at it.

**3. Sirius encerrado.** 64:33: “He had been a person in a kitchen for ten months, and it had done things to him.” → “He had been a person in a kitchen since the break-out, on Moody’s orders, and it had done things to him.”

**4. El pañuelo.** 64:97: “He had thought it was in Wiltshire.” → “He had not seen it since he put it down on a stair, at thirteen.”

**5. Línea a línea.**

- 64:69: “said that Weasley had the emotional range of a Quaffle” → “said Weasley had the emotional range of a Quaffle”.
- 64:69: “He found that he was looking at the fire” → “He found he was looking at the fire”.
- 64:73: “it had got to be a thing that he kept for the bottom of the stairs.” → “it had got to be a thing he kept for when something had gone wrong.”
- 64:93: “She did not say that it was all right.” → “She did not say it was all right.”
- 64:97: “as one wipes a child’s” → “as you’d wipe a child’s”.
- 64:103: “There were two small hard spots of colour on her cheekbones.” → “She had some colour at last.”

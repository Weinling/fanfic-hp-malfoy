In Little Whinging—a place with not a single wizard besides Harry Potter—Dementors had appeared.

This was not a normal situation. Harry had learned through hard experience that abnormal situations always carried someone's intent behind them.

But whose?

Dementors were the guards of Azkaban. They were supposed to be feeding on the emotions of prisoners, keeping watch over them.

So what were two Dementors doing wandering a back alley here? Who had sent them, and why?

"No, please... help me... I'm sorry..."

At the far end of his line of sight, Dudley was stumbling about in a daze. Muggles couldn't see Dementors, but they could feel the cold—and the fear.

Dudley had clearly been out looking for someone to bully, as usual, when he'd blundered straight into them. The Dementors, apparently thrilled by the taste of human emotion after so long, were closing in on him.

Harry disliked the Dursleys, but he didn't want Dudley dead. He sprinted toward his cousin. With every step closer to the Dementors, the cold bored deeper into his skin, and beloved voices began to surface in his mind.

'Lily, run! I'll hold him off!'

'Not the child!'

'Potter. This is goodbye.'

Harry gripped his throbbing head and forced himself to focus. He still couldn't understand why other people's Boggarts weren't Dementors. How could anyone not be afraid of something that left you feeling this wretched?

The one saving grace was that Harry had mastered the Patronus Charm well over a year ago. He steadied himself and cast the spell.

"*Expecto Patronum!*"

Summoning a happy memory was effortless. He only had to pluck one moment—any single moment—from the countless hours he'd spent with Carina last year. A silver fox burst from the tip of his wand and charged forward, slamming into the nearest Dementor.

The Dementor lurched violently, then wheeled around and fled. The silver fox, having driven them off, padded back toward Harry with unhurried steps. It stopped before him, chin lifted with imperious dignity, and looked up at him as though expecting to be praised.

"Even after we've parted, you still make things difficult for me."

Harry murmured the words and scratched beneath the fox's chin. The fox accepted his attention the way it accepted everything—without a trace of coyness, as though it were simply its due.

When it had taken its fill, the silver fox vanished in an instant.

Dudley, coming back to himself, was turning his head left and right in bewilderment.

"A pleasant dream, gone in a moment—and what's left in front of me is Dudley Dursley..."

Harry muttered gloomily and walked over to him. He took his cousin by the arm and steered him back toward Privet Drive.

"Never thought I'd be lending you my shoulder to lean on."

He sighed.

No doubt a tremendously warm welcome was waiting for him back at Privet Drive. The Dursleys would shout at him, and a letter from the Ministry of Magic would arrive to call him to account for his crimes.

The Minister had been openly working against Dumbledore lately, which meant he might well take aim at Harry too—anyone he considered to be on Dumbledore's side.

"Honestly. Adults can't even let children rest in peace."

Harry muttered darkly to himself and disappeared into the shadowed alley.

As expected, a letter from the Ministry arrived. Citing the Decree for the Reasonable Restriction of Underage Sorcery, it summoned Harry Potter to stand trial for unauthorized use of magic.

A letter from Dumbledore arrived at the same time. It told Harry to sit tight and not do anything rash. It added that the moment he left Privet Drive he risked being attacked, and that people were being sent to collect him.

Harry left the Dursleys to their noise and packed his trunk. Then he spread out the stack of Daily Prophet issues he'd been collecting.

Igor Karkaroff.

Former Headmaster of Durmstrang. Murderer of Barty Crouch Sr. Former Death Eater.

The Ministry had attributed Crouch's death entirely to Karkaroff's villainy. In doing so, they had dredged up his dark past at length, reframing him as a man of singular cruelty and depravity.

The Ministry refused to acknowledge Voldemort's return—officially or otherwise. The Daily Prophet kept repeating the line about Karkaroff acting alone, while churning out article after article attacking Dumbledore for claiming otherwise.

In short: Dumbledore was under political siege. And anyone perceived as standing with him was being targeted indiscriminately.

How bad had it gotten? Bad enough that a fifteen-year-old student—Harry Potter—was being described in print as an "attention-seeking fraud who cheated his way into the Tournament," dressed up in the most refined language the paper could manage.

The Ministry was probably delighted by the Dementor incident. It gave Dumbledore yet another political burden to shoulder in Harry's defense.

But.

Harry began to write. Two letters—one to Ludo Bagman, one to Cedric Diggory.

As long as the Ministry held its current position, there was no way to make people believe Voldemort had returned. But people didn't need to believe in Voldemort's return to start preparing.

If anything, people prepared for a storm they thought they could weather—not for one they believed would destroy them regardless.

While Dumbledore's vast umbrella absorbed every blow aimed at him, Harry and the others—the members of the Order of the Phoenix—were quietly spreading the truth and building their numbers.

A slightly adjusted truth.

Igor Karkaroff was, in fact, You-Know-Who's most devoted follower, and had been working to bring him back. The sequence of events had been rearranged only slightly. The truth itself remained.

"You little—! Tell me right now! What did you do to Dudley—!"

"He nearly had his soul sucked out. Give him some chocolate. It won't do much for his diet, but then again, he's still alive, so."
Harry left Uncle Vernon gaping behind him and finished gathering his things. A loud bang from the front garden followed shortly after. Someone from the Order had arrived to collect him.

That fast? It had to be Sirius. Harry made his way to the door.

"I'm leaving."

"Says who?! After what you did to Dudley! I've had enough of you. Get out! And don't you dare take a single thing out of this house!"

"So you want me to go or not?"

When faced with contradictory instructions, Harry made a habit of interpreting them in whichever way suited him best. He took it as permission to leave, shoved past Uncle Vernon, and strode toward the front door.

Uncle Vernon grabbed him by the arm, planted himself in front of the door, and bellowed.

"Where do you think you're going, you ungrateful little—!"

"Harry!"

Bang.

The door flew open. Uncle Vernon sailed through the air. Aunt Petunia let out a shrill scream—whether because of her husband's sudden flight or because he'd taken half the crockery with him on the way down, Harry couldn't say.

Sirius stepped inside, wand raised, his expression urgent. He paid no attention to Uncle Vernon sprawled on the floor or Aunt Petunia shrieking in the background.

"You cast a Patronus? What on earth happened?"

"It's a long story."

Harry glanced back. Uncle Vernon was clutching his head and hauling himself upright.

"Uncle Vernon. I could explain everything if you like, but you wouldn't believe me anyway, and you don't actually want to hear it. So I'll keep it brief. Something very bad nearly happened to Dudley. I stopped it. You're welcome. Don't mention it."

Harry delivered this with crisp finality and turned on his heel.

"Sirius. Do you have any chocolate left?"

"I grabbed some when I heard about the Dementors, but—"

"Give it here."

Harry took the chocolate frog Sirius handed him and threw it hard across the room. It sailed through the air and landed squarely in front of Dudley, who was sitting at the kitchen table in a stupor.

Even in his daze, Dudley's hands moved on their own, tearing open the wrapper.

Harry watched for a moment, then turned sharply away.

"Let's go."

"Right."

Sirius stepped aside. In the front garden, Lupin, Tonks, and Moody stood watching the door, poised to charge in at the first sign of trouble.

Harry smiled at them.

"Lupin, Tonks—sorry to drag you out. You're still newlyweds."

"We're newlyweds who aren't on active duty, so we might as well be useful for something."

Lupin scratched the back of his head with a slightly sheepish look. Tonks was leaning lightly against his arm, as she always seemed to be these days.

It was a perfectly pleasant sight. So why did it sting, just a little?

Harry smiled quietly and fell into step beside them, heading for the Order of the Phoenix's headquarters: 12 Grimmauld Place.

"Petition. Ludo Bagman, Head of the Department of Magical Games and Sports. Dear Minister, as you are no doubt aware, Harry Potter narrowly missed claiming victory in last year's Triwizard Tournament—a remarkable achievement for a student of his age. Throughout the competition, he demonstrated sound judgment and swift, accurate responses to every challenge he faced. It is my firm belief that Harry Potter had every reason to cast a Patronus Charm, and I have no doubt that his reasons were entirely justified... Hmph."

Cornelius Fudge was deeply uncomfortable.

Harry Potter, a minor, had cast a Patronus Charm. It had seemed like a perfectly good opportunity, and Fudge had seized it—convened a trial, put Harry Potter in the defendant's chair.

But things were not going the way he'd planned.

Petitions had flooded in from every direction the moment word got out that Harry Potter was being tried, and the jurors he'd assembled were proving distinctly lukewarm. They simply didn't think a child casting a spell was serious enough to warrant their presence.

"This is absurd! Are you suggesting that Dementors simply wandered into Little Whinging by coincidence, for no reason at all?"

"I'd like to know that myself, Minister."

Harry Potter spoke with the air of someone who had been genuinely wronged.

"If I hadn't been able to cast a Patronus—if I hadn't happened to learn it—my Muggle cousin Dudley and I would have been Kissed..."

"Is that true, my boy?"

The Ministry's trials ran on a jury system. A panel of distinguished witches and wizards was assembled, the case was heard, and a verdict was reached by show of hands—a somewhat old-fashioned method, but one that was generally swift and reasonably accurate. Its weakness was that juries were susceptible to emotion.

Harry Potter, in particular, was a minor, an orphan, and the Boy Who Lived—the child who had ended Voldemort's reign of terror. Most of the jurors had lived through the war. They came to the proceedings with a baseline of goodwill toward him already in place.

"You truly conjured a corporeal Patronus? What form did it take?"

"A silver fox. Small and rather imperious—doesn't listen particularly well."

"And you've cast it before?"
"Yes. Professor Lupin taught me the charm in my third year. I've used it a few times since."

"A Patronus! That's advanced magic of the highest order. Remarkable. If only you had your wand with you now—I'd ask you to demonstrate on the spot!"

"If we happen to meet outside, I'd be happy to show you. In a place where magic is permitted, of course—somewhere like Hogwarts or Hogsmeade, not in front of Muggles."

Madam Amelia Bones gave a nod of evident satisfaction. Several of the older jurors—the sort who prized ability above all else—followed her lead.

Fudge watched them, and watched Harry working the room, and his expression soured.

Harry was being scrupulously polite to the jury while quietly making the case for his own innocence. That was the problem. He was genuinely likeable to look at, and he was making full use of his circumstances. The jurors kept asking him questions, one after another, and Fudge couldn't get a word in edgewise.

"Order! The defendant has a prior record of underage magic use. In his second year—"

"I handled that situation poorly. My parents died when I was very young, and my Muggle relatives knew nothing about magic. I didn't fully understand what magic was or how it worked back then. I certainly never imagined that a house-elf's magic would be attributed to me—"

'Oh, the poor dear.'

'How dreadful.'

Murmurs of sympathy rippled through the courtroom. Fudge clenched his fist.

Why does his dead parents keep coming up in every single statement he makes?

The tactic was transparent. Appeal to the fact that he had no parents; collect sympathy from the older witches on the panel. It was obvious. It was tired.

But tired tactics had proven their worth across centuries of use. With so much warmth being directed at Harry, Fudge couldn't afford to press him too hard. Push too aggressively, and he'd be the Minister with no compassion, no heart—perhaps no love for children at all. That was not a rumor he needed circulating.

By now the jurors had moved past sympathy into something closer to genuine admiration. If not for the formality of the proceedings, half of them looked ready to go shake his hand the moment it was over.

There was no way to put any real pressure on Dumbledore through this. Fudge clicked his tongue, but he had no good move to make, so he sat and watched.

In fairness, he hadn't intended to destroy the boy entirely. Expelling Harry Potter? Sending him to Azkaban? That would be going far too far. A quiet term in office required a minimum of scandal. This trial had been nothing more than a light prod at an available opportunity.

"Very well. We will now proceed to the verdict... All those who find Harry Potter guilty, please raise your hand."

As he'd expected, not a single hand went up.

Fudge's face twisted. He brought down the gavel.

"Harry Potter. By unanimous verdict... not guilty."

Unanimous. That, at least, he hadn't anticipated.

When the trial ended, the jurors descended from their seats one by one to shake Harry's hand. Harry accepted every handshake and made a point of remembering each face.

"You're Susan Bones's aunt. Thank you for voting for my acquittal."

The witch with the square spectacles and the no-nonsense manner answered crisply.

"I didn't vote in your favor because you're in the same year as Susan! I simply concluded, after examining the evidence from every angle, that a wizard capable of producing a Patronus would hardly cast one merely to show off!"

Even so, Madam Bones allowed herself a faint smile as she continued.

"You meant it, didn't you? About showing us the Patronus later?"

"Of course. If you ever have occasion to visit Hogwarts, send for me."

"Hmm. I may be attending as an O.W.L. examiner..."

Allies. Potential allies. And enemies.

Harry sorted them quietly as he shook each hand. Unsurprisingly, the vast majority fell into the first two categories. Anyone who had welcomed Voldemort—or once had—was unlikely to be sitting on a jury panel.

But.

Cornelius Fudge. And the aides standing at his shoulder.

These were not merely people who refused to accept the truth. They were people who actively worked to obstruct Harry and Dumbledore. As long as they held their positions, any hope of the wizarding world uniting against Voldemort was next to impossible.

What was to be done? Harry observed them with quiet calm and made his decision.

Plan B, then.

He arranged his expression into something suitably troubled and spoke.

"But... being found not guilty doesn't mean it's over. Who sent those Dementors after me?"

"Don't worry yourself about that. The Dementors are under Ministry supervision—it will be sorted out soon enough."

"If they were properly supervised, this wouldn't have happened in the first place. What if someone sent them specifically to target me?"

"Who would bother targeting a student?"

Harry let the pause stretch, as though weighing his words carefully.

"Someone like Karkaroff, for instance. He has a history of coming after me—he did it during the Triwizard Tournament."

Build up Karkaroff. The more dangerous he appeared, the more urgently Fudge would want him caught. And if Fudge was chasing Karkaroff, he would at least be slowing Voldemort down.

If you won't help, then at least don't help the other side.

The war had already begun.

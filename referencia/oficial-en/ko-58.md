As Harry had anticipated, Umbridge was spectacularly incompetent and insufferably self-righteous. What grated on him most was the way she performed the role of a teacher who genuinely cared about her students—as though the performance itself were enough.

The moment they filed into Defence Against the Dark Arts, Umbridge spoke.

"Everyone, please put your wands away."

A ripple of unease moved through the class. Not a single wandless lesson in living memory had ever been worth attending.

The students muttered and stowed their wands. Umbridge opened her handbag and produced a wand that was abnormally short, then tapped the blackboard with it twice. Words appeared.

— Defence Against the Dark Arts —

Back to Basics.

"You have all been taught Defence Against the Dark Arts in a haphazard, non-standardised fashion by a succession of underqualified teachers. Each year brought a new instructor, and it appears that most of them failed to follow the Ministry-approved curriculum. You are significantly behind the standard progression. This will make your O.W.L. examinations this year extremely difficult."

Professor Umbridge smiled and tapped the board again. The first text vanished and was replaced by new writing.

Course Objectives

1. Understand the principles of defensive magic.

2. Learn to identify situations in which defensive magic may be legally employed.

3. Study the various categories of defensive magic.

"From this point forward, please copy down everything I write on the board."

The students moved their quills across their parchment in silence. The only sound in the room was the dry scratch of writing. When everyone had finished, Umbridge spoke again.

"But do not worry. I will teach you systematically and efficiently, following the curriculum provided by the Ministry. Now, everyone please take out your copy of Defensive Magical Theory by Wilbert Slinkhard."

The thud of books hitting desks came from every direction. Umbridge smiled with evident satisfaction and continued.

"Open your textbooks to page five and read Chapter One, 'The Basics for Beginners.' Once you have done so, we will discuss our learning objectives and what lies ahead."

Harry did as he was told and began to read.

Reading a book with no explanation, no lecture, nothing—it was mind-numbing. He stifled a yawn and barely managed to finish a single page. Before turning to the next, he glanced sideways and found Ron not even pretending to read, idly spinning his quill between his fingers. Harry felt a distinct sense of having been cheated.

When he looked to his right, he saw something unexpected. Hermione had not opened her book at all. Her hand was raised.

Hermione defying a teacher's instructions—not even opening her book—was unprecedented. Umbridge had been studiously ignoring her, as though she expected the girl to simply lower her arm and give up.

But Hermione did not lower her arm. As the minutes passed and students began looking up from their books one by one to stare, Umbridge could no longer pretend not to notice. She pointed at Hermione.

"Your name, dear?"

"Hermione Granger."

"Very well, Miss Granger. Have you finished Chapter One?"

"Yes."

"And you have a question?"

"I have no questions about the book, Professor."

Hermione's voice was precise and unhurried.

"I have a question about your teaching policy. This book contains not a single word about how to actually perform defensive magic."

"Perform defensive magic?"

Umbridge said it as though the idea were faintly absurd.

"I cannot imagine what situation would ever require you to use defensive magic. Surely you don't think you'll be attacked during class, Miss Granger?"

"But—"

"I haven't finished, Miss Granger!"

Hermione fell silent. A full twenty seconds passed before Umbridge spoke again.

"You are safe. The Ministry works tirelessly to ensure the safety of students, and Hogwarts is protected by ancient magic of many kinds. Given all that, I fail to see why you would need to learn counter-jinxes in a classroom."

"So we won't be learning any magic at all? In Defence Against the Dark Arts?"

Ron called out loudly. Umbridge raised her voice over his as though to drown it out.

"In my classroom, students who wish to speak will raise their hands! And you are—?"

"Weasley."

Ron answered flatly. Umbridge beamed at him, then turned away. Ron raised his hand with a look of deep dissatisfaction, but she never called on him again.

Harry muttered under his breath.

"Pathetic."

Umbridge had invented the rule that questions required a raised hand, then proceeded to ignore every raised hand and run the lesson however she pleased. It was exactly the sort of authoritarian, bureaucratic manoeuvre you'd expect from a Ministry employee—petty power dressed up as procedure.

In that case, he'd treat her like a bureaucrat.

Harry thought of Uncle Vernon and how he'd handled council workers. With anyone he considered beneath him, Vernon blustered and bellowed. But with anyone he thought outranked him—he never confronted them directly. He'd just mutter his complaints just loudly enough to be heard.

Fancy learning the art of the difficult customer. Harry smiled thinly and let his voice carry just far enough to reach Umbridge's ears.

"Then what was the point of raising our hands?"

"Students who wish to speak in class will raise their hands!"

"Ah—my apologies. That was thinking aloud."

Harry said it with unhurried ease, and slowly raised his hand. Before Umbridge could respond, he continued without pause.

"I do have a question, Professor."

"If you have a question, raise your hand, Mr. Potter."

"I'm the only one with my hand up, so it's my turn?"

As he said it and looked around, the other students hastily lowered their hands. Harry pressed on before Umbridge could get a word in.

"So, if I understand you correctly, Professor—we learn theory in class and are expected to master the practical magic on our own? But wouldn't that be more dangerous, practising without supervision?"

Umbridge blinked her toad-like eyes. She turned a smile on Harry—the kind she probably considered benevolent, though to Harry it looked thoroughly warped and unpleasant.

"I fail to understand what you mean, Mr. Potter. Why would any of you need to actually use magic?"

So Harry pulled his own mouth into a smile. His eyes didn't move at all.

"Because the O.W.L. examinations this year include practical magic?"

"Oh, Mr. Potter. Don't worry. If you read the textbook repeatedly, reflect carefully, and run through the theory in your mind, there is no reason you should be unable to perform the spells when the time comes."

"Without ever having practised beforehand?"

"As I said—if you study the theoretical foundations thoroughly, you will be perfectly capable of casting the spells in the examination hall. Provided you follow my teaching."

"You'll only be here a year anyway. Are you planning to ruin our exams and then simply walk away? That's rather irresponsible."

"I beg your pardon, Mr. Potter?"
"Oh—sorry. That was thinking aloud again."

Harry brushed it off and rose from his seat. He swept a glance over the Gryffindors, who were watching him with barely concealed anticipation, and raised his voice.

"But Professor—four years of Potions has taught us that following a recipe to the letter doesn't guarantee a working result."

A few students snorted. Harry rode the current and added one more.

"And even producing the correct result doesn't necessarily satisfy the professor. That rather depends on whether they're in a mood to throw a tantrum that day. If we can't reliably brew a potion we've made before, how are we supposed to cast spells we've only read about? Surely the minimum standard for having 'learned' something is being able to do it repeatedly?"

The class erupted. Only one person in the room was not laughing—Umbridge, who watched Harry with a smile that had gone very stiff indeed.

The laughter died down one by one as the silence stretched. Umbridge waited until not a single student made a sound, then fixed Harry Potter with a hard stare.

"The teaching methodology is mine to decide, Mr. Potter."

"Well, it's hardly mine to decide, is it? I was only asking—"

"That's enough."

Umbridge arranged her face into a smile of excruciating pleasantness.

"I simply cannot imagine what situation would require any of you to defend yourselves against the Dark Arts. Is someone threatening you?"

"At the very least, I'd say I need to learn. Igor Karkaroff genuinely came close to killing me."

"He is nothing more than a criminal. The Ministry is actively investigating, and highly skilled Aurors are in pursuit. It is not your job to learn counter-jinxes to stop him. That is the Aurors' responsibility. Your job—as students—is to focus on your studies as though nothing is amiss."

"Then perhaps they should catch him quickly and put our minds at ease. Several months have passed and they haven't so much as found a lead. If the Ministry is that incompetent, how are we supposed to feel safe?"

Harry's tone was pure mockery. Umbridge's smile remained fixed only at the corners of her mouth.

"Ten points from Gryffindor."

A deduction—the price of a question. That was blatant abuse of authority. And a declaration of war. Harry smiled back, all challenge.

Umbridge pressed both hands flat on her desk and leaned toward the class.

"The Ministry is working in cooperation with the Azkaban guards to pursue him. He is on the verge of capture, and the moment he is apprehended, he will be handed over to the Dementors and taken to Azkaban. He is nothing more than a deranged wizard—the sort that has always existed and always will. There is no reason for excessive alarm."

"Wonderful! Dementors! How tremendously reassuring!"

Harry applauded with great enthusiasm. Clap. Clap. Clap. The dry sound of his hands echoed alone through the classroom.

The Gryffindors, however, heard the contempt and scorn beneath every word. They knew from experience that Harry in this particular state was one step from detonation, and they sat rigid, waiting for whatever came next.

Sure enough, Harry's voice rang out with cutting sarcasm.

"I might have believed that a little more if it hadn't taken them a full year to catch Sirius Black—only to find him in the back garden of Hogwarts!"

He clapped once, hard, and pressed on.

"Though I suppose that's actually a case where the Ministry's incompetence worked in everyone's favour? Sirius was innocent, after all! Negative times negative equals positive—two failures compounding into a happy accident! Truly remarkable!"

Umbridge's mouth twitched as she stared at him. Harry stared back and kept clapping.

The standoff had begun.

Umbridge held her smile and held her glare. Harry held her gaze and kept his hands moving until his palms had gone red. They continued—on and on—like a duel fought entirely in silence.

The students swivelled between them, helpless.

"Harry…"

Hermione was about to say something, worried about his hands, when Umbridge broke first.

"Mr. Potter. You will serve detention after school as a penalty. It appears you require some special instruction."

"I'd prefer it not to be Ministry-certified, if it's all the same to you. I'd rather not become any more incompetent than I already am!"

Harry gave back every word without flinching.

Professor McGonagall looked back and forth between Harry and the letter from Umbridge he had brought with him. She read it through to the end, then sighed.

"Potter. Sit down."

Harry dropped into the chair. McGonagall settled into the seat across from him, her brow furrowed.

"Is everything written here accurate? You criticised and mocked the Ministry?"

"Yes."

"Right. Knowing you… I suppose that's not surprising."

McGonagall set the letter down and spoke with an expression of grave seriousness.

"Potter, you must be careful. Dolores Umbridge is not an ordinary teacher."

"I know. She's one of Fudge's people."

"And knowing that, you still chose to provoke her?"

"Was there a reason not to?"

Harry answered without a trace of shame. McGonagall's voice took on a cautioning tone.

"Potter. Listen carefully. The Minister has the authority to appoint teachers to Hogwarts by personal recommendation when no suitable candidate is available. Dolores Umbridge is precisely such an appointment—Fudge's own choice."

"And?"

"She is not an ordinary teacher. She is a deeply frightening and cruel woman, perfectly capable of being merciless even to a child. You need to learn when to lower your head in front of her."

Harry burst out laughing. He clutched his sides, then wiped the corners of his eyes, still snickering.

"Come on. Surely she can't be worse than the noseless man who came to personally kill me when I was a baby? Or Snape, who started a staring contest with a first-year the moment I arrived at Hogwarts?"

He was talking about Voldemort and Snape. McGonagall, momentarily lost for words, swallowed her worry and tried again.

"…Potter. Your confidence is admirable. But when an opponent comes at you hard, you must know when to yield. Whatever else she may be, she is a teacher at Hogwarts and she has the legal authority to discipline you. So please—at least in front of her—be civil."

"Ha. No."

Harry cut it off cleanly. McGonagall's expression turned sharp, but Harry paid it little mind.

"I've had rows with Snape, I've actually come to blows with Professor Moody. I fought Quirrell and a Basilisk with my life on the line. When have I ever bowed my head because the other side was strong?"

"Potter! This is not merely a matter of your pride! Professor Dumbledore, in accordance with his duty, informed Minister Fudge of the truth—and Fudge refused to believe it and instead signalled his intention to interfere with Hogwarts. Your behaviour today has only given them greater cause for suspicion!"

McGonagall jabbed a finger at one section of the letter.

"Look at this. She intends to keep you in detention every evening this week—and to continue calling you in for as long as she sees fit! Potter, are you going to waste this critical period on her?"

"Well, as it happens, I've got time to spare since Carina and I aren't together anymore. Convenient."

Harry said it with a dry edge.

"I'll keep Umbridge occupied—consider it my contribution. Everyone else can enjoy their evenings in peace while she's busy with me. I'll carry all the sins on my back."

"Potter!"

"Professor McGonagall."

Where McGonagall was agitated, Harry was entirely at ease.
"Before you advise me, I'd suggest taking it up with Professor Dumbledore first. Whatever I've done, it was Professor Dumbledore who appointed Umbridge as Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher at the Ministry's request. If you bring it to him, wouldn't that resolve things far more quickly than talking to me?"

Warnings about the future, concerns about what lay ahead—none of it was of any use to Harry. With the final confrontation against Voldemort already on the horizon, the future held only one thing for him.

Kill or be killed. That was the fork in the road coming for him.

McGonagall exhaled slowly and tried a different approach.

"Potter. He cannot refuse a lawful request from the Ministry. He is already stretched thin—please don't add to his burdens."

"You're right. That's exactly the kind of person Professor Dumbledore is."

Dumbledore was bound—more than necessary—by law, convention, and procedure. He had told Fudge the truth plainly, regardless of whether Fudge believed him, and had borne the political weight that came with it without complaint. He had not refused when Umbridge was installed at Hogwarts.

In some ways it was the frustrating conduct of a man who never thought to defend himself—but both Harry and McGonagall understood why he behaved as he did. Which was why neither of them said much about it.

Still.

"It seems you don't want to place any greater burden on Professor Dumbledore—but, well. Does it have to be that way?"

Dumbledore sought to become a standard unto himself. As though to embody law and morality in his very person, he strove to be not merely a wizard but something more—a principle made flesh.

In that case, Harry was perfectly willing to use him.

"This is who I am. I'll handle the detentions myself—please take everything else to Professor Dumbledore."

Every evening at five o'clock from now on. Hours spent in the company of an old toad whose very presence turned his stomach—it would be wretched. But Harry had made his decision, and he would endure it.

Whatever happened, the day Harry bowed first would never come.

Meanwhile, in Slytherin's Defence Against the Dark Arts lesson.

Carina endured the extreme tedium with a matching measure of patience.

A lesson that amounted to nothing more than reading a book.

No lecture. No explanation. No discernible philosophy behind it. You could replace Dolores Umbridge at the front of the classroom with Neville's toad and the difference would be negligible—if anything, the toad might be easier on the eyes.

An incompetent teacher, instructing someone without the slightest effort or care. It was the situation Carina held in the deepest contempt—and yet this once, she buried her feelings and smiled.

"I find myself in complete agreement with you, Professor. In a world this safe, to take one isolated threat and present it as some kind of broader pattern—how terribly… reactionary."

The reason was simple.

The woman was useful.

As expected, Umbridge practically leapt with delight at Carina's words.

"If only there were more students at Hogwarts with Miss Malfoy's level of understanding!"

Umbridge beamed, her protruding eyes brightening further as she lavished praise on Carina.

"Exactly as Miss Malfoy says. A common criminal—reprehensible, certainly, but a Dark wizard who has killed one person and gone on the run—I cannot fathom why anyone would make such a fuss!"

"There's likely another reason for it as well."

Carina produced a copy of The Quibbler. It was a back issue she had ordered through Luna Lovegood.

Luna had probably been delighted that even Slytherin students were taking an interest in the magazine. She couldn't have known it would be put to this particular use.

Umbridge took the magazine Carina offered. She opened it, pulled a face of undisguised revulsion, and flipped through the pages rapidly—as though she couldn't bear to look at them. Then, abruptly, she stopped at one page and began to read the article with genuine attention.

Igor Karkaroff is preparing a resurrection of the dead.

The intended subject: He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named.

He is currently travelling to various locations around the world, studying the magic of life and death in preparation for this ritual…

Umbridge's eyes went wide. Carina added her commentary at precisely the right moment.

"This sort of cheap monthly rag churning out baseless rumours—that's why the children are making such a fuss over nothing. Resurrection of the dead? Ha. As if that were even possible."

Umbridge devoured the article, eyes nearly bulging from her head, then snapped the magazine shut with a sharp crack and began to breathe through her nose in short, agitated bursts. The smile she had been wearing at the corner of her mouth had gone rigid.

"This is absurd… it's a conspiracy. A grand act of sedition… something must be done…"

She muttered to herself with frantic intensity, then turned to Carina.

"Miss Malfoy—you said you're the Slytherin prefect?"

"Yes, Professor Umbridge."

"Good. Very clever indeed."

Umbridge nodded with evident satisfaction.

"This cannot stand. If fabrications like this continue to circulate, people will live in a state of constant anxiety. Fear and falsehood will drive everyone to arm themselves, and the wizarding world will descend into chaos and disorder."

"Precisely the kind of thing a stable society must prevent."

"Quite right. Miss Malfoy—the Minister has promised me his unconditional support. Given how serious the situation has become, he will be taking a far more direct hand in matters."

Umbridge raised the subject without preamble, then turned a warm smile on Carina.

"When that time comes, I would very much like the assistance of students as intelligent and discerning as yourself."

In plain terms: become my informant inside Hogwarts.

It was a bold declaration of intent—to use Carina Malfoy as the vanguard of an invasion of Dumbledore's domain. Faced with that intent, Carina took a careful measure of the woman before her.

Woefully lacking in the qualities a teacher ought to possess. The fact that she had marched into Dumbledore's territory with so little preparation suggested an equal lack of situational awareness. Unremarkable in appearance, unremarkable in ability. Sufficient appetite for power, and a degree of political instinct—but the type who made enemies effortlessly, which meant she wouldn't last.

Unqualified. Under any other circumstances, I wouldn't give her the time of day.

Carina had her standards. If she was going to work under someone, she wanted to receive her orders from someone who had earned the right to give them.

She had no desire to work alongside a woman this incompetent.

But.

…Incompetent, and yet far too useful.

Too convenient a tool to leave lying on the ground. She would have to pick it up, even if it meant killing something inside herself to do it.

Close the gate. A lock clicks shut somewhere in her chest. A thick steel door seals over Carina's true feelings without a single gap.

She loathed the woman too much to exchange pleasantries—but the woman had her uses.

Carina let not a single fragment of feeling show, and smiled beautifully.

"It would be an honour to be of service to you, Professor. It has always been the true witches and wizards who guide the foolish."

She could play the villain whenever the role required it. Carina was that kind of person.

Whatever her true feelings, she buried them with practiced ease. She was not ruled by emotion. She manufactured smiles in the service of the best possible outcome.

This small, private feeling—this emotion that could never be permitted—must never be seen by anyone. If it were, it would become a weakness, and that weakness would close around her throat.

Deeper. Deeper still.

She submerged the feeling where no one would ever find it, and met Umbridge's satisfied smile with one of her own.

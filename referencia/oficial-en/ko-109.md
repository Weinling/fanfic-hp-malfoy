The item that arrived by Muggle post was unlike any letter he had ever received. No sender's name—only the recipient's name and address written on a plain envelope. Through the paper, the rigid outline of something solid was already visible. Lucius Malfoy tore open the envelope and extracted its contents.

The first thing his hand closed around was a CD case, roughly the size of his palm. Next came a USB drive, no larger than a finger. Tucked alongside it was a small Post-it note bearing a brief inscription. Lucius read it.

Google Drive

ID : Goldsun

PASSWORD : NTR_f2d

He hadn't the faintest idea what any of it meant. He cast a detection charm on the paper, but found not a trace of magic on it. Lucius set it aside for the moment and placed the note on the table.

He picked up the CD and the USB, but being a wizard through and through, he had absolutely no idea what to do with either of them. One resembled a gleaming disc that might fit over a finger; the other was a rectangular piece of metal vaguely reminiscent of a key. No matter how long he stared, he could detect nothing remotely magical about them.

Some Muggle prank, perhaps. He was on the verge of discarding the rigid contraptions when a half-forgotten memory surfaced.

"Wait. Muggles, they used to..."

Lucius had never had occasion to encounter modern devices such as computers. He had no concept whatsoever of cloud storage or USB drives—but a CD was something he had glimpsed in passing. Recalling that Muggles used round discs to view moving pictures, he took the CD and made his way to the Muggle high street.

What he was actually thinking of was an LP record, but it was fortunate enough that he had arrived at a roughly similar idea. Lucius walked into a shop that dealt in CDs and demanded to know whether the contents of this disc could be viewed. The proprietor found nothing particularly strange about this aristocratic-looking customer. In recent years, as computers and televisions had begun dropping CD players altogether, it was not uncommon for people to come in hoping to play old videotapes or CDs they had unearthed at home.

Lucius settled into a seat in the Muggle shop and had the disc played. A soft crackle of static.

A black screen appeared. He was frowning at it, leaning forward to peer more closely, when he suddenly lurched upright—his torso nearly launching itself out of the chair.

[Do we really have to film this?]

The voice belonged to his daughter—Carina Malfoy, now a fugitive.

[You know why. Magical objects can be traced. The only way to send something without being detected is through Muggle means.]

Harry Potter's voice followed immediately after, grating on his nerves from the very first syllable. The two of them were evidently living together. He had known as much—and yet hearing it confirmed sent a fresh wave of revulsion through him.

Carina's fugitive status was entirely Harry Potter's fault. Voldemort had resided within the boy's soul, and the Ministry had been watching him closely. The Ministry and Harry Potter had agreed to use every resource at their disposal to seal what remained of Voldemort inside him.

And then, on the very day it was to happen—the day Harry Potter came of age—Harry Potter had taken Carina and fled.

Carina had been placed on the wanted list overnight, and Lucius himself had naturally come under investigation. Ministry Aurors had tramped in and out of Malfoy Manor so frequently they had nearly worn through the Floo powder. To avoid suspicion, the Malfoy family had been compelled to disclose the locations of several private retreats.

That had been a painful loss in itself—but not as painful as losing Carina. Because of the Ministry's surveillance, Lucius had not been granted so much as a single opportunity to see her. He and the rest of the Malfoy household had been left to pace and fret, unable to confirm whether she was even alive.

Word had reached him, in roundabout fashion, that Harry Potter had defeated Voldemort's soul alone before fleeing—but could a family's worry truly be so easily extinguished?

Profound anxiety, tangled with fury and grief. And yet at this stage, there was nothing Lucius could do. He fixed his gaze on the black screen and waited for the image to appear.

Carina and Harry were talking, their voices easy and familiar.

[Father won't have any idea how to watch this.]

[Oh, come on. We're sending a CD, a USB, and uploading it to Google Drive? One of them's bound to work.]

[You underestimate my father. He has an absolute aversion to anything Muggle.]

[Still, there's no harm in filming it, is there? You said you wanted to let him know you're all right. Whatever the method, you still need the footage first.]

[That's... well.]

[Let's just do it. We can figure out how to send it after.]

With those words, the screen brightened, and there she was—his daughter, standing against the backdrop of a small room. Lucius had been about to smile at the sight of her when his eyes went wide instead.

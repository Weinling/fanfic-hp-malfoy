The next day, and the day after that. Harry walked into the room without a word and wrote with the same pen.

With every line he wrote, the wounds cut deeper. At some point they stopped healing between sessions, bleeding freely enough to drip.

But Harry had no intention of saying anything Umbridge might enjoy. The only words he ever spoke in her office were "Hello" and "Goodbye." In the silence between, the two of them held their ground like opponents in a duel, neither willing to yield a syllable.

By Thursday, the wounds from the night before hadn't healed by morning. Harry showed no sign of pain. He wrote with almost cheerful energy—cursive once, print once, deliberately sloppy a third time—methodically adding to the tally carved into his skin.

"Very well. Give me your hand, Mr. Potter."

Harry offered it without protest. Umbridge shook her head.

"Hmm. It seems you still haven't learned your lesson. Very well—that will do for today."

"Do I have to come back tomorrow?"

"Of course. One more session ought to drive the lesson a little deeper, don't you think?"

"But tomorrow you said we'd have independent study because of your Ministry visit. With you away, how could someone as deficient in guidance as myself possibly manage detention alone?"

Umbridge smiled broadly.

"That kind of clever scheming tells me we have quite a way to go yet. Don't worry, Mr. Potter. I've arranged for someone to supervise in my absence."

"Oh dear. I do wonder about the qualifications of such a person. What sort of extraordinary individual could possibly stand in for Professor Umbridge? I can't even begin to imagine."

"Don't worry. This person is quite authorised to administer your detention."

Could she be bringing Snape? Unlikely. Snape would never waste his precious time watching a student copy lines—not even for Harry Potter. He had too much contempt for that kind of errand work.

Well. Tomorrow would tell. If it came to it, he could always overturn the room and walk out.

"Goodbye, Professor Umbridge."

Harry said it as pleasantly as he could manage and made his way back to Gryffindor Tower.

The time lost to Umbridge's detention meant every remaining hour had to go toward homework. He barely finished his essays by two in the morning, and the next day he dragged himself to the Great Hall feeling like something scraped off the castle floor.

The one mercy was that Defence Against the Dark Arts was independent study today, which meant he could work on other assignments. He managed to finish practising his Vanishing Spells just before the period ended.

On his way to Umbridge's office, Angelina came charging at him, furious. She demanded to know whether he had any sense of responsibility as Seeker, and told him he might as well skip detention entirely.

But that was a last resort. He needed to keep that option in reserve for the day Umbridge decided to hold the Quidditch matches themselves hostage.

"Is it really that hard to just say you're sorry once? Potter, if you want to keep your place as Seeker, you'd better be working twice as hard as everyone else at practice!"

Angelina snapped. Harry did not say then find another Seeker. Suggesting he might give up the position would send her into a fury that no apology could contain.

He managed to escape only after promising Angelina that if the supervisor turned out to be a Slytherin student, he would hit them with a Confundus Charm and walk out.

Harry trudged toward Umbridge's office.

Truth be told, he was exhausted. He'd fought Dementors over the summer, sat through a Ministry tribunal, and now spent every evening in what amounted to torture disguised as detention. No one—not even Hermione—could have kept up with a schedule like his.

But the world was not particularly gentle, and there weren't many people with the luxury of offering comfort. Harry pushed open the office door, tired and irritable.

"Excuse me."

He shoved it open and stepped inside. As always, the room greeted him with its aggressively girlish décor—all lace and china cats and the particular shade of pink that made his eyes ache. And waiting for him inside was—

A green cardigan. Bright platinum hair.

"You're late, Potter."

Carina Malfoy, prefect badge pinned neatly to her chest.

Harry had never expected to feel anything resembling pleasure in Umbridge's office, so he stopped and assessed the situation with cool precision. He cast a quiet detection spell around the room, checking whether Umbridge had left some enchantment behind, watching Carina carefully.

Nothing. The detection spell found no trace of magic. The person before him was unquestionably Carina—the same self-assured expression, the same faint scent that drifted toward him whenever she was near. Exactly as he'd always known her.

"What's this? Did you come to see me?"

"Professor Umbridge asked me to supervise your detention."

Carina tapped the desk twice. Beneath its lace-trimmed cloth sat a single sheet of paper and the familiar pen.

She smirked.

"You must have made quite an impression, Potter."

"I tend to be harsh with unpleasant women."

Harry said it flatly and sat down. Carina settled herself on the corner of the desk.

Lace and frills on every surface. Carina watching him from close enough to touch.

Even if he couldn't reach out to her—not here, not now—Harry found himself wondering, with genuine bewilderment, whether this was some kind of gift Umbridge had accidentally arranged.

"She doesn't know about us."

Carina said it quietly.

"Even so, don't think about slacking off, Potter. I'm here to supervise your detention. If you don't copy those lines properly, I'll report it to Professor Umbridge."

"Then I'd better work especially hard today. That way she might arrange this again."

"...Let me be clear, Potter."

Carina kept her voice as cold as she could.
"Whatever we were before—right now, you and I are enemies. I will oppose you. I will make things difficult for you. I will make sure you can't get through school in peace. The more this detention wears you down, the better it is for me."

Stating it like a vow didn't wound Harry. A declaration of what one intends to do is proof enough that one hasn't done it yet.

Harry answered with an easy grin.

"What a shame. With you watching, I don't find this particularly difficult."

"...Be quiet and copy your lines."

He did as he was told. He picked up the pen and began, exactly as he had every night before. I must not tell lies.

Red letters formed on the paper. Simultaneously, a wound opened on the back of his hand, then faded. The scar tissue from days of abuse was inflamed and raw, on the verge of bleeding freely.

Harry glanced at Carina and angled his hand so the back of it was hidden from view.

Carina read the words he'd written and murmured to herself.

"*I must not tell lies*... rather a good line, actually."

"Isn't it ironic? To avoid writing it, you'd have to tell one."

"What's wrong with lying to get out of a situation? It's something ordinary people do all the time."

"I suppose I'm not ordinary."

Carina's brow furrowed slightly.

"Potter. Why not bend a little? If you'd just keep quiet—at least while you're at Hogwarts—you wouldn't have to sit through tedious punishments like this."

"I can't do that. My enemies don't care whether I'm at school or not."

"That's because you keep starting things that are beyond you."

Carina's voice carried a thin edge of contempt.

"Did you really think a sensationalist article in some rubbish magazine would make people take notice? No, Potter. No one with any sense believes a word printed in a publication with no authority and no credibility. It'll be a brief curiosity—nothing more than an entertaining piece of gossip. Nothing will change. Nothing."

She gestured at the line he was writing.

"The only thing you can do from here is sit and write I must not tell lies."

Harry smiled faintly and wrote the line. I must not tell lies.

Then he glanced at Carina and added one more sentence beneath it. I love you.

Carina stared at it in silence for a moment, then turned scarlet and snapped at him.

"...That's five points from Gryffindor. Stop that and write properly."

Harry wrote Understood in red letters.

A dull stretch of time passed. The scratch of the pen. The lines accumulating on the page. And the wounds deepening—invisible to Carina, but marking the passage of time as surely as a clock. Outside the window, the sky darkened.

Carina, perched on the corner of the desk, muttered with poorly concealed boredom.

"Copying lines. That's all this is. She told me it would be interesting—where exactly is the—"

Drip.

A small sound. Carina looked toward it, puzzled.

A red stain was spreading across the carpet. Fresh, still wet. Above it stood Harry Potter—or more precisely, the left hand he had tucked against his side.

"Wait. Is that—blood?"

Only then did she see the wound on the back of his hand.

I must not tell lies. The same words he'd been writing, carved into his skin in the same script, bleeding freely.

"What—what is this—"

The wound was on Harry's hand, but the colour drained from Carina's face. Her voice came out unsteady.

"Don't tell me—that pen—"

"Oh, this?" Harry lifted his hand slightly. A bead of blood ran down and fell to the floor with a soft tap. Carina flinched back sharply.

Blood. Red blood, flowing without stopping. Days of reopened wounds had become scars that refused to close.

Harry looked at her stricken expression and said, with perfect calm:

"What's the matter? Compared to Voldemort, Umbridge is nothing. This is barely worth mentioning."

The Cruciatus Curse. Any number of other tortures he'd survived. Against those, this was, quite literally, a minor physical punishment. Extreme by any ordinary standard, yes—but not life-threatening. For someone who had crossed the line between life and death more times than he could count, it was manageable.

For Carina, though—raised in careful comfort, sheltered from this kind of ugliness—it looked like something else entirely.

"...You're an idiot. Why were you hiding it? You handle everything else so well!"

"Being summoned every evening by a toad of a woman and having something sharp carve into your skin—it's a bit embarrassing to admit, isn't it."

"Of all the stubborn, pointless—!"

She'd known he was serving detention every night for hours. She'd assumed it was lines. Copying. She had never, not for a moment, imagined it was this.

The raw wound. The blood dripping steadily. A memory surfaced—first year, Harry walking toward her with blood on him, one step at a time. She couldn't remember exactly what had happened then. But the sight of red life leaving his body—Carina pressed her lips together and watched with something close to terror.

Harry smiled.
No matter how cruel she tried to be, she couldn't manage it. Someone who went pale with fear at another person's wound—how could she ever be a villain?

He wrote something on the paper.

"Carina. You said you were my enemy."

I love you. The red letters appeared on the back of his hand and faded. Carina didn't look pleased, or flustered. She only looked frightened—frightened of the wound on his hand.

Harry smiled and said:

"Shouldn't a proper enemy be able to drink my blood with a smile? That's the minimum requirement, I'd think."

Carina's expression twisted. She knew he was teasing her. But every time she looked at the wound, it hurt as though the injury were her own.

The back of Harry's hand was bloodless white. As though every drop of red had been drawn out of it and expelled—pale enough to belong to a mummy.

"...Stop."

"What?"

"Stop writing, I said!"

She forgot entirely that she was supposed to be supervising him. Harry let out a quiet laugh.

He couldn't stop, of course. The page was barely half full. If he stopped now, Umbridge would conclude that either he or Carina had been negligent. That would only make things worse.

Carina knew this too.

"Hold my hand," Harry said.

Carina bit her lip several times, then turned her face sharply away and extended her hand without looking at him. Harry took it with his bleeding left hand and pulled it toward him.

"Can I write on the back of your hand?"

Before she could answer, Harry picked up the pen and began writing on the back of her hand. The sharp nib scratched across her skin. Red ink glittered briefly, like fireflies, then dried almost instantly.

A stinging pain. But Carina couldn't let it show.

This was nothing more than a pen nib grazing skin. Just that.

But Harry Potter—who carved into his own flesh with every line he wrote—was enduring something many times worse.

Knowing that, Carina kept her mouth shut and watched the letters take shape.

"Ngh..."

Harry worked with the careful precision of a practiced tattooist, letter by letter, on the back of her hand. Each character he inscribed on her skin appeared simultaneously as a wound in the same script on his own.

When he finally lifted the pen—

Harry Potter's owned.

Written in blood-red letters, the words appeared on Carina's hand—and on Harry's.

"If you're going to be my enemy, you'll need to be prepared for all sorts of things, Carina. This kind of thing will happen often. And it might not be me who suffers—it might be you."

Harry brought her hand to his face. The scent of her skin reached him, threaded through the iron smell of blood. He pressed his lips briefly to the red letters, then spoke against the back of her hand.

"If I win—you'll find yourself in a very difficult position. Are you prepared for that?"

Carina answered through clenched teeth.

"...Go ahead and try."

Whether she was mocking him—certain he couldn't—or quietly asking him to, Harry couldn't tell precisely. But if it was the latter, he intended to give her far more than she was expecting.

He kept her hand in his and went on copying his lines.

His blood filled the page, line by line.

On the way back to the Slytherin dormitory after the supervision ended, Carina walked with heavy steps down the corridor.

How was he so composed, bleeding like that from the back of his hand? Did it not even hurt? A few more nights of that and he'd collapse from blood loss.

Just give in a little. What was the point of all that stubbornness?

"Doing something so foolish..."

Carina clenched her fist, then remembered the words Harry had written on her hand. If she didn't remove them before reaching the dormitory, there would be questions she had no interest in answering.

She raised her fist in front of her face and tilted it toward the thin strip of moonlight coming through a window, examining the spot where the letters had been.

But the words had changed.

Harry Potter's owner.

The red script was nearly identical to before—except for the last word. What had been faintly humiliating, faintly shameful, had been reversed entirely in meaning.

Carina stood still for a moment and looked at it. Each letter written with full attention. Written in blood, which made it worse somehow—more painful to look at.

But this too was a mark that had to be erased. She rubbed at the red letters with her finger. They didn't come off with a dry hand. She needed water—

"...Inconvenient."

Carina brought the tip of her tongue lightly to the letters. The faint, metallic taste of blood. She wet the ink just enough, then rubbed at the letters again with her finger.

Like sand taken by a wave, the words scattered and disappeared.

Carina stood looking at the blank skin where they had been, then turned and walked on toward the dormitory.

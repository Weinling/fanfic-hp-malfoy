# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 98: Snape's Worst Memory

Back at Hogwarts, Harry threw himself into motion with a kind of desperate urgency.

His instincts had been right. That night, Carina had been a flower laced with poison. Having tasted its nectar, his attachment to life clung to him like the memory of that evening—viscous, stubborn, refusing to let go.

Perhaps he should never have opened that door in the first place. But now, Harry found he could not bring himself to speak of death so easily.

He didn't want to die.

Had the desire to live always been this sweet, this comfortable? Harry felt himself sinking into a mire. Deep within, he thought he could hear Riddle's mocking laughter, crowing that he too would come to crave eternal life.

That's not it.

Even if it were—I'm nothing like you. I won't crave life by killing someone else.

Harry shook his head fiercely and threw himself into searching for another way to survive.

Then one day, Professor Slughorn came to find him.

"Harry. There's something you need to do."

"What is it?"

"It concerns Severus."

Harry stopped what he was doing and ran a hand through his hair.

Whenever he thought of Snape, he felt a different kind of obligation settle over him. Everyone who had died for Harry until now had cared for him—had sacrificed themselves to save him.

But Snape had despised Harry until the very moment his life went out, and even the echo conjured by Priori Incantatem had shown no warmth toward him. Harry had felt the same.

And yet—when the Killing Curse had come flying, Snape had not hesitated for a single instant before throwing himself in front of Harry.

That must have been for the greater cause. Harry could only conclude as much.

A man who had endured years as a spy without complaint, who had taken on the most dangerous assignments, and who had ultimately given his life for it. Harry, whose attachment to life had grown precious over the course of a single night, could not stand before Snape's memory with any dignity.

Harry bit his lip. Professor Slughorn watched him carefully before speaking.

"After the funeral… we tried every avenue to settle Severus's estate. But his family were all dead or gone, and we had to search for more distant relatives…. And then, in the middle of all that, his will turned up."

A man who had lived as a double agent could have died violently at any moment. It was only natural he'd have left a will.

But Harry found himself wondering something slightly different.

"If it's Professor Snape's will, why come to me?"

Slughorn silently held out a piece of parchment. Harry sensed at once that it was the will, and received it with both hands and a solemn expression. Whatever it contained, it was Snape's will. It deserved to be treated accordingly.

Harry unfolded it slowly and read each word with deliberate care.

[I, Severus Snape, hereby declare the following. All objects and property in my office at Hogwarts, including all potions and reagents stored in the cabinet therein, are to be bequeathed in their entirety to Harry Potter. However, all items must be disposed of by him personally; this authority may not be delegated.]

Having read it through, Harry was bewildered. Snape? To him? Harry was an optimist by nature, but he harboured no illusions that Snape had secretly liked him. Snape's hatred and contempt had been too consistent, too thorough to be feigned.

And the terms of the will were strange. Everything in the office had to be disposed of by Harry personally? No one else could handle it?

While Harry was lost in thought, Slughorn cleared his throat.

"Ahem. Harry. As you know, Potions professors tend to keep their own ingredients and finished potions stored away. Dragon's blood… Acromantula venom… Veritaserum and any number of other preparations…. They're enormously dangerous, and equally valuable—they require an expert's hand. If you'd like, I could help—"

"No. I'll manage it myself."

"…Is that so?"

The will had emphasised personally. Unless the document itself was some elaborate trap designed to torment him, there had to be something in the office that Snape had intended for Harry to find.

Leaving a crestfallen Slughorn behind, Harry went straight to Snape's office.

The door was unlocked. Harry pushed it open and stepped inside. The room that greeted him was exactly as he remembered from Occlumency lessons—cluttered in its own particular way, yet somehow organised.

Perhaps because Snape had been teaching Defence Against the Dark Arts this year, the glass cabinet held noticeably fewer potions than the year before. What space the potions had vacated seemed to have been claimed entirely by books; the shelves looked ready to burst. Harry decided to check the potions first, and began examining them one by one.

Draught of Living Death. The one that had been used to subdue Carina. Its antidote sat beside it. Dangerous, but not lethal, and without any obvious use. He set it aside for now and moved on.

Veritaserum and its antidote. Snape, in his role as a spy, had apparently always kept an antidote to Veritaserum on his person. There would be some use for these. Harry slipped them into his bag.

Beyond those, there was little else worth noting. It seemed Snape had disposed of most of his potions when he stepped down from the Potions position.

What on earth is supposed to be here? Harry muttered to himself as he pulled open a drawer.

"Oh?"

Clink. Thud.

Rolling along with the motion of the drawer came a silver crystal vial, filled with a shimmering white powder. Harry picked it up and held it to the light. On the label, written in a spiteful, cramped hand, was a name.

[To that insufferable Harry Potter.]

"Honestly. Right to the end."

It seemed Snape wanted Harry to view his memories. Harry snorted and closed his fingers around the crystal vial. Whatever memories lay inside—

Harry tipped Snape's memories into the Pensieve and plunged his face in.

He spun wildly and fell somewhere. When he collected himself and looked around, he was in the middle of the Great Hall. Instead of the usual four House tables, hundreds of small desks stood in rows, all facing the same direction.

Harry turned his head inside the examination hall, filled with the scratching of quills. Snape's memory—he had to be here somewhere. It didn't take long to find him. He sat hunched over a desk, his greasy hair hanging down over the parchment as he wrote furiously.

So the greasy hair wasn't something that had developed with age. It was bad enough to leave marks on the paper.

Harry leaned over to look at the exam.

Defence Against the Dark Arts, Standard Wizarding Level.

A memory from Snape's fifth year. Harry frowned. Of all things—what could possibly be found in a memory from when Snape was only fifteen?

Harry wandered slowly through the examination hall as the time stretched on. Then he spotted familiar faces.

James. Sirius. And Lily.

His parents, both long dead.

A dull ache settled in his chest. They had had a youth like this—people who had given their lives for him. People who, had Harry never existed, might have lived lives just as brilliant as his own. They had sacrificed themselves to save him.

And yet here he was—unable to let go of life because of a single night's weakness. If he were to face them in the afterlife, would he be able to hold his head up?

The exam ended. Students cried out or cheered, gathering in small groups and filing outside. Harry followed naturally. Snape was muttering to himself, reviewing his answers, while James and his friends walked in a noisy cluster.

"Moony, what did you think of question ten?"

"Splendid, actually. Name five questions you could ask to identify a Werewolf. Quite an impressive question."

"Think you got it right?"

"Not entirely certain, but more or less."

Lupin put on a deliberately grave expression.

"First: he is wearing my clothes. Second: he is sitting in my seat. Third: his name is Remus Lupin."

Even Harry, who had only been listening, burst out laughing at the joke.

His father and friends claimed a spot beneath the beech tree by the lake. His mother was chatting with some other girls, and from the look on her face—a grimace of dismay—she'd gotten something wrong.

Meanwhile, Snape had settled by the lakeside, cross-referencing his exam paper against a book. When he'd checked through to the last question, he snapped the book shut with a self-satisfied smile and tucked it into his bag.

He was insufferable even back then. Something about those smugly squared shoulders was deeply irritating.

By a remarkable coincidence, it turned out Harry wasn't the only one who found Snape objectionable. James, wearing a mischievous expression, sauntered over and started in on Snape. Snape reacted with the reflexes of someone who had been attacked many times before—his response was swift, but he still ended up losing his wand to James and Sirius.

"What—"

James looked enough like Harry that Harry had the unsettling sensation of watching himself bully a young Snape. Two against one wasn't enough—they had to make him foam at the mouth, and humiliate him in front of everyone…

"Oh."

It seemed that at this age, his father had been something of a bully with a rather underdeveloped conscience. From everything Harry had observed, Snape hadn't actually done anything to provoke this—James had simply noticed him, and that was apparently sufficient reason to hang him upside down in front of a crowd and make a spectacle of him.

Wearing a face that looked just like Harry's, laughing like he was having the time of his life.
No wonder he used to scowl every time he looked at Harry. Harry could understand Snape's feelings, at least a little.

"I'm my own person. I'm separate from my father. If you're going to take it out on someone just because they look alike, you've got no business being a teacher."

Harry drew the line simply. His mental fortitude was too solid to be shaken by something like this. Besides—wasn't this a memory Snape had edited himself? There was no need to pass judgement without knowing the full story.

His illusions about his father had cracked somewhat. That was all. Harry settled in comfortably and watched the scene unfold.

James and Sirius tormented Snape with cheerful cruelty. Pettigrew and Lupin watched from a distance—Pettigrew looking as though he lacked the ability to join in, Lupin looking as though his conscience had gotten the better of him and he'd turned his face away entirely.

Whatever the case, it was clear Snape's reputation wasn't a good one. With this many witnesses, not a single person moved to stop James and Sirius—they were all too busy watching and laughing.

All except one.

"Potter, would you stop this pathetic nonsense!"

A girl with the same green eyes as Harry and long red hair came striding over. Harry's mother—Lily Evans. James lit up at once, as though he'd been waiting for exactly this.

Harry understood then why James had made such a conspicuous scene.

"So that's what this was. He was doing it to get Mum's attention?"

If that was the case, why not simply walk up to her? Why drag some innocent bystander into it as bait? Was Snape just a lure to draw Lily out? Harry was beginning to feel a creeping disillusionment with his father and was about to look away—

—when, by chance, his eyes met Snape's. There was no way the Snape in the memory could see Harry, but Harry, a stranger to this memory, could read the emotions in those eyes with perfect clarity.

A face stained with humiliation. Self-loathing at having been seen in such a state. And beneath the jealousy and the twisted sense of inferiority, there was something else—something harder to name.

Perhaps because he had lived with something similar for a long time. Harry recognised it immediately. It was suppressed affection. Crushed and twisted by something external until it had lost all recognisable shape.

The moment that realisation struck, Harry thought of the image he had glimpsed during Occlumency lessons. A red-haired girl with a bright, open smile. Snape's first love, and perhaps his last—

"…Ha. Surely not. That would be absurd."

An impossible assumption—but if it were true.

What was Harry Potter to Snape?

From somewhere in the distance came the sound of James's laughter.

"Snivellus, how do you know Evans, anyway? Evans is nothing like those Death Eater friends of yours. So why does she always come running to help you?"

"Shut up! I don't need help from some filthy Mudblood!"

The words rang out, raw with feeling.

The scene shifted.

"I'm sorry, Lily. I didn't mean it…."

Snape was apologising with everything he had. To Harry, who had known Snape only as a professor, it was a startling sight. This was a man who had seemed capable of expressing no more than mild regret even over a death—and yet here he was, pleading with desperate sincerity.

But despite the wholehearted apology, Lily's expression remained cool.

"It's fine. I know you were too worked up to think before you spoke."

"Then, that means—"

Snape's face brightened. He smiled—an awkward, hopeful smile, the smile of someone naively convinced that things could go back to the way they were.

But that smile—

"What I want to ask about is the fact that you called me a Mudblood 'without thinking.'"

—shattered.

Lily fixed Snape with a look of cold hostility.

"You've been spending time with people who call themselves Death Eaters, haven't you? Avery—the one who attacked a Muggle-born not long ago and laughed about it afterwards. I've heard you're often seen with him."

"That—that's just because we're both in Slytherin—"

"Really? Can you swear on our friendship that you have nothing to do with the Death Eaters?"

Snape couldn't bring himself to nod. Lily watched him with cold eyes, then slung her bag over her shoulder and stood.

"Lily!"

"If you want to keep our friendship, end your relationship with your new friends. If you won't, I can't accept you."

Lily said it coldly and turned away from Snape. His expression crumbled.

The scene shifted again.

Snape had ultimately been unable to abandon his new friends. Intoxicated by the power and glory they offered, he became a fervent follower of Voldemort. Even after graduation he continued working in his service, and earned a place among the inner circle—the Death Eaters.

"Well done, Severus!"

And now, in the present of this memory, he had achieved something no one else had managed.

He had eavesdropped on Dumbledore and Trelawney's conversation and, by chance, stolen a prophecy. The moment Snape heard the prophecy about the Dark Lord's adversary, he went straight to his master.

"Truly well done! You surpass every expectation I have of you…."

The chosen child, born as the seventh month dies. He shall be the Dark Lord's equal. It was an extraordinary achievement. Even Snape, a half-blood, would secure a firm standing among the purebloods. Snape allowed himself a faint smile, unable to conceal his satisfaction.

But then—

"Lily… Lily Potter, and James Potter…?"

When he realised who they were—

"My—my Lord. The child—only the child needs to die, does it not…? Please, spare the woman. I beg of you…."

He fell to his knees and pleaded. With a desperation more raw than anything he had ever shown.

Voldemort looked down at him and replied with indifference.

"I shall consider it."

In all likelihood, that consideration would go no further.

Snape went to find someone else who could protect Lily. Some might have called it betrayal—but Snape gave no thought to such trivial concerns.

Whatever anyone said, Snape had to save Lily. That was all.

Harry had never seen Dumbledore wear such a cold expression in his life. Having heard Snape out, Dumbledore regarded him with a gaze like ice.

"You disgust me."

He meant it. Even the kind headmaster of Hogwarts, who had always found room for even his most wayward students, looked down at Snape as though he were something beneath contempt.

"So. You are saying that as long as Lily Potter can be saved, the others may die? James Potter—and an infant who knows nothing of any of this?"

He was right to feel that way.

But it wasn't simply the attitude that her husband and child were expendable.

If James Potter and Harry Potter died, Lily Potter would be left alone…. If one were to expose the sordid motive buried beneath that—he deserved every condemnation.

And yet… Harry found it difficult to condemn him. Because if he were to hear a rumour that Carina had married Pansy and had a child, he could not be certain he wouldn't make a similar choice.

The moment that thought crossed his mind, Harry groaned.

"I really shouldn't be getting this invested."

Of course, by the time he realised it, he was already in over his head. The person Harry had found himself identifying with in this memory was neither his father nor his mother.

If Snape was anything like Harry—then Snape, too, would inevitably make that choice.

Just as Harry had expected, Snape grabbed the hem of Dumbledore's robes and begged.

"Then—protect all of them…. Hide them from the Dark Lord!"

"Are you aware that what you are doing constitutes a betrayal of Voldemort?"

"I don't care!"

A sudden lurch.
The memory extinguished, and another surged up in its place.

Snape was in despair. Harry looked at his face—the face of a man who had lost everything—and understood at once that this was after Lily and James had died.

His eyes were hollow, emptied out. Rage stirred his body awake while grief rendered him powerless in the same breath. He was full of force with nowhere to direct it, and so it turned inward and destroyed him.

Snape cried out in a trembling voice.

"You said you would protect them…. You said…!"

"They trusted someone who should not have been trusted."

Dumbledore answered with quiet composure. Snape raised his head, eyes bloodshot, then let his body go slack.

"It's over. It's all over. There's nothing left now…."

"Not quite. One thing remains. The only legacy that survived because of her sacrifice."

"Harry Potter? James's son?"

"Her child as well."

"That has nothing to do with me! Whatever happens to that boy! He is nothing to me now!"

"Is that truly so?"

Dumbledore's words struck precisely at the thing Snape could not let go of. Snape's face contorted for a long moment; then he clenched his fist and slammed it down hard on the arm of his chair.

"James! He's James's child! A boy. He looks exactly like James—every feature!"

"His eyes are Lily's. Quite beautiful green eyes, they were."

"Damn it all! How am I supposed to—how could I possibly—"

"Protect him. Is that what you want me to tell you? That you must?"

It was despair. Snape had failed utterly, and that failure could not be undone. There was no one to blame. He was the one who had killed her.

But cruelly, she had left something behind when she died. Protected by the powerful sacrifice charm, her child—hers and James's—would be swept into the eye of fate without understanding any of it.

Even if that choice would carve him hollow for the rest of his days, Snape had no choice but to protect Lily Evans's blood.

Because it was all he had left of her.

The scenes blurred past, and at last the final memory appeared.

It was recent enough that Snape looked as Harry had always known him. The eccentric professor with the cold, penetrating gaze and no expression to speak of.

The man who seemed to have severed himself from all feeling spoke in a dry, flat voice.

"…You said the boy must die?"

"Yes."

Dumbledore was as composed as ever. It was impossible to tell whether he felt grief or nothing at all. Snape, perhaps accustomed to that manner by now, showed no particular change in expression.

"As I mentioned before—the connection between Harry and Voldemort is unlike anything ordinary. They share more than memories; they share sight, emotion, and power. It is difficult to explain without positing a connection of souls."

"Then Harry is, after all—"

"Yes. A living Horcrux. The last Horcrux he created without intending to. To kill Voldemort, Harry must die. And by magic powerful enough to destroy a soul."

Harry could no longer read any emotion in Snape's face. It was beyond Occlumency—it was as though feeling itself had been excised. Snape stared fixedly at a single point and asked in a toneless voice.

"Is there no other way?"

"Not that I know of. Over more than fifteen years of life, the two souls have fused. Cutting away only one of them cleanly is impossible."

"And if Harry is not killed—what then?"

"The worst. Voldemort would use Harry's body to return."

Snape fell silent and thought.

That he needed to think at all meant he was looking for another way. Dumbledore, recognising Snape's resolve, looked at him over his spectacles.

"You cannot accept it?"

"I have spied for you, lied for you, and driven myself to the edge of death for you. All of it was to protect Lily Potter's son. And now you are telling me to destroy the only thing she left behind."

"How very moving. Severus—have you finally come to care for the boy?"

"Care for him?"

Snape cried out.

"*Expecto Patronum!*"

A silver doe descended into the headmaster's office. It bounded around the room, then crossed it in a single leap and vanished through the window.

Dumbledore watched the silver shape until it disappeared, then spoke.

"All this time?"

"Always."

Dumbledore looked at him and smiled. A warm, gentle smile—one he had never shown Snape before.

"Is there a way?"

"The spell she used at the end."

"Do you believe the boy is worth your life?"

"A strange question. What value does my life hold that would outweigh anything else?"

Snape said it without inflection.

"My life ended on that day, fifteen years ago. What remains is nothing but the dregs of guilt and obligation. If there is still something I must do, it is to protect what she left behind."

"You intend to entrust everything to him?"

"That boy will do what I could not. I failed—but he succeeded. If he survives, he will truly see it through to the end."

Snape had despised Harry. Harry had loathed him in return.

Harry was the product of Snape's most devastating failure—the living proof of it, the thing that painted over his failure and made it worse. The one who had accomplished what Snape could not, making Snape's wretchedness all the more complete.

But jealousy is also longing. Even in another's success, a man who has failed hopes that hope itself still exists somewhere. He wanted to see proof of a possibility he himself had never found.

Even hating Harry—

He had wanted him to overcome his failures. To endure, to prevail, and in the end, to find love.

Dumbledore, reading that wish, let out a quiet sigh.

"You see yourself in Harry, Severus. As though he were your successor."

"I won't deny it."

"If that is your wish, then."

Dumbledore folded his hands on the desk. His beetle-bright eyes gleamed with something like curiosity.

"I, too, must do my very best."

And then—the memory lurched. Harry's body spun upward and was flung out of the Pensieve.

When it came to the most promising talent of the year, one name could not be left out: Cedric Diggory.

A student who had stood against a corrupt and negligent administration, and ultimately brought the truth to light. The champion who had defeated Harry Potter—The Chosen One—to win the Triwizard Tournament. A man whose contributions had been recognized by Rufus Scrimgeour himself, who now served diligently as the Minister for Magic's personal aide.

Cedric Diggory.

But he carried another identity as well.

"—Oh, Harry. What, what? You've found his base of operations?"

The Order of the Phoenix. The organization Dumbledore had assembled to stand against Voldemort.

Harry had asked Cedric to join.

For the Minister's aide, accepting was not something he should do. However close Rufus Scrimgeour and Dumbledore might be in their alliance, the Minister would hardly welcome the news that his own aide had embedded himself in Dumbledore's private organization.

Yet Cedric owed Harry a great deal. During the Triwizard Tournament, Harry had given him invaluable help. When Umbridge had seized control of Hogwarts, it was Harry who had first stood against her. The very position Cedric now held owed no small part to Harry's role in events.

It was a bitter thing. He had agonized over it, and he had no shortage of excuses ready. But in the end, Cedric had chosen loyalty.

"No. Even if we hurry, tomorrow is the earliest we can manage. Several members are out on assignment, and a few others have accompanied the Minister to Bulgaria. I'll need to report to him first— What? You want me to summon them?"

But Harry's request went beyond anything Cedric had imagined.

"There are Death Eaters among the Aurors? What? You want me to summon them and buy time? You're asking me to send a few of the trustworthy ones your way?"

It was overreach. Aurors were assets of the Ministry, answerable to the Minister for Magic and the Department of Magical Law Enforcement—figures of considerable standing within the Ministry. And Harry was asking Cedric to move them.

It wasn't impossible, strictly speaking. If he reported Voldemort's location and the traitors within the Auror ranks to Rufus Scrimgeour, the Minister would be irritated that the Ministry hadn't discovered it first—but he would ultimately listen.

The problem was doing it right now. That would require Cedric to impersonate a direct order from the Minister for Magic. That was the sort of crime that would land him in Azkaban without a word of protest.

Even Harry couldn't ask Cedric to commit a crime. That wasn't right.

But.

"…If it isn't done within three days, Malfoy will be in danger…."

Harry delivered the words with quiet composure, but Cedric could feel the desperation beneath them. And beneath that, a low, steady anger.

He had no choice. This might cost him his position when it was all over. It might set his career back years.

But Cedric Diggory was young. He could afford to lose that much.

It was better than losing a friend.

"Understood. Wait."

He cut the connection, exhaled slowly, and began forging the Minister for Magic's orders.

Fenrir Greyback had not forgotten the humiliation. It had grated on him—watching the Malfoys, who had been crawling on the floor to appease Voldemort's displeasure, suddenly strutting about as though killing one small child made them something. The sight had turned his stomach.

Carina Malfoy most of all. Narcissa and Lucius made no effort to hide their contempt for Greyback, but their precious princess had sealed away every trace of her loathing and disdain behind a flawless mask. As though she fancied herself some great actress.

Paradoxically, that composure had only provoked him further.

Fenrir Greyback was a Werewolf. His hobby was biting children and turning them into Werewolves like himself.

By any measure—objective or otherwise—he was a creature of revulsion. Fenrir had long since grown accustomed to that fact; more than accustomed, he wore it as a point of pride. Whether someone recoiled openly and tried to put as much distance between themselves and him as possible, or whether they swallowed their disgust out of immediate fear—both reactions were a feast to him.

And so Carina Malfoy, with her sealed emotions and her perfectly composed expression, had been a rare delicacy wrapped in beautiful packaging. Rare enough that even he, a man with no patience to speak of, had found himself willing to wait.

Then, without warning, she had attacked him—all because she'd killed Harry Potter, apparently. No dignity. No preamble.

It had trampled his anticipation and insulted him in the same stroke.

'Let's see if you can pull the same trick with a Werewolf's teeth right in front of your face.'

Voldemort generally paid little attention to such matters, provided no one actually killed anyone. Greyback had made his way to Carina's door and was reaching silently for the handle—

A soft chime rang out, and the protective enchantments surrounding the room shattered. The room, now exposed, was full of people. They were emerging from a cabinet at the center of the space, steadying one another as they stepped out, as though disembarking from a train.

Was this not the right room? Greyback tilted his head back to check, but it was unmistakably the same room Narcissa had brought her daughter to.

Then why were there so many people swarming inside?

He was still cocking his head in bewilderment when—

"What are you doing here?"

Carina Malfoy was standing beside him. He flinched. The corridor had been empty when he arrived; she had appeared as abruptly as if she'd stepped out from beneath an Invisibility Cloak.

She looked at Greyback with undisguised contempt.

"You filth. Even I've never stooped to sneaking into a girl's room."

"W-wait. There are people in your room—"

"Oh, them?"

She pushed her door wide open with a faint smirk. Inside stood a young man who looked barely of age, several older wizards who might have been professors, and others dressed in every manner of clothing—

"Friends from school. They're all kind, decent people. I do hope you'll get along."

She said it the way a parent might speak of a child who'd fallen in with bad company—those cool, pale green eyes fixed on him as she spoke.

Pale green eyes, was it? Greyback was still puzzling over that when—

"Ah. Time."

She reached into her pocket and put on a pair of round spectacles. They were clunky, graceless things, entirely at odds with her aristocratic face. The lenses were so large that her features seemed to warp strangely beneath them.

…No. Her features were actually warping. She seemed to grow slightly taller; her platinum hair fanned out in every direction, darkening from the roots upward.
Seconds later, Carina was gone, and Harry Potter stood in her place.

The moment Greyback registered that something had gone very wrong, he lunged—eyes wide. But Harry already had his wand in hand.

"*Incarcerous.*"

Ropes shot from the wand and bound Greyback tight. He snarled and tore at them. Harry drew his arm back, and Greyback's instinct was to close the distance before the next spell could be cast—sink his teeth in, leave a wound that wouldn't wash out. Tonight wasn't a full moon, but he could still do lasting damage—

But Harry didn't cast a spell. The drawn-back arm wasn't preparation for magic. He gripped his wand tighter, set his jaw, and drove his fist straight into Greyback's oncoming face.

It was fast enough to catch even Greyback off guard. The fist blurred, then materialized in front of his face as though it had Apparated there. He hadn't even blinked.

"You vermin—how dare you touch Carina—!"

The wand-gripping hand cracked into his eye socket. The ropes had left him unable to block in time; Greyback staggered, his balance gone. Before he could recover—

"*Impedimenta.* Stay down, you bastard."

Harry jabbed him with the wand in a short, sharp motion. Greyback's body began to topple with the slow, submerged heaviness of a man sinking through water. Harry shook out his fist and stepped toward him with an almost casual ease.

Greyback could do nothing. He could only blink. Harry smiled at him, laced his wand-hand fingers together, and all the while Greyback continued to fall—unhurried, as though gravity itself had slowed to accommodate the moment.

Harry raised his arm. A shadow fell across Greyback's face. Before Greyback could form a single word, Harry brought the handle of his wand down onto the philtrum of his still-falling face.

"Have! Some! Decency! You! Disgusting! Bastard!"

Each word landed with a crash of his fist.

Crack. His nose broke. Blood poured freely from his nose and mouth. A crooked tooth flew loose, and something foul splattered in every direction.

Greyback lost consciousness on the second blow—Harry's hands were that brutal. But Harry's fury wasn't spent. He raised his fist again. Since he'd already subdued the man, he might as well reduce the enemy's combat strength while he was at it.

He lifted his blood-soaked fist and shouted—

"Right, last one. This is for saying Carina's name with that filthy mouth—!"

"Harry. That's enough."

Someone caught his arm. Harry turned. Lupin had stepped forward from among the people he'd been guiding out of the room, one hand extended.

"Professor Lupin?"

Harry asked, genuinely puzzled.

"Why? This is just ambulatory rubbish. I'm doing the work of Britain's negligent sanitation service. Besides, he's the one who bit you, isn't he?"

"He is. Truly a man who deserves to die."

Thud. Greyback, still falling in slow motion under the Impediment Jinx, finally met the floor. His eyes had rolled completely back; he would likely be barely conscious for days. Lupin glanced at him and said:

"But that doesn't mean you need to be the one to kill him."

"I wasn't particularly planning to."

"You weren't planning not to, either. If you'd only wanted him unconscious, you'd have used your wand."

He couldn't argue with that, so he didn't.

Fenrir Greyback. A villain who had spent years expanding the Werewolf population and driving its average age downward. A piece of human refuse who had terrorized wizarding families and bitten the children of those who refused to comply, turning them into Werewolves. Harry felt no particular obligation to concern himself with whether the man lived or died.

Lupin released Harry's hand and spoke quietly.

"Harry. The reason we tell children not to fight—the reason the Ministry doesn't teach you offensive spells—there's a reason for that. You might think it foolish, but for a wizard, the intention to harm someone is a genuinely dangerous thing."

"Why?"

"Because our wands have the power to reshape reality. That is why violent hatred is dangerous. It can manifest in any number of forms."

Lupin drew Harry by the shoulder. Fenrir Greyback disappeared from Harry's field of vision, and through the wide-open door he could see the Order members watching him and Lupin steadily. They were waiting for him. For the battle ahead.

"I'm not saying it's wrong to fight back. Sometimes there's no choice. But Harry—don't fight in order to make your enemy suffer. Your fight is not for their despair. It's for the happiness you intend to claim."

He was right. Harry wasn't fighting to defeat and destroy his enemies. He was fighting to clear the way—so that he could return to Carina, sleeping like the dead in the Headmaster's office. To remove the obstacle before it could stop him.

The moment he thought of Carina, the rage that had filled his head began to ebb.

Carina had faced a brutal choice and chosen the option that required no one else to die—sacrificing herself instead. If Harry dirtied his hands in her name, she would not be pleased.

Harry accepted that, and turned away from Fenrir. Lupin smiled broadly and gave a small nod.

"Thank you."

"Don't mention it. What do you think about when you fight, Professor?"

"Only one thing: finish quickly and get home to Tonks, Teddy, and Edward."

"Edward?"

Harry turned the name over for a moment, then nudged him.

"When did you add another one?"

"…I was careless. I'd planned to wait until all of this was over…."

"You two are very devoted. At this rate you'll have a full Quidditch team within a few years."

"I'll remember this humiliation, Harry. Let's see how you manage."

It was a strange thing. Trading nonsense seemed to drain the venom from his fingertips and the soles of his feet, as though it had found a crack to seep out through.

When all of this was over and he returned to Hogwarts, a sleeping princess would be waiting for him. No one would be able to stand in his way. Picturing the vague, warm happiness waiting at the far end of the coming battle, he felt the tension leave his eyes and his vision open wide.
Harry exhaled—a long, hot breath. There was more than anger in that heat.

"Right. Let's set aside minor nuisances like Greyback for now and proceed as planned."

"Very well…."

Lupin left it at that—and drove his foot hard into Fenrir's ribs where he lay on the floor. Harry stared at him. Lupin had the grace to look slightly abashed.

"And that's part of the reason I told you to stop, incidentally. You have to leave some for me, Harry. He is my enemy, after all."

"I wasn't being considerate enough."

A brief laugh passed between them. The right kind of laugh for the moment before a battle—light enough to lift the spirit without dissolving the resolve.

Mad-Eye Moody, who had been watching through his magical eye, came stumping out with a bark of laughter.

"I thought he was playing the teacher for a moment—turns out it was all for his own revenge! Ha! Right, you can't let someone else settle your scores for you!"

Moody's rough hand swept across Harry's hair. Then his expression hardened.

"But Harry, you need to be careful too. A wand fulfills your wishes—it manifests your will in the world. So you cannot let yourself fall into the darkness inside you. If you do, darkness is all that will come out of it."

Moody delivered the warning in his graveled voice, then stumped out of the room. Harry noticed Moody's false leg come down squarely on Fenrir Greyback's midsection as he passed, and chose not to notice.

After that, the room emptied steadily.

Arthur Weasley and Molly Weasley came out, followed by Fred and George. Behind them came the rest of the forces Dumbledore had been able to assemble on short notice, each concealing their unease in their own way as they filed through the door.

Toward the back, Ron and Hermione emerged together. Molly kept glancing at them with visible unease—they were underage, and their presence here clearly did not sit well with her. Hermione received the looks with equanimity. Ron, less so.

Ron had stepped out with a suitably grave expression for someone about to go into battle—then he saw Harry, and immediately clapped a hand over his mouth. His face contorted into something caught between laughter and solemnity as he stared.

"Harry, pfft—hk. You."

"What? Oh."

Harry looked down at himself—at the Slytherin uniform two sizes too small, the skirt that barely reached his knees—and scratched the back of his head with mild embarrassment. Several people had been avoiding his eyes, and now he understood why. He was still wearing Carina's clothes.

"I need to change, but there's no time…."

"We'll manage. Engorgio!"

Hermione pointed her wand and spoke. Harry's clothes expanded gradually until they fit his arms and shoulders properly. With practiced efficiency, she turned her wand on the skirt and transfigured it into trousers. Harry straightened himself out.

"Fighting your enemies in your girlfriend's clothes. Quite the romantic adventure."

Harry said it lightly, and several people laughed. The brief release of tension passed; they drew themselves back together, and the laughter faded as they prepared for what lay ahead.

Harry addressed them all.

"Before long, our… operative on the inside will neutralize the protective enchantments around the target. Professor Dumbledore will launch his attack at the same moment—but that's not the critical part. You each have one task."

Harry swept his wand through the air with a sharp whoosh. His holly wand sang as it cut through the air—as though it were singing in anticipation of the fight to come.

Harry said quietly:

"Get me in front of him."

A hush settled. People swallowed and waited for him to continue.

"That's all I need. Get me in front of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named. I have a trump card—enough to face him on equal terms. While I hold his attention, Professor Dumbledore and the rest of you deal with the Death Eaters. Understood?"

There was no need to persuade them. This ground had been covered dozens of times, hundreds of times. Mad-Eye Moody gave a single nod. Molly Weasley's eyes glistened. But she did not move to stop him.

Because there was no other way to face He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named and win.

"I'm not sacrificing myself to stop him, so please don't cry."

Harry wasn't without fear. He had a trump card, yes—but his odds of victory, generously estimated, were no better than even. And that was only achievable by exhausting every resource available to him.

Against an opponent no ordinary wizard could hope to face, even odds were remarkable. But the fact remained: Harry's life was on the line just the same.

He was afraid. Even Harry, who performed fearlessness so well, felt it.

He feared death itself. But more than that, he feared never seeing the people he cared about again. He feared the expression on Carina's face when she heard the news. If he didn't come back—if only his wand was delivered to her—the thought of her grief was enough to make him feel he couldn't die even if he tried.

And so Harry had to stand tall in the face of that fear. It was the first thing the wizarding world had taught him.

Don't tremble even when you're terrified. Bury the feeling deep. Reach past defeat and take the victory. Harry lifted his chin and smiled.

"Right. Now let me introduce our ally. Dobby?"

With a soft pop, a house-elf appeared, hunched and tightly wound. Thin and small, dressed in rags, Dobby spoke in his high, squeaking voice:

"Oh, Harry Potter! Does Harry Potter need Dobby's help?"

"Yes. Dobby, I need your help."

Dobby's enormous eyes went wide with something that looked very much like being moved to tears.

"If Harry Potter asks, Dobby will help with all of Dobby's heart! And the Mistress told Dobby to do whatever Harry Potter says! Dobby can do anything for Harry Potter!"

"Good. Dobby—lead us to where he is."

Dobby's enormous eyes grew wider still, and his expression became one of a creature on the verge of overwhelmed collapse. Harry looked away to give him a moment to compose himself.

"Right. Dobby will guide us. We have other allies as well—we go with them. The battle cry is: 'What is your master's name!' Use it well. It'll come in handy."

Harry said it with a grin.

The advance began.

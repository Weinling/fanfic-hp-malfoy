The third task was drawing near. The Ministry of Magic was doing everything in its power to make it the crowning jewel of their achievements.

Word had it they'd imported a rare Sphinx, constructed traps modelled after pyramids, and painstakingly selected creatures that were extraordinarily rare yet not quite lethal enough to fill the maze.

Everyone was buzzing with excitement.

Carina alone could not enjoy a single moment of it.

The rain hammered down on the lake in heavy sheets. Thunder and lightning tore through the sky and struck the earth. Every time the wind surged, the mast let out a long, groaning creak.

Aboard the Durmstrang ship, Carina was listening carefully to what the man across from her had to say.

"...Miss Malfoy. The time has come to make a choice."

Igor Karkaroff. Once a Death Eater, he had narrowly escaped Azkaban by selling out his comrades, and now he trembled with fear—more acutely sensitive to this particular threat than anyone else alive.

He looked utterly haggard, and yet his nerves were wound tighter than she had ever seen them.

"There are rumours that Bertha Jorkins, a Ministry employee, has gone missing. Are you aware of this?"

Carina opened her mouth slowly.

"...Only what I caught briefly in the Daily Prophet. It seems she still hasn't been found. If she had been, they'd have run a front-page story telling everyone to rest easy."

"Yes... the Ministry of this country seems determined to keep that fact buried... I found that suspicious, so I used my own contacts to gather news from outside Britain."

A low, snickering laugh escaped him. He spoke in a state of dishevelment and frailty that seemed impossible to reconcile with the headmaster of a wizarding school.

"Albania. Bertha Jorkins apparently made it there in one piece. But after that, she vanished without a trace. Albania... Albania. A quiet country, steeped in ancient magic. Quite ideal for a wizard in hiding... Bertha Jorkins must have encountered someone there. The last place she was sighted was a Muggle tavern..."

Slowly, the truth one does not wish to know approaches in a way that cannot be resisted. Once you begin to sense it, the horizon beyond is already black with storm clouds as far as the eye can see.

"That place also happens to be the last known location of the Dark Lord, Miss Malfoy. I trust that a sharp-minded young woman like yourself understands what that implies."

He was suggesting that Bertha Jorkins had been captured by Voldemort. Which meant Voldemort had already recovered enough strength to overpower a Ministry witch.

It was a truth she had always been warned of, yet had turned away from because she could not bring herself to believe it—and now Karkaroff was pressing it into her hands as well.

"Miss Malfoy. Do you think I'm speaking nonsense?"

First Moody, and now Karkaroff.

The world seemed to have contracted some disease that would kill it unless it kept forcing the truth upon Carina.

"The Dark Lord has recovered all of his power. Do you know why, even so, he has not appeared before his old followers? Because he trusts no one!"

He raised his voice.

"Lucius is no exception. After the Dark Lord disappeared, Lucius publicly denied him at every turn. And his daughter—good heavens—going about with Harry Potter, of all people, as though they were the best of friends!"

"...I'd take issue with that characterisation."

Lies and truths swirled together like a current. The only thing Carina could do in this torrent was carve out one or two escape routes.

She placed a hand over her chest, schooled her expression into something cold and imperious, and denied her own feelings.

"It's true that Harry Potter is fond of me, and I'll admit I see a certain talent in him. But surely that doesn't compare to the Dark Lord?"

"...Heh heh. So you'd discard what's no longer useful? You really are a Malfoy, Miss Malfoy."

Fortunately, a man consumed by suspicion cannot properly read another person's emotions. Karkaroff heard Carina's words and accepted them at face value.

Please let Voldemort be the same way... She offered a brief, silent prayer, then concealed her true thoughts and continued.

"And there seems to be a misunderstanding. Our family has always been among his most devoted servants. When he returns, we will be the first to welcome him. So—"

"I should rest easy? Ha. Miss Malfoy, the Dark Lord trusts no one. The very fact that he hasn't appeared before Lucius, even after recovering his strength, is proof of that!"

"Even so, we have our uses."

Carina said it and looked down at Karkaroff. Unlike him—a man who had survived by betraying and selling out countless comrades—the Malfoys, who had survived through political acumen, still had room to manoeuvre.

Karkaroff's face hardened. The emotions that crossed it were despair and fear.

"M-Miss Malfoy. L-let me see him. Please."

"How would I manage that? I don't even know where he is."

"I-I do. He'll be at the Ministry."

The Ministry? It was the first Carina had heard of this.

But if she showed too much interest, Karkaroff would try to use that information to hold her in his grip.

She pressed him on with a look that said: go on, then.

"No matter how incompetent the Ministry is, they couldn't fail to find one Bertha Jorkins. If they'd deployed their people properly... it must be that he's pulled some strings. So..."

"So?"

"...If you connect me to him, I'll handle the rest myself. Lucius could bring me to the British Ministry. If you do that for me, I'll make sure to mention your contribution..."

His contribution.

Karkaroff's intentions were so base and transparent that Carina could read them in an instant. She arranged a manufactured smile on her face and gave a small nod.

The rain fell. She had cast an umbrella charm to keep it off, but the wind drove the water sideways, soaking her from the hair down.

Her body was cold. The chill of the rain seemed to seep into her veins.

Her sleeves grew damp. Water crept into her shoes. The hem of her skirt turned cold. Carina made her way back to Hogwarts.

"...I have to tell him."

As she stepped inside, Harry happened to come into view. Without thinking, Carina began to move toward him, his name already forming on her lips.

"Har—"

Fortunately, she caught herself just before the sound escaped her throat.

Now that his return was all but certain, Carina could not be seen approaching Harry.

If she did, jackals like Karkaroff would use it to get their claws into her.

"Hagrid would happily put Blast-Ended Skrewts in the maze, you know. Harry, you'd better be ready to deal with those things!"

"Oh, come on. Those are illegal crossbreeds. Even Hagrid has some sense—he wouldn't actually put them in there... would he?"

Harry blinked, and the others nearby dissolved into snickers. Among them, Ginny Weasley was laughing with her eyes—

Ginny Weasley, was it. That family with their insufferable abundance of children managed to produce a daughter who grated on Carina just as thoroughly as the rest of them.

But this time, she found she had no room for jealousy. Carina, who was about to redraw the line between them, had no right to it.

Just once, though.

Carina slipped on the ring and settled into a chair in an empty classroom. She had something to tell him, but she could not be the one to seek him out—so she sat and waited, with no particular end in sight.

The heavy clouds that had filled the sky gradually retreated, and the moon rose through a gap between them. Carina sat motionless, having skipped dinner entirely.

If Harry put on his ring—if he looked at the Marauder's Map—he would find her sitting alone and come to her. Under his Invisibility Cloak.

If he didn't find her...

'I'll have only wasted a little time.'

Not long after the dinner hour had ended, Carina felt the hollow pull of hunger. She had been wandering from place to place since lessons ended without eating a thing, and now her body was making its demands known.

Undignified.

At a moment like this—when no one knew how he might return—to be hungry. Her stomach had truly no sense of occasion.

She was swallowing against it, waiting in silence, when the atmosphere of the room shifted. Carina saw nothing, but she felt the air around her change.

"Watch out for Karkaroff."
She spoke with quiet certainty.

"He's been entertaining the idea of selling you out. The notion that it would actually work is laughable, but a man backed into a corner will do desperate things. Tell Professor Moody and have him take precautions."

"We haven't spoken in ages and this is all you have for me?"

Harry's voice came from just to her right. Carina turned toward the sound. He was pulling off his Invisibility Cloak, and his hand moved naturally to settle around her shoulder.

Carina knocked it away without hesitation.

"Yes, this is all I have for you. Listen carefully. This is the last thing I'll ever say to you."

"The last?"

Before Harry could say anything more, Carina spoke quickly.

"Karkaroff believes he's taken control of someone high up in the Ministry. I think so too. Hogwarts is far too safe right now. Dumbledore, Moody, the Ministry's own deployed wizards... even if the wizarding world had a king, he wouldn't be guarded as heavily as you are. You can sleep soundly."

"I'd sleep a lot more soundly with you beside me. We've been dancing around this long enough—want to share a bed?"

It was a joke with a half-serious edge to it, but Carina neither flustered nor blushed. She let out a small, quiet laugh, then composed her expression.

Any other time, she would have gone red and snapped at him for daring to tease her. Tonight, not a trace of that showed.

As though she had already decided that the joke could never come true—that it was nothing more than an idle fancy.

Harry's voice wavered slightly.

"I mean it. I won't hold back."

Carina did not answer that. She looked up at the window and spoke.

"Harry. There's something I need to ask you. Swear to me you won't lie."

Reluctantly, Harry let the playful smile fall from his face.

"I swear."

Carina looked at him steadily. Those dreadful glasses—as expressionless as a pharaoh's mask—obscured half his face, but at this distance they did very little.

He had grown noticeably taller. His features were clean and even. His eyes were cool, yet whenever they turned to her they always carried warmth—a warmth that made her feel as though she were being treated as something singular.

He wore his fringe pushed back, leaving the lightning-bolt scar fully exposed, but no one looked at it anymore and thought of the terrible war, or of the name that must not be spoken. He had taken a mark of fear and refused to hide it, making it simply his own.

For just a moment, Carina felt a quiet pride in having someone like that as her own.

But...

"First question."

She turned to face him and asked the question she had been putting off for a very long time.

"My father. What do you think of Lucius Malfoy?"

"A man I owe a debt of gratitude to."

"Not that."

Lucius Malfoy.

The father who, in Carina's childhood, had seemed capable of anything—and whom she still respected, if a little less unconditionally than before.

"...I have to tell the truth, don't I?"

Carina answered without words.

With the eyes she fixed on Harry, with the hand that tightened over her chest, with the lips she pressed firmly shut—she told him: yes.

Harry closed his eyes briefly, then opened them. There was cold hostility in his gaze.

"A fool who lives off the Ministry's favour while scheming to overturn the very order that sustains him. A hypocrite who persecutes Muggles and then strolls into the Ministry without a shred of shame. And yet... the man who handed us the diary. A useful enemy."

Far too harsh a thing to say about a lover's parent.

And yet Carina nodded, as though she had known it would be exactly this.

"...Yes. I thought as much. Of course."

Exposing the diary so carelessly had been a foolish move.

But at the time, Arthur Weasley of the Ministry had been investigating the dark artefacts left in the Malfoy estate. Lucius had narrowly weathered that crisis, but for a man who had believed Voldemort truly gone, the diary had become a serious liability. And so he had disposed of it in a way that would simultaneously embarrass Arthur Weasley and check Dumbledore—by slipping it into Ginny Weasley's bag.

One could not call it a moral act. But Lucius Malfoy's reasoning had been sound. The cursed object had sat in Malfoy Manor for centuries; it was the law that had changed without warning.

To evade the enforcement of an unjust law, and to strike down a political enemy who had moved against him, Lucius Malfoy had placed the diary into the bag of a child who knew nothing.

"I'm not like you, Harry. It isn't because he's my father—it's just... I can't bring myself to deny him."

The demonstration at the Quidditch World Cup had been the same—the frustration of those who could not keep pace with the law, finally boiling over. What had been done freely in uninhabited wilderness was now constrained because Muggles had the audacity to claim the land as their own. Even the Quidditch World Cup—the one great festival of the wizarding world—had been reduced to something resembling a rebel gathering. There was no shortage of those who were furious, and they had come together to march, binding and tormenting Muggles as they went.

"I understand my father."

The Ministry would need to be far more careful about choosing the venue for the next Quidditch World Cup.

It was not a dignified thing to do. But Carina could not bring herself to condemn them entirely.

This was the greatest difference between her and Harry.

"Because I also believe that those with ability should stand above others. That is the natural truth that runs through the world."

Carina confessed it to Harry. The deepest reason why the two of them could never truly align.

"Even Muggles do the same. They always envy and admire those who surpass them. Yet they raise no particular objection to being ruled by those above them. The more capable take more. That is common sense even among Muggles."

Before returning to Hogwarts each year, Carina would pause briefly at King's Cross station. Dressed and groomed to perfection, leaning against a pillar for a moment—anyone who passed would look up at her.

Malfoy Manor was famous even among the Muggles of the nearby village as the great house on the hill. Children would wonder who could possibly live in that beautiful estate above them.

Even without magic, the Malfoys had always been the ones who ruled.

Add magic to that, and—

"Wizards possess the gift of magic. Magic is something Muggles can never have. It is only natural that wizards should live more richly than Muggles. That is the order of nature. And yet the Ministry works to suppress it unnaturally."

And yet, at present, there were wizards living in more wretched conditions than many Muggles.

The International Statute of Secrecy. Wizards must not reveal the existence of the magical world to Muggles.

Enacted long ago and still in force, it carried the weight of long history and tradition. And at the same time, it was the law that had created the very concept of the 'impoverished wizard.'

To make money, one did not even need the Imperius Curse. With nothing more than Transfiguration, a simple Confundus Charm, or a Memory Modification spell, a wizard could live in extraordinary comfort in the Muggle world.

The reason they could not was the International Statute of Secrecy, and the Aurors who enforced it.

"I have no intention of abolishing the Statute itself. Show Muggles too much and their envy would destroy them. But that doesn't mean wizards should be condemned to poverty."

The Malfoy family had opposed that law for generations. Carina had been raised with that understanding. It had allowed her to form her own views earlier than most.

She had seen too many times how proud pureblood families—the finest names in the wizarding world—had fallen and come to the wealthy Malfoys with their hands outstretched. Carina had once looked down on them with a sense of superiority, but however diminished they were, they were still her kind. Not like Muggles.

A Muggle living in comfort, and a wizard living in poverty. Looking at the two side by side, she felt as though the natural order had been twisted out of shape.

"I'm afraid of him too—the one whose name must not be spoken. I have no desire to be near him. But."

Carina hesitated before she spoke.

"...Even so. My thinking is different from yours."

Different.

The worlds they had grown up in, the worlds that surrounded them, were different.

And therefore.

"I cannot be with you."
Carina ended it.

She was not Sirius Black. She could not deny everything her family stood for, abandon her kin and her convictions, and walk away with nothing but herself. That was not who she was.

A brief, sweet interlude—it had been nothing more than a single day's dream. Carina felt with painful clarity that the time had come to wake.

"Don't joke."

Harry replied with an unsteady smile. He seemed to be desperately hoping that all of this was someone's trick—refusing to accept what Carina had just declared.

But Carina had not come here on a half-formed resolve.

"Do I look like someone who jokes?"

"You don't look like someone who'd say something she doesn't mean, either!"

Harry's voice was almost pleading.

"Is this because of Professor Moody? It's all right. He hasn't returned yet. Before he does, we can find and destroy every last Horcrux, root out the ones working in the shadows, and send them to Azkaban. That's all we need to do—and you're saying we should end things just because a threat is drawing closer?"

"Isn't it the other way around, Potter? A threat is drawing closer—where exactly is the time for something as trivial as a romance?"

No matter how beautifully one dressed it up, it was nothing more than the romance of children barely into their teens. That such things ended when circumstances demanded it was perfectly ordinary.

Harry felt the firmness of her resolve and let the smile go. His voice dropped to something quieter and more settled.

"...So you'll side with Voldemort? Become his instrument—give him everything, body and mind?"

"Who can say? If he requires it of me, I suppose I'll have no choice but to comply. And if I succeed, he'll reward me accordingly."

"If Voldemort finds out that your father was the one who exposed the Horcrux, do you really think he'll still offer a reward?"

A threat with a vicious edge to it. But Carina had an answer.

"At the very least, it's better than everyone dying for treason. Wouldn't you agree?"

To bow one's head and beg forgiveness for one's sins—that was the only way to survive. Carina answered him in earnest.

"And your side should want that secret kept as well, Potter. The longer it takes him to realise that a piece of his soul has been destroyed, the better it is for you."

Even enemies could reach an agreement. If keeping that secret served everyone's interests, then Malfoy and Potter alike would hold their tongues.

The Harry who had smiled with careful ambiguity was gone. The Harry who had argued in anger was nearly spent.

Having turned her words over long enough, Harry now spoke from a place of cold, settled reason.

"Even so, you'd be better off staying with me. From Voldemort's perspective, you're a rather valuable source of information—he won't be quick to discard you."

The same cold face he had shown when facing Professor Moody surfaced now. The calculating, unsentimental side of him that he had never let Carina see—he let it show, and attempted to negotiate.

"And I can promise you sufficient protection. You need that too, don't you? I can make sure you provide just enough information to keep yourself from being discarded, while keeping you safe at my side."

Even as an enemy, he was saying—just stay close. It was, for Carina alone, a very appealing offer.

But having heard it out in full, Carina let out a short, dismissive breath and rose from her seat. She stepped in front of Harry, grabbed him by the collar, and pulled him toward her.

"Don't push your luck, Potter. Do you honestly think I need your protection?"

Even if she remained among Voldemort's followers, Carina Malfoy was still a Malfoy. A method of survival that depended entirely on someone else's goodwill was not worth considering from the outset.

If she was to be an enemy, she would be a complete one.

She would sooner give it all up than survive by clinging to Harry's feelings for her in some half-defined arrangement.

That was her pride.

When she had finished, Carina released her grip on Harry's collar. Harry held her gaze to the very end.

But Carina had already made her decision, and nothing Harry did would change it.

Harry drew a slow, deep breath and forced the words out.

"A farewell kiss?"

"What?"

Carina asked again. Harry looked up at her with an expression of complete seriousness.

"If you're going to end things this abruptly, the least you could do is give me a farewell kiss. Don't you think? From your side."

"You really are—even now, in a situation like this—"

"What, am I asking for too much?"

Carina exhaled.

If anything, it was a brazen request. Perhaps it would be better to use this one last kiss to put her feelings in order and be done with it.

She could manage that much.

"Turn your face up."

"No. You come to me."

Harry said it shamelessly, still seated in the chair. Carina's brow furrowed.

He had his chin tilted slightly down and his legs together. The only way to kiss him from this position was to climb on top of him.

"You..."

"Is this the extent of Carina Malfoy's resolve? Voldemort gives you a task and the moment it makes you the slightest bit uncomfortable, you'll be making faces about it."

At the mention of Voldemort, Carina's body gave a small, involuntary shudder. She bit her lip, then gathered her skirt slightly and took a step forward.

She placed her knees on the long, wide chair. Then she lowered herself gently onto Harry's lap. Their upper bodies came together until they were nearly touching.

There was no maintaining composure in a position like this. Carina suppressed her embarrassment and whispered.

"This is the last time."

"We'll see about that."

Harry pulled her closer. Close enough to feel each other's heartbeat, chest pressed against chest.

Carina closed her eyes and kissed him.

For a very, very long time, they shared their farewell kiss. As though desperate—as though this moment, once past, would never come again.

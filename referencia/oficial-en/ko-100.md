[I'm sorry, Carina. It seems I won't be able to obtain Veritaserum after all.

Thanks to your efforts, I've been able to move about with a fair degree of freedom. But a potion like Veritaserum—one that carries the risk of illegal use—is simply too difficult to acquire. On top of that, the Ministry has been consuming more of it lately, so there's no surplus to be had.

Might you be able to obtain some at Hogwarts? In my day, the Potions professors always kept at least one vial on hand. There were no shortage of stories about a few drops finding their way into a student's drink when homework went undone.

As it happens, Professor Slughorn is at Hogwarts now. He was teaching Potions when I attended as well. If you speak to him, I expect he could spare a vial.

You work so hard. It is thanks to your efforts that our family has been spared from harm. I am rooting for you.

P.S. Though it needn't be Harry Potter, specifically.]

Carina let out a small, involuntary laugh at that last line. Then, just as quickly, the weight of the problems stacked before her settled back in, and she exhaled a short sigh.

Veritaserum, brewed by Slughorn. Carina had stolen it before Potions class some time ago.

She had taken only what she needed of most potions, storing the rest away—but the Veritaserum she had taken in its entirety, replacing it with water. Colourless, tasteless, odourless: no one would notice the switch, and no one would be the worse for it, she had reasoned.

But the Veritaserum she had pilfered was used up entirely when Harry had slipped into the seaside villa. Neither Slughorn nor Carina had any left.

"I should have saved a little…."

It wasn't as though there were no way to obtain more. Harry—who had, by some strange turn of events, inherited Snape's estate—would surely be able to procure it if she asked. That much was certain.

The problem was whether Harry would see through her plan.

"…There's no other way."

Better than making the journey to Nurmengard and returning empty-handed. Carina turned and made her way toward Snape's office.

Books fluttered open without a hand to touch them. Pages turned of their own accord, stopping precisely where they were needed. Harry sat surrounded by dozens of volumes and thousands of sheets of parchment suspended in mid-air, utterly still, lost in thought.

Even with the power and knowledge he had absorbed, he could not comprehend all of this at once. On his own, it would have taken a lifetime. But there was a single thread running through everything Snape had gathered—a unifying theme—and following that thread was something Harry could manage alone.

After several days with barely any sleep, poring over every page, Harry finally understood what Snape had been researching.

He murmured, hollow with the realisation.

"Sacrifice…."

Ancient magic. From a time before the discipline had been properly codified—when wands were merely one of many instruments, when sorcery and spellwork had not yet been distinguished from one another. A form of magic born in that primordial era.

A binding that exacted a price, and in doing so, produced a powerful effect upon whoever met the conditions.

The moment he grasped what it was, Harry could only laugh bitterly.

"Devoted to the end…."

Snape's reason for studying this magic was simple enough.

It was the last magic Lily Potter had cast before she died—Harry's mother. And it was her remnant, still flowing in Harry's blood.

Magic with no practical use, demanding a price too terrible to contemplate… and Snape had spent his life studying it anyway, unable to let go.

Harry flicked his wand. The books and parchment that had been arrayed around him like a folding screen fell into neat rows and settled onto the desk. A tap of the wand against the surface, and a thick leather cover yawned open, beginning to swallow the books and documents one by one. Thousands of pages in total—and yet the leather binding consumed them all, orderly and unhurried.

When the last page had disappeared, Harry exhaled slowly.

"I have a rough understanding. What the magic is. How it works. But…."

Would it actually unfold the way the theory described? Could Harry do it? Did he have the resolve? What if he failed?

Could he convince Carina?

Harry was not ready. Not now—not when the desire to live burned this fiercely inside him. In this state, he could never go through with it.

What if he erased the memory of that night? Could he steel himself again, find his resolve anew? But the will to live was like a flame—removing the fuse now would do nothing to dispel the heat already filling him.

No. That wasn't the only reason.

Harry didn't want to erase that memory.

A night watched over by moonlight. She had been spread across the bed like a work of art—something so extraordinary he had been afraid to touch her, as though to do so would be a kind of desecration.

But human beings are creatures of desire, and desire had overcome reverence. Harry had opened Pandora's box that night. He had drunk the red liquid from the grail.

All of it, exactly as Carina had intended. Harry had become someone who could not bring himself to die. In a way, perhaps that too was a kind of sacrifice.

"Haa."

He was mid-sigh when the door swung open.

Harry glanced toward it, caught sight of that beautiful face, and let out another hollow laugh.

Carina Malfoy must have taken up Legilimency. There was no other explanation for how she managed to appear every time he thought of her, as if to test him.

She looked as though she'd stepped out for a casual stroll—no jumper, just a shirt—and she was walking into the room as though she owned it. Harry watched her and said:

"Someone who's usually so particular about manners—why is it you never knock?"

"Don't be ridiculous. We're past the point of needing to knock."

Carina closed the door without a sound, then reached behind her and locked it.

Whether she'd done it deliberately or without thinking, the effect was the same: in that brief moment, a sealed room for two had been created. Harry attempted a moment of meditation to steady himself.

Wait. Why am I the one who has to hold back?

Why should he restrain himself? It had happened once. There was no reason it couldn't happen again. And this room—Snape's room, technically, but still—was about as private a space as one could ask for.

Harry exhaled softly and ran his tongue over his lips.

"I heard the things in this room were left to you. Is there something here—"

Carina was stating her business as she approached the table. Harry had been watching for his moment, and when she drew close without her guard up, he moved—quick and deliberate. He caught her by the waist and pulled her down onto his lap, wrapping both arms around her from behind. Before she could so much as gasp, Carina found herself thoroughly trapped.

She was his quarry now. Settled on his lap, held fast by both arms, he was just beginning to consider where to start—

Carina turned her head just slightly and met his eyes. As if she'd expected exactly this. She continued speaking without missing a beat.

"…You have Veritaserum, I'm told. Give it to me."

Perhaps it was Harry who had been hunted all along. He smiled faintly. From the moment she'd walked through that door, everything had been within her calculations. He felt vaguely as though he'd been manipulated—and yet, oddly, he didn't mind. He could stand to be manipulated like this a few more times.

He tightened his hold and asked:

"What do you need it for?"

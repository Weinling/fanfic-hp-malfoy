Despite the various complications along the way, Harry and Carina finally arrived at Malfoy Manor.

A picturesque estate perched atop a hill, encircled by a stone wall. The front gate was wrought from silver iron, and as the Rolls-Royce drew near, the iron doors swung open as if to welcome them.

The moment they passed through, Harry felt the brush of magic against him—a faint but unmistakable trace.

Surveillance, detection, identity-revealing, misdirection, confusion, evasion of trackers, protection and defence. Every variety of protective enchantment imaginable wrapped itself around the estate. The old Harry would have registered only a vague sense of wrongness and moved on, but the Harry who now carried Voldemort's power and knowledge could enumerate each spell by type and effect. Warding a home was common enough, but the protective magic layered over Malfoy Manor went far beyond the ordinary. Had he not arrived in Lucius's car, Harry would have been forced to break through a gauntlet of enchantments that would have identified him as an intruder.

Not that the current Harry couldn't have managed it—but that sort of show of force would have served no purpose. These wards had been cast not against him, but to protect a family from Death Eaters.

The Rolls-Royce rolled to a stop before the manor. The Malfoy family and Harry stepped out, and with a flick of Lucius's wand, the car glided off on its own to find the garage. The luxury vehicle moved away without a sound, smooth and unhurried.

The beautiful stone manor looked like nothing so much as a refined, miniaturised Hogwarts. In place of the deep lake harbouring a giant squid, a lush pond bloomed with lotus flowers; in place of the forest teeming with monsters and magical creatures, a garden of exquisite blossoms. The elegant white-brick building seemed to have been designed with a craftsman's painstaking care from the very first stone—every window, every soaring tower, composed with an eye for form. It was what you might get if you stripped Hogwarts of everything superfluous and kept only the beautiful.

While Harry was taking in his surroundings, the Malfoy family had already opened the door and gone inside. Carina noticed him lagging behind and reached back to pull him along.

"Come on, Harry."

"Hmm. I'd planned to visit my girlfriend's house when her parents weren't home. This is considerably less appealing."

Lucius, who had somehow caught that remark, turned to fix Harry with a withering look. Before he could say anything, Carina glanced at Harry with an expression of weary contempt.

"Hmph. As if you'd know what to do with it anyway, you idiot."

From Harry's perspective, that was deeply unfair. He'd picked up on every signal with uncanny precision—had deliberated, held himself back, exercised extraordinary restraint for a variety of reasons, chief among them the soul of Riddle. If anything, he deserved commendation, not insults. He was nearly indignant enough to say so.

But whatever Harry felt was nothing compared to Lucius. The man turned to his daughter with a bewildered, slightly strangled voice.

"Wh—what do you mean by that, Carina? You mean he—he hasn't—"

Carina tossed her hair back and replied with cool irritation.

"Exactly what it sounds like. You heard me perfectly well."

"Carina—Carina?"

Carina did not answer her father. She simply strode down the corridor at a brisk pace. Lucius followed in her wake, his expression that of a man who desperately did not want to believe what he'd just heard.

Harry was about to follow when Narcissa placed a hand on his shoulder and spoke in a gentle tone.

"Potter. Is what Carina said true? You haven't laid a hand on her—not once?"

Harry felt, for the first time in a long while, the sensation of being tested. What was the correct answer here? Confirmation? Denial? A carefully calibrated half-truth?

Even Harry understood that he needed to earn the goodwill of at least one of his prospective in-laws. Better to make a favourable impression where he could.

"I've laid hands on her. The problem is that's where it stopped."

He said it with a grumbling tone, but the subtext was unmistakable—a quiet appeal to his own restraint. Emphasising care and consideration for a child tended to go over well with parents, regardless of which one. When Harry said it, Narcissa's expression filled with something warm and quietly proud.

"I'd been anxious about entrusting Carina to you, given your reputation… But this is a relief. It seems you care for her very much."

A contented smile spread across her pale face. In that moment, Harry found himself thinking of all the time he and Carina had spent together. The hours side by side beneath the Invisibility Cloak. The two of them alone in the prefects' bathroom. The steady, accumulating closeness between them. To call it 'caring for her' felt like a generous understatement—he'd come very close to the end of the road. He simply hadn't crossed the final line.

Harry looked away from Narcissa's gaze. The goodwill and trust she directed at him were too heavy to hold for long. He hesitated for a moment, then exhaled and answered honestly.

"To be truthful, I wasn't always so careful. There were circumstances each time that made me hesitate—it wasn't restraint so much as bad timing. If it had been purely a matter of inclination, I'd have done something about it long ago. As you know, things between us haven't exactly been straightforward."

The feeling of a sinner in the confessional suited Harry rather well just then. He braced himself for Narcissa's anger, or her sense of betrayal, or some sharp rebuke. But contrary to his expectations, she continued to smile with the same warmth.

"That is precisely what I mean. If you cared for each other through all the time that wasn't straightforward… then there is reason to hope for what comes next."

Narcissa said this, then reached out and took Draco's hand.

"Draco, I think we ought to give them some space."

"What? Why?"

"Because what they have to discuss won't be of much interest to you. Come along. You said you'd made the Quidditch team as a Seeker? I found a practice Snitch for you…"

"Really?! Where? Show me, show me!"

The moment they were home, Draco came alive like a fish returned to water, practically bouncing on his heels. Narcissa and Draco disappeared up the staircase.

Harry shrugged. He'd secured one ally. That would do nicely for whatever further skirmishes lay ahead with Lucius.

Beyond the entrance hall, a wide corridor stretched out, its floor laid with deep red carpet. Enchanted torches along the walls lit themselves in sequence, welcoming their master home. At the far end stood a door of dark chestnut wood, which swung open of its own accord as Carina approached, revealing a sitting room furnished with elegant chairs and a solid oak table.

Harry, Carina, and Lucius made their way to the sitting room without exchanging a word. Whenever Harry and Lucius happened to meet each other's eyes, they locked into a staring contest immediately, neither willing to look away first. When both men stood rooted to the spot, glaring at each other, Carina had no choice but to clap her hands.

"Dobby!"

At her call, there was a sharp crack, and the house-elf Dobby appeared. The small creature, dressed in a pillowcase, clasped his hands together and squeaked at the top of his voice.

"Miss called for Dobby, miss!"

"Bring drinks and biscuits. We'll be talking in here."

"Leave it to Dobby!"

Dobby announced this and vanished with another crack. Carina stepped between Harry and Lucius, who were still engaged in their silent war of nerves.

"Harry. Sit here."

"Ah, we're finally sitting down. I was starting to wonder whether those four-legged things were actually fanged beasts in disguise. You've been keeping your guest standing this whole time."

Carina shot Harry a cool look, then physically took Lucius by the arm and steered him toward a chair. Even Lucius, who had been standing as rigid as a statue, moved—slowly—when Carina guided him.

"…Father, sit here as well."

"A fine idea, Potter… I've been thinking, in fact, that I ought to have a chair made that bites unwelcome guests on the backside, for future occasions such as this…"

How long was she expected to stand between these two? And one of them was her father, whom she respected, and the other was the man she loved. Both of them were scraping the bottom of the barrel simultaneously, and Carina was so mortified on her own behalf that she could have died.

Oblivious to her feelings, the two men pressed on with their petty campaign.

"The manor is grand, but the man inside it seems rather small."

"The fact that I permitted you to set foot in this manor at all is a generosity beyond measure. Though I suppose it's rather pointless to discuss breadth of character with someone who hasn't even a home to invite guests to."

"Oh, I'm enormously broad-minded. I've got a whole Voldemort living inside me—shall I show you—"

"Miss! Dobby has brought drinks and biscuits!"

If Dobby hadn't appeared at precisely that moment and set down the grape juice and biscuits, Carina might have fled the room in sheer embarrassment. She snatched the glasses and plate from Dobby and set them down on the table with a decisive clatter. Then, wearing an expression of undisguised displeasure, she opened the conversation. If she didn't start it, it was clear no one would.

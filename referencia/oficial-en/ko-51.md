The term had ended.

Before everyone returned home, still carrying the excitement of the Triwizard Tournament—

Harry Potter called for Cedric Diggory.

"Cedric. Have you heard the news about Mr. Crouch?"

"My father told me. I still can't believe it. He was the one who handed me the trophy at the very last moment..."

Cedric's reaction was exactly as expected.

Confusion, and bewilderment.

He had just won the Triwizard Tournament, and now a murder had occurred out of nowhere—he couldn't decide whether to feel joy or grief.

"They're saying Igor Karkaroff—the Durmstrang headmaster—killed him."

"He seemed like a man living in fear, but I never imagined he'd go as far as murder. I can't make sense of it at all."

A perfectly normal reaction.

The explanation that Karkaroff had done it out of sheer madness was far too thin.

A man who served as headmaster of Durmstrang, murdering a senior Ministry official in a fit of madness—it simply didn't hold up to reason.

Harry nodded in sympathy, then spoke to scratch at the itch he knew Cedric was feeling.

"Cedric, keep this between us. The person who put my name in the Goblet of Fire—it was Karkaroff."

"What?"

Cedric asked, visibly stunned.

"Why would he do that? Having two Champions from Hogwarts wouldn't benefit him at all."

"You've read the articles about Karkaroff being a former Death Eater, haven't you? After Moody arrested him, he made a deal—gave up his fellow Death Eaters in exchange for his freedom."

"I know. But wasn't that all finished and done with?"

"According to Moody, some of the remaining Death Eaters who held a grudge against him for that attacked him when he came to England to watch the Quidditch World Cup. He was captured, and he only won his release by promising to kill me during the Triwizard Tournament—make it look like an accident."

"What? Is that really true, Harry?"

"If Moody wasn't lying. Either way, he devised all sorts of schemes to kill me, but thankfully, enough people helped me along the way that I barely managed to survive."

The pieces connected in Cedric's mind.

The Daily Prophet had been running story after story painting Karkaroff as a monstrous criminal, and Cedric himself had never had a good impression of the man.

Add to that Karkaroff's history—and the Triwizard tasks that had been unusually brutal toward Harry.

Harry, a fourth-year, had faced the Hungarian Horntail, said to be the most ferocious of dragons, and had descended to the bottom of the lake without having studied advanced Transfiguration.

Even after his victory, Cedric had harboured quiet doubts. Now, with the information Harry had just given him, those doubts snapped together into a single, clear picture.

Cedric spoke as though something had just clicked into place.

"Don't tell me. Is that why you ran into so many more obstacles than anyone else, even though you were the first one into the maze?"

That was actually because Harry had been deliberately wandering in circles to trigger the traps himself. Harry kept a perfectly straight face about that particular detail.

"Who knows? I can only guess."

"What a despicable man. To use the Triwizard Tournament—where everyone is competing at their very best—as a means to harm someone."

Righteous, earnest Cedric was outraged... exactly as Harry had anticipated.

Harry smiled inwardly.

"He killed Barty Crouch and fled. Some say it was out of spite, but Moody told me something else as well. He said it was Karkaroff who cast the Dark Mark into the sky at the Quidditch World Cup."

"That's... You-Know-Who's mark, isn't it?"

"Apparently it was on the orders of the Death Eater remnants who had been threatening him. He fired the Dark Mark into the chaos and ran—a signal that he intended to keep his promise to kill me. But Barty Crouch happened to be there and grew suspicious of him."

"So he killed him before he could be exposed?"

"What other reason would the headmaster of Durmstrang have to seek Crouch out and murder him?"

Cedric Diggory was Hogwarts Champion.

He carried a debt of conscience toward Harry, who had finished a narrow second due to injury.

That made him all the more inclined to take Harry's words seriously. Especially when the theory was as plausible—and as gripping—as this one.

"Good God. You were in real danger. And I had no idea..."
"It was a fair competition. Don't worry about it. Whatever scheming he did behind the scenes, what happened inside the arena was the same for all of us."

Harry knew that framing it this way would press on Cedric's guilt.

Cedric Diggory—Hogwarts Champion, Hufflepuff's pride—would now become a herald for Harry's cause.

Voldemort's return was too momentous a thing for ordinary people to simply accept. Cornelius Fudge's reaction would be the typical one.

So the truth needed to be bent, just slightly.

Revealed only enough to make people want to know more—only enough to be of use to Harry.

That way, it might help, even by a hair's breadth.

At roughly the same hour, Sirius Black was meeting with Ludo Bagman.

"Good day, Mr. Bagman."

"Ah—Mr. Black."

Ludo Bagman wrung his hands together, visibly on edge. Sirius settled deep into his chair and crossed his legs with unhurried elegance.

He studied Bagman for a long moment before speaking in a measured tone.

"As you know, I was once a fugitive. My name has been cleared, but I'm still known to many as a traitor and an escaped convict."

"I heard the news. That it was all a false accusation..."

"It was. But clearing up a misunderstanding requires considerable effort. The older the misunderstanding, the more effort it demands—exponentially so. I have the Black family inheritance, of course, and given time and work, it's possible to restore my reputation. However..."

Sirius maintained an air of complete ease throughout. Bagman, by contrast, was fidgeting like a man seated across from a debt collector.

Sirius continued.

"However, word has reached me that you've lost a rather significant sum in a certain... wager."

"A trifling thing. That's the nature of betting, isn't it? Sometimes you win, sometimes you lose."

"Of course. But wagering against goblins does seem rather rash, wouldn't you say? Goblins are so very—forgive me for putting it this way—mercenary about money."

Bagman nodded vigorously.

"You're absolutely right! There's no reasoning with them. They threatened to take everything I have if I don't produce the gold!"

"And to think they'd treat a man of your standing—Head of the Department of Magical Games and Sports—in such a manner. No matter how large the debt, a man's position doesn't simply vanish. A few steady years of repayment and it would all be settled. If they can't wait even that long, can they really call themselves civilised?"

"Exactly!"

Sirius uncrossed his legs and placed both hands on the desk. Between his fingers dangled a coin purse heavy with gold.

Hope flooded into Bagman's eyes.

"In the interest of restoring my reputation and upholding the dignity of a wizard, I'd like to lend you the gold, Mr. Bagman."

"Thank you—thank you, Mr. Black!"

Bagman looked ready to bow. Sirius smiled pleasantly, and Bagman's hand began to drift slowly toward the purse.

"However. There is a condition."

Sirius closed his fingers around the purse and drew it back. Bagman looked at him with trembling eyes.

"I've been told that Fred and George Weasley—the Weasley twins—have yet to receive their winnings. Is that correct?"

"Ah, that... yes, that's true, but it's fine, really. The amount they wagered was only seventeen Galleons, so—"

"What do you mean, fine? If a bet was made, you don't repay what was wagered—you pay what was won. That's the agreement."

Bagman's body went rigid.

The Weasley twins had bet on a long shot—Bulgaria catching the Snitch, but Ireland winning the match—and it had come through, earning them roughly three hundred Galleons. Bagman, unable to pay on the spot, had fobbed them off with Leprechaun gold and then proceeded to ignore them entirely.

From Bagman's perspective, the twins' winnings were a nuisance: a small stake that had ballooned into an enormous payout.

For the past year, they had pestered him relentlessly for the money. But they were underage wizards. Compared to the goblin debt, they ranked nowhere near the top of his priorities.

Bagman had simply intended to stiff them.

Sirius shook his head slowly.

"That won't do. You may cheat goblins out of their gold, but breaking a promise made to children is another matter entirely. What face would you show them? Pay the Weasley twins first."

Bagman's thinking was that of a man in the grip of a severe gambling addiction. To him, the Weasley twins were nothing but a pair of small-time thieves trying to squeeze three hundred Galleons out of a seventeen-Galleon stake.

Bagman twisted his lips.

"I don't need to pay them. There are more pressing matters—"
"It seems I've misjudged you."

Sirius pocketed the purse and rose from his chair. Bagman lurched to his feet in alarm.

"Mr. Bagman. I'm lending you this money for the sake of my own reputation. But if the very man receiving my gold won't even pay out to a pair of children, what does that make me? I'd simply be using my money to line the goblins' pockets."

"Ah, that—"

To his credit, Bagman had enough sense to read the room. This Sirius Black clearly had a soft spot for the Weasley twins. If Bagman refused to pay them, the money wouldn't be forthcoming.

When someone is holding the gold, they hold all the power. Bagman looked at him and cried out.

"I wasn't thinking clearly. I'll pay the children first!"

"Good. In that case, let me lend you this much to start."

Without warning, Sirius tossed the purse across the room. He had been retired for years, but he had once been the star Beater of his team. Ludo Bagman caught the heavy purse and staggered under its weight.

Bagman shot Sirius a brief glare for throwing it without a word of warning, but the moment he glimpsed the brilliantly gleaming gold coins inside, he swallowed hard.

The purse contained well over five hundred Galleons—enough to pay the twins and still buy the goblins a brief moment of reason.

"Thank you—thank you so much, Mr. Black!"

"I'm only lending it. I'm doing so because of the Quidditch player you once were. I trust you'll repay it someday."

"Without question!"

Bagman snatched up the purse with unseemly haste. Sirius watched the graceless display, then remarked flatly.

"Oh—and though I'm sure it won't come to that—if you happen not to pay the Weasley twins first... I'll hire goblins with the remainder and have it extracted from you one way or another. Just so you know."

Bagman froze mid-step, but quickly forced a smile and extended his hand to Sirius.

"Of course!"

Sirius took his hand and shook it without a word.

The sight of a man in middle age—spineless, without conviction—was almost physically repellent to Sirius. But the reason he had come here to lend Ludo Bagman money was solely because Harry had asked him to.

With the Minister for Magic refusing to believe Dumbledore's word, Harry wanted to build connections throughout the Ministry. Ludo Bagman—head of the Department of Magical Games and Sports, a man with genuine public recognition—was a perfectly suited target.

If spending surplus gold could win them an ally, it was a worthwhile trade. That was what Harry had said.

Using money and influence to manipulate people. It wasn't Sirius's preferred method.

"What is it that's driving you so hard, Harry?"

Sirius had followed Harry's wishes.

No parent can win against their child. Even when that parent is only a godfather.

Peter Pettigrew could communicate with rats.

This was not because he possessed any special magical gift, but simply because he had spent so many years living as a rat—a skill acquired through long practice, both a power and a side effect of being an Animagus.

Because of it, Peter Pettigrew could understand the language of rats.

Harry had decided to put him to work doing what only he could do.

"Mr. Pettigrew."

A Secret Keeper bears both the secret and an obligation. In the wizarding world, nothing is one-sided. A Keeper who betrays their charge and reveals the secret they were entrusted with takes on a corresponding burden in return.

That leash was now held by Harry Potter—the one who had survived beneath the secret that had been exposed.

"Your task is simple. Commune with the rats and find where the snake dwells. He will be there."

Pettigrew was bound by a constraint—a particularly punitive one. Outside of Harry and Dumbledore's presence, he could not breathe a word of anything pertaining to them.

Harry spoke quietly.

"People still believe you're dead, so move like the rat you are and gather information. We'll uncover their hiding places, one by one."

He hated it.

The wild, frightening, dangerous life—he was utterly sick of it. He wanted to live in a comfortable bed, eating good cheese.

But in order to survive—

"The only person who can forgive you is me. Do your work well, and... I'll let you live."

He had no choice but to follow Harry's orders.

Pettigrew transformed into his rat form and scoured the length and breadth of Britain. Searching for the place where the snake resided.

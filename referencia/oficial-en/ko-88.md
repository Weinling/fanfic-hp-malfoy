# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 88: Victory...

---

The Killing Curse was absolute—but for that very reason, it could only claim one life at a time. Even when cast by the Dark Lord himself, that rule held.

Snape's life was the price it took, and with that, the curse was spent. His body toppled slowly. Before his back ever met the ground, he was already gone. He crumpled like a puppet with its strings cut—head snapping sideways, striking the floor, bouncing once like something hollow, then falling still. His neck could no longer hold him. The movement of a body emptied of life was grotesque in a way that left no room for doubt.

Harry Potter's eyes went wide. Voldemort regarded Snape's corpse and murmured, unhurried.

"So love means dying in someone's place, one by one. How illuminating. But Potter… there's no one left to die for you now. What will you do?"

Could you grieve for an enemy's death?

Harry had always assumed that if his enemy died, he'd stand over the body and laugh. That if the man was truly his enemy, his death would be a spice—something that made life richer, more vivid.

But when Snape threw himself in front of Harry and died—

Harry's vision flooded red. He snatched up his wand and fired everything at Voldemort—every tangled, unresolved emotion, every scrap of fury he had.

"*Expelliarmus!*"

"*Avada Kedavra!*"

The two spells collided in the space between them.

And in that instant, the phenomenon Harry and Snape had waited so long for finally came to pass.

The red light and the green light bled into each other like pigments dissolving in water, and between the two wands a brilliant golden beam blazed into being. The alien sensation made both Voldemort and Harry stare, wide-eyed, each tightening his grip.

As though utterly indifferent to the struggle of their masters, the two wands resonated with each other and burned hotter still. The song of the phoenix whose feather lay at the heart of both filled the entire room like a wind instrument, drawing out a single, soaring note. It was a reunion between two creatures who spoke in song—warm and familiar, a sound that felt like recognition.

Two wands sharing the same core. The moment they connected, a channel opened between Harry and Voldemort. Like water flowing from high ground to low, Voldemort's power and magic poured along the golden rope and into Harry—flowing until the levels equalized, and then continuing still.

The prophecy had bound them. Voldemort had tried to kill Harry and failed. And now two wands of the same core were aimed at each other. All three conditions made this connection possible.

In this moment, Harry held power equal to Voldemort's own.

The golden beam erupted skyward, then fractured into thousands of threads that rained down around them—as though constructing a cage to hold them both. The cage was so beautiful it seemed designed to imprison the soul rather than the body.

"What are you—!"

Voldemort's scarlet eyes were wide with fury as he strained to sever the connection. His bloodless white hands trembled violently. Harry gripped his wand in both hands and forced himself to concentrate, holding the link together.

The original plan had been to stall for time. To hold on somehow, keep Voldemort pinned until Dumbledore had dealt with everything else and arrived. That alone would have been enough.

But Harry had no intention of merely stalling.

Even as the connection drained the strength from him, even as his wand hand burned, Harry bared his teeth in a grin.

"Can you feel it yet? Death, closing in?"

The phoenix song swelled. Each time the golden bars vibrated, a sound that seemed to belong to no earthly instrument filled the room to its rafters. The golden cage was itself an orchestra—wind and string and percussion at once. The phoenix's cry had become a full ensemble, singing Harry's victory.

"Parlour tricks—! You think stealing my power will make you win?!"

Voldemort thrust his wand forward. The golden beam began to slide—slowly, inexorably—toward Harry. A torrent of immense force crashed against him, and his wand shook violently in his grip, as though a storm were coiling around its tip.

In moments that force would shatter his wand. Then it would tear him apart. The only way to survive was to break the connection now—

Carina.

But Harry steadied himself. He ignored the burning in his palm, ignored the blisters forming, and held on.

He painted a future in his mind. The brilliant, happy future waiting on the other side of this victory. A world where no one hunted him, where nothing was held against him, where conflict ended in petty arguments and no one lived in fear—and in that world, he was with her.

To lose now would be a betrayal of Carina. He had to defeat Voldemort and survive.

That was why he had to win.

"Carina…"

Harry breathed her name like an anchor. The orb of light that had been pressing toward him faltered. Perfectly balanced, trembling—and then, slowly, slowly, it began to move the other way.

"Just let me make it back alive. I'll…"

Whether to whisper something sweet, or to kiss her senseless, or simply to press close and talk through the night until dawn—

All of it. Obviously all of it. The only question was the order.

Every time he reached for the future—for hope—the orb climbed back along the beam. Voldemort's eyes flew open in disbelief. Slowly, but without question, the orb was moving toward him.

But then—what did any of that matter. Harry smiled faintly and murmured.

"…First things first. I'll have to say thank you."

At that moment.

The orb touched the tip of Voldemort's wand. A hiss, like something scorching, and Voldemort began to scream—a sound full of nothing but pain.

"This can't—!"

"People always say that right before it does."

And then something neither Harry nor Voldemort had anticipated happened.

From the tip of Voldemort's wand, something far larger began to billow and rise. It looked like a bust sculpted from dense grey smoke. The shape gathered itself, grew hair, sprouted limbs, and tore free from the wand's end.

If there was a second moment in Harry's life that had truly stunned him, this was it. The smoke was a ghost wearing Snape's face. It seemed too solid to be a ghost—but there was no other word for it. After all, Snape's body lay plainly at Harry's feet.

Snape's ghost glanced once at Voldemort, then moved toward Harry.

"Focus, if you want to live."

"You're the one who broke my concentration. This is the second most astonishing thing that's ever happened to me. How exactly am I supposed to focus?"

For the record, the first had been when Carina fell through the ceiling of the boys' lavatory. Harry's heart had nearly stopped—in more ways than one.

Snape's ghost twisted his lips in that infuriating way and looked down at Harry with contempt.

"Insolent to the last… This is your final lesson. Listen carefully, Potter. This time your life is genuinely at stake, so if you fail to pay attention, the consequences will be rather severe…"

Still insufferable, even as a ghost. Harry snorted. Snape began to explain in his slow, unpleasant drawl.

"*Priori Incantatem* is a phenomenon that replays spells. It regurgitates the magic cast most recently and works backward through everything the wand has ever done. In that process, the echoes of those killed by the Killing Curse also appear. I died most recently—hence, I emerged first…"

"So… you won't be the only one to appear."

"No…"

Before the word had fully left him, more figures began to emerge from Voldemort's wand.

An elderly wizard whose name Harry didn't know. A Muggle family. Amelia Bones, whom Harry had seen at his hearing. A grey-haired Muggle businessman. Those Voldemort had murdered rose from the wand one after another, paused in brief confusion, then walked toward Harry as though it were the most natural thing in the world.
"Are you facing the Dark Lord?"

The old wizard said it quietly and placed a hand on Harry's shoulder. In that instant, a faint but unmistakable surge of strength returned to Harry's body.

"He—he killed my family! That man—!"

The Muggle family cried out in anguish, their words directed at Harry. The moment that raw grief reached him, his mind sharpened and his vision cleared.

"Mr. Potter… I'm sorry we meet like this. But I cannot tell you how glad I am to be of some small help…"

Madam Bones, whom Harry had seen at the Wizengamot, smiled and laid her hand against his back. A portion of the golden beam threaded itself through every part of Harry's body. That light had its origin in a phoenix feather. The phoenix's power of renewal moved through him—healing him, breathing life back into his exhausted mind.

After her came more. The number of people Voldemort had killed was beyond counting, and every one of them stood behind Harry and held him up.

As they faced him—all those he had murdered—Voldemort's eyes widened with something that looked, unmistakably, like fear. With each face he recognized, his expression twisted further. All he could manage now was to barely hold the orb of light at bay.

And yet—which was to say—even now, Voldemort was holding it back with tremendous power.

Snape murmured.

"*Priori Incantatem* is not an offensive spell. Its effect is simply to expel spells from the wand. All of this is merely the re-enactment of past echoes…"

"…Could you summarize? I know you're good at it—you've been phoning in lessons for years."

The sarcasm was genuine, but so was the exhaustion. Harry was on the edge of collapse. Only a fierce will to live, combined with the strength the echoes had lent him, was keeping him upright. Any ordinary wizard would have lost consciousness long ago.

Snape reached out with evident reluctance. His hand touched the golden beam.

"But an echo with will can resonate—and resonate again—until it shatters glass."

At that, the ghosts—no, the echoes of the dead, shaped from the residue of magic—seized the golden beam one by one, as though taking hold of a rope in a tug-of-war. The beam resonated with them, and golden ripples sank into their forms.

"An echo with will…"

Harry, barely holding himself together, forced the words out.

"…The way I understand it, you and the other victims are about to charge Voldemort all at once."

Snape said nothing. Harry read the answer in his silence. Snape stepped to the front and led the dead forward.

"I despise you, Potter."

"What a coincidence… I despise you too. At the very least, I should have hit you once. With my own hand."

"I did not sacrifice myself for you. There was simply no other way. The only person who ever sacrificed anything for you is Carina. Give her my regards."

With that, Snape launched himself at Voldemort. Behind him, the countless dead joined hands and charged. They seemed almost exhilarated—that they could have this much revenge, even now—and they threw themselves at Voldemort's wand, wreathed in golden light.

Voldemort saw them coming and let out a scream of pure horror.

"Dead—things—how dare—!"

The golden beam shuddered violently. Each time one of the dead struck it, the beam blazed hotter and brighter, until it was burning white.

And then, at last.

As though summoned by the memory of a long absence, Voldemort's wand—which had been still for some time—trembled again. From its tip, a woman's head slowly bloomed into being.

Snape recognized her from the outline alone. That long hair. That bright expression. That slight frame. She was exactly as he remembered her.

Lily saw Snape and her eyes went wide. Her mouth moved, startled.

It was far too generous a gift for a final moment. He was already dead—but Snape closed his eyes and turned away, afraid of what longing might do to him.

"…Tell her I'm sorry."

Lily had appeared, which meant James Potter would follow. Facing him would be deeply uncomfortable. And watching a family reunite would be agony.

Better to let this echo dissolve before that happened. Snape kept his gaze fixed on Voldemort and did not look back. The mountain of corpses Voldemort had made. Crushed beneath the weight of their echoes, Voldemort could barely breathe.

Voldemort's scarlet eyes met Snape's black ones. Voldemort screamed.

"How dare you—!"

"My Lord…"

Snape reached toward the golden beam. At the tip of the wand, the golden sphere blazed like a sun. Without hesitation, Snape closed both hands around it.

Crack. A sound that should never come from a wand. The force, the heat, the light—all of it was climbing back up through Voldemort's wand. The echoes of the dead roared. Each time they struck the golden beam, the fractures in the wand spread wider. Beneath it all, the phoenix's sacred song hummed softly.

"…Thank you, Sev."

A quiet voice of gratitude, from somewhere behind him. Snape—for the first time since her death—smiled with joy.

His hands burned. From the fingertips inward, he turned to ash and scattered into the air. Still he did not yield. Snape smiled, at peace, and gripped the golden sphere in both hands—and with everything he had left, drove it into Voldemort's wand.

"…It is time for you to experience true death, my Lord. As I did."

"Severus Snape—!!"

Crack. Crack. The fractures deepened. The entire wand seemed to be burning gold. The golden flames leapt to Voldemort's white hands and fed on him, growing as they consumed.

Voldemort's face contorted beyond recognition as he screamed.

"I cannot die! I will return—I will always return! And when I do—every last one who betrayed me will be destroyed!"

Hairline cracks spread across Voldemort's body. The magic detonating from within him broke through his flesh like a hatching egg, spilling outward. From his eyes, his nose, his mouth, from every fracture across his body, white light erupted—

And then, at some moment that could not be precisely named—

The golden resonance ceased.

The souls that had been hammering against Voldemort and crying out fell silent all at once.

A quiet stillness descended. The sound and light that had filled the room until a heartbeat ago were gone. Through a broken window, a single thread of wind moved through the room alone. It scattered the remaining ash into the air.

Harry had won.

It was over. Voldemort died unwatched, unmourned, alone. What he left behind was only ash.

Harry, wrung of every last reserve, sank to the floor. Lily and James came toward him. They had appeared last, and still held their shape—but they were fading, slowly dissolving.

Harry managed a tired smile.

"Mum. Dad."

Lily and James opened their mouths and spoke, but Harry heard nothing. With the golden resonance gone, their voices had gone with it.

It didn't matter. There wasn't enough time left to fill with words anyway.
Instead, Lily and James placed their hands on his shoulders. He felt almost no weight—but warmth spread through his chest.

"If you stayed just a little longer, I could introduce you to your daughter-in-law."

His parents broke into wide, radiant smiles. They looked at each other the way people do when reaching for an old memory, then turned back to Harry—Lily giving him a thumbs-up, James raising a fist in encouragement.

"She's a Malfoy. What do you think?"

James looked rather taken aback—but Lily caught his hand and bent it firmly, and he gave a faint smile and nodded. Harry laughed under his breath.

Their arms and legs grew translucent. Just before they dissolved entirely into the air, Lily opened her mouth wide. No sound came, but Harry read her lips.

Be happy.

He would be. How could he not be, with the future they had made together?

Harry nodded. Then Lily and James were gone—carried away on a breath of wind.

A moment later, the door burst open and Ron and Hermione came running in. They looked exactly as they had when Harry had last seen them. Behind them came Dumbledore and Moody, and behind those two, Molly Weasley and Narcissa Malfoy maintained a careful, mutual distance, each apparently unwilling to enter before the other.

"Harry!"

Hermione ran to his side.

"What happened? What about him?"

"Dead."

Harry shrugged.

"If you want a body, you'll need to sweep up the dust nearby. He burned to ash and vanished."

"G—good heavens. You actually—? You beat him, alone—?"

"Not alone. Over there—"

Harry tilted his head to gesture somewhere, then stopped.

Severus Snape.

The name surfaced in his mind, and with it came a tangle of feelings he couldn't easily sort through.

By no generous measure had Snape been a good man. He had hated Harry without real cause, been profoundly unfair, and at times genuinely sinister. The business of steering Carina into drinking the poison herself—whatever the outcome—was something Harry could never forgive, regardless of how it had ended.

And yet. Death had always been the most complete absolution ever devised. Harry found it difficult to hold any resentment toward a man who had thrown his life away to defeat Voldemort.

He had been the worst of teachers. The fact that he had stolen even the opportunity to hate him properly made it worse.

A man who had loved Harry's mother. Who had despised Harry for it, and yet could not stop himself from protecting him. Harry closed his eyes and offered him a brief, silent farewell.

"S—Snape… Professor Snape is… dead…"

"Professor Lupin is still alive! Ennervate!"

In any case, it was over. While Moody helped Lupin to his feet, Ron and Hermione looked at each other with expressions of pure disbelief.

"Merlin. Really? We won?"

"He—he's gone? Harry actually—You-Know-Who—?"

"Ron, I've been meaning to say something—your word choices are bothering me."

"What? Why?"

"'You-Know-Who.' You keep saying it like this fight is still going on. And even now that it's done, you're still phrasing everything as a question."

"What's wrong with that? What did I say that was so terrible?"

Their bickering ended when Harry let out a low groan. He looked up at Ron and Hermione.

"What about the others?"

"They're over there keeping watch on the Death Eaters. Fred and George's 'Full-Body Binding Contraption' is doing excellent work. I thought they'd charge for it, but they didn't take a single Knut… Why do they always squeeze money out of me specifically."

Ron looked ready to elaborate at length, but Hermione cut him off.

"No one died. Professor Dumbledore dealt with all of them on the way up! It was extraordinary—every time he flicked his wand the floor split open and—anyway, because of that, no one… well…"

Hermione's voice trailed off as her eyes fell on Snape's body.

To have fought so fierce a battle and lost only one person was a tremendous victory—but with that one person lying there, it was impossible to simply rejoice.

While Hermione stood uncertain, Dumbledore spoke in his gentle voice.

"Let us grieve, but not despair. Severus risked his life as a spy and fought harder than anyone in this room. To mourn the victory he helped achieve would be to dishonour him. You may be glad. Only—let us take a moment for him first."

Dumbledore removed his hat and closed his eyes. Everyone did the same, as though by agreement, and paid their respects to Snape. Even Narcissa Malfoy was weeping quietly.

After a brief silence, Dumbledore replaced his hat and murmured.

"We'll need to arrange a funeral when we return… I had hoped no one would have to die…"

He raised his wand, and Snape's body began to rise slowly into the air. Dumbledore arranged his limbs with care, then drew a white cloth over him with his own hands.

Then Dumbledore turned to Harry, still sitting on the floor, and extended his hand.

"Well done, Harry. Truly, truly well done. You worked so hard."

Harry exhaled, the last of his strength leaving him in a long breath.

If Dumbledore had arrived sooner, perhaps Snape would have lived. But in the end, throwing himself in front of Harry had been Snape's own choice. Without Snape, Harry would have died. Which meant Harry owed him gratitude, whatever else he felt.

Either way—whether Snape died or Harry died—only one person had been lost. For a war, that was a success by any measure. What ending could have been more complete than this…

"I'd like this to be the last time I have to work this hard."

"I imagine so… Though I can't promise that with complete certainty."

"At a moment like this, you could at least pretend to promise."

Harry murmured it with a faint smile, then reached up with his free hand and took Dumbledore's. He pulled, and Dumbledore pulled back, and Harry hauled himself to his feet. The effort made them both sway for a moment—

And in that instant, Harry saw himself reflected in Dumbledore's eyes. Strangely, the Harry Potter looking back at him had red eyes. Scarlet eyes, like Voldemort's—

The Harry in Dumbledore's eyes whispered in a voice that was not quite his own.

"*Avada Kedavra.*"

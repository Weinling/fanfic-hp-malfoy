"Hey! Harry Potter!"

Draco came storming over to the Gryffindor table.

A Slytherin visiting Gryffindor. And not just any Slytherin—a second-year, no less, marching straight up to Harry Potter, who could be called the very embodiment of Gryffindor. Approaching, without a second thought, a wizard who might be harbouring Voldemort within him.

The Gryffindors turned to look at Draco and—

"Good morning."

"How's being Slytherin's Seeker treating you? You're so small out there, we can barely spot you."

"Want some walnut pie, Draco?"

—greeted him warmly.

They were all in Dumbledore's Army together, for one thing. He was young and had an undeniably adorable face, for another. And above all, he was the little brother of Carina Malfoy, who was utterly mad about Harry Potter. He could be a bit arrogant, yes, but at his age, that was easy enough to find endearing.

Draco himself, however, found their goodwill thoroughly irritating.

"Oh, leave it! I hate walnuts! And height has nothing to do with being a Seeker! I was going to make a brilliant debut in the next match and beat Harry Potter! But then—"

Draco grabbed the Firebolt and shouted.

"Why did you run away! Why did you quit the Quidditch team!"

"Hmm."

Harry scratched his chin, trying to work out how to explain it.

The truth was, he simply didn't have time for Quidditch right now.

According to Snape's memories, Snape had known Harry was a Horcrux. And he had done something—something intended to save him.

But the rest of those memories had left no clear clues as to what that something was.

When Harry had returned to Snape's office, all that remained were shelves packed solid with materials on Defence Against the Dark Arts. On a whim, he had begun pulling them open.

And that was when he understood why Snape had always wanted the Defence Against the Dark Arts post.

Magic that couldn't be found even in the Restricted Section of Hogwarts Library was in those pages. Secret, ancient spells that no ordinary wizard would ever have access to. Sacrifice, the soul, life and death. Old arcane arts that belonged less to magic than to something older and darker.

Harry had been consumed, day and night, with learning and testing the spells contained within. Every last one of them was dangerous, and there was simply no room left for anything else.

That was why he'd handed off the Seeker position.

Too tedious to explain all that, Harry settled for a simple summary.

"Well, someone has to leave so the balance between houses stays fair, don't they?"

He said it with a smug little smile.

Harry had no more desire to give a lengthy explanation to small children than anyone else would. He was fairly certain Draco wouldn't grasp the full picture—and, if he was honest, he rather hoped he wouldn't.

Draco, stung by the provocation, puffed up and shouted.

"Fight me! I'll beat you this time, I swear!"

"Ha. Why don't you beat my replacement first? Do that, and I'll consider it."

"Who's your replacement?"

"Someone you know well enough. The brilliant new rising star of the Gryffindor Quidditch team—Ginny Weasley. Ginny, show Draco what you're made of in the next match!"

Harry gestured toward Ginny with a cheerful grin.

Ginny met his gaze—and silently rose to her feet. She fixed both Harry and Draco with a sharp, wordless look that carried something difficult to name, then turned and walked out of the Great Hall.

The coldness of it left both Harry and Draco standing there awkwardly. Harry watched her go without so much as a farewell, and scratched the back of his head.

"Is Ginny going through a phase, Ron?"

"You'd do well to keep your mouth shut."

Ron replied flatly and put a forkful of food in his mouth. Having received a chilly reception from both siblings, Harry turned his attention back to Draco.

"Right then, Draco. If you want to challenge me, win the last match of the season. Let's see—if Slytherin wins by more than two hundred points, you might even take the Cup."

"Don't get smug just because you're a little ahead! I'll demolish your team!"

How many people in the world could march up to another house's table and boldly declare they'd demolish the team? Anyone else would have found themselves surrounded by furious Gryffindors in seconds.

But Draco could get away with it.

The Gryffindors crowded around him, ruffling his hair and laughing, tossing remarks at him one by one.

"You'll need to grow a bit first. Short arms mean you'll never catch the Snitch."

"Oi, you can't be a picky eater. Nuts have essential nutrients, you know."

"Tsk, tsk. Look at that hair. One day it'll poke you in the eye and then you'll learn."

Draco thrashed furiously in the middle of what they apparently considered words of encouragement.

"Ugh, leave me alone! I'm going to grow taller! Taller than Harry Potter!"

"Yes, yes. And to do that, you'll need to eat properly first, won't you? Come on, let's go."

Harry steered him away with patient authority, back toward the Slytherin table.

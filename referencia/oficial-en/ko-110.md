Rita Skeeter had always been a tabloid journalist.

It had started in a bathroom—overhearing friends exchange whispered secrets by chance—and from that moment, gossip became both her hobby and her life. She was forever hunting it down, never letting a juicy detail slip past her. Looking back, it seemed like destiny, and luck had followed her accordingly. She'd exposed the fact that several teachers who'd been notoriously strict about student romance were themselves carrying on affairs, and everyone had praised Rita Skeeter for it. Gossip had ceased to be a pastime and become her very purpose.

The trouble began when someone shouted at her, "You're exactly like a journalist!" From that day on, Rita Skeeter's ambition was to become one, and the moment she graduated, she walked straight through the doors of the Daily Prophet. She intended to breathe new life into that stiff, fusty institution. Young and magnetic, she roamed everywhere, digging up fascinating truths.

But when the editor took the story she'd worked so hard to uncover and tossed it into the rubbish bin right in front of her, she felt as though the world had shattered. As Rita raged, the editor intoned in his dusty voice:

"We are in the business of telling people what they want to know."

What Rita had brought him was a story about an improper relationship between a senior Ministry official and his mistress. Interesting enough—but on the grounds that it would stir public anxiety, or more precisely, that it would irritate the Ministry, her article became Ashwinder fodder in the fireplace.

An ordinary journalist might have broken her quill or simply fallen into line. Rita was not ordinary. She rewrote the piece to be even more incendiary and sold it to Witch Weekly. The provocative, sensational article caused a tremendous stir, and for a brief period the magazine's circulation surpassed that of the Daily Prophet. Rita Skeeter rode the wave and kept the articles coming. Witch Weekly became the number-one favourite magazine among witches in Britain.

The publisher was furious. The old, fusty editor was sacked, and Rita stood before his replacement with her chin held high and graciously bestowed her articles upon the Daily Prophet as well. It was the moment popular appetite dismantled tired authority.

Rita Skeeter felt a thrill run through her. This was her life. Seizing people's attention with provocative stories, then wielding that attention as power. She went looking for even more incendiary truths.

But the world is a conservative place. Genuinely compelling material doesn't come along every day. Chasing ever more exciting stories, she hit a wall quickly. No matter how much legwork she put in, the results were the same dull, unremarkable pieces she'd seen in the Daily Prophet every morning.

Yet Rita was already addicted to gossip. Craving a sharper thrill, she made a choice no journalist should ever make.

When the material wasn't there, she manufactured it inside her own head.

She twisted ordinary information into something malicious and stitched together a shambling monster.

Her method of writing changed too. She would craft an article bloated with intrigue and insinuation, and when the subject pushed back, she'd whip up public opinion against them—then quietly slip away once interest cooled. Rita had drawn eyes to herself that way more than once.

It was around that time that she became an Animagus. The supply of material obtainable through legitimate means had nearly run dry. Rita deployed every tool available in her pursuit of gossip, of stories.

It worked well enough at first, but having stumbled upon a few secrets she was never meant to know, Rita earned the wariness of the world. She walked a tightrope between truth and gossip, and kept on living the life of a journalist.

And so, and so, and so.

Growing ever more sensational—yet even that sensation turning routine.

On a day that had been threatening to become truly dreadful for Rita.

While covering the Triwizard Tournament, she stumbled upon something that read exactly like a romance novel.

And that became the second turning point of Rita Skeeter's life.

Carina walked toward the mountain of stacked books.

The cover bore an illustration of a man and a woman, each holding an oversized staff, standing back to back. They were dressed like ancient sorcerers—wide-brimmed hats and robes that swept from collar to floor. A bright yellow band across the cover carried a promotional tagline in large, bold letters.

[Medieval. Magic. Academy. And a Forbidden Love! The Final Volume of Leta Sticker's Romance Series!]

That Leta Sticker was Rita Skeeter's pen name was obvious without a second glance.

Carina opened the book with trembling hands.

As it happened, the page she landed on showed an illustration of a girl lying with blood at the corner of her lips. The girl in the picture wore a heartbroken expression as she clutched someone's hand.

[Kill him... in my place...]

[No. Absolutely not!]

The man bent his head over the woman's lips as she coughed up black blood. He kissed her with fierce urgency, as though trying to draw every drop of poison out of her.

And then, a miracle unfolded. A burst of brilliant golden light gathered around them as if in blessing...]

"Aaaargh!"

Carina flung the book away as though it were something monstrous. It struck the pile beside it, and the whole stack came crashing down. The people standing nearby snapped their eyes toward her and glared.

"Excuse me, young lady!"

A middle-aged woman stepped out of the queue and scolded her.

"You can't go throwing books around like that! Do you not see the queue?"

"...Ugh."

She was right. A long line of people stood waiting for something. These books had been prepared for today's visitors, and no matter who Carina was—even if she happened to be the model for this book—she had no right to damage them.

Carina pressed her lips together and began gathering the fallen books. The woman grumbled but helped her tidy them up.

As they worked in silence, the woman spoke to Carina in a gentle, reproving tone.

"Today is Leta Sticker's signing event. For those people out there, this book will be the greatest book of their lives. Today is going to be a truly magical day! So imagine how heartbroken they'd be to receive a crumpled copy."

The middle-aged woman spoke with the devotion of someone utterly lost in the story. And so Carina found she absolutely could not say it.

That she herself was the model for this romance novel. That she was a real witch.

"Carina!"

Harry came jogging over from a distance, a coffee in each hand. He set them down in front of Carina, who was crouched on the floor gathering books, and said:

"Did you actually read it?"

"...Yes."

"Oh. Impressive! Even I couldn't get through it without burying my face in my hands."

"Only one page."

"One page is more than enough to make our little shrinking violet curl up with embarrassment."

"You make me curl up with embarrassment just as much..."

While she grumbled and finished tidying the books, the queue of people suddenly erupted into noise. Carina looked up to find everyone facing the same direction, whistling and waving.

She followed their gaze.

There was Rita Skeeter, drawing every eye in the room as she strolled forward with breezy delight. She looked like an enormous peacock—draped in every glittering ornament imaginable, her billowing fur and shawl trailing so low they nearly brushed the floor.

Rita was returning the crowd's greetings when she spotted Harry and Carina and came toward them with a warm smile.

"Oh, my muses! Come here, come here!"

Harry sprang to his feet and extended his hand.

"Good to see you, benefactor."

"My goodness. I'm genuinely moved. To think I could become the patron of the Malfoy and Potter heirs—the two who stand to inherit the greatest fortunes in the wizarding world. Who could ever have imagined it?"

Carina looked Rita up and down. The expensive violet suit, the gleaming spectacles, the fur coat draped over her shoulders—all of it announced her vanity without apology.

She must have earned enough to satisfy even herself. By selling Carina and Harry's story.

Carina muttered with barely concealed resentment.

"...Congratulations. You've become the world's greatest tabloid journalist."

"Ho ho! All thanks to the two of you! Oh, look at me—I've left my honoured guests standing!"

Rita clapped her hands with theatrical fuss. From somewhere behind her, Peter Pettigrew came scurrying forward in an oversized suit and began holding back the crowd pressing in around Rita.

"One moment, please! One moment! Leta Sticker is currently receiving an important guest! The signing begins in ten minutes—please wait until then!"

While Peter squeaked at the crowd in his thin, reedy voice, Harry and Carina followed Rita deeper into the building.

The event hall was large and bustling, as though determined to advertise how much money had been spent on it. Rita swept through the crowd, waving and smiling at everyone she passed.

Then, in the inner room prepared for her, Rita let the smile drop, exhaled, and sank into a chair.

"Sit down, both of you. Goodness. I'm exhausted."

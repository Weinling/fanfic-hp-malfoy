Brring, brring—.

The phone rang. Carina picked up her smartphone, pressed the answer button, and brought it to her ear. She swept her platinum-blonde hair back so it wouldn't get in the way, gave a small, deliberate cough, and spoke.

"Hello?"

[Ahem, ahem. Yes, good day, ma'am. This is Lloyds Bank calling. We're contacting you regarding an issue that has arisen with your account.]

A rough, unfamiliar male voice came through the line.

Lloyds Bank—that was where Carina had recently opened an account.

Harry had explained it to her: Muggles received only numbers written on a piece of paper called a bankbook. There were no vaults assigned to each family.

When she'd asked why, he'd told her it was because Muggles didn't possess treasures or enchanted objects. Carina had understood immediately. If the only currency in the world were a single Galleon, there would be no need to distinguish yours from mine. They're all the same Galleon, after all.

In any case, Carina turned her attention back to the voice coming through the phone.

"What sort of issue?"

[Ahem. We at Lloyds Bank have observed suspicious activity on the cards of several customers. It appears, ah, that your card may have been cloned.]

"Cloned?"

Carina clicked her tongue.

"This is precisely the problem with Muggles. How does one even manufacture a card that can be cloned?"

[I beg your—pardon?]

"It would be unthinkable at Gringotts. Then again, they verify with a wand there. I suppose goblins managing things is rather more competent than Muggles."

[I—I'm terribly, sorry?]

The man stumbled over his apology. Muggle banks, it seemed, couldn't even manage their staff properly. Carina clicked her tongue again and continued.

"Very well. What am I supposed to do? Should I withdraw everything from the account?"

[Please read us your card number.]

"My card number?"

[Yes. If you would kindly read us the number printed on your card, we'll take care of everything from our end. Heh heh.]

A Muggle with not a single redeeming quality, right down to that witless laugh. Carina muttered irritably and picked up her card.

"You claim to have detected the problem from your own bank, yet you don't even know the card number. Utterly incompetent."

[Er...]

"Never mind. Can you at least write this down?"

[Of course!]

There was a scrambling sound from the other end of the line. Had they been face to face, one look at Carina's withering expression would have rendered him speechless entirely. Making no effort to conceal the contempt in her voice, Carina read out the number on the back of the card.

When she'd finished, she replied in a tone that made clear she found the whole affair tiresome.

"Done. Sort it out quickly."

Click. The line went dead without so much as a goodbye. Carina set the phone down and returned to what she'd been doing.

"Carina, I'm back!"

Just then, Harry came through the hotel room door. Carina, who had been wearing a thoroughly sour expression, brightened at once and greeted him as though nothing had happened.

"Harry. Just now, I—mmph."

The rest of Carina's sentence was swallowed by Harry's mouth. With complete ease, he slipped an arm around her shoulder and walked her backwards—using nothing but lips and tongue—until they were nearly at the bed.

The two of them forgot whatever they'd meant to say and lost themselves in each other. The mood had ripened to perfection, and Harry was just reaching for the hem of her thin shirt when—

Something surfaced in Carina's mind.

"There was a phone call just now."

"What? You only got that number last week. Who from?"

"Some man. He said my card had been cloned by the bank."

"Oh?"

Harry listened as Carina recounted the whole thing, then nodded slowly.

"That's vishing."

"Vishing?"

Harry explained. It was a scheme where someone impersonated a bank or a trusted contact, extracted financial information, and drained the account. People who didn't know better fell for it all the time.

Carina, having heard the explanation, could no longer contain herself.

"That man—he tried to deceive me?"

"Easy, Carina. Let's just freeze the card first, all right? There'll be some loss, but there's a spending limit, so they can't take everything—"

"Out of my way! I'm calling him back!"

Carina shoved Harry aside and marched toward the phone. Harry clicked his tongue in disappointment, cast a longing glance at the bed, then stood and followed after her.

"Carina. That number probably won't go through. So just let it—"

"Hello! You—how dare you deceive me?!"

"Wait, it's ringing? How?"

While Harry stared in disbelief, the man on the other end burst into laughter.

[Well, well. It's the mark from before, isn't it? Ha! I'm making excellent use of that card!]

"You picked the wrong person."

[Oh, I picked exactly right! Got myself a fool who thought something was off and still read out every digit!]

"A fool?"

Carina exhaled softly—a short, quiet breath—and pushed her hair back, her voice dropping to something quietly ominous.

"Listen carefully. I am going to find you. And when I do, I will make you pay for this."

[What, are you Liam Neeson now? Ha. How exactly are you going to find me? You think the police are going to mobilise for some ordinary person's complaint?]

"No. Someone else will find you for me. We happen to have exactly the right person for this sort of thing."

The corner of Carina's mouth curved upward. It was a smile she hadn't worn in a very long time—the one she used to put on when she was about to make someone's life thoroughly unpleasant.

"Voldemort, specifically."

[Vol... demort? Who on earth is that?]

"Found you."

The man must have felt something cold pass through him, because he hung up. But it was already too late. He had spoken that name aloud.

Carina was on her feet in an instant, pulling on her clothes. Harry clicked his tongue wistfully and looked at the bed, then stood and followed her.

"That spell on his name... you never lifted it?"

"It's useful to have around."

She answered offhandedly, tugging on a pair of skinny jeans and a loose hoodie, then picked up her wand. Harry shrugged back into his jacket and went after her.

Vishing could be a professional operation, but just as often it was ordinary people acting on impulse. No particular expertise was required, and without the enthusiastic cooperation of law enforcement, perpetrators were rarely caught.
Investigations could, in theory, track them down. But without a glaring mistake, it was nearly impossible to distinguish professional rings from amateurs—and professional vishing operations were effectively uncatchable—which meant understaffed investigators had little incentive to pursue small-scale cases.

The ones who'd called Carina were exactly that sort. They'd been blasting calls at people who'd recently opened bank accounts, and this time they'd scored a tidy sum.

Their mistake was the target.

"Well then. Anything you'd like to say?"

They had, unfortunately, chosen to swindle a witch.

"Nothing? I suppose that's fair. You'd need a conscience to have words."

A willowy young woman—model-tall in jeans and a hoodie—was strolling around the room, having subdued them all by what could only be described as magic.

First had come the shock of a beautiful woman appearing at their door. Then the horror of learning she was connected to something called Voldemort. Then a brief, ill-advised attempt to underestimate her, which had ended badly. They'd been flung across the room and knocked flat more times than they could count. Now they lay on the floor, too drained to resist.

The girl who had put them all down in moments perched on the edge of a desk and addressed the boy who had followed her in.

"Harry. How much did they take?"

"Let me check."

The boy looked at his phone and let out a low whistle.

"Seven thousand five hundred pounds? They didn't hold back." (Approx. 12,000,000 KRW)

"Seven thousand five hundred pounds... how much is that, roughly?"

"About the price of a used car."

"A car? One of those Muggle transport things? So, roughly the resale price of a secondhand broomstick?"

"I'd say a bit more than that, actually."

People who fell for vishing tended to be one of two kinds: too worn down by life to think clearly, or too comfortable to bother.

No wonder there was so much money on that card. And no spending limit, either...

This girl, it seemed, was a sheltered rich girl with no idea how the world worked.

"That money—Skeeter put it in, didn't she?"

"Yeah. Something about depositing reader donations, she said."

"She's doing well for herself in the Muggle world, then? Though I suppose it suits her. She was always more novelist than journalist. Even her articles read like fiction."

"Come on, give her some credit. She's our patron right now."

Mid-conversation, the girl glanced back toward them. Those who'd been watching the pair from the corner of their eyes quickly looked away.

"Where did you spend the money?"

Should we say?

We haven't spent it yet.

That's true...

After a silent exchange of glances, they decided not to answer. Carina looked down at them and spoke.

"Right. No desire to cooperate. Then how do you intend to repay what you stole?"

What do we do?

Keep your mouth shut! Seven thousand five hundred pounds doesn't grow on trees!

But they came here to get their own money back.

The moment it came to us, it became ours!

That, too, went unsaid.

Strange powers or not, seven thousand five hundred pounds was seven thousand five hundred pounds. Enough to live on for months. And they'd already moved it through enough channels to make it untraceable—there was no way a pair of civilians, police or otherwise, could find it.

Had they thought a moment longer, they might have recognised that two people who weren't police and had still tracked them down were far more dangerous than any officer. But their thinking didn't run that deep.

"Oh? Staying quiet, are we."

The girl tapped her short wooden wand against her palm. The gesture had the air of someone deciding how best to deal with a problem, and it sent a chill down their spines. She studied them for a moment, then turned to the boy called Harry.

"I suppose I'll have to look inside their heads directly."

"Easy, Carina. That's illegal."

"But there's no other way to find out. If I wipe their memories afterwards, it should be fine."

Look inside our heads? How? By cracking open our skulls?

Legal or not—can she actually do that?

Wipe our memories afterwards?

Is this Men in Black? How is she saying that like it's perfectly normal?

Fear spread through the room. Fear comes from the unknown. The way she'd found them, the power she'd used to subdue them, the things she said—none of it made any sense, and the incomprehensibility of it all was what frightened them most.

After a string of unhelpful answers, Carina's patience snapped.

"Fine! Then what am I supposed to do—crack the password on one of their phones?"

"Hold on, let me think. We're still being tracked by Aurors."

"You said you could shake off their tracking easily enough. You just don't want to point your wand at Muggles."

"Ha. Caught me. You're right—I don't want to use this power carelessly on Muggles. So bear with me."

"...Fine."

Aurors? Tracking? Shake off? Wand?

Were these two criminals? They'd traced them from a single phone call—maybe they were known figures in the underworld. The unease in the room kept growing.

While the boy deliberated, the girl swept her gaze across the desk and picked up someone's phone. She appeared to be checking it for evidence. But even small-time crooks had their screens locked. The girl stared at the little padlock icon swaying on the display and made a noise of irritation.

"Honestly. A lock."

Without warning, she pointed her wand directly at the phone. Hey, my contract isn't up yet—the phone's owner had barely finished muttering when the girl tapped the screen with her wand and murmured:

"*Alohomora.*"

Ding—.

A clear, bright sound, and the phone unlocked. Every eye in the room snapped to the girl.

Carina looked at the unlocked screen and gave a short, dismissive laugh.

"Hmph. A Muggle lock. Of course."

It was perfectly logical. Alohomora was a spell that undid anything locked. A phone was, in its own way, a Muggle sort of vault—but a lock was a lock, and Alohomora would undo it. No matter how carefully Muggles buried their secrets inside these devices, there was always a way in.

Carina began navigating through the applications with her fingers.

"W—wait! Who do you think you are!"

One of the men lurched to his feet and lunged at Carina. He appeared to be the phone's owner. Carina smiled faintly and gave the phone a little wave.

"Think of me as a witch who picks locks."

"That's—that's impossible! I had a proper password set!"

The man charged at her to snatch it back. He didn't make it two steps before his own feet betrayed him and he went sprawling. With a tremendous crash, he rolled across a heap of rubbish.

Harry flicked his wand.
"Huh. That works too."

"Of course it does. Why wouldn't magic be able to undo a Muggle lock?"

"When you put it that way, I suppose it does make sense."

The man on the floor thrashed his arms trying to get up, but no matter how he scrabbled, he only slid further across the ground. He cried out in desperation.

"What did you do to me!"

"Hmm? Well, since we're going to wipe your memory shortly anyway, I may as well tell you."

Harry answered with an easy, teasing smile.

"We'll be taking back the seven thousand five hundred pounds you stole. It's ours, so you won't have any objections to us taking it, will you?"

"Ngh! But without my password and fingerprint, my account—"

"*Alohomora.*"

The security measures built into the banking application were neutralised one by one. The fingerprint prompt flashed on its own and displayed a confirmation message; numbers filled themselves into the password field without a hand touching the screen. To the man's dismay, every last detail of his private account lay open before Carina.

Carina began operating the phone as though it were her own. Right before his eyes, a stranger was draining his account. For a man who had spent his career draining other people's accounts, it was a uniquely unbearable experience.

"Nooooo! My money!"

Betrayed by the security he'd trusted, he lost his nerve entirely to outrage and threw himself at Carina.

"*Petrificus Totalus.*"

A futile gesture, naturally. He froze rigid where he stood, every muscle locked. Harry stepped in front of him and said:

"And the rest of the money... we'll leave it open for the police to find when we drop you off at the station. That should do it, shouldn't it?"

The man, who had just watched everything he'd earned evaporate in an instant, screamed with eyes full of impotent fury.

"What in the world is that wand?! Is it some kind of hacking device from America?!"

"It's a top-of-the-range piece from Ollivanders. Something you couldn't buy even if you had the money. Which, of course, you no longer do!"

"No—!"

"And don't do this again. You never know who you might end up calling next time... well."

Harry levelled his wand and gave a small shrug.

"Not that you'll remember any of this. Obliviate."

Flash.

Harry and Carina vanished with the light. The cramped room was left as it had been—two men who ran vishing schemes, right where they'd always been.

Though not entirely as they'd been.

The last hour or so of their memories was gone. And so was every pound they'd made from vishing.

Which of the two losses stung more was not a question worth asking.

"Honestly. We've barely been out a few days and I nearly lost the money."

Harry checked the account. Seven thousand five hundred pounds, deposited from [Anonymous]—in several instalments, all returned.

Having confirmed the balance, Harry said with a teasing lilt:

"I never would have imagined Carina Malfoy falling for something so transparent."

"Hmph. I couldn't help it. I have no knowledge of Muggle customs."

Carina shot back primly and took a long sip of her juice. For all her protests of ignorance, she had blended into this Muggle café with complete ease. If anything, it seemed as though her presence had quietly taken the place over.

Skinny jeans and a hoodie—the outfit she and Harry had bought together at the department store—worn with an expression of mild displeasure as she sucked on her straw.

"Muggles run rather low-quality scams, don't they. What were they thinking, getting caught like that?"

"The idea is not to get caught. There are so many Muggles—if you really try to hide, it's difficult to find you."

"Complacency. Assuming nothing will happen to them. That's exactly what makes it a low-quality scam."

Carina grumbled, her expression still ruffled. She said all the right things, but it was plain enough that being taken in by Muggles had wounded her pride considerably. Harry decided not to press the point.

"Anyway. Did you find out what you wanted to know?"

"Yeah. I wrote to Ron using Muggle post."

Harry produced a letter. The envelope was so thoroughly plastered with stamps that the addresses were barely visible beneath them. Ron clearly had no idea what stamps were actually for.

"He's probably not with Hermione at the moment. If she were there, she'd have fought tooth and nail to prevent this catastrophe."

Harry shrugged and tore open the envelope to reveal Ron's handwriting, which had the unmistakable energy of someone barely containing excitement. Harry traced the lines with his finger as he read.

[Harry. Moody told me everything. You and Malfoy climbed into a trunk together, locked it from the inside, and didn't come out?]

Pfft. Carina spat out a mouthful of juice and stared in horror.

"Weasley! You traitor to your own kind! What on earth has he written?!"

"Carina. You calling someone a traitor to their own kind is a bit rich, don't you think?"

"He has betrayed me, which makes him a traitor to wizardkind!"

Harry snickered and moved his finger along.

[Apparently the Aurors tried to open the trunk and it just rattled from the inside but wouldn't budge. They were all on high alert, convinced you'd had some kind of awakening as that person, apparently.]

Carina's face went the colour of a tomato. Harry stole a glance at her and read on.

[Moody and Lupin probably had a fairly good idea of what was going on in there. They were Hogwarts professors, after all... but you know how it is. They're both decent men, and they couldn't bring themselves to explain it to the Aurors.]

"I cannot believe this. I simply cannot believe this!"

[So when the two of you finally crawled out, you couldn't convince the Aurors of anything and had to make a run for it. Moody and Lupin said to pass on their apologies. They just... ahem. Couldn't make the case without bringing that up.]

Carina had buried her face in the table. Harry folded the letter away, searched for an angle, and lowered his own face to the table beside her. Through the gap between the tabletop and her face, he could see her cheeks burning scarlet.

Harry pressed his cheek to the table alongside hers. Carina slid a sideways glance at him, then immediately drove her face back down.

"The greatest humiliation of my life... I should never have let the mood get the better of me..."

Harry grinned.

"What are you on about. You enjoyed yourself."

"Be quiet. I am not an animal. I am a perfectly respectable witch with dignity and common sense. And someone like that, trunk or no trunk, to have—"

"Oh, come on. So I'm the animal?"

"Your conduct was indistinguishable from one."

Carina pressed her ear to the table and glared at him sideways. They looked at each other for a moment, and then—neither one first—they both broke into quiet laughter.

"Ha."

"Pfft. Idiot."

They turned toward each other at the same moment. Cheeks still resting on the table, they kissed—ears flat against the surface, faces tilted together. A brief kiss. When it ended, they both sat up at once and reached for their drinks as though nothing had happened.

Quite a few Muggles had been watching them. A pretty girl and a striking boy, their small, unguarded moment of affection—it was the sort of thing that was easy to linger on.

Though far more Muggles hadn't noticed at all. Most of them were absorbed in their own lives, moving through the world at their own pace. Savouring a drink, tapping the glass with an impatient expression while a flustered server fumbled about, or grabbing a coffee to go and rushing straight back out the door.

Amid the bustle of all those lives moving at once—

two fugitive witches from the magical world lay hidden among them, quietly, peacefully, enjoying an ordinary afternoon.

Remarkably, Snape had assessed Harry as having reached 'a level where one could barely call it mastery.' Which, run through the Snape translation filter, meant he had become skilled enough that Snape found it irritating to admit.

The following day, Harry knocked on the headmaster's door. He had fulfilled the condition Dumbledore had set, and now he was here to collect on the promised information.

At the news that Harry had mastered Occlumency, Dumbledore rejoiced as though it were his own achievement. And yet his gaze still carried a thread of concern.

"Harry. I still believe it is too soon to share this with you."

"Why? Because Voldemort might rifle through my memories? But isn't that precisely why I learned Occlumency?"

"Oh, Harry. No. What lies within the Department of Mysteries is not so great a secret... Yes, well, a promise is a promise—I shall tell you. Hear it out and judge for yourself."

Dumbledore retrieved one of the silver instruments that crowded his cabinet shelves—a peculiar object with a long neck and a tripod stand. When he lit the base, whatever was contained within the silver flask began to bubble and billow white vapour. Silvery mist, glinting like moonlight, drifted upward and began to float above Dumbledore's head.

With his preparations complete, Dumbledore began to explain.

"The Department of Mysteries is the most secretive and enigmatic division within the Ministry of Magic. The things housed there are of little practical use in daily life, yet they are so wondrous and inexplicable that they never fail to stir human curiosity. Prophecy, for instance. Life and death. Thought and emotion. Time and space..."

The silver vapour shifted and reformed with each word, becoming a glowing orb of prophecy, then a boundary between life and death. It showed planets in alignment, then a room filled with clocks all turning at their own pace.

"...and so forth. They study and observe things of a mysterious and elevated order—things extraordinarily difficult to handle, and which no one ought to tamper with carelessly."

Harry, unable to contain his curiosity, spoke up.

"Life and death? Could they offer Voldemort a clean death, then? Or banish him using magic tied to time and space?"

"This is precisely the sort of curiosity I was referring to, Harry. I held my tongue because I feared you might take a genuine interest in such things."

Harry shrugged, but he hadn't entirely abandoned the possibility. Dumbledore addressed him with patient firmness.

"I can say with certainty that nothing within that department can defeat Voldemort. Those forces are too vast, too elevated to be wielded at will. When it comes to harming a person, a single blade or a wand is far more useful."

"But if they're vast and elevated, wouldn't that make them better? An attack that is death itself, or magic that bends time and space—surely even Voldemort couldn't block that?"

Dumbledore spoke as one might to a young child.

"Harry. If you wished to drink a glass of mead, would you tilt the cup toward your lips—or would you reverse the direction of gravity across the entire world so that the mead fell into your mouth?"

Harry blinked.

"...It's that impractical?"

"More so. Why else would neither I nor Riddle have ever bothered with it?"

"Tch."

Dumbledore offered one final warning.

"For now, you will simply feel the disappointment of a door closed. But if you ever find yourself in circumstances more desperate than these, what I have told you today will circle endlessly in your mind. Should you then succumb to temptation and attempt to make use of what lies within the Department of Mysteries... the consequences could be more terrible than death itself. I do not wish that for you."

"I understand, Professor. I won't so much as dream of using any of it."

Harry gave his word, and only then did Dumbledore appear to relax, if only slightly. Harry muttered under his breath.

"It would have been so much simpler if the Ministry were on our side."

"The world does not always flow in the direction one wishes."

"It doesn't have to flow in the exact opposite direction, either."

Harry grumbled, but Dumbledore only smiled with gentle patience.

In any case, he had heard everything he needed to hear. Harry left the headmaster's office and walked the corridor.

The Department of Mysteries. Voldemort. The battle being waged around them both. There was only one way to bring this conflict to a swift end.

The Ministry—if only the Ministry could be given reason to believe in Voldemort's return, or at the very least to entertain the possibility. Then they could seal off the Department of Mysteries and keep Voldemort in check far more effectively.

He had felt it time and again: a stupid ally who trips you up is more troublesome than any enemy. Harry clicked his tongue and turned toward the Room of Requirement.

Tonight was a meeting of Diggory's Arm rather than Dumbledore's Army. Fred and George, who had enjoyed considerable success with their Howler bombardment, were now searching for new methods after the Ministry introduced a Howler inspection system.

Harry had decided to join in this time. Making the Ministry's life miserable was something he had very much wanted to do himself.

The first to greet him was Neville, sitting in silence and staring at the wall. Harry gave him a cheerful wave.

"Hey, Neville."

"...Hey, Harry."

Neville answered without energy and turned back to staring blankly at the wall. It was entirely unlike him. Harry watched him carefully, then sidled over to Fred and George.

"Fred, George. What's wrong with Neville?"

Fred glanced around to check their surroundings, then spoke quietly.

"He's been like that ever since he heard about the Azkaban breakout."

"Ah."

Neville's parents had been celebrated Aurors. They had fought against Death Eaters during the war, but in the unguarded moment following Voldemort's fall, they were ambushed by remnants of his forces and subjected to brutal torture. So brutal, in fact, that it had driven them entirely out of their minds—to the point where they could no longer recognise the child they had brought into the world.

Neville Longbottom was the boy at the centre of that tragedy, and the remnants who had tortured his parents were among those who had recently escaped.

With the people responsible for his parents' suffering now walking free, it was only natural that Neville was struggling.
Fred clicked his tongue.

"Honestly. If you let prisoners escape, you ought to be receiving Howlers saying sorry. What have they got to be proud of, refusing them?"

"Now we've got leftover stock because of it. A hundred Howlers still unsent..."

Harry, Fred, and George put their heads together, but no clever solution presented itself. All they had was an old enchanted printing press, and if the other side refused to receive anything, there was nothing to be done from this end.

Just then, Luna Lovegood drifted over at her usual unhurried pace.

"Harry. The Ministry has banned The Quibbler."

"What?"

That had happened months ago. The Quibbler, having fallen out of the Ministry's favour after publishing Harry's interview, had been slapped with a sales ban.

It was genuinely unfortunate—but why was she bringing it up now?

Harry chose his words carefully.

"Right, Luna, I'm sorry. You got into trouble trying to help me. I've always felt bad about that."

"Because of the ban, the issues are just sitting in our house as unsold stock."

"Oh..."

Was she asking him to buy up the entire backlog? It wasn't impossible, but Harry had no desire to fill his room with stacks of that maddening magazine. While he was still deliberating, Luna gazed slightly into the middle distance and continued.

"But lately, people have been coming to find my father. They were curious about the issue that got banned. Since it couldn't be sold, he gave copies away for free. And then even more people became interested and came looking."

"Right. So because the Ministry has been behaving erratically, people got curious about the magazine?"

"We know that too. Tell someone not to go somewhere, and they want to go there all the more. So what exactly are you trying to say?"

Fred pressed her, and Luna lowered her gaze to look at Harry. Her expression remained as unreadable as ever, but there was something quietly expectant in it.

"My father is under Ministry surveillance, so he can't publish The Quibbler. But here—here it might be possible."

Her meaning, distilled to its essence, was simple. Let's make The Quibbler at Hogwarts.

Harry's first instinct was scepticism. He had never made a magazine before, and he had no particular desire to make The Quibbler of all things. And the fact that the proposal was coming from Luna made him uneasy in ways he couldn't quite articulate.

But each time he turned the idea over calmly in his mind, Luna's suggestion looked a little better than it had before.

The timing was right. The Azkaban breakout had happened. Citizens were beginning to doubt the Ministry's competence. If a magazine that had been banned by the Ministry were suddenly to resurface—

He had nothing better to do anyway, and at least this was a way to give the Ministry a proper two fingers. Harry nodded and adopted the plan.

"Neville, come over here. Time to get to work."

"Sorry... Luna. I've got things on my mind. Could you leave me alone for a bit?"

"No. If you stare at a wall for too long, you might be possessed by a Smilack Ghost."

"...What kind of ghost?"

"A Smilack Ghost. It's a ghost that's so overcome with shyness that it moves along walls. If you keep staring at it, its shyness fades and it draws closer to you. Then you, in your low spirits, open your heart and accept the Smilack Ghost—and end up possessed."

"I—is that right?"

Whether it was real or not was impossible to verify, but regardless, Neville allowed himself to be dragged over by Luna. Luna wore a rather satisfied smile, and Harry found himself wondering whether she simply invented imaginary creatures on the spot whenever they were useful to her.

Harry presented Luna with a hastily fashioned armband.

"I hereby appoint Luna as interim editor-in-chief of the D.A. Luna will lead the D.A. members in producing the Hogwarts edition of The Quibbler."

Clap, clap, clap. Everyone applauded, and Luna gripped the sides of her skirt and bowed repeatedly in acknowledgement. Wearing a smile as though she had been handed the whole world, she issued her first directive as editor-in-chief.

"First, we need to write articles... A magazine isn't a newspaper. It needs content that will capture people's interest. Like the Crumple-Horned Snorkack, for instance."

Harry pointed out the flaw.

"Luna. 'People' is a plural noun. I hate to say it, but I think the number of people at Hogwarts who are interested in the Crumple-Horned Snorkack is exactly one."

"...You're all not interested in the Crumple-Horned Snorkack?"

Luna looked around at everyone with an expression of disbelief.

Naturally, they all shook their heads, and Luna accepted this with a look of profound disappointment.

"I thought everyone loved the Crumple-Horned Snorkack..."

The desolation on her face was apparently too much to bear, because Neville raised his hand with timid hesitation.

"I—I might be a little interested..."

Luna's head snapped toward him. Perhaps it was the joy of finding a kindred spirit—a smile bloomed across Luna's face. Faint and pale, but unmistakably there, opening like a bud on a face that rarely showed such things.

Watching Neville go red, Harry muttered with an expression that had gone quite cold.

"Tch. My stomach hurts. Some of us are dying of nerves just trying to be in the same room as someone."

In any case, Harry had a means of addressing the situation at hand. They needed articles to fill the magazine, and fortunately, Harry had one journalist he could reach.
"I suppose it's time to contact Rita Skeeter. If I ask her, she can fill in the gaps well enough."

Rita Skeeter had continued her career as a journalist even after being barred from the Daily Prophet. She would have a few articles squirrelled away. Stories she couldn't publish anywhere—if Harry offered to buy them, she'd have no reason to refuse.

Luna had another suggestion.

"And since we're distributing within Hogwarts first, we'll need a bit of gossip as well. A hook article, so to speak."

"Gossip? Does The Quibbler do gossip?"

"Of course. Grabbing people's attention with a provocative headline is essential. You get them to open the magazine that way, and then you show them the serious articles."

"...Right, sorry. Did you actually put serious articles in your magazine? I thought it was all hook articles."

Luna fixed Harry with an unwavering stare and began slowly opening and closing her fist. He wasn't entirely sure what it meant, but it felt very much like some kind of curse known only to Luna.

While Harry dodged the curse, Lee Jordan snapped his fingers as though a brilliant idea had just struck him.

"You know how Cho and Cedric had that row at the last D.A.—Dumbledore's Army meeting? Cho had Cedric completely cornered!"

Gossip was nothing without a romance angle. It was undeniably interesting material, but Fred and George reacted sharply.

"Oi, Lee. Cedric is our main body, whatever else you might say. We're his left arm."

"We don't do things that would embarrass him."

"What kind of operation falls apart by embarrassing its own people? Go stand in the corner and think about what you've done."

Having received a word each from Fred, George, and Harry, Lee Jordan slumped off to stand facing the wall.

This time, Harry offered a suggestion.

"What about Snape's first love?"

Harry recalled a memory that had flickered past during an Occlumency lesson. He said it lightly, without much thought—but Fred and George spun around, eyes gleaming.

"Merlin's beard. Snape had a first love?"

"Harry, if that's true, this isn't just an article. This is the discovery of the century!"

Fred and George's reaction was more enthusiastic than he'd anticipated. Harry hesitated, caught slightly off guard, and debated whether to say more.

What he had glimpsed in that memory: during his school years, Snape had been in love with a red-haired girl from Gryffindor. It seemed he still remembered her, even now.

Publishing that as an article would certainly provoke a fierce reaction from the student body—but.

Harry felt a vague, inexplicable unease. Something told him that if he let that information slip, Snape would come looking for him with murder in mind—and beyond that, Harry himself would spend a very long time regretting it.

After deliberating for quite a while, Harry decided to back out.

"I just thought it might be funny. But it's too far-fetched, isn't it?"

"What, it was fake news?"

"Even if it's fake, it's remarkably compelling. If that article ran, every single person at Hogwarts would be unable to resist buying this magazine."

Harry had to work up a considerable sweat talking them down. Despite his persistent efforts, they were reluctant to let go of the irresistible subject of Snape's romantic life.

Finally, worn down by the argument, Harry shouted.

"Fine. Say we do write about Snape's romance—who's the subject? Umbridge?"

Fred and George clapped their hands together. They looked at Harry as though he were a great inventor and gave him a double thumbs-up.

Audaciously, the Weasley twins intended to spread rumours of a love affair between Umbridge and Snape. Harry shook his head at their sheer nerve—but felt a wave of relief that it was a completely fabricated scandal.

If he had accidentally let slip Snape's real memory, the Weasley twins would have inevitably tracked down the red-haired Gryffindor girl from Snape's era, and Snape would have inevitably tracked down Harry and throttled him.

The content was decided. Harry sent an owl to Rita Skeeter, while Luna and Neville threw themselves into editing. Fred and George channelled every ounce of their mischievous ingenuity into crafting the gossip piece.

Harry sacrificed sleep to pour himself into producing The Quibbler. The pace was so relentless that even Luna eventually collapsed with exhaustion, and Neville dozed off and woke with ink smeared across his face.

The overworked printing press shuddered violently. It screamed as though it might fall apart at any moment, but Harry and his companions refused to allow it. They repaired and reinforced it with magic again and again. At last, trembling and rattling, the press began to spit out long sheets of paper.

And so the magazine was finished. The freshly printed pages smelled pleasantly of ink.

Harry opened the completed issue.

The Quibbler

— All the Way to Azkaban?! Igor Karkaroff: Formidable Wizard, or Proof of Ministry Incompetence?

— A Mysterious Death at St Mungo's Hospital. Secret Experiments on Patients by Healers?! Wizards Attempt the Muggle Practice Known as 'Surgery'!

— On What He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named Has Lost: The Nose and Hair Edition.

— Cornelius Fudge: Goblin Slaughterer or Shrewd Operator?

— Umbridge Reveals Her Ideal Type... A Hooked Nose and Cold, Impassive Eyes? Surely Not?

"Ha. Unhinged."

Harry let out a hollow laugh at the monstrous thing he had helped create.

Whatever the unsettled atmosphere, Harry Potter's declaration sent shockwaves rippling outward. The knowledge that He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named had not truly vanished—that he now resided within Harry's body—planted a new species of dread in people's hearts.

What if Harry Potter could no longer contain him? What if he rose again and drowned the world in darkness?

It was, of course, far better than Voldemort walking free, but it fell well short of the news that he was gone forever. The peace that had arrived after decades of terror now hung by the thread of a single boy. Ordinary people grew anxious over a war that had not yet ended.

And if one supposed Slytherin was pleased by any of this—they were not.

"Inside Harry Potter…?"

"He's in there…?"

"What are we supposed to do? We can't exactly serve Harry Potter!"

Harry Potter had always been Slytherin's natural enemy. The thought of him—already armed with pure malice toward their house and an infuriating gift for mischief—now wielding Voldemort's power as well was simply unbearable. There seemed to be no conceivable way to win. Some students were seriously considering whether it was too late to transfer houses. Hufflepuff excluded, naturally.

The students who had kept one foot in the Death Eater camp quietly withdrew from sight that very day. And then—

"Malfoy. Hey. Can you spare a moment?"

Goyle called out to Carina. Even at the sound of that familiar voice, Carina raised her head slowly. Goyle stood before her in an awkward half-posture, desperately concealing a faint wounded pride behind a clumsy attempt at a smile.

She could guess the purpose of the visit from the tone alone. Goyle was trying to reattach herself to Carina's coattails.

The Malfoy name was a kite with its string cut, true enough—but the family's wealth remained intact, and their influence was far from negligible. More to the point, Carina was practically Harry's official partner. The possibility that Voldemort, sealed within Harry's body, might one day resurface… was not something Goyle or Crabbe could afford to ignore, given how precarious their own positions had become.

Carina's silver-grey eyes cut straight through her.

"You don't need to do this, Goyle."

"Wh—what? What do you mean?"

"I mean you don't have to do something you don't want to do just to protect your family."

Carina brushed off her knees and rose from her seat.

"You don't need to watch my moods anymore. Crabbe, Goyle—the two of you are already lumped in with my father as far as anyone's concerned, so as long as you keep your heads down, the Ministry won't bother you. Father has already arranged it that way."

"Oh. Um. I—"

"You don't have to thank me. I only did what needed doing. And…"

Carina offered a small smile.

"Thank you. You were truly good friends. From now on, do whatever you like. No one will force anything on you."

With that, she walked out of the common room. Goyle stood rooted to the spot, turning Carina's words over in her mind.

Were good friends. No one will force anything.

Carina hadn't exactly been a good friend—honestly, being around her had been exhausting and draining, the kind of company you wanted to escape. But to draw a line like that?

Goyle felt a surge of irritation she couldn't quite justify. After all the hard work had been dumped on Crabbe and herself, Carina was using this moment as an excuse to cut ties? She hadn't been expecting any reward, but had the whole relationship really been so easy to discard?

She turned away with a huff. Behind her, Crabbe let out a quiet snicker.

"Heheh. Malfoy's all grown up."

Goyle rounded on her with an incredulous look.

"What are you laughing about, you idiot?"

"Hm? Why not? Malfoy just apologised to us."

"Apologised?"

Crabbe blinked, genuinely puzzled.

"She said she wouldn't force anything on us, didn't she?"

"How on earth is that an apology?"

"Well… we only got to know Malfoy because our parents introduced us. And we were told to get along with her and not get on her bad side."

Crabbe let her mind drift back to the day she had first met Carina.

In those days when she knew nothing of the world, she had gone to a grand manor house holding her father's hand. Ivy wound in thick coils along the great fence enclosing the wide gardens, and the silver-barred front gate recognised their arrival and swung open of its own accord. Even the rose garden—designed to lead visitors astray—guided Crabbe along the correct path, as though it recognised the invitation she carried.

Her father, who always walked with his shoulders squared at home, seemed to shrink within himself the moment the manor loomed before him, as though the sheer scale of the place had cowed him. He had looked unusually small that day, and Crabbe, born of that small man, had felt herself growing smaller too. Under ordinary circumstances she would have reached out to touch the red roses out of curiosity, but today her arms seemed unwilling to stray from her sides. She had clasped both hands to her chest and followed her father in silence.

And then she had come face to face with her. Carina Malfoy—a doll-like child, smaller in frame than Crabbe, yet one who always seemed to be looking down at her.

Her father had gripped her by the shoulders and given her firm instructions. They were the same age, so she was to get along with that girl. Young Crabbe had asked why.

Her father had not told her the truth. He had concealed the adult reality—that the Malfoy family had extended considerable financial favours to the Crabbes, and that their trust needed to be earned. Perhaps he had been ashamed. Or perhaps he had simply not wanted his daughter to grow up too quickly.

Instead, he had added one thing: act as though you are the older sister, and give way to the younger one.

Perhaps that had always been the difference between Crabbe and Goyle. Crabbe had been exasperated by Carina's arrogance and selfishness more than once, but she had never hated her. The same way you don't abandon a high-maintenance younger sibling just because they drive you mad.

It had begun as her father's command. The bond that formed from that day had carried them all the way to now.

And it had probably just ended a moment ago.

"She's saying she's sorry for everything up to now, and that she wants to start fresh—on our own terms, without being forced into anything. Isn't that… actually a bit better than before?"

"Who apologises like that?"

Goyle, however, did not possess Crabbe's depth of patience. She shook her head as though she had had quite enough.

"I don't know. Either way, the business is settled, so she can sort out the rest herself. Unlike you, I'm sick to death of being pushed around by Malfoy."

Crabbe's eyes went wide.

"What?"

"A comfortable distance suits me just fine. The kind of relationship where you exchange a few words and a greeting now and then. I don't need to spend much time around someone that exhausting."

"You—you're saying you're done being friends? You're really cutting ties?"

Goyle snorted at the question.

"Cutting ties? You don't have to be joined at the hip to be friends."

"I… I suppose not."

"This distance is exactly right for me. We've been too tangled up with Malfoy for too long. Time to start paying attention to other things."

Goyle turned away without a trace of lingering regret. Crabbe felt a flicker of resentment at how swiftly she moved on—but this was surely a conclusion Goyle had reached after careful thought. There must have been things she had given up during all those years spent at Malfoy's side.

Goyle stretched with a satisfied expression and muttered to herself.

"Hmm. Maybe I'll take up broomriding as a hobby…"

But that remark did not pass unchallenged. Crabbe fixed her with a flat look.

"Absolutely not. You're terrible on a broom."

"No I'm not! I just never had time to practise! If I'd been riding regularly—"

"Please. You borrowed Malfoy's Firebolt once and nearly wrecked it. If Malfoy did one thing right, it was keeping you away from broomsticks entirely."

"What?!"

It seemed she had acquired one more high-maintenance person to look after. Crabbe thought this, and smiled quietly to herself.
The Hogwarts headmaster's office stood vacant. Minerva McGonagall, appointed acting headmistress, had been granted the right to use it—but she showed no inclination whatsoever to move her things in.

In her stead, Harry had been given temporary use of the office. Voldemort's soul was sealed within his body, and resolving that problem required somewhere to store the various equipment and texts he needed.

There was another reason as well. Too many people came looking for Harry, and he could hardly receive them all in the Gryffindor common room.

Today, too, the headmaster's office received a visitor from the Ministry.

"Come in, Cedric."

Harry opened the door and welcomed him. He looked Cedric up and down and said:

"Well. You're starting to look like a proper Ministry official."

"A proper official? We're only two years apart."

Cedric, dressed in a black suit and robes, accepted the remark with an expression that fell short of pleased. He removed his hat and cloak and gave his wand a flick; the hat and cloak sailed off to find the coat rack on their own.

Harry guided him to a chair. Once they were both seated, Harry slid a goblet of mead across to him and said:

"How are things with Cho Chang? Are you two all right?"

"Why is Cho coming up now?"

"I was curious. Besides, you came here to talk about something similar, didn't you."

Cedric tried to deflect, but Harry pressed on with characteristic persistence. Cedric exhaled and replied.

"We haven't broken up. Not yet."

"Not yet? That sounds like there's a problem."

"Harry, you know how busy I am. Every day I have close to thirty letters that absolutely must be reviewed, and if you count the minor ones it's over a hundred. Among those, the letters from Cho… even keeping up with them daily is a burden. And the contents—it's nothing but complaints about feeling lonely lately, this and that. I have a mountain of urgent matters to deal with, and finding the time to reply to every one of those is genuinely difficult!"

Once the floodgates opened, Cedric poured out his feelings with startling intensity. Harry rounded his lips into a small O of appreciation.

"Oh. Good thing I asked, or this might have festered. You've been carrying quite a lot."

"And when I do reply, she replies back. When I reply to that, she replies again… the infinite chain of letters is exactly like asking which came first, the phoenix or the fire! The act of replying itself multiplies my workload! So I end up ignoring the letters, they pile up, I can't bring myself to throw them away… I'm at a complete loss!"

"The classic problem with long-distance relationships. Mm."

"Exactly! I told her I was going abroad on Ministry business, and rather than cutting back, I came home to find even more letters waiting! And none of it is particularly important. French wizards favour brightly coloured robes and take great pride in their cuisine… as if I didn't already know that!"

It seemed that trying to change the subject had only made things worse. Sensing that this would go on indefinitely unless he intervened, Harry raised his goblet in a toast to cut Cedric off.

"That's just your conscientiousness working against you. You try to answer every single question the other person asks, and that's what does it."

"You're one to talk. Harry, do you have any right to say that? You confessed to the whole world that he's sealed inside you—because of Malfoy!"

Harry let out a small sound of genuine admiration. The timing of that riposte was an art form. Cedric had clearly picked up a few things about verbal sparring during his time at the Ministry.

Not that he was a match for Harry yet. Harry shrugged.

"My situation is different. For me, Carina comes before work. Even if a hundred letters arrived, hers would be at the top of the pile. I wouldn't glance at anything below it until I'd written her reply."

"…Right. Easy to say when things are good. But spare a thought for ordinary people. Do you have any idea how much the Minister is worrying?"

"But it was necessary."

Harry straightened in his chair and his expression turned serious. Cedric fell quiet and gave him his full attention.

"The remnants of the Death Eaters—they didn't believe Voldemort was dead. They knew he had returned once before, so they believed it could happen again, and they were continuing to operate beneath the surface. Trying to attack Malfoy as a traitor, for instance. Or planning another raid on the Department of Mysteries beneath the Ministry. Simply drawing their attention to me seems like it's already done enough, doesn't it?"

Cedric nodded, his expression grave. In truth, no body had been found, and because of that, few people were willing to believe in Voldemort's death. Only the fact that Harry had fought him had been made known; everything that followed remained shrouded in mystery.

Harry folded down another finger as he continued.

"On top of that—isn't it more coherent for me to have made a drastic choice in order to maintain the seal, rather than simply dropping dead out of nowhere a few months from now? If I suddenly keel over in the middle of all this chaos, wouldn't that make people even more frightened?"

"…I take your point. It all holds together."

Cedric pressed his fingers to his jaw and sank into thought. Harry's reasoning was tight—almost elegant, even.

But it only held if Harry was genuinely prepared to sacrifice himself. If, on the other hand—

Cedric pressed his fingers to his temple and asked carefully.

"Harry. I won't blame you—just be honest with me. Is it possible that you…"

Harry smiled an unreadable smile and gave a small nod. Cedric let out a long, slow breath—half relief, half the quiet despair of someone who has just sensed a far greater complication taking shape.

"Right. If you were truly prepared to sacrifice yourself, you wouldn't be spending your time with Malfoy…"

"I'm sorry."

"What are you sorry for? The problem was never you—it was that you were left to carry something this enormous entirely alone."

Even so, he could not let emotion be his only guide. Cedric was, at the end of the day, a Ministry employee—and a wizard who feared Voldemort's return as much as anyone. That return had to be prevented; if it could not be prevented, it had to be prepared for.

"Is there a way?"

"I've narrowed it down to a few options. For instance—binding myself with a restraining spell before my birthday arrives, or… I don't know, commissioning a Dementor to suck out only his soul?"

"…Neither of those seems particularly viable."

Harry shrugged at Cedric's assessment. Placing magical constraints on his own body was, in the end, no different from harming himself—and Riddle might find some wholly unpredictable method of slipping free of them regardless.

The Dementor remark was, well—Harry Potter's brand of black humour. No one would ever seriously argue that a Dementor could selectively consume only the darkest portion of a soul, and no one would believe it either.

Harry let his shoulders drop and said:

"There is a last resort. I make an Unbreakable Vow never to hold a wand again, and then I break it."

"Would a vow actually hold?"

"An Unbreakable Vow."

"Ah. That might actually work. If he accidentally picked up a wand, he'd immediately— oh. Sorry."

An Unbreakable Vow staked on one's life might indeed be capable of binding Riddle.

Of course, the maxim do not place blind faith in the Unbreakable Vow had been handed down alongside it, like a warning label on a dangerous product. The methods of circumventing such vows, and the tragedies born of poorly worded ones, were too numerous to catalogue.

And wizards, much like Muggles, had never been in the habit of reading warning labels. Whatever loopholes or side effects existed, the Unbreakable Vow remained one of the most powerful binding magics known.

"That should be enough to put the Minister's mind at ease. Thank you for being straight with me."

"Of course. I told you because I trust you to keep it to yourself, Cedric."

"What?"

Cedric looked puzzled. Harry promptly rose and settled into the chair beside him, draping an arm over his shoulder with easy familiarity.

"Cedric. Keep today's conversation between us."

"From whom?"

"Everyone."

"Why?"

Harry said nothing, only smiled. Cedric, unable to read his intent this time, shook his head slowly.
"Why? A plan like that would give the Minister reasonable reassurance. He might assign someone to keep an eye on you, but that's unavoidable."

"That's exactly it."

Harry put his finger on the problem. If Harry broke his wand and swore off magic, most people would be reassured—but a residual unease would linger. What if he found some other means of reclaiming his power? What if Riddle seized control of his body and rose again?

Wizards would undoubtedly use every spell at their disposal to monitor Harry's movements. He would live under constant surveillance and suspicion, stripped of any semblance of a private life.

If he were alone, he could endure it. But Harry had decided he would not remain alone.

He spoke plainly, without drama.

"I'll be no better than a Muggle. I don't mean that as a complaint—I lived that way for over ten years, so I can manage the inconvenience. But Carina is part of my life now, and I won't spend the rest of it under constant watch. So, Cedric—tell the Minister that I'm still holding to my original plan. The one where I… end it."

"You're asking me to lie for you?"

"I'm asking you a favour."

"W—wait a moment."

Cedric felt the weight of it and raised a hand. To lie with everything on the line—and not even for the greater good. This was a request to file a false report that could put the world at risk, and it was entirely for Harry's sake.

Cedric opened and closed his fist and muttered:

"Let's say I agree. What if He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named… suppresses your consciousness and resurfaces?"

"Then."

Harry spoke with quiet, settled certainty.

"Carina will stop him."

"Ugh. Oh. Oh no."

Cedric buried his face in his hands and wrestled with himself. Magic was everything to a wizard—it was the whole of who they were. He had just been told that Harry intended to surrender all of it and disappear with his partner, and stripping him of his privacy on top of that was simply too much.

But could he leave things alone and call it safe? He couldn't be certain. One moment of inattention, and Voldemort—inhabiting Harry's body—might return at the head of a Death Eater army.

While Cedric was still groaning, Harry said, with studied casualness:

"Cedric. You didn't happen to run into Cho Chang when you came to Hogwarts today, did you?"

Cedric went very still. Harry smiled with quiet wickedness.

"I'll tell her."

"…Tell her what?"

"That while you were at the Ministry… you read every single one of my letters and wrote back."

Cedric began to tremble.

Even when Cedric had been ignoring Cho's letters with barely concealed revulsion, he had continued to read Harry's without fail—and whenever something changed, he had replied. Cedric had burned every letter he received, for security's sake, but Harry, sitting safely at Hogwarts, had had no reason to do the same. If the letters and replies had been accumulating somewhere…

"You wouldn't."

"Wouldn't I? Heh. Do I really seem like someone who couldn't manage that much?"

"N—no. Your letters were genuinely important. The movements of the Order of the Phoenix, intelligence on You-Know-Who…"

"Ah—so Cho's letters weren't particularly important? Shall I pass that along as well?"

Cornered with no way out, Cedric squeezed his eyes shut and surrendered.

"Fine! I'll do it!"

"Thank you, Cedric. You're a truly excellent pushover."

"Could you at least choose your words a little more carefully?!"

"You are a true Hufflepuff."

"Please—just do this to me and leave the others alone… oh, I'm done for…"

Cedric snatched up his cloak and hat, muttering something under his breath as he left the room. Harry waved him off.

The door clicked shut. Harry's expression went still. A long breath escaped from a face that had been holding tension in place.

He had persuaded Cedric—but what did any of it mean, in the end? If Riddle pushed Harry's consciousness aside and emerged, every plan he had made would be rendered worthless. An Unbreakable Vow? Breaking his wand? What good were they? Even if he could no longer use magic, if Riddle took possession of Harry's body, Harry would be as good as dead.

Harry leaned back against the sofa and closed his eyes. He focused his mind and let his consciousness sink—down, down, into the deep.

To face Riddle, who lived within his soul.

The magic Dumbledore had cast remained beyond all understanding. But its effect was undeniable: Riddle's consciousness lay submerged in the depths, beneath the lake of the mind, and had not surfaced. Since that day, Harry had not felt his sense of self threatened for a single moment. If anything, he had been able to draw freely on that power while remaining entirely himself.

Harry had assumed the power had become his own. But before long, he had realised the truth—Riddle was actively lending him that power.

At first, he had not understood why. Why would Riddle lend strength to Harry, who was as good as an enemy? Why pass on knowledge that allowed Harry to use his abilities freely?

But now he understood.

Power is, in its very nature, the will to live.

To use his power is to struggle toward life.

And the more Harry struggled, the less he could escape Riddle's shadow.

Even if you call it attachment to love—love is only possible while you are alive. The greater the love, the greater the hunger for life.

His consciousness sank deeper. Harry closed his eyes and concentrated. Occlumency could not keep out Voldemort, who shared his very soul—but this kind of mental discipline allowed him to distinguish his own mind from what was not his.

Snape's final legacy, one might call it. Harry descended beneath the lake. As he moved toward the deeper, darker reaches, a voice as cold and unsettling as the lakebed itself resonated around him.

Dumbledore spoke as though love transcends even death… but I find that rather unconvincing. In the end, one must be alive to practise the love he so fervently extolled.

Riddle. Do you remember the proposal I made last time?

Ah. The one about sharing your body with me? Hmm. I find it rather unappealing.

Really? You seem quite attached to life. If I die like this, won't that be inconvenient for you as well?

Why should it be? The stronger your desire to live, the stronger I become. The more you cling to life, the greater my foothold grows. You are quietly tightening your own noose—why on earth should I make concessions?

…Is it wise to tell me that? If I decide there's no hope and kill myself, what then?

You have no intention of doing so. You are genuinely enjoying that brief life of yours. The time you have left has grown precious to you, and that very preciousness has only deepened your hunger for life…

You never know. I might clear away every last attachment and go through with it.

Ha. How amusing. The very notion that you could clear away your attachments… If you are so bound to life, then submit to me. Swear your loyalty and surrender everything you have. In return, I will grant you one hour each day—time to spend with the one you hold dear…

Negotiations have broken down. Come back with better terms next time.

You would do well to reconsider my offer. When we meet again, I will not be this generous…

The exchange was over. Once again, the negotiation had ended in failure. Harry turned away without a backward glance and rose toward the surface. With a sensation of his body surging upward, his consciousness returned to the waking world.

They shared the same body, which meant neither could deceive the other, and neither could make false promises. They shared sensation and experience alike—each could see the other's hand laid bare.

The thirty-first of July. The cruelest birthday that would ever come. Before that day arrived—what answer, if any, would Harry manage to find?

Harry raised his wand. Every light illuminating the headmaster's office went dark, and a deep, absolute blackness settled over the room.

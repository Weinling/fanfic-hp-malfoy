A few hours earlier.

Marietta Edgecombe of Ravenclaw. Her parents worked at the Ministry of Magic.

Her parents trusted the Ministry the way ordinary witches and wizards did, and Marietta trusted her parents the way ordinary children did. She had joined the D.A. at the urging of her close friend Cho Chang, but she had never truly shared the convictions of the other members.

As far as she was concerned, the D.A. was Harry Potter's private organisation. Its members swallowed everything Harry Potter said without question, offered no real vision, and did nothing but complain. She had attended in silence because the Defence Against the Dark Arts practice was useful enough—but once she learned that D.A. stood for Dumbledore's Army, and that the group stood in opposition to the Ministry's policies, she had been drifting away ever since.

Then Harry Potter and several other members had printed and published The Quibbler under the D.A.'s name. A magazine the Ministry had designated a banned publication.

That was the moment. Marietta made up her mind to inform.

"Professor Umbridge?"

None of this was Marietta's fault. She believed that without a shadow of doubt.

The fact that she had made this choice was the responsibility of people who had gathered others under the pretence of a study group, only to quietly transform it into a political organisation. She was sorry to her friend Cho Chang, and to Cho's boyfriend Cedric—but Marietta was simply making a rational, sensible decision. If Cho was a true friend, she would understand.

Telling herself this, Marietta told Umbridge everything about the D.A.

What kind of group it was. When they met. How large it had grown.

Umbridge was nearly beside herself with delight.

"Thank you, Miss Edgecombe! Your parents must be so proud to have such a devoted and loyal daughter!"

"Thank you, Professor Umbridge. I—but... some of my friends are in there. They only joined because another friend pressured them into it. Could you perhaps go easy on them?"

Umbridge narrowed her eyes and considered the request.

The reason people were kind to informants was simple: informants could never go back. If you gave them nowhere to go, no one would ever think to inform in the first place. There were still plenty of potential informants left at Hogwarts.

Her calculations complete, Umbridge gave a brisk nod.

"Of course. We'll set a trap before their next meeting and wait for them—so tell your friends to skip that day. That way, both you and we can all..."

But Umbridge's plan came to nothing. She stood with her mouth hanging open as she watched Marietta's face change.

Marietta felt something wrong with her body and let out a scream of pure terror. In an instant, ugly hives erupted across her face, spreading until they covered every inch of it. She looked grotesque—as though her skin had turned to a troll's hide.

"H-help me!"

"Wait, Miss Edgecombe! Er—*Finite Incantatem*!"

But the hives did not stop. When all the changes had run their course, the word SNEAK was spelled out across Marietta's face in angry red welts.

Marietta burst into helpless, wordless sobs. In her pocket, a Galleon glowed red-hot and spun in slow circles—as if announcing to the world that there was an informant in the room.

There was clearly no time to set a calm, careful trap. Umbridge left Marietta where she was and immediately sent a message to Minister Fudge.

And Minister Fudge, using his emergency summons authority, called for Dumbledore.

Even a Minister for Magic had limits to how many Aurors he could assemble on short notice. Two Aurors who happened to have no assignment and were working late—that was the sum total of what he could call upon.

However masterful Aurors were in combat magic, two pairs of hands were not enough to apprehend dozens of students. Literally. Aurors were trained to arrest Dark wizards, not to discipline schoolchildren.

Fortunately, Umbridge had prepared for exactly this sort of occasion.

Umbridge summoned the Inquisitorial Squad.

Carina, having received the briefing from Umbridge, immediately called the other Inquisitorial Squad members together. They assembled in the common room with wands in hand and gleaming badges pinned to their chests, ready to move out the moment preparations were complete.

Just then, Draco—who had apparently been on his way somewhere—spotted Carina and asked:

"What's going on? Playing at war?"

He said it with a sneer, but something in Carina's set expression seemed to tell him this was no ordinary matter.

Carina crossed to her younger brother and spoke quietly.

"Stay inside, Draco. Things are about to get loud."

"Why? I'm supposed to meet some friends in a bit."

Carina exhaled.

She wanted to keep Draco away from whatever was coming, but since when had younger brothers ever listened?

"Then whatever you do, don't go near the seventh-floor corridor. Something significant is about to happen there. It could be dangerous—so stay somewhere else if you can. Understood?"

Draco said nothing. Carina studied his expression with a slight frown, but at that moment Montague called out to her from across the room and broke her train of thought.

"Carina, we're all here!"

"Right!... Draco, you heard me? Stay well away from it."

With that warning delivered, Carina turned and walked toward the Inquisitorial Squad. The last member, Pansy, was just then sprinting breathlessly out of the boys' dormitory...

What Carina did not know was that Draco—a first-year Slytherin—was a member of that very group, and that he was rather fond of it.

Draco slipped out of the common room the moment Carina's back was turned and ran straight for the Room of Requirement.

He did not agonise over his position the way others might have. He was simply a child responding to what was directly in front of him. Draco went straight up to the seventh floor, paced back and forth three times, kicked the door open, and shouted:

"My sister's coming!"

Back to the present.

Having heard the rough outline from Ron, Harry pressed a hand to his throbbing head.

"Hold on. So Dumbledore's been summoned and is gone—and right now Umbridge is leading Aurors in here? Looking for evidence that we've been defying the Ministry?"

"Er... yeah? We didn't know all of that."

In Voldemort's memories, Lucius had mentioned that Fudge and Umbridge were forming some kind of plan. Harry had assumed it was still some way off and let his guard down—but they had moved faster than he'd expected.

And of all the timing—right when Voldemort was making his way to the Ministry in person.

Most of the staff had gone home. Those who remained had been drawn to Umbridge and Fudge. At this rate, the Ministry would be virtually empty.

Voldemort would walk into the Department of Mysteries without a single obstacle. He would take the prophecy, gain his certainty, and begin to extend his reach across the wizarding world in earnest.

It had to be stopped.

"Thanks to Draco's warning, most of them got away—but a few of us were caught. They keep asking where you are. It seems like they came here to arrest you."

"How could they—!"

From Professor McGonagall's direction came the sound of teeth grinding. Ron and Harry both looked over.

Harry could say with absolute certainty that he had never seen Professor McGonagall this angry. Her fury was so intense her hair seemed to bristle with it; she drew her wand.

"How dare they—! Bringing Aurors into Hogwarts? Are they suggesting the students are Dark wizards? Even he never dared touch Hogwarts... How dare they... How dare they...!"

Harry and Ron fell silent. They had seen McGonagall angry in Transfiguration class more times than they could count, but looking at her now, they realised that had been a pale imitation. McGonagall in genuine fury was more terrifying than any witch in a fairy tale.

"Weasley, lead the way—now! They will not arrest students within these walls!"

"Oh—yes..."

"Wait."

Harry stood still and murmured to himself. Umbridge. The Aurors. And him, somewhere in the Ministry.

He gathered his thoughts and spoke.

"They came through the Floo Network, didn't they?"

"Probably."

"Good. Then that's what I'll use. I'm going to Umbridge's office."

Ron spun around.

"Are you mad, Harry? Why would you go there?"

"He's heading for the Department of Mysteries. He wants something that's kept there. I can't stop him on my own—but at the very least, I can alert the Ministry's idiots that he's back."

Harry lifted his head sharply. What flickered in his eyes was the same light that gamblers get when they decide to play their final card.

"And if I go to the Ministry, the other Aurors will follow me. However thick they are, they'll find traces of him. This is the moment to show the whole world that he's alive."

"So you're saying you want to go alone—to the place where he is? That's too dangerous!"

"...There's no one else who can go right now. It has to be me."

Harry looked at McGonagall and Ron.

"Please help me, Professor McGonagall."

The plan Harry had devised was simple. McGonagall would hold the Aurors off from above; in that window, Harry would run to Umbridge's office. Once Harry used the Floo Network to reach the Ministry, Fudge and Umbridge would come rushing back to catch him—and in doing so, they would come face to face with the one who had gone to the Department of Mysteries, and everyone would know he had returned.

Simple as it was, the objective and the outcome were clear.

Harry set off with McGonagall and Ron toward the staircase. They were about to charge down when they sensed a presence and stopped.

A middle-aged wizard had barricaded the staircase with an assortment of objects and was standing guard with his wand drawn, watching every direction.

"An Auror. What do we do?"

Harry and Ron were still thinking when McGonagall raised her wand and stepped forward. She spoke in a voice that barely contained the fury about to detonate inside her.

"Leave this to me and go, Potter."
"He's an Auror. Are you sure you'll be—"

Harry looked at Professor McGonagall's face and closed his mouth. Judging by the force radiating off her, it seemed like a lorry-load of Aurors wouldn't be enough to stop her.

"When I clear the way, go. Don't look back."

"Yes!"

The moment she finished speaking, Professor McGonagall leaned out over the staircase and swept her wand.

"*Reducto*!"

The crash was enormous.

McGonagall's magic was a waterfall. The clutter that had been stacked to block the staircase was swept away by her suppression spells like water down a drain. Wind tore through the corridor and splinters of wood flew in every direction. Through the storm, McGonagall descended the stairs with perfect composure. She raised her wand again.

The Auror reflexively cast a Shield Charm, and McGonagall's magic detonated against it. The force was so tremendous that Harry's robes billowed from where he stood at the top of the stairs.

McGonagall shouted:

"Go!"

Harry and Ron sprinted through the gap she had opened. The Auror couldn't even think about stopping them—he had McGonagall's fury to contend with.

"Who are you to stand blocking the staircases of Hogwarts?"

"W-we were sent on the Minister's orders—"

"Aurors? Are you implying the students are Dark wizards? Correcting the misconduct of children is the role of the professors—not Aurors! Leave at once!"

"But I—"

He might have felt hard done by, having only followed orders—but he should have left then.

An enraged McGonagall was stronger than an Auror. She was stronger than any ordinary Auror even without the rage, of course, but that had never been widely known because in the past, words had come before curses. Unfortunately, the Auror knew McGonagall was angry but had no idea how powerful she was. He learned the truth of it when a Stunning Spell connected squarely with his solar plexus.

Thanks to Draco's warning, several students had managed to slip out of the Room of Requirement in time.

But Hermione and a handful of others had stayed behind to dispose of the evidence inside the room and hadn't managed to escape. The D.A. members who were caught were seized by the Inquisitorial Squad and brought out. They were made to kneel on the seventh-floor corridor.

Umbridge herded them together and shrieked in her high, grating voice:

"Where is Potter? Tell me this instant!"

Everyone pressed their lips shut and looked only at one another. Most of them genuinely didn't know where Harry was—but even those who did would not have sold out a friend.

Umbridge screamed in fury:

"The Aurors are here, and we already have all the evidence we need. If you don't talk, the punishment will be far worse!"

A few glanced around nervously, but those who remained to the last were, by nature, the ones with the strongest sense of purpose. Hermione, Neville, Luna, and the other core D.A. members kept their mouths firmly shut.

As the unanswered interrogation dragged on, Pansy Parkinson seemed to lose patience and rounded on Hermione.

"You! Granger! You know, don't you?"

"I don't. Harry wasn't here from the start."

"Liar! Everyone at Hogwarts knows he's the ringleader!"

"You can say that all you like. I genuinely don't know."

Pansy snarled and reached out to grab Hermione by the hair. At that moment, Cedric came sprinting from the far end of the corridor.

"Stop!"

Cedric brandished his wand as he shouted, and Pansy flinched back. He gave Pansy a brief, sharp look, then turned and called out to Umbridge:

"Professor Umbridge! What on earth is going on here?"

"Oh, Mr Diggory! Well timed!"

Umbridge flung her arms wide and cried:

"These students have been holding regular meetings in violation of the Educational Decrees, and they are suspected of publishing subversive material. It has come to light that they have been actively defying the Ministry! Have you anything to say in their defence, Mr Diggory?"

"The charges haven't even been confirmed yet. Matters of school rule violations should be handled by calling in the professors—that's the proper procedure!"

"...What?"

"I will go and fetch Professor Dumbledore and the other teachers. If students have broken the rules, their guilt should be established and their punishment decided with the professors present—not handled unilaterally!"

From Umbridge's perspective, that was a proposal she could never accept. Fudge had lured Dumbledore away; she needed to find the evidence and finish everything before he returned. Hogwarts's professors were Dumbledore's people, loyal to him through years of service. If the professors got involved and things dragged on, it would be the Ministry facing the interrogation.

"...How dare you defy me?"

Umbridge murmured, her voice low and dangerous. Cedric protested:

"It isn't defiance—it's a perfectly reasonable suggestion—"

"Silence!"

Umbridge raised her wand like a woman possessed.

Cedric Diggory was no longer Head Boy—he was nothing. A troublemaker, an ordinary student, defying her.

This was justice, pure and simple. Anyone who defied the Ministry—who defied her—had to be removed.

With that thought, Umbridge swept her wand and cast the spell.

"*Stupefy*!"

A red bolt of light flashed. The Stunning Spell struck Cedric full on and hurled him backwards. He hit the floor back-first and went limp.

She had done it. She had used magic against a student. Umbridge thrust her trembling wand forward and screamed with vicious triumph:

"You're not even Head Boy anymore! The Ministry is justice. You are the ones in the wrong!"

"Cedric!"

"Oh God—she actually attacked him!"

The students scrambled to their feet at the sight of Cedric crumpled on the floor. But Umbridge levelled her wand at them and they sank back down, one by one. Umbridge swept her wand in a wild arc and shrieked:

"Everyone—stay exactly where you are. Unless you want the same! This one dared to defy me, and I'll see him in Azkaban myself—!"

But Umbridge had badly misjudged this group. These students had spent months practising Defence Against the Dark Arts, and they had an enormous amount of admiration for Cedric Diggory—who had stood alone against Umbridge. And more than that, Cedric had not come alone. Behind him were D.A. members who had escaped earlier and come back when they saw him.

The moment Cedric fell, something snapped in the watching students. They drew their wands as one. As if by prior agreement, they demonstrated the results of their recent lessons directly on Umbridge.

"*Expelliarmus*!"

"*Stupefy*!"

Red light ricocheted down the corridor. Umbridge and the Inquisitorial Squad members were hit by the ambush and went down screaming.

"Mutiny! This is mutiny!"

Umbridge shrieked. In that instant, every wand in the corridor swung toward her. Her high, grating voice was an excellent target in a firefight.

"*Stupefy*!"

"*Impedimenta*!"

"*Petrificus Totalus*!"

"*Expelliarmus*!"

The spells came in a torrent. With a tremendous bang, Umbridge was blasted backwards, arms and legs flung wide. She slapped against the wall like a toad and slid down it, unconscious, sprawling across the floor. Everyone thought the same thing: a stunned toad.

The Inquisitorial Squad members fought back, but they too, unfortunately, were victims of Umbridge's theory-only curriculum. Ginny dodged a Leg-Locker Curse and retaliated with a Bat-Bogey Hex that left Montague in misery; Hermione planted a Stunning Spell squarely in Pansy's face. Within minutes, every member of the Inquisitorial Squad lay flat on the cold corridor floor.

The students, still buzzing with the adrenaline of the fight, came back to themselves one by one. They looked at Umbridge and the Slytherin students laid out before them—and felt a cold wave of dread.

"W-what do we do now? We attacked a professor!"

"That's not a professor. That's a toad wearing a human face. What kind of normal professor fires a Stunning Spell at a student?"

"Even so! Did you see the Aurors earlier? If they come back—!"

They had acted in the heat of the moment, but now they had no plan. Anyone who might have led them was either gone or unconscious.

Hermione, who had the most presence of mind among those remaining, was the first to speak.

"This is beyond what we can handle on our own now. A few of us need to get out and tell the other professors what's happened."

"The Aurors Umbridge brought are guarding the staircases. What do we do about them? Should we attack?"

"No. Attacking Aurors feels like a step too far... Let's put on the Slytherin students' robes and badges. The Aurors are new to Hogwarts—they'll have trouble telling friend from foe."

Those who agreed began gathering Slytherin cloaks and robes. Hermione looked around for a set in her size.

Then, in the middle of it all, Hermione noticed something and cried out:

"Malfoy's not here!"

Carina Malfoy—the undisputed leader of the Inquisitorial Squad—had not been there from the very beginning.

Harry and Ron were running toward Umbridge's office. There was a commotion somewhere above them, but it had the useful effect of leaving this corridor completely deserted. They reached the door without encountering a single obstacle.

Harry steadied his breathing as he spoke.
"Umbridge would have used the Floo Network. For the Ministry, connecting the network to Hogwarts would have been simple enough."

"Right. So we just need to get to the Ministry?"

"Ron, I need to ask you something."

Ron shrugged.

"It's going to be a favour I have to agree to anyway. What is it?"

"Use the Floo Network to explain to the Order of the Phoenix—or anyone else you can reach—where I'm going and what I'm doing. Please. You're the only one who knows the full picture."

"Knew it was going to be that kind of favour..."

Ron muttered, then snapped his head up.

"Hold on. So you're going to the Ministry alone? Where he is?"

"Not exactly where he is. I'll be moving around, staying out of sight, and setting off the alarm. I'll be prioritising escape, so it won't be as dangerous as you're imagining."

"But—"

"Staying hidden is easier alone. And there's almost no one else who knows the whole story. Ron, please."

"Ugh, fine!"

They had reached the door to Umbridge's office. Ron grabbed the handle and pulled—but it held fast, as though it had been properly locked.

"Seriously? She locks up even when she's stormed in to raid the place? What's she hiding in there..."

Ron grumbled as he pulled out his wand and began to cast. In that instant, Harry's instincts fired a warning.

Someone who had broken into Hogwarts—locking the door on their way out? Why would they bother?

Harry lunged to pull Ron back—

"*Alohomora*!"

—but he was a fraction too late. The lock clicked, the door swung open, and a wand slid smoothly out through the gap.

A hawthorn wand, elegant and familiar—one Harry had seen many times before.

"*Petrificus Totalus*."

A voice came through the gap—aristocratic, precise, each syllable perfectly placed. A voice he hadn't heard in days, and had spent days wanting to hear. The tip of the wand flashed, and Ron's body went rigid. Harry snapped his own wand up.

The moment after casting, a dueller is at their most exposed. Whoever was behind that door had counted on exactly that—hiding behind a locked door, waiting.

But Ron had taken the hit, and Harry was unscathed. Now it was Harry's turn—except he couldn't bring himself to attack.

Because the one who had stunned Ron was Carina.

Carina looked at Harry, still standing, and clicked her tongue.

"Tch. Weasley... useless to the last."

Through the half-open door, with Ron's fallen body between them, Harry and Carina faced each other in a brief standoff. Harry spoke first.

"You knew I'd come. Here I am. Did you wait long?"

He asked it with an easy, almost teasing smile. Carina raised her wand to eye level.

"You can't go, Potter."

"Why? Because Voldemort's at the Ministry?"

"...Don't say his name so carelessly."

At the name, Carina's face—visible through the gap—tightened. Harry smiled faintly and began to push the door open. The wand tip protruding through the gap, and the silver-grey eyes fixed unwaveringly on him, gradually resolved into the full picture of Carina as Harry pushed the door wider.

The door swung all the way open. Carina stepped back slowly, wand still raised.

Harry found himself thinking, with some surprise, that Umbridge's office had always been rather beautiful. The frilled and lace-trimmed furniture, the fussy wallpaper and ornaments, the slightly childish carpet—all of it suited Carina remarkably well.

A space was ultimately just a backdrop, it seemed. What mattered was what it framed. The same room that had seemed nauseating around Umbridge looked, with Carina at its centre, like a craftsman's work of art.

If only the circumstances were different, he would have taken her by the wrist and pressed her back against the wall right now. A genuine shame.

Harry smiled and said:

"Step aside. I have to go."

"Not a chance. There's nothing you can do. Surrender quietly, Potter."

"Stopping here is precisely what doing nothing looks like. I'll keep moving until I can't."

"...Then let me put it differently. You won't be able to do anything at all!"

Carina cried out and swept her wand. Harry swept his at the same moment.

"*Petrificus Totalus*!"

"*Expelliarmus*!"

Carina's Full Body-Bind and Harry's Disarming Charm collided in mid-air. The two beams struck each other and deflected—one into the wallpaper, one into the ceiling, leaving sizeable marks in both.

Carina fired again without pause, as though she had expected exactly this.

"*Impedimenta*!"

Harry wrenched the door shut. It slammed with a bang. A spell struck the other side of it a moment later.

Harry had used the door as a shield. He threw it open again. Carina had her wand trained on him once more—but this time Harry was faster.

"*Reducio*!"

Harry dragged his wand across the carpet and cast the Shrinking Charm. The carpet contracted from the point of his wand outward, and Carina, standing on it, lost her footing and slid. The spell she fired went wide and scorched the ceiling.

Carina gritted her teeth and pushed herself upright—but Harry was already on top of her. He slid across the floor and drove his wand forward in a short, stabbing grip, pressing the tip against her leg. The hard end of the wand touched her. Harry spoke the incantation.

"*Petrificus Totalus*."

The Full Body-Bind. The spell that stripped the body of all freedom.

From the tip of the wand, an irresistible magical force poured out and drove itself into Carina's body.

It was a cold beyond measure. From her feet upward, sensation began to vanish.

"Ah..."

Even her tongue was paralysed; she could not shape a single word. Carina's body went rigid, and then she began to topple slowly backwards.

At this rate she would hit the floor. Harry shot his hand out behind her and caught her—his palm against her back, his other hand cradling her head. They said a limp person was heavy, but Carina seemed indifferent to that particular rule; she was light.

That the two of them ended up in something resembling an embrace was, in part, deliberate on Harry's part.

He held her tilting body against his chest, allowed himself one brief moment of it, and then carefully lowered her onto the carpet. Her body settled against the soft pile.

He rolled the edge of the carpet into a makeshift pillow, smoothed her platinum hair back into place, and folded her hands neatly at her sides. She looked, for all the world, like a princess who had simply chosen to lie down and sleep.

A sleeping princess deserved a kiss. At least, that was how Harry Potter saw it. He leaned down and pressed his lips briefly to her forehead.

"I'll be back, Carina."

Caught in the Full Body-Bind, Carina could not answer.

She had meant to subdue him. Immobilise him with the Body-Bind, then tuck him away in the wardrobe for a while. It was the only way she could follow Umbridge's instructions—and her father's orders—while resisting them as passively as possible.

But the plan had fallen apart entirely. Harry would go to the Ministry on his own two feet. To that place, with its traps and its ambushes.

Carina pleaded with her eyes. Don't go. They already know you're coming. They're waiting. She tried to say it with her gaze, tried to beg him with it.

But Harry was no Legilimens, and there were far too many things in this world that could not be conveyed without words.

"I hope you'll greet me with a kiss when I get back."

Harry got to his feet, went to the door, and lifted the Body-Bind from Ron. Ron hauled himself upright on tingling limbs.

"Bloody hell—hitting the back of my head on the way down was bad enough, but lying there fully conscious and watching that little display while completely paralysed was somehow worse..."

"Ron, that favour I mentioned—please. Oh, and look after Carina while you're at it."

"You're still going on about Malfoy even now—honestly, forget it."

Harry strode to the fireplace. He grabbed a fistful of Floo Powder and scattered it over the flames. The fireplace blazed green in an instant. Harry stepped into it without hesitation.

Fear crashed over her. The sight of his back as he walked toward the fireplace felt, with terrible certainty, like the last she would ever see of him. Carina reached out with desperate urgency—but the arm caught in the Body-Bind did not move so much as a fraction.

If only she had told him everything from the start. If only she hadn't wavered between her father's demands and Harry's life, but had followed her heart without hesitation. Would things have been different now?

All that remained was regret and helplessness. She had not stopped him, and he would not stop himself. Whatever the end of this might be.

However much Carina might regret this moment.

"The Ministry!"

A short, certain voice—and green flames roared upward. A flash of light, there and gone—and Harry had vanished without a trace.

Harry had been carried away through the Floo Network to the Ministry.

But to Carina's eyes, it had looked like the flames had swallowed him whole.

In Albania, there was a wealthy man's villa.

The man was a businessman who had come over from America—a back-room elder who had handed the day-to-day running of his affairs to professional managers and stepped away from the front lines. He still kept his title on his business card, but for all practical purposes he had retired; he himself considered it a half-retirement, unwilling only to surrender the designation.

Plenty of money, plenty of time. As wealthy men commonly do, he combined those two surplus quantities and made a habit of spending one on the other.

His hobby of collecting old castles was part of that. He would travel across Europe, inspect castles that had come up for auction, appraise their asset value, and then, once he had won the bid, have them fitted out as private retreats.

Buying an ancient castle and turning it into one's own villa. It was a hobby that demanded a great deal of both money and time.

He was a reasonably well-known rich man, though not among the wealthiest in any country, and the castles he owned numbered perhaps five—bargain lots that had come up cheap. The annual upkeep was, of course, a sum ordinary people could not dream of, but for him it was a cost well within his means.

He was an ordinary rich man, living an ordinarily wealthy life. Had he never come to Albania for a holiday, his end, too, would have been ordinary.

But.

"Don't you find it laughable, Karkaroff?"

After wandering deep into the forest on what he called a stroll, and encountering some terrible presence there—his life had been turned entirely upside down.

Vertical pupils gleamed. The man who had once been described as a slightly eccentric old fellow now possessed a voice of boundless cold and cruelty.

"Even this one is little better than a beggar compared to the Muggle millionaires. Muggles revel in every manner of wealth and power, living with brazen ostentation. They make no effort to conceal their lives from anyone. On the contrary, they proclaim it to the world at the top of their voices—I have this much money, I have this much power. How laughable, when all of it is nothing more than a spring dream that could vanish in a single night."

He turned his head sharply. On that wrinkled face, the sinister presence that had once clung to the back of Quirrell's skull flickered briefly into view. Scarlet pupils, a flattened ruin of a nose, and a mouth worn away as though it had been erased—something that had once been human was lodged within his body.

As a child, he had wished to become a wizard, just like every other child. But this, one could be certain, was not the manner he had wished for.

"Blinded by the money and power before their eyes, they cannot see what truly matters. How foolish. Is that not so, Karkaroff?"

Igor Karkaroff, Headmaster of Durmstrang, bowed his head toward his former master.

"Quite right, quite right... Master..."

He panted with fear.

Voldemort's red eyes turned toward his former subordinate—and informant—with something like curiosity.

"Oh, Karkaroff. My foremost 'former' friend."

"M-Master..."

"You were at Hogwarts today. How did you find this place?"

"Malfoy... told me."

"Lucius?"

Voldemort's voice, calling the name of his old servant, was thick with interest.

"Was he more loyal than I thought, or more capable than I thought? I know this much: after I disappeared, Lucius had no interest in me whatsoever. He never so much as thought to search for me. I certainly never left him any clues... How did he know?"

A sound like a serpent dragging itself across stone reached his ears. Karkaroff watched a great snake glide past his feet and swallowed hard.

The snake was behaving as though Karkaroff were not there—but should Voldemort give the order, it would make a meal of him without hesitation.

"Lucius's daughter... She informed him from Hogwarts. I heard what she said and followed Barty Crouch... Sure enough. The great one... he who once punished us from the high bench of the court had become your instrument, Master... And there I found... a former colleague."

"A daughter? Lucius's?"

Voldemort spoke.

"You're saying it was Lucius's daughter who detected the Imperius Curse? Clever—very clever indeed. Then again, Lucius was always clever in his way. Though his inclinations ran in a somewhat different direction... The moment he believed me dead, he renounced me without hesitation and extricated himself from the courtroom..."

Voldemort murmured, as though recalling something fondly, then turned back to Karkaroff.

"Interesting. I had intended to simply kill you, but I find my mind has changed. What can you offer me?"

"Everything! Master, you have proven your immortality by returning. There can be no further doubt about—!"

Karkaroff broke off mid-sentence, seized with terror, and clutched at his own throat. He choked and retched, crawling across the floor.

Voldemort's cold voice rang through the castle.

"Must I prove myself? Must I exert effort to dispel your doubts? Is your belief something I must earn?"

"For-give, gkh—!"

Click. Voldemort snapped his fingers, and the force crushing Karkaroff dissolved.

Karkaroff drew a ragged breath. Voldemort withdrew his gaze and murmured.

"I am power. I am fear. I am immortal, and I am invincible. All must fear me. They must hesitate to let my name pass their lips. I will transcend mere existence and become fear itself—I will return to punish those who have forgotten to be afraid! With the blood of my nemesis!"

A young man with stiff blond hair walked slowly in, carrying a glass vial. He cast a look of contempt at Karkaroff, then raised the vial—filled with a swirling crimson liquid—in both hands and presented it before Voldemort.

"Master. The blood of Harry Potter."

"Well done, Bartemius. My faithful servant."

"I only did what was required."

Bartemius Crouch Jr. answered with composed deference.

Barty Crouch Sr. had a son. While the father had been fighting dark wizards at the height of his career, the son had been seduced by Voldemort's ideology—had killed, had tortured the Aurors, the Longbottoms. Because of it, Barty Crouch Sr. had been forced to sentence his own son to a life term in Azkaban.

That decision had been the right one. The son was the sort of wretch who would cast the Imperius Curse on his own father for Voldemort's sake.

Voldemort extended his hand. The fat, wrinkled hand of the old merchant grasped the crystal vial. He muttered with profound distaste.

"I dislike this method. No mystery, no reverence—action undertaken purely for its end. A vulgarity fit for Muggles... How many precious words did I have to spend merely to obtain this—Harry Potter's blood..."

"Forgive me, Master. My abilities were insufficient..."

"Bartemius. I am not blaming you. How could I blame you? I have not forgotten. You—controlled for ten years beneath your father because of his twisted notion of family love, living in suppression all that time—and yet the loyalty you showed me never wavered..."

As Voldemort murmured, Bartemius bowed his head deeply and replied.

"Master, with respect, that was not family love. He could not bring himself to kill his only blood with his own hands, and so he kept me close and controlled me instead—a horrific captivity maintained through the Imperius Curse. He intended, at some suitable point, to use me as a stud to continue his line. Dreadfully, he planned to... graft me with a Muggle!"

Violent fury blazed in his eyes. He spat out the rage and hatred directed at his father—a man no longer present, and who would never be present again.

"That is not a father. That is not family! My only true home is at your side, Master, and my only family is my comrades! I will give everything I have to you—you who delivered me from those terrible years under my father's dominion!"

"It was not I who delivered you. It was the hatred within you. Had you not seized upon that moment of weakened control to send up the Dark Mark, I would never have known you were there."

Karkaroff reflexively clenched his own wrist.

The Dark Mark is no mere tattoo. It is a brand that carries a specific magical covenant. Every Death Eater is bound to answer when Voldemort issues a summons, and can also send a signal to Voldemort through the Dark Mark.

There was no certainty that Voldemort would respond to any given signal—but at the very least, he had responded to Bartemius's.

"He would not have known. That when the Imperius Curse is used for a prolonged period, a channel forms between the caster and the subject... He would have done well to think a little harder about why I do not simply use the Imperius Curse to rule the wizarding world. Thanks to that channel, you—controlled for ten years—slowly drained your father of his strength and his reason. And even... his position."

"I am only glad to have been able to serve you with my father's legacy."

"Indeed. It was your best effort. I know the lengths you went to. I merely lamented that this stage was so squalid, so Muggle-like. If I had my way, I would have used a Portkey to invite Harry Potter to my palace—I wished for him to witness my resurrection..."

Voldemort tightened his grip on the vial. The borrowed hand of the old man was so feeble it could barely hold even a glass vial without exerting its full strength.

Voldemort gave voice to his contempt for his own condition.

"With Dumbledore watching, I could hardly arrange for Harry Potter to grasp it at a fixed time. I have never felt the lack of power so acutely as this. That I—I—should have had to scrape blood from inside a filthy beast... It is humiliating."

Bartemius had manipulated his father to prepare countless traps. Barty Crouch Sr., as the head of the organizing committee, had held the authority to decide what obstacles would be placed inside the maze.

Plants with steel teeth, blood-sucking leeches, thorn-trap snares. Whatever trap Harry Potter stumbled into, it would draw blood.

All of it to obtain blood—that blood through which the protective magic flowed—and present it to his master.

"But the humiliation ends here. The curse that woman placed upon me, the one that stopped me... If I am reborn through this blood as the medium, I will be free of her curse. Harry Potter will no longer be able to evade my magic. Once more I will reign as the fear of all!"

Voldemort cried out.

A pall of darkness settled over the castle. An ominous surge of magical energy wrapped itself around the old stone walls, as though heralding the Dark Lord's resurrection.

And this was not even his full restoration—yet the power was already this great. Karkaroff trembled, but inwardly felt a flicker of relief.

Even if he had fled, a Voldemort restored to full strength would have killed him.

Perhaps bowing his head now and begging forgiveness was the better choice after all.

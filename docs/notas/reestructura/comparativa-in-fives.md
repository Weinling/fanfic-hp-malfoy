## Parte común

He took one step. She took the other two, because he was being slow about it, and she got him by the front of the jumper and kissed him as if she had been meaning to all day, which she had; and his arms went round her, and for once there was nobody standing far enough away to look superior.

It went on for rather longer than the others had. It would have gone on longer still, but his glasses had got hooked in her hair. She discovered this by trying to turn her head. She had to stop and see to it, with one hand still in the jumper, feeling for the pin; and he stood perfectly still under her fingers, with his glasses hanging off one ear and his eyes on her face, as if the pin were the last thing holding up the castle.

“There,” said Lyra.

“Thanks.”

She had not meant to laugh. It got out very small, and then he had started, without a sound, and she could feel it going through him from the jumper; and it was ridiculous, there was nothing in the world funny about a hairpin, and she could not remember when she had last been so happy. She put his glasses back on him, crooked. She went back to what she had been doing. Nobody had told her to stop.

At some point she found that she had her forehead against his collarbone, and that they were both simply standing there, on the sixth floor, breathing. His heart was going like a rabbit’s. His hands were holding on to the back of her robes, in two fists.

## Versión A: cerca de la muestra (los puños se abren)

There was a seam in her ear. She shifted her shoulder, about half an inch, and the fists opened at once.

“No,” said Lyra, much too quickly.

He had begun to draw back. He stopped.

“It was a *seam*. It was in my ear. I was moving a seam, Potter, not leaving the country.”

“Right. Sorry.”

He had gone red; she could see it arriving at his collar without lifting her head. His hands came back, and lay against her robes quite flat, very carefully, like the hands of someone who has been told not to touch the exhibits. It was extremely well-mannered, and she hated it. She wanted the fists. She had not known that until they were gone, and she stood there on the sixth floor as outraged as if somebody had picked up her doll.

“Could you—” said Potter, and stopped. She waited, with a great deal less composure than she would have preferred. “Stay a bit.”

She took a fresh handful of the jumper, further round, where there was no seam.

“I haven’t gone anywhere,” she said, into it. “You’re holding me like a tea tray. Do it properly.”

He let out a breath that was mostly a laugh, and did it properly.

Somebody in a portrait further down the corridor turned over in his sleep. She had the rest of the sixth floor to walk and a book to write up, and she had plenty of time to think of both of them, and of several other things, and they could all wait.

It was she who let go. It took her some time.

## Versión B: la alternativa (los puños se cierran)

There was a seam in her ear. She shifted her shoulder, about half an inch, and the fists closed.

It was not much. She did not think he knew that he had done it. She had been got hold of by a number of people in fifteen years, by the elbow, in company, and she knew exactly what she thought of it. She waited to think it now. What came instead, from the top of her head to the soles of her feet, was a wild, disgraceful, entirely private satisfaction, as if she had put a very large sum on a horse and watched it come in.

“There’s a seam in my ear,” said Lyra, to the collarbone.

“Sorry.”

He did nothing whatever about it.

“You’re not in the least sorry.”

“No.” It shook a little. “Could you— Stay a bit.”

She had seen him write *Please*, once, smaller than the other words, and pressed harder. It sounded like that. She had rounds. She had the rest of the sixth floor to walk, and a book to write up, and Dolores Umbridge’s opinion of her to keep in repair. She moved the seam herself, with her chin, and took a fresh handful of the jumper, and set her feet.

“I am on my rounds,” she said. “This is the sixth floor. I’m inspecting it. I expect to be here for some time.”

He laughed, or something did; she felt it come up through the wool. The fists stayed where they were. Somebody in a portrait further down the corridor turned over in his sleep. She could feel that he was not going to be the one who let go, and she considered it the handsomest thing that anybody had ever failed to say to her.

It was she who let go. It took her some time.

Carina had taken care with her appearance for the first time in a long while. She had spent nearly an hour bathing, and nearly another hour on her hair. She dried each strand with a breath of conjured wind, then worked in a glossy hair potion and finished with a spritz of perfume. A delicate fragrance settled into the silk-fine spaces between her hair and stayed there, close to her.

She looked in the mirror. Even by her exacting standards, the girl looking back at her was beautiful. A full night's sleep had erased much of the fatigue that had been gnawing at her radiance, and her eyes had stopped their restless wandering, settling into something deep and still. When she let a faint smile surface, her lips curved into it naturally. She looked like a flower that had endured a long, closed season and finally opened.

The appointed time was drawing near. Carina left the Slytherin common room a little early. The students chatting in the lounge lost the thread of their conversation the moment she appeared, heads turning as one. Entranced gazes trailed after her like shadows. Only her lingering scent remained where she had passed.

The one who dared to step into the path of this princess's outing was none other than Carina's friend, Crabbe.

"Carina? Where are you going, all dressed up like that?"

Carina approached her with a soft smile. Crabbe seemed flustered by the sudden closing of distance—unaccustomed to it—but she held her ground and looked straight back without retreating.

What a good friend she was. Better than Carina deserved.

The only pity was realizing it so late. I could have treated her so much better. Carina leaned close and murmured into Crabbe's ear.

"I have something to take care of. I won't be back to the dormitory tonight, so make up some excuse for the others."

"Where are you going? The Room of Requirement?"

Carina gave a small nod. Crabbe nodded back—then seemed to connect the image of Carina so carefully dressed with the words Room of Requirement, and her eyes went wide.

As though she desperately didn't want to ask, but couldn't help herself. Crabbe's voice came out unsteady.

"You're not… going to meet Harry Potter, are you?"

"It's not what you're imagining."

She didn't deny the meeting itself. But she cut off any wilder speculation cleanly.

Crabbe's expression said she was weighing whether to follow. Carina wrapped an arm around her from behind and pulled her into a brief, firm embrace. Crabbe sputtered in surprise.

"Wh—wha—"

"Thank you for trusting me, Crabbe. You really are a good friend."

She gave her a light pat on the shoulder, and for the first time in a long while, Carina made a genuine request—not a command wearing the mask of one.

"—Look after Draco."

She left those words behind and walked toward the exit.

Her nerves had made her finish preparing far too early, and Carina arrived nearly thirty minutes ahead of time. It didn't matter much, as it turned out—Harry Potter was already there, waiting. Carina caught sight of him leaning against the wall and let out a short, dry laugh.

"Did you come to be looked over, Potter?"

He was wearing a blazer over a shirt, his hair swept entirely back. The uncharacteristically neat clothes and the fine leather belt he'd put on told her he had made just as much effort as she had.

Carina herself, for her part, was in her school uniform.

Harry caught sight of what she was wearing and looked away.

"…Any man who gets an invitation from a girl to come alone to a room does this."

"You look like a puppy that got its hopes up and then got abandoned. It's rather endearing."

"Shut up."

His ears had gone scarlet. It was an expression rarely seen on someone who usually carried himself with such easy confidence. Carina laughed aloud at the small satisfaction of it, and Harry grumbled.

"Don't enjoy yourself at my expense. Now that we're here, I'm going to hear what you have to say."

"Fine."

As expected, Harry had come alone. She couldn't fault him for carelessness. Anyone who had just been kissed by a girl and then received an invitation to meet privately would react exactly as Harry had.

He had no one he could readily confide in, and his wariness of Carina had worn thin. On top of that, Harry Potter was confident in himself—alone was enough, he would have thought.

…Whether he'd regret coming alone, there was no way to know until it happened.

Carina walked the corridor before the Room of Requirement three times with Harry at her side. She closed her eyes, then opened them—and there it was, a small, neat little door, waiting for her.

"What room did you picture?"

Harry asked.

"A room where we can be alone."

Carina watched Harry's throat move as he swallowed. She decided to pretend she hadn't noticed. She went in first; Harry followed, took hold of the door handle, and drew it slowly shut behind him.

A soft click as the latch turned, and the corridor outside was left with nothing but a blank, unmarked wall.

It was a room made for exactly two people. A small table for two held a pair of glass tumblers, and the two wooden chairs facing each other across it were likewise a matched set. The cups and saucers in the cabinet, the forks and spoons—all of it came in identical pairs.

The only thing that came in a single was the bed. Harry's grip tightened.

"Sit down."

Carina settled into her chair with unhurried grace and reached for the glasses. Harry moved stiffly, tension in every joint—the complete inversion of every previous encounter, where it had been Harry's easy manner that left Carina off-balance.

Perhaps it was because she was the one controlling the situation. Or perhaps it was the difference in how prepared each of them had come.

Harry, unable to hold out against his own unease, spoke first.

"…Why did you bring me somewhere like this?"

He was doing his best to appear composed, but Carina had not missed the way his eyes had kept drifting toward the bed moments ago. Harry Potter, who had always seemed so effortlessly assured, turned out to be quite hopeless when actually put to the test.

"I told you earlier, didn't I? I needed somewhere we could be alone."

"That's not what I'm asking. I'm asking why you wanted to be alone with me."

"Because if we're alone, no one else can interrupt what I need to say."

Harry narrowed his eyes, as though trying to take the measure of her intentions. Carina thought she heard something click into place—a switch turning over.

In an instant, the flustered, hapless boy was gone, and in his place stood a seasoned wizard who had survived more near-deaths than could be counted. Without a trace of humor, Harry fixed his gaze on her lips with absolute focus—not in anticipation of a kiss, but to catch every word that might come from them.

Harry Potter was, without question, the Boy Who Lived—worthy of standing against that man.

"Have you finally decided to come over to our side?"
"My. What makes you think that?"

"If you want to avoid the public eye, it means you want to do something the public wouldn't expect of you. And publicly, you're one of his most devoted followers."

"Ha. Is that so?"

Carina let the words trail off and glanced briefly at her wrist. Through the gap in her sleeve, she could see the snake's tongue flickering.

In this moment—sharing a room with its master's sworn enemy—the snake had every nerve drawn taut, coiled as though ready to strike at any instant. Poised to sink its fangs in the moment it sensed betrayal.

So as long as the snake's eyes were open, Carina could not say yes. The snake would count it as treason.

"It depends on how you act, Potter."

She deflected, but Harry would not let it go so easily.

"Carina, I'm sorry to say this, but I'm done being accommodating. You used my feelings to lure me here, and the moment we're face to face, you start talking business."

Harry said it with enough edge to mean it.

"I'll say it plainly. If what you say or how you act doesn't satisfy me, my response will change accordingly. Consider it the price for trampling on my feelings."

"What a petty man."

"I was generous, and all you did was run. I suppose I'll have to keep you somewhere you can't escape."

Speaking to each other face to face, she understood it clearly.

Harry Potter was strong.

He knew his own strength, could look his weaknesses in the eye, and adapted fluidly when circumstances shifted. And when retreat was not an option, he fought back with everything he had.

He was nothing like Carina—who had let herself be dragged along without a spine, who had failed to watch her own footing and been pushed back, back, until she found herself at the edge of a cliff.

That was precisely why she was certain. If it was him—he could endure this too.

"Now, before we begin. There are glasses, so there must be something to drink?"

Carina glanced around and found a tightly sealed bottle of Butterbeer in the cabinet. She leveled her wand at the cork—a bright pop, and it shot upward. The sound of butter-foam fizzing and bursting filled the small room, and a warm, nutty scent spread through it in moments.

With a small smile, Carina poured the Butterbeer into the waiting glasses. The golden liquid, thick with foam, swelled against the glass as it filled. A generous head of white, buttery froth rose to the brim.

She slid one foaming glass across to Harry and drew the other toward herself.

"Words don't come easily from a dry throat. Shall we wet it first?"

Carina lifted her glass and spoke in a dry, even tone.

Harry's gaze settled on her for a moment. Something like a storm moved through his eyes.

A private meeting, engineered by her. A drink offered. The situation was so well-worn it was almost tedious.

The freshly opened bottle, the two glasses poured from the same source—neither guaranteed safety. She had arranged all of this; the means available to her were limitless. Anyone who raised that glass to their lips here would have to be a fool.

—And yet.

"Why not."

In front of Carina, Harry was a fool. Pulling the corner of his mouth up in a show of ease, he lifted the glass of Butterbeer. His eyes found hers over the rim.

To meet him, Carina raised her glass in turn.

Clink.

The glasses touched. The Butterbeer foam trembled with a soft slosh. Eyes meeting, each wearing a smile the other couldn't quite read—Harry and Carina brought their glasses slowly to their lips.

It might be poisoned, and still neither stopped. Glass touched lips; white foam left its mark; hands tilted, and Butterbeer flowed into waiting mouths. Harry and Carina. At the same time.

There was no going back from here. Each held the Butterbeer in their mouth for a moment, then lowered their glass.

Well, then. What will your reaction be, now that you've drunk it?

Carina watched Harry with a faint, restless light in her silver-grey eyes.

Harry swallowed his Butterbeer and wiped his mouth. Then—he looked down at his glass with a puzzled expression.

"It's good?"

He picked the glass back up and turned it this way and that, examining it. Then he brought it to his lips again and drank the rest down in long, steady swallows.

He set the empty glass down with a satisfied sound and tilted his head.

"I'll be honest. I expected something to be in this glass."

"Oh?"

"Seems I was wrong. I don't feel anything. Sorry for the suspicion. Though—if you were going to do something to me, you wouldn't use something so obvious."

Harry laughed, a little deflated. As though the taut, high-stakes tension of the moment itself had become embarrassing. As though he were denying that either of them had ever wagered their lives on this—

But Harry was wrong. Carina had put a potion in Harry Potter's glass. She had done it the day before, when she brought the Butterbeer here. She had added it to his glass in advance.

It simply hadn't worked.

What she had put in his drink was neither poison nor Veritaserum. It was a base, cheap liquid born of her own insufficient faith—in some ways more dangerous than poison.

A love potion. Amortentia.

The snake in the tattoo slowly opened its eyes.

This was the limit. Everything Carina had done up to this point fell within what the tattooed snake could permit. Luring him with beauty, arranging a private meeting, slipping a love potion to Harry Potter—his master's sworn enemy—to bend him to her will: all of it could still be excused as mission.

But not what came next.

Carina swallowed the Butterbeer she had been holding in her mouth. Her throat moved—a single swallow—and sent one mouthful of Butterbeer down into her stomach. Then, steadying a voice that wanted to tremble, she said to Harry what she had not been able to say before.

"Thank you…."

The most powerful love potion in existence. Amortentia fails to take effect in only one circumstance.

When the target already loves you.
Harry still loved Carina, and he had lifted his glass in trust of her goodness. She had confirmed it. Now it was time to answer that trust.

Carina smiled—a sad smile, the smile of someone on the eve of a farewell.

"Carina?"

The poisoned glass.

Was hers.

An eruption of agony tore through her from the inside, volcanic. Every passage from her mouth to her stomach felt as though it had caught fire. A thin line of blood ran from her lips. Dozens of magically compounded poisons joined forces and burned through Carina's body.

Harry, realizing what had happened a beat too late, vaulted over the table.

"Carina!"

Her face convulsed. Not from will or effort—it was the involuntary distortion of pain pushed beyond endurance.

He was lunging toward her in horror. Carina thrust out a hand and stopped him. Then, gathering what strength remained, she pressed her wand to her own temple.

"Please… my father, and my mother…."

She left those words behind like a binding spell, and forced herself to concentrate, to reach back through memory.

Snape had told her.

The snake in the tattoo would watch her until the very moment of her death.

To keep her life, she had to take someone else's. Harry Potter or Dumbledore—one of the two had to die, or Carina would. Choose one person from three to die: a perfectly simple problem, he had said.

Just as Professor Snape had said, it was a simple problem, if you thought about it for even a moment. The answer was easy to find. Of those three, there was only one Carina could bring herself to kill.

It was only the choosing that was hard.

The poison was killing her by degrees. Sensation faded from her fingertips outward, and death licked at her, tightening its hold.

The tattooed snake that had been watching her—as the smell of death rose from Carina, it slowly closed its eyes and fell into something like sleep. Whatever she did now, it would never carry word back to Voldemort. The snake had no interest in what a corpse did.

So this was the only way.

Only this moment—with death close enough to touch—was the one chance to betray Voldemort.

Light began to unspool from her temple. Memory-dust, glittering like the Milky Way, wound itself into a skein and trailed along her wand, curling into a small vial. Even as her fingertips shook violently with the force of the pain, Carina poured her memories into the glass bottle with a concentration that bordered on the inhuman.

The Horcrux in the Room of Requirement. Everything she knew about Voldemort. The means she had been trying to fix. And her happy memories, too.

She gathered all of it—every last thing she had left—and sealed it inside the small bottle.

Then Carina's body gave way.

"Carina!!"

Something caught her as she fell. Through the narrowing gap of her eyelids, she could see Harry Potter. His green eyes were wide with horror and fear as they looked down at her.

"Spit it out—quickly! Damn it, this poison—! Why would you—!"

It was a very, very simple reason.

When she had drunk the Felix Felicis, Carina had learned where Voldemort's Horcruxes were. The means to kill Voldemort. The Horcruxes that had resisted every search had come into her hands. From that moment, her course had been all but decided.

Felix Felicis had known that Carina's fortune lay in Voldemort's death.

If she hadn't known, she might have stumbled. But once she knew, there was no other way.

"Felix…. Thank you…."

If she had been left with even a shred of lingering regret, perhaps things might have been different. But Felix had been both very kind and very cruel—it had tidied away every last one of her regrets with perfect thoroughness. Enough that she could have walked away without much trouble.

"I didn't give you that to make you drink poison! I gave it to you so you could—damn it, don't talk! An antidote—!"

Slughorn's compound poison. There was no antidote. Even if the professor came now, it would take three hours to brew one. By then, Carina's body would already be cold.

She hadn't wanted to part like this. Having come this far in her betrayal, she had wanted to stand at his side and walk forward together. She had wanted to trade away her loyalty and take happiness in return.

But if Voldemort learned of her betrayal, her mother in the manor would die a terrible death. And Voldemort, realizing how few Horcruxes remained, would go to ground and become impossible to reach.

This was the only way to hide it from the snake's eyes.

"Hold on just a little longer! I'll go outside and find an antidote—!"

Harry moved to rise. Carina caught his sleeve. With death's shadow already falling across her face, she wrung out the last of her strength and pleaded.

"Look at me…."

The original plan had been to use the love potion to make him fixated on her. She had thought that was the only way he would grant her final request—to help her family.

But it hadn't been necessary. Harry loved her already, without any potion at all.

She had done something unforgivably cruel to him. If he loved her, she would die before his eyes as she was; if he didn't, she would make him love her first, and then die before his eyes. What a selfish, ugly plan it had been.

But there was no other way. If I hadn't done this, he would have known of my betrayal immediately. Mother would have died that day. Father would have been dragged from prison and executed. Draco would have had to spend the rest of his life holding his breath inside Hogwarts.

It's selfish, I know. But I staked my life too. Forgive me this much indulgence.

I'm leaving it to you. I hope you win—

No.

I hope you live.

With that, her consciousness began to recede. Even the superhuman will that had kept her body upright gave way, and she went limp.

So this is what it feels like to fade. Slowly, from the fingertips, Carina's existence dissolved. Even the roaring pain grew distant. A sensation like falling through darkness, like floating, came over her.

And yet—the feeling of Harry's hands holding her remained sharp and clear. Through her dimming vision, Harry seemed to be shouting something. He might have been swinging his wand. His voice, his expression—they were being carved into her.

She didn't want to leave. She wanted to stay just a little longer. Even now, dying, she kept wanting more.

Until the very last moment before her consciousness went dark. Carina reached out toward Harry. But a large pair of hands cupped her face, and pulled her toward him with rough, desperate urgency.

# Crítica externa: capítulo 59, *In Public*

*Lectura a ciegas. Continuidad con grep. Nota: 9,5, la más alta del libro junto al 56.*

## 1. Veredicto

Hace reír de verdad, y no es ingenio repetido: la comedia sale de una idea nueva (el odio ensayado, con guion y nota) y no de otra ronda de réplicas. El «I love you» colado en la bronca es el mejor momento romántico de toda la historia. Hay dos fallos serios: el motivo del teatro contradice lo que Lyra pactó en el 57, y Harry vuelve a acabar sentado encima de ella.

**Lo que preguntáis:**

- **¿Hace reír?** Sí: *Six… He let you down on the exit*; *It was four words long!*; *I don’t want it fresh. I want it unanswerable*; *She’s gone up to tens*; *RUBBISH*; *He underlines with a ruler*; *lino*. Lo más flojo es la cucharada de Cho (segundo chiste de cuchara del libro, sin fuerza) y *Thomas… that sort of finish*.
- **La D en Historia.** Funciona muy bien. Es el mejor chiste de todo el concepto de la segunda vida, y lo es porque ella se ríe hasta que le duele, y eso hacía falta tras el Crucio.
- **El «I love you» como insulto.** Pesa todo lo que debe. *It had simply got in among the others* es lo que el autor pedía: dicho en voz alta, ridículo y humano. *He looked as if somebody had dropped something on a stone floor in a very quiet house* no se toca.
- **Los 133 días.** Exactos: del miércoles 4 de octubre de 1995 al miércoles 14 de febrero de 1996 son 133, justo 19 semanas. Que los haya contado él callado es lo que le da el golpe.
- **El zorro.** Muy bien medido. El miedo dura un párrafo, nadie lo nombra, él solo acerca la mano. El zorro que cae del estante, y el de él que invierte `cap-22:245`, lo resuelven con risa. *On account of the smoke* es perfecto. Y lo que lo hace funcionar sin decirlo: la memoria vieja ya no sirve, y esta sale de un chico con tinta en la oreja.
- **¿Momentos sin remate?** Casi ninguno. Todas las escenas cierran con botón. El único respiro (59:67) lo corta el chiste. Falta un rato de nada (corrección 6).
- **La carta.** Corta sin presagio barato, porque no es un guiño: es trama, y trae una orden. Y el final es un acierto: la tostada untada hasta las esquinas y Goyle empujando la rejilla repiten la mañana de Hopkins (50:245-251) sin decirlo.
- **¿Olvida a Hopkins y a Draco?** Para un capítulo de felicidad, está bien. Draco queda en la carta; las manos y la corona, en 59:67. Hopkins es el único ausente, y 59:189 le da el sitio perfecto con una sola línea.

**¿Roza algo sexual o sensual?** Sí, dos cosas. Escritas con inocencia, pero con dos menores no deben estar:

1. 59:195-227: ella tiene el libro en la rodilla y él *sat down on her book*. O sea, se le sienta en el regazo, y luego escribe apoyado en su rodilla. Es lo mismo que la pierna del 58.
2. 59:65: *an odd thing to be doing in that room* y *They had not touched one another, except by accident* insinúan qué es lo que suele hacerse allí.

Ambas se arreglan sin perder el chiste (correcciones 2 y 3).

## 4. Rastro de IA

Muy poco. Lo único que asoma es **la ironía de distancia**: *She found she was making a sound*, *It seemed to be coming from*, *She seemed to have stopped*, *He seemed to find*. Es una voz de Lyra muy buena, y en 59:205 es exactamente lo que toca, pero ocho son muchos: ese recurso conviene reservarlo para ese momento. Ecos: *Say that again / I shall do nothing of the kind* (49:91-93), *precedent*, *Hold still*, *right out to the corners*. Todos pagados.

## 5. Correcciones listas para aplicar

**1. Por qué se odian en público.** 59:37: “Nobody could be seen to stop hating Harry Potter; not Lucius Malfoy’s daughter, not this year, with her father’s owl on the table every Wednesday; and since it had to be done in any case, it had seemed to her only sensible that it should be done properly.” →

> Her father knew about the window. Nobody else could: not Umbridge, not Weasley, not forty Slytherins with fathers of their own. A boy who confides must believe that nobody knows, her father had written, and his owl was on the table every Wednesday; and since she could not be seen to stop hating Harry Potter, it had seemed to her only sensible that it should be done properly.

**2. Que no se le siente encima.**

- 59:195: “She sat in it with her arms along its arms and her book open on her knee,” → “She sat in it with her arms along its arms and her book open on the left one,”.
- 59:195: “and without any further remark he folded himself up and sat down on her book.” → “and without any further remark he folded himself up and sat down on the arm of the chair, on her book.”
- 59:219: “He settled on her knee.” → “He settled on his own knee.”

**3. La insinuación.** 59:65:

- “It was, she felt, an odd thing to be doing in that room; but it was on the timetable,” → “It was, she felt, an odd thing to be doing on a Friday night; but it was on the timetable,”.
- “They had not touched one another, except by accident, and once with a ruler.” → “Nobody had been hit, except by accident, and once with a ruler.”

**4. La lechuza fuera de día.** 59:235: “It was her father’s, the grey one. It held out its leg as it always did.” →

> It was her father’s, the grey one. It held out its leg as it always did. It was a Thursday. He wrote on Tuesdays, and it came on Wednesdays, and it had come yesterday.

**5. Hopkins, una línea.** 59:189: “Down the Hall, every head at the Hufflepuff table had turned.” →

> Down the Hall, every head at the Hufflepuff table had turned, except for one, long and mild, that went on with its porridge, left-handed.

**6. Un rato sin remate.** Tras 59:117, antes del ⁂:

> They went back to the goblins, since it was on the timetable. She read out the dates, and he got them wrong. The fire burned down a little, and he put a log on it. Nobody said anything for the rest of the hour, and nobody gave it a mark.

**7. Menudencias.**

- 59:37: “She gave him a mark every evening, out of ten, in red.” → “She gave him a mark after every performance, out of ten, in red.”
- 59:107: “Twice! You’ve *failed* it, haven’t you?” → “You’ve *failed* it, haven’t you?”
- 59:113: “He seemed to find that he did not mind.” → “He found he did not mind.”
- 59:117: “She kissed him. It seemed to be the only way of stopping.” → “She kissed him, as the only way of stopping.”
- 59:127: “from his Potions essay” → “from his Transfiguration essay”.
- 59:145: “I was four tables away.” → “I was three tables away.”
- 59:219: “with a hand that did not seem to work very well” → “with a hand that was not working very well”.

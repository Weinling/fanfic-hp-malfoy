It was pure chance.

An open cabinet—beyond where the Butterbeer had been—a small jar sat in plain sight. The kind Harry had seen countless times while rummaging through Potions ingredients. He recognized it immediately, summoned it with a quick charm, pried off the lid, and drew it out.

"…A Bezoar."

A small, shriveled stone drawn from a goat's stomach. An antidote capable of saving a life from the most lethal of poisons.

Harry pried open Carina's mouth, pulled back her tongue, and forced the Bezoar deep into her throat.

He exhaled sharply and stared at Carina's ashen face. She was still breathing—shallow, but continuous.

The instinct that had struck him out of nowhere had worked. He'd thought it through during Potions class: if the poison Carina had brought was a compound mixture like the ones used in lessons, then forcing a Bezoar down her throat might be more effective than trying to brew individual antidotes for each component.

As it turned out, he'd been right. Carina was still in critical condition, but she was alive. Harry let out a long, slow breath.

"Thank god…."

What if he hadn't been paying attention in Potions? What if the idea of using a Bezoar had never occurred to him? What if there hadn't been one in this room at all? He would have had to watch Carina die, helpless. He would have held her in his arms and done nothing but scream until her last breath left her, until the candle of her life burned down to nothing.

The thought alone was unbearable. Harry pressed his forehead against hers. Her skin was cold with sweat, but beneath it he could feel a faint warmth, and her thin, shallow breaths tickled against his face.

Carina was still alive. Harry decided that was enough.

"…Of all the places to try dying—right in front of me? I'll never forgive you, Carina."

He wouldn't. Not ever. But the debt she owed him—that would wait until she was well again, until all of this was over. He would collect it. He absolutely would.

Harry gathered her up in his arms. She was as light as one of the battered old Cleansweeps stacked in the broom shed. He carried her to the bed, laid her down, and pulled the blankets over her twice.

His shirt was soaked through with sweat, clinging to his back. He shrugged off the suffocating dress robes and dropped them on the floor.

Even if the worst had passed, Carina's condition was still critical. She needed complete rest and professional treatment. Harry stepped out of the Room of Requirement, pressing a hand to his chest as the tension slowly began to drain.

Truly—what would have happened if the Bezoar hadn't been in this room? He thought again how fortunate it was, and then—

A sudden wrongness.

"Why was there… a Bezoar?"

Why would a Bezoar be in a room meant for just the two of them? It was a reasonable question, but Harry set it aside for now. Carina's treatment came first.

He folded the question neatly and tucked it away, then reached for the door leading out.

The moment he opened it—

"Is it done?"

A cold, hollow voice. A hook-nosed man in a black cloak stood waiting at the door as though he'd been there all along.

Harry's eyes went wide.

"You—how did you get here?"

Snape ignored him and strode into the room. One sweeping glance, and he seemed to understand everything. His gaze moved between the Bezoar rolling on the floor and Carina on the bed.

Harry followed him in slowly. Snape spoke without looking at him.

"A Bezoar. Yes. It has antidotal properties against several poisons. Fortunately, you found the same answer she did."

"…Professor. Did you know a Bezoar would work as an antidote to Professor Slughorn's poison?"

"I did. The curriculum hasn't changed since I was taught it."

The tone of someone who had anticipated all of this. The composed expression of a man whose own House student lay collapsed before him. And above all—the fact that he had been standing outside the Room of Requirement, waiting for Harry to emerge.

Something inside Harry snapped.

A Bezoar—a Potions ingredient—had no business being in a room conjured for two people alone. Someone had placed it there deliberately.

Why? So that if someone drank poison, they could be saved.

Then who had placed it? And how had they known Carina would drink poison herself? Harry's questions stopped there—but only for a moment.

"…No. I need to ask a different question."

With Snape's arrival, every piece fell into place.

Why had Carina drunk the poison herself?

And why had Snape been waiting outside the Room of Requirement as though he'd known exactly when to appear?

It was a very, very simple problem. So simple it barely warranted deliberation.

"…Was it you, Professor?"

Something darker than death rolled off him in waves. A despair as thick and black as molten tar. He had bitten his lip so hard that blood was running from the corner of his mouth, soaking into his collar—but Harry didn't notice. He gripped his wand.

His voice was cold enough to freeze hard liquor.

"I asked you a question. Professor Snape. Was it you?"

"There's something you should confirm first."

Snape gestured to the glass vial on the table. Carina's memories—the ones she had staked her life on delivering—swirled inside it in a small, luminous spiral.

Harry's killing intent turned toward it in an instant. The force of his will alone was magic. The table cracked with a sharp sound. A fracture began at the edge and snaked inward like a serpent, reaching all the way to just beneath the vial. One more moment and it would devour the glass entirely, shatter it to pieces.
But Harry couldn't bring himself to break it.

The memories Carina had nearly died to pass on. He hated the sight of that vial—it felt as though it had been born from her very life force—but it was still a part of her. He couldn't destroy it.

Snape was watching him with that infuriating blank expression.

"The explanation you want is in there. Go to the Headmaster's office and view the memory."

The rage consuming Harry went quiet.

Not extinguished. Only set aside. Confronting Snape could wait. What mattered now was finding out what Carina's memories contained—what she had been willing to die to tell him. He owed her that.

It was his duty. And his right.

When they reached the Headmaster's office, Dumbledore received them without a word. He asked nothing as Harry walked to the Pensieve and poured the memories from the vial. He simply prepared a place for Carina to rest and provided Snape with the materials and instruments needed to brew the antidote.

Harry stepped into Carina's memories alone.

The memories drifted past like smoke.

Carina in the memory bowed her head before Voldemort. She wore a perfect mask, performing the role of a loyal servant with flawless grace. Not even Voldemort could have seen through something so elegant, so beautiful. Harry knelt before her and looked up at her downturned face.

The scene shifted. The night he had given her the Felix Felicis. Carina leaned against her bed, holding the small bottle in both hands. For nearly an hour she stared at it, then at the dormitory ceiling, her gaze distant and unfocused. Harry sat beside her and wrapped his hands around her small ones. This Carina was only a memory's echo—no warmth, no response—but he held her hands anyway.

The scene shifted again. Carina had drunk the Felix Felicis to steal the poison from Potions class. Then, in a chain of fortunate accidents, she had repaired the Vanishing Cabinet, kissed Harry, arranged to meet him again, and discovered that the object hidden in the Room of Requirement was Ravenclaw's Diadem. Harry walked beside her, step for step, and witnessed all of it.

And at the end of that journey—watching Luna and Neville walk away hand in hand—and gazing up at the diadem set upon the bust of Rowena Ravenclaw—

Carina had smiled, sadly, as though she already knew where she was headed. Unable to simply watch, Harry pressed a brief kiss to her lips, knowing it meant nothing here.

The day before she was to meet him, she had entered the Room of Requirement carrying three bottles. The same room—conjured for two people alone. She placed the Butterbeer in the cabinet in advance, then poured what she had brought into the two glasses on the table.

Into her glass: poison.

Into his: a love potion.

She concealed both with a simple glamour charm, then walked out of the Room of Requirement, trembling.

That night, Carina had tried to write a will. She couldn't finish it. If it became known that she had died by her own hand, Voldemort might grow suspicious and investigate. Even facing death, she couldn't leave behind the words she wanted to say. She crumpled sheet after sheet of parchment.

Harry could only watch as the person he loved wrote her final testament.

These were memories as information. Fragments of Carina's life, edited down to only what was necessary, assembled into a brief sequence of scenes.

And so Harry understood everything, yet felt nothing.

What she had wrestled with. What she had felt. How difficult the choice had been, how much resolve it had taken to act on it.

He had no way of knowing any of that.

He wanted a single word from her lips more than all the memories glittering in that cold glass vial. Her voice—confident, warm, shaped by her own mouth—was worth infinitely more than memories stripped of feeling. The world reflected in her silver-grey eyes was more precious than anything contained in this place.

But she had been driven to the edge, and she had faced death, and she had given him everything. The last Horcrux she hadn't been able to find—Voldemort's hiding place—and the Vanishing Cabinet that would lead him there.

Carina had asked him, as she was dying, to defeat Voldemort. It was another kind of magic. A curse staked on her own life.

Either way, Harry had to kill Voldemort. To honor Carina's request. And to make sure he never lost her again.

But first.

Harry pulled himself out of the Pensieve. He knew every truth now, but his expression was nowhere near relief. His face was utterly still, and his hand gripped his wand hard. He crossed the room toward Snape like a man about to throw himself at someone.

"…You arranged all of this, didn't you?"

Recalling the words he had heard Snape speak in Carina's memories, Harry bore down on him, white-hot with fury.

"To suppress the Dark Mark, one must die or come close to it. One of the three must die. Well. I'm not sure whether that's true—I don't have that hideous tattoo. But there are a few things I do know. That you were the one who said it."

The Dark Mark watches Carina until she is on the verge of death.

One of the three must die.

The right answer must be chosen from among them.

His words had carried something in them—damp and clinging as cobwebs—a deliberate intent. An intent that placed Carina's life on the table as one of the options, and made sure she noticed it.

"You knew what mission Carina had been given."

Kill Harry Potter or Dumbledore. And woven into that, as though it were simply another option, the possibility of Carina's own sacrifice.

"You kept watch over what she was doing and tried to insert yourself into it."

What on earth are you doing—come to me if you need help. He had said it more than once.

"You knew Professor Slughorn's curriculum in advance, and from that you calculated when Carina would steal the poison."

Professor Slughorn had said it himself. Lily Evans had been his student—a Muggle-born, and his most beloved pupil despite it. And Harry, he'd said, seemed to have inherited her gift entirely.

But his mother had met his father at Hogwarts and married him, and Snape had clashed with his father at every turn during their school years. Which meant Snape had attended Hogwarts at the same time as his mother. If Slughorn had taught her directly, then Snape would have taken Slughorn's N.E.W.T.-level Potions course as well.

The same curriculum. Which meant he could have calculated exactly when Carina would get her hands on the poison.

"And you knew she was in the Room of Requirement—even though she never told you."

There had been no Bezoar in Carina's memories. She had only placed the Butterbeer in the cabinet in advance. When she faced Harry, she had drunk the poison without a shred of hope that she would survive.

The resolve she had carried in that moment. The pain of it, the loneliness. Harry could not begin to fathom it.
Carina had been a piece on his board from the very beginning.

"Draw your wand."

Harry had never been this furious. The rage was so complete that blood vessels had burst in his eyes, staining them red. He didn't care. He snarled at Snape like a man ready to attack at any second.

"You leaked the prophecy and got my parents killed. You fed information and nearly got Carina killed too. As long as you're alive, nothing I care about will survive. I'm settling this before I deal with him—draw your wand."

Whether he won or lost didn't matter. That Dumbledore was watching didn't matter either. He had to fight. He had to let this enormous rage out somewhere.

"A duel. Severus Snape. Right now—draw your wand!"

Harry slashed his wand through the air. The arc split the atmosphere with a wrenching crack. Silver instruments tumbled from the shelves, and Fawkes burst upward into the air. Harry leveled his wand at Snape, eyes red.

Watching a student challenge a Defense Against the Dark Arts professor, Snape curled his lip and rose from his seat.

"How passionate of you, Potter. I confess I would dearly love to correct that insolent attitude of yours."

Snape rolled back his sleeve. On the wrist opposite the Dark Mark, a mark remained—something that looked like a red serpent coiled tight around the skin.

"But I am bound by an Unbreakable Vow. I have obligations that take precedence over… educating you."

"What? An Unbreakable Vow? The one to protect Carina? Don't make me laugh!"

Harry's voice cracked like a whip.

"How can you call that protection—! She's lying over there right now!"

"She is alive because I protected her, and I am alive because of it. Simple logic, Potter."

Snape replied as though it were self-evident.

"Last night, when Carina was making her preparations in the Room of Requirement, I slipped inside before the door closed and left the Bezoar. So that if anyone drank poison, their life could at least be saved. I was concerned you might not know how to use it—but fortunately, you did pay attention in my class."

The sight of that insufferable blank face and that faintly contemptuous manner made Harry's stomach turn.

It was true that the Bezoar had saved Carina's life.

But what of the resolve Carina had carried? The suffering she had endured alone, in fear and isolation? Who was supposed to answer for that? The fact that Carina—his Carina—had been made into someone else's instrument sat in Harry's chest like a splinter, and he kept his wand trained on Snape, still burning.

Snape looked at him and spoke, unhurried.

"The Bezoar kept her alive. I protected her. I gave her information so she had grounds to act. I helped her. Now I must fulfill the final vow. I must see through what Carina set out to do."

"…What she set out to do."

"To stand against the Dark Lord, with your help. Potter."

Snape gestured toward Carina on the cot. She had taken the completed antidote, and her face had eased into something calmer, her breathing slow and regular.

He shouldn't let it—Snape had only cleaned up the mess he'd helped create—but the sight of Carina's peaceful face bled some of the hatred out of Harry regardless. With every breath she exhaled, another piece of the fire inside him broke away. His wand arm lowered, slowly.

Snape did not smirk. He simply said, without inflection:

"I have given her the antidote. But when her condition improves, the Dark Mark will wake. I intend to give Carina the Draught of Living Death, Potter. If you've sat through N.E.W.T.-level Potions, you know what that means."

The Draught of Living Death. A potion that sends a person into a sleep indistinguishable from death.

Its effects were thorough enough that no magical examination could tell the difference. For three days, no one would know Carina was alive.

"You have three days to defeat the Dark Lord. If you want her to live."

Not a moment to waste, Snape was saying.

He was right. However much Harry despised him, the enemy that needed to be dealt with was Voldemort. Without killing him, Carina's sacrifice meant nothing. He could not allow that. Not for the sake of the family Carina had nearly died to protect—and not for the sake of the Carina who would wake in a few days' time.

The only gift Harry could offer her when she opened her eyes was Voldemort's corpse.

"Don't mistake… where your wand should be pointing."

The rage that had been pouring out of him changed direction. Harry thought of Voldemort—the one who had given Carina that merciless assignment. The noseless, hairless, snake-fondling freak. The moment that loathsome face surfaced in his mind, a violent hatred surged up from somewhere deep.

Harry pulled his wand back and sheathed it at his hip.

"…I'll have this duel after I've dealt with that bastard whose name I can't even be bothered to say."

"As I had hoped."

"You have a plan, I assume? Let's hear it. I'd like it to be at least as thorough as whatever you did with Carina."

A fuse blew. The anger had exceeded its limit, and his mind simply refused to process it any further. The fury was no longer felt—it was measured, calibrated, and transformed into something else entirely.

That something was called Harry Potter.

A mind assembled for the sole purpose of killing Voldemort, and a rage vast enough to fuel it. By any means necessary. Harry would do whatever it took to kill him.

"Right, then! Let me tell you what I saw in the Pensieve. With that information and the two of you—I think we can put together a plan that'll actually finish the bastard off. Don't you think?"

The blood-red eyes curved into something almost gentle. Harry smiled—a smile with something broken in it.

Ravenclaw's Diadem, which might be the final Horcrux. And the Vanishing Cabinet that led to Borgin and Burkes.

"The Room of Requirement. Everything's there. The Horcrux that refused to show itself—and the door that'll take us wherever we need to go."

Voldemort's spare life, and the blade to drive through it.

The gift Carina had bought with her life was worth so much less than the life she'd paid for it—and Harry laughed, a short, sharp sound, like something giving way.

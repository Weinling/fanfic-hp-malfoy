"Hey! Harry Potter!"

Draco came storming over to the Gryffindor table.

A Slytherin visiting Gryffindor. And not just any Slytherin—a second-year, no less, marching straight up to Harry Potter, who could be called the very embodiment of Gryffindor. Approaching, without a second thought, a wizard who might be harbouring Voldemort within him.

The Gryffindors turned to look at Draco and—

"Good morning."

"How's being Slytherin's Seeker treating you? You're so small out there, we can barely spot you."

"Want some walnut pie, Draco?"

—greeted him warmly.

They were all in Dumbledore's Army together, for one thing. He was young and had an undeniably adorable face, for another. And above all, he was the little brother of Carina Malfoy, who was utterly mad about Harry Potter. He could be a bit arrogant, yes, but at his age, that was easy enough to find endearing.

Draco himself, however, found their goodwill thoroughly irritating.

"Oh, leave it! I hate walnuts! And height has nothing to do with being a Seeker! I was going to make a brilliant debut in the next match and beat Harry Potter! But then—"

Draco grabbed the Firebolt and shouted.

"Why did you run away! Why did you quit the Quidditch team!"

"Hmm."

Harry scratched his chin, trying to work out how to explain it.

The truth was, he simply didn't have time for Quidditch right now.

According to Snape's memories, Snape had known Harry was a Horcrux. And he had done something—something intended to save him.

But the rest of those memories had left no clear clues as to what that something was.

When Harry had returned to Snape's office, all that remained were shelves packed solid with materials on Defence Against the Dark Arts. On a whim, he had begun pulling them open.

And that was when he understood why Snape had always wanted the Defence Against the Dark Arts post.

Magic that couldn't be found even in the Restricted Section of Hogwarts Library was in those pages. Secret, ancient spells that no ordinary wizard would ever have access to. Sacrifice, the soul, life and death. Old arcane arts that belonged less to magic than to something older and darker.

Harry had been consumed, day and night, with learning and testing the spells contained within. Every last one of them was dangerous, and there was simply no room left for anything else.

That was why he'd handed off the Seeker position.

Too tedious to explain all that, Harry settled for a simple summary.

"Well, someone has to leave so the balance between houses stays fair, don't they?"

He said it with a smug little smile.

Harry had no more desire to give a lengthy explanation to small children than anyone else would. He was fairly certain Draco wouldn't grasp the full picture—and, if he was honest, he rather hoped he wouldn't.

Draco, stung by the provocation, puffed up and shouted.

"Fight me! I'll beat you this time, I swear!"

"Ha. Why don't you beat my replacement first? Do that, and I'll consider it."

"Who's your replacement?"

"Someone you know well enough. The brilliant new rising star of the Gryffindor Quidditch team—Ginny Weasley. Ginny, show Draco what you're made of in the next match!"

Harry gestured toward Ginny with a cheerful grin.

Ginny met his gaze—and silently rose to her feet. She fixed both Harry and Draco with a sharp, wordless look that carried something difficult to name, then turned and walked out of the Great Hall.

The coldness of it left both Harry and Draco standing there awkwardly. Harry watched her go without so much as a farewell, and scratched the back of his head.

"Is Ginny going through a phase, Ron?"

"You'd do well to keep your mouth shut."

Ron replied flatly and put a forkful of food in his mouth. Having received a chilly reception from both siblings, Harry turned his attention back to Draco.

"Right then, Draco. If you want to challenge me, win the last match of the season. Let's see—if Slytherin wins by more than two hundred points, you might even take the Cup."

"Don't get smug just because you're a little ahead! I'll demolish your team!"

How many people in the world could march up to another house's table and boldly declare they'd demolish the team? Anyone else would have found themselves surrounded by furious Gryffindors in seconds.

But Draco could get away with it.

The Gryffindors crowded around him, ruffling his hair and laughing, tossing remarks at him one by one.

"You'll need to grow a bit first. Short arms mean you'll never catch the Snitch."

"Oi, you can't be a picky eater. Nuts have essential nutrients, you know."

"Tsk, tsk. Look at that hair. One day it'll poke you in the eye and then you'll learn."

Draco thrashed furiously in the middle of what they apparently considered words of encouragement.

"Ugh, leave me alone! I'm going to grow taller! Taller than Harry Potter!"

"Yes, yes. And to do that, you'll need to eat properly first, won't you? Come on, let's go."

Harry steered him away with patient authority, back toward the Slytherin table.
There were two ways the Slytherin table received Harry. Either they shrank away in fear, quietly vacating the space around him, or they kept a cautious distance while maintaining a carefully neutral friendliness.

Those with ties to the Death Eaters didn't dare meet his eyes. They drifted away like goldfish fleeing a pike. Those with no such connections acknowledged him normally enough.

And then there was one—Carina Malfoy. She stared at him for a moment, blankly, and then—

"…"

—snapped her head away.

It was a distinctly frosty reaction, but Harry didn't make anything of it. If anything, he was having just as much difficulty meeting her eyes.

The memory of that night had lodged itself in both their minds with a vividness that refused to fade. Even Obliviate probably couldn't dislodge it. From the tips of her platinum hair to her small, neat feet—the soft movement of her hands, the faint trace of her scent—everything called that night back. Even the school uniform she always wore was no longer ordinary. When he let himself imagine what lay beneath it, even the most mundane clothing took on a weight it hadn't carried before.

It was fortunate they were in the Great Hall, at least. If they'd run into each other somewhere with no one else around…

No. What was he thinking.

Harry shook his head sharply and gave Draco a nudge forward.

"Carina. Take your brother."

Harry said it stiffly. Carina said nothing—she simply reached out one hand and drew her brother toward her. She settled him beside her, then offered a brief acknowledgement.

"…Right."

"…See you later."

Harry returned the words in kind, equally brief, and walked away with a slightly stilted gait. The unnatural stiffness of his steps made it look very much as though he was fleeing from her.

The oddness of it puzzled those nearby. Hadn't there been a time when the two of them seemed incapable of existing without each other? And now, the moment the Easter holiday ended, they were stiff and awkward as strangers?

Crabbe, who had been watching, nudged Goyle and said:

"Look at those two, Goyle. Doesn't something seem off? Did they have a fight over the holiday?"

"Idiot. No. That's not it."

Goyle studied the space between Harry and Carina. Slightly flushed cheeks. Eyes that couldn't quite meet. And yet every bit of their attention was fixed on the other. It wasn't dislike—it was closer to embarrassment.

Ah. Something clicked. Goyle half-closed his eyes and muttered.

"They did it. Finally. Took them long enough—the way they were carrying on, you'd have thought they'd be married the minute they graduated."

"Hm? What did?"

Goyle ignored Crabbe's innocent question and turned back to his food.

"None of your business."

"But what is it!"

Whatever was going on between those two, paying it any more attention would only be aggravating. Goyle tuned out Crabbe's persistent pestering and looked away.

While Harry was buried in Dark Arts materials and struggling through them, Carina had not been idle.

Lucius Malfoy had passed along information about Grindelwald. When the holiday began and Harry had to return to Privet Drive—in that brief window of time—Carina intended to visit Nurmengard, the prison where Grindelwald was held, and speak with him directly.

But she couldn't simply walk into the presence of the worst dark wizard of the previous generation on the strength of Lucius's word alone, with no preparation whatsoever. Carina set about finding what clues she could while still at Hogwarts.

When it came to the affairs of a previous generation, one ought to ask someone from that generation. Carina sought out the oldest professor she knew—excluding Professor Binns, who was a ghost—and paid him a visit.

Professor Slughorn received the box Carina offered him with a sour expression.

"You lot really do ask nothing but the most inconvenient questions… Do you have any idea how dangerous a question that is?"

Carina smiled pleasantly and replied.

"I'm sorry. But this is for Harry Potter's sake. If Grindelwald—the dark wizard who once matched Dumbledore himself—knows anything, he might have a way."

"Bold… very bold. Most people, even if the thought occurred to them, would never act on it."

Slughorn lifted the lid of the box slightly and brought his nose close, sniffing. Then he closed it again and gave a short, reluctant laugh.

"Crystallised pineapple… Now, who told you I was fond of these?"

"Harry Potter, Professor. Your most beloved student."

"Yes, yes, that sounds right… I've told very few people. Very well. Come in."

Slughorn grumbled and shifted his considerable bulk aside. Carina dipped into a small curtsy and stepped into the room.

He still wore an expression of vague dissatisfaction. He clearly wasn't particularly pleased by her visit. Muttering and complaining under his breath, he set the crystallised pineapple down on the table.

"Stealing potions wasn't enough—you had to go and drink the poison yourself… If it weren't for that business with him, I'd have thrown you out of Potions on the spot… The trouble it caused me… And now you turn up out of nowhere asking about Grindelwald…"

Slughorn said it as though talking to himself, but pitched it perfectly so Carina could hear every word.

Carina couldn't deny it entirely—she had done what she'd done. But if she came across as too deferential, this wily old professor would have her wrapped around his finger. She kept her smile easy and met his words head-on.

"I can only be grateful for Professor Slughorn's assistance. Thanks to the potion you made, I was able to lower his guard and reach the very heart of enemy territory. One could argue, without exaggeration, that you made a decisive contribution to defeating him. And then there was everything you did for Harry as well."

"Ahem. Well. That is true."

The phrase decisive contribution to defeating him did its work. Slughorn cleared his throat, mollified, and shifted the subject.

"Now then—who told you about Grindelwald?"
"My father. He told me that Grindelwald is currently imprisoned in Nurmengard, and that it's possible to arrange a meeting through diplomatic channels."

"Yes. A meeting can be arranged. That much is certainly possible."

Slughorn nodded slowly and fell into thought. He was the sort of man who, at any mention of Voldemort, would grow visibly anxious and do everything in his power to change the subject—yet he seemed to feel no particular unease about Grindelwald. Perhaps the dark wizard of the previous generation simply didn't inspire the same dread.

Curious, Carina asked.

"Aren't you afraid of Grindelwald, Professor?"

"Afraid?"

Slughorn's eyes went wide, and he waved a hand.

"Oh, no. Grindelwald was certainly a formidable wizard. But… he was closer to Albus than to He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named."

Carina wasn't sure she'd heard correctly.

"Albus Dumbledore…? A dark wizard? Closer to Dumbledore?"

"Yes. He's called a dark wizard now, and in the end he fell at Albus's hand… but the two of them were once the closest of friends. Grindelwald had Albus at his side, and Grindelwald was the only person who could stand as Albus's equal. Like Albus, Grindelwald was a wizard who embodied the currents of his age."

Slughorn's gaze drifted somewhere far in the past. Old memories moved behind the old wizard's eyes.

"Those were dark times. Two hundred years had passed since the International Statute of Secrecy was enacted. The Muggles who had once hated magic and hunted witches had turned their weapons on one another. Wars broke out on every side. Muggles who had been friends died overnight. Even young or inexperienced wizards were swept up in the violence and madness. Hogwarts, concealed behind powerful enchantments, was safe—but nowhere else in the world was. And among wizards, a certain feeling began to take hold."

Something came through in his voice that could only come from someone who had lived through it—a raw, distilled emotion born of experience.

"Shouldn't we step in, before the Muggles destroy themselves? That was Grindelwald's position."

Like most wizards, Carina had no deep knowledge of Muggle history. She had only a vague understanding from what she'd absorbed in the modern section of History of Magic.

Muggle wars. Wizards intervening. History that had passed in a few lines of text now unfolded from the mouth of someone who had been there.

"It's said that Albus agreed with him, at first. Then one day they had a complete and final falling out… and Grindelwald chose a more extreme path. He attempted to manipulate one of the warring nations from behind the scenes. From that point on, the war was no longer purely a Muggle affair. Wizards and Muggles alike were caught up in it…"

"Was that war… worse than what we've just been through?"

"It might have been. But just as wizards were raising wands against each other, just as bullets and curses were about to collide—at the very moment that foreseen catastrophe was poised to happen—Albus appeared. I only heard it as rumour, years later, but their duel was said to have been extraordinary. Every sweep of a wand, they said, sent rivers bending and changing course…"

The great wizard. The one who had brought down the curtain on three eras. The one person that even he had feared.

Albus Percival Wulfric Brian Dumbledore.

To Carina, he had been nothing more than an ageing, pedantic headmaster. But in Slughorn's words, he was a wizard powerful enough to reshape the landscape—a hero who had saved the wizarding world not once, but many times over.

Why such a vast difference in perception? Perhaps in the years before Carina was born, extraordinary things had kept happening, one after another. Things she simply didn't know.

She was still turning over that disorienting gap in understanding when Slughorn seemed to collect himself and brought his account to a close.

"…In any case, he stepped forward himself, defeated Grindelwald, and imprisoned him in Nurmengard—the very prison Grindelwald had built. And with that, the war ended."

"Imprisoned? And that was the end of it?"

"His wand was taken, of course. It's a prison built for wizards, so there are certain restrictions in place. But beyond all that—Grindelwald accepted his defeat without resistance and simply stayed there. As far as I know, he has never once attempted to escape."

"Why not?"

"No one knows for certain. But… there is a rumour that's circulated over the years. That there was no point in escaping, because as long as Albus was alive, his goals could never be achieved. So he simply remained."

Which meant—

Now that Dumbledore was dead, was Grindelwald poised to reach for dominion over the world once more? Carina's colour drained, and Slughorn moved to reassure her.

"Don't alarm yourself. Grindelwald is an old man now, whatever else he may be. Even Dumbledore couldn't outrun time—what do you suppose forty-two years in a cell does to a person?"

"But I need to obtain something from Grindelwald. If he demands something in return…"

"The man has no wand. He's an old man. You could subdue him easily enough, as long as you don't hand him one yourself. And besides—Harry will be with you, won't he? I hesitate to say this, but if Harry survives with that power intact… he may well become the most formidable wizard alive. With Harry there, even Grindelwald would…"

"No. I can't tell Harry."

Harry's experiences were shared with the one inside him. If Grindelwald revealed a method and that one came to know of it, it would only give him time to prepare a counter.

Slughorn heard that and his expression grew grave.

"…It's not my place to say, but Riddle was the most knowledgeable student I have ever known when it came to the Dark Arts. He was experimenting with terrible dark magic as early as his sixth year… Whether Grindelwald truly knows more than Riddle does…"

"But he's the only hope I have."

Grindelwald might not know the answer. But if such a method existed anywhere in the world, the person most likely to know it was the dark wizard Grindelwald.

Then what Carina had to do was already decided.

Slughorn seemed moved, in some small way, by her resolve. He rose from his chair, rummaged through his desk, and produced a single sheet of parchment, which he held out to her.

"I wrote Albus's eulogy. There's a longer version—take this, and bring Grindelwald the news of Albus's death. Whatever else they were, they were friends and rivals. The person who brings him that news will be received, in one way or another."

"Thank you."

"Ah, and… something I happened to learn. Harry has Veritaserum. It was Snape's."

The most powerful truth potion known—three drops, and the truth would pour out of anyone. Even from Grindelwald's lips, it might compel an answer… Carina turned her head sharply. Slughorn averted his gaze and said:

"I am not encouraging you to use Veritaserum without authorisation. That would be illegal. I merely informed you that Harry may have something of interest. Understood? Whatever you choose to do is no responsibility of mine!"

With that, Slughorn hustled her out in a flurry of nervous energy. Carina found herself deposited in the corridor, holding nothing but a single sheet of parchment.

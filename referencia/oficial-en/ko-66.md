Educational Decree Number Twenty-Six. No teacher at Hogwarts is permitted to discuss topics outside the designated curriculum during class hours.

Educational Decree Number Twenty-Seven. All students at Hogwarts are prohibited from forming privately organised groups, teams, societies, or clubs. Only the High Inquisitor may authorise the reformation of any team, society, or club, and any student found participating in an unauthorised group may be expelled at the High Inquisitor's discretion.

When the new Educational Decrees were passed, no one was angrier than Cedric.

"This is completely unacceptable!"

He wore an expression no one had ever seen on him before. That handsome, gentle face was filled today with fierce, undisguised hostility.

"Club activities are a student's right. Even the Ministry of Magic has no business taking that away! That's it. As student body president, I'm going to lodge a formal protest with that woman!"

"Cedric...!"

As Cedric made to stride off somewhere, Cho Chang seized his hand.

"You can't defy her!"

"Cho. Don't try to stop me."

"How could I not? She's the Hogwarts High Inquisitor—she's from the Ministry itself. She could make your school life miserable, and your future along with it! As your girlfriend, I can't just stand by and—"

Cho couldn't finish. She buried her face against Cedric's chest. He looked down at her, torn, and moved to wrap his arms around her trembling shoulders.

But instead of reaching for her, he closed his hand into a fist.

He was the very embodiment of Hufflepuff. His good nature and steadfast character pushed him forward. He could not abandon duty. He could not abandon justice.

"...I'm sorry, Cho."

Cedric tightened his fist and looked toward something farther away.

"I'm the student body president. I have to carry the weight of every student's sweat and tears."

A short, sharp tsk cut through the air, though Cedric didn't seem to hear it. Or perhaps he heard it and chose not to.

What was certain, however, was this: Cedric had to move forward.

The following morning, Cedric walked into the Great Hall carrying a square placard. Students who recognised him called out with grins.

"Cedric, what's that sign for? Going to play softball with it?"

"It's a bit wide for softball, isn't it? Maybe he's staging a protest or something."

"Ha! Come off it."

Ernie Macmillan burst out laughing. Cedric only smiled and said nothing.

With a brief morning greeting, Dumbledore flicked his wand, and breakfast filled the tables. Salads and simple sandwiches, sausages and pickles—all the light fare that sat easily on a morning stomach—appeared in abundance.

The hungry students tucked in, while those who rarely bothered with breakfast focused more on conversation than food.

Then it happened. Cedric shouldered his placard and rose to his feet. With every eye in the hall on him, he walked to the very centre of the Great Hall, set the placard down with a resounding thud, and raised his wand.

Every person in the hall—staff included—turned to look at him. Cedric was a diligent, model student; in the best possible sense, he was someone who rarely drew attention to himself. The sight of him standing there with a placard made everyone stare with undisguised curiosity.

"Respected professors, Headmaster, and High Inquisitor. I am Cedric Diggory, President of the Hogwarts Student Body. I stand here as the representative of every student in this school."

Cedric raised his wand and pointed it at the placard. Black ink bled across the surface, and words rose into view: THE EDUCATIONAL DECREES ARE ANTI-EDUCATIONAL!

Dumbledore smiled. Professor Sprout's eyes went wide before she cast an anxious glance toward Umbridge. Snape looked on with characteristic indifference, but most of the staff wore expressions that mingled pride with worry.

Umbridge, for her part, looked as though her eyes might fall out of her head.

"The formation and operation of clubs, societies, and teams is a student's right! No one—not by rule, not by law—has the authority to suppress students' freedom of assembly! Hogwarts is a school, but it is not merely a place for study. It is a place where witches and wizards meet, where we exchange ideas, where we cultivate compassion and solidarity!"

Cedric swept his wand again. New words appeared: EDUCATIONAL DECREE NUMBER TWENTY-SEVEN IS NULL AND VOID! Umbridge lurched to her feet, pointing at Cedric with a finger that shook violently.

"You—you—you—! Someone stop him—!"

"YEAH!"

But Umbridge's words were swallowed by Harry's roar. Harry, absolutely delighted, leapt up onto the table and bellowed:

"Null and void! Null and void! Null and void!"

And that wasn't all. Fred and George seized the moment and set off fireworks in the air. An impromptu display of pyrotechnics lit up the hall at the crack of dawn.

The atmosphere shifted in an instant. The Gryffindors and Hufflepuffs chanted as one: "Null and void!" Their voices filled the Great Hall, echoing off every wall.

Umbridge, white as a sheet, screamed:

"Headmaster! Do something about these children!"

"I'm afraid I can't, Dolores. I don't believe any of the Educational Decrees contain a provision against students making noise during breakfast."

Dumbledore addressed her with a serene smile.

"Nor, for that matter, do Hogwarts' own rules. You'll have to find another avenue."

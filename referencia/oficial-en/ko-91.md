# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 91: Corruption

Words carry weight—that much is certain.

Simply wrapping something in language and releasing it into the open air had made Harry's heart feel light enough to float. Secrets kept are suffocating; secrets spoken bring relief. There was a reason people had always been warned to guard their tongues. With one burden lifted, even his attachment to life seemed to have diminished, if only slightly. The only unease that remained was the sense that whatever weight he'd shed had been transferred directly onto her.

Harry reached up and touched Carina's face. Her expression was rigid, but her skin was soft—warm in a way that didn't match the frozen atmosphere around her. She always showed two things at once, this girl. Like the only living thing moving through a world of still life. Something stirred in him without warning, and before he knew it, his hands had begun to draw her close.

But what stopped him was nothing more than a single year.

If he reached out carelessly, pulled those slender shoulders toward him, pressed his lips to hers—

If he squandered her brilliant years on a moment's selfishness—

If he filled a burial urn with memories that would soon be buried underground—

What a cruel thing that would be to do to Carina.

Harry took hold of her shoulders and gently pushed her back. Her face receded by that same distance. Into the space left between them, he placed cold words.

"This is a secret known only to Ron, Hermione, and a few others who were there. Now you're included. Professor Moody and Professor Lupin said they'd look for a way. But if there truly isn't one, then there's no choice but to… before my birthday comes…."

"Before?"

Moody and Lupin had said they would find a way—but everyone knew it was a promise with no foundation beneath it. Even Dumbledore, who had known every truth, had done nothing more than spend his own life pushing Riddle's soul beneath the surface. Could Moody and Lupin really succeed where he had not?

The ending was already written. They were only delaying it for as long as delay was possible. Harry answered with a heaviness he couldn't conceal.

"They're going to kill me."

"Nonsense."

Carina dismissed it without hesitation. Harry had been about to reassure her, but something in her posture and bearing made him pause.

She wasn't trying to deny reality. She was denying his conclusion. Not fleeing from a truth she couldn't bear to face—but genuinely, methodically, condemning the flaw in his reasoning.

"That's not worth a second thought. Die? Be killed? By whom, and on what authority?"

"Carina, you saw my memory. If I don't die—"

"Riddle's soul will emerge. So what?"

She held his gaze and shot back without blinking.

"You think stepping forward will solve everything on its own. You speak as though offering your life will restore peace to the world. You really are a Gryffindor. Arrogant, self-willed, and hopelessly optimistic."

"But if I don't, Riddle's soul will—"

"Shut up, Harry. I'm going to teach you how a Slytherin thinks. Listen carefully."

Could Carina actually find a way? Harry knew she was brilliant, that she carried knowledge of obscure and secret magic—but he didn't believe she could solve this. Still, watching her work her mind furiously on his behalf would be its own kind of comfort. He waited for her to speak.

At last, she did.

"Kill Riddle before your birthday arrives. Separate that soul from yourself and reclaim your own, whole and intact. If you can't manage that, then kill him the moment he takes over your body. That way only Riddle's soul dies, and yours may reclaim what's yours."
It was blunter and more violent than he'd expected. Harry couldn't help it—he laughed. Carina's brow drew together.

"I said you were allowed to smile in front of me. I never said you could mock me."

"Ha—sorry."

But her concern for him had come through clearly enough, and Harry smiled with something close to contentment as he tried to reason with her.

"Riddle is powerful. You saw what he can do. The strength I drew on in the Slytherin common room—that wasn't mine. All of it was Riddle's. Professor Dumbledore, Voldemort at his peak… it would take something on that level to stop him."

"And that's impossible?"

Obviously. Harry nodded. Riddle's power fell slightly short of Voldemort's, but only slightly—it was comparable. Even in an exhausted state, he had subdued Moody, Lupin, Molly, and Narcissa in a single moment and stripped them of their wands.

And now Dumbledore was gone. The moment Harry came of age and Riddle claimed his body—no one would be able to stop him.

Which was why Harry had to end his own life before that day came—

"Then negotiate with him. He won't want you to kill yourself either. Use your life as leverage—give what must be given, take what can be taken. Let him have your body only while you sleep. Or give him the hours from noon to midnight, and keep the rest for yourself."

The smile faded from Harry's lips.

He had started out speaking to her gently, as though correcting a child—thinking she was being stubborn, throwing a tantrum in the face of a brutal and necessary choice. But Harry understood now, belatedly, how shallow and foolish that thought had been.

Carina was not bound by sides. She didn't care about justice, or peace, or the fate of the world. What she cared about was—

"If none of that works, then surrender. Get on your knees. Beg for mercy. He gathered his Death Eaters not because he enjoyed standing above others, but because he needed followers who would fear him more than anyone else. If you fear him and submit to him, he'll be pleased—he'll keep you alive. And in that state, holding even a fragment of your body… keeping beside him the one audience who fears him most…."

Harry's life. Only that.

She was not the Chosen One, not a warrior destined to stand against the dark. She was an extreme egoist—satisfied as long as she could protect her family and the small circle around her. A traitor who had betrayed the Dark Lord himself to protect Harry, and those she loved.

A Slytherin who could be as cowardly as the situation required, in service of what she wanted. Carina Malfoy whispered:

"Stay by my side. Even like that."

It was a serpent's temptation. A beautiful serpent named Carina Malfoy was offering him the forbidden fruit. Swallow the sin and chase happiness—the devil's whisper tickled at his ear.

Harry opened his mouth. His voice, he realized, had begun to tremble.

"That would be a betrayal."

"Betrayal is only a word that applies when there was trust to begin with. In my case, Volde—"

She seemed to stumble on the name, not yet accustomed to saying it aloud. But it didn't take her long to push past the hesitation.

"—mort. He never trusted me at all. He took my family hostage, branded a means of surveillance onto my wrist, and threw me into enemy territory. He never trusted me—so my betrayal was, in a way, the only honest response to his faith."

"My situation is different. They trusted me. And Professor Dumbledore—"

"Not at all."
Carina answered without pause.

A smooth tongue shapes desire into the mask of logic. The forbidden fruit wrapped in those small lips was too sweet—sweet enough that he wanted to bite into it right now, to pry it open and take the red flesh inside.

"Dumbledore said he didn't tell you the truth because he was afraid you'd betray them. He feared that if you knew your death was the only way to defeat him, you—who feared death—would abandon them and side with the Dark Lord. So he betrayed your effort and your devotion, and left you to die somewhere without ever knowing why.

Isn't that unjust? Is that the nature of justice? No. No, it isn't. Justice must be fair—and if you were suspected, then that suspicion grants you the same right in return.

Harry, the proper answer to distrust is betrayal. After everything you've given, is this how you deserve to be treated? Why must you be forced to sacrifice yourself? Why must you die before you've even come of age, without ever doing the things you wanted to do? Is a faith that demands your death truly a faith that exists for your sake?"

Closing his eyes was useless. The serpent's sweet tongue didn't discriminate between senses—it came for all of them. A feverish voice murmured at his ear. Somehow, without his noticing, her weight had settled against his chest.

The saints who had wrestled with demons were truly extraordinary beings. If their tempter had been even half of what Carina was, someone like Harry would have become a servant of darkness long ago.

Harry forced his attention elsewhere and spoke.

"He's powerful. You saw it yourself. If he's left unchecked, he could threaten the entire world."

"You were the one wielding him."

"He's evil. Nothing but harm will come of it."

"Nothing is worse than your death."

But some things simply couldn't be allowed, no matter what. Above all else, Riddle knew Carina existed. He knew the nature of Harry and Carina's relationship. He had even absorbed something of those feelings. If he ever took Harry's body, Carina would be in grave danger.

He hated saying it—it felt like a threat—but there was no other way. Harry shut his eyes and forced the words out.

"…He said he would sever the tendons in your arms and legs, and keep you as a doll that whispers love to him, displayed in a room. I can't allow—"

Carina's fingers pressed against his lips. Neatly trimmed nails grazed his philtrum, then traced down through the curve of his lips to his chin. Through the parting of his mouth, her taste seeped in. Whether or not she knew he was savoring it, Carina held his face in her hands and whispered.

"We can. Right now."

Harry opened his eyes. Carina was hanging from his neck, her arms wrapped around him.

It was always like this. So hard to build, so easy to bring down. Hope crept into a life half-surrendered, and the wall he had built from death crumbled in an instant. The senses he had been forcing himself to ignore began to take on color again.

The first thing to fill his eyes, to brush his ears, to reach him with its scent, to stir his appetite—was Carina. Harry exhaled. Whatever heat had been building inside him, the breath that left him was warm.

"Provoking me isn't a good idea, Carina. There's no one coming to the headmaster's office right now."

"If there were, I wouldn't be doing this, you idiot."

He'd meant to hold back, and yet here he was, drawn in before he'd noticed. Perhaps Carina Malfoy, whom he'd always thought so untouchable, was rather more dangerous than he'd given her credit for.

Tension gathered in his body. He pulled her close, and against his chest came the soft pressure of her, and the touch of her fingers. Her hands—which he'd thought were pushing him away—moved across him like fingers finding keys on a piano. Whether she was steering him or playing him, he couldn't say.

What now? Harry asked himself.

The answer was already there.

# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 84: Polyjuice Potion

Waves crash against the dark sea. The night ocean, black as oil, seems to swallow light whole. The darkness reflected in those deep waters ripples like fog. It surges over the land like an anglerfish rising from the abyss, reaching hungrily for whatever lies above.

The sound of waves breaking against the cliffs—a death rattle of foam, a howl of despair from waters that cannot crest the rock. Only the salt-bitter smell of the sea remains, mocking their futile persistence.

An island fortress, cut off from the world.

In the manor ringed on all sides by sheer cliffs, darkness coiled with ominous intent. It spun and danced as though in worship of the Dark Lord's triumph—celebrating the victory feast now unfolding within those clifftop walls.

In the space where all eyes converged, she spoke.

"Harry Potter drank the poison and died."

Carina Malfoy had returned. She had entered Hogwarts under the Dark Lord's secret orders, and now she stood before him with perfect composure, drawing every gaze in the room as she began her report.

"Even if he was The Chosen One, he was nothing more than a foolish boy drunk on heroism. I lured him in, then slipped poison into his drink while his guard was down. The careless fool never thought to suspect it. It became his last supper. My Lord, I have completed the mission you entrusted to me."

She finished speaking and bowed deeply. Gracefully. Beautifully.

Voldemort sat at the head of the room, listening to her report. He lounged in imperious repose, looking down at Carina with an expression of vague displeasure. With every shift of his face, the hearts of his followers lurched and stuttered.

After a long silence, he spoke, slowly.

"Severus Snape. I will ask you… Is Harry Potter truly dead?"

"My Lord, I witnessed it with certainty. He was foolish enough to drink the magical poison and perish. He was pale-faced, blood at his lips. Nothing short of an Inferius could rise from that state…."

"You are certain?"

"By the moment I observed him, recovery was already impossible. Not even phoenix tears could have healed him."

"I see…."

Voldemort did not rejoice. He did not look relieved. He did not praise Carina. What settled across his face was profound disappointment.

"Disappointing…. I thought he resembled me. Raised by lowly Muggles, carrying sufficient hatred, capable of dominating others through fear—I believed he was such a person. I thought that boy might transcend what Dumbledore called 'love'… and reach me."

She allowed the faintest twitch at the corner of her mouth. Voldemort's words contained far more than their surface meaning.

Remarkably, Voldemort knew that Carina and Harry had been involved with each other. He had simply intended to mock it.

And the reason Voldemort had ordered Carina to kill Harry was not to test her loyalty—it was to test Harry's 'love.' To see whether the two of them could accomplish something through it.

"And yet, in the end, he followed that 'love' and drank the poison… Disappointing. Is that the value Dumbledore opposed me for? Such a paltry thing?"

Voldemort had sent Carina expecting her to die or be subdued in the field. Contrary to his expectations, she had returned with the mission complete. Harry Potter had, in the end, failed to surpass even Carina. He was dissatisfied that the prophesied adversary had died so emptily.

For the Dark Lord, it would have been preferable had Carina failed and died….

And in the face of one mother who had grasped that meaning, an expression arose that defied all description.

"Perhaps… I overestimated him. In any case, you have completed your mission…. I commend you. Carina Malfoy."

"It is an honour, my Lord."

She bowed her head deeply, then raised it. Once more, her eyes met Voldemort's. And then—

"Carina!"

Narcissa pushed through the crowd and broke free of it. She was weeping—so overcome with joy at her daughter's safe return that tears streamed freely down her face. She seized her in an embrace with a strength that seemed impossible from such a slight frame. Carina—caught off guard for just a moment—raised her arms in return and held her mother back. And in that instant—

Eyes trembling with fear, an expression of boundless desperation. She looked straight through him.

"Is Carina alive? Is she at Hogwarts?"

Narcissa whispered it against his ear, in a voice too small for anyone else to hear.

Harry smiled faintly. He had watched her steadily for five years. He had practised with that in mind, and thought he'd done a rather decent job of it. It seemed he still had a very long way to go. A mother's love was powerful enough to see straight through Polyjuice Potion.

Harry whispered back, holding her in return.

"Yes. She's there."

Narcissa let out a sob as though she might break into tears entirely, and pulled him closer still. When the brief embrace ended, she bowed her head toward Voldemort and made her request.

"My Lord, my daughter has only just arrived and is exhausted. Please allow me to spend some time with her."

Voldemort appeared indifferent to everything now. He waved a hand with languid disinterest.

"Do as you like."

Harry was led out of the hall by Narcissa's hand.

Narcissa walked the corridor with her hand resting on Harry's shoulder. To any observer, they were a perfectly devoted mother and daughter.

But neither the mother nor the former boyfriend wearing her daughter's face was deceived by the other. They spoke with full awareness of what they were.

"That's the bathroom, and that's the dining room. If you need anything, call the house-elf. The house-elf's name is—"

"Dobby, isn't it?"

"Yes. Use Dobby for most things. Avoid contact with others as much as possible. They may know little about Carina, but there's no harm in being careful."

"Thank you. Mother-in-law."

"…You."

Narcissa swallowed her tears and spoke with a look of quiet anguish.

"If you can only bring that child… safely through this crisis. Then you may call me that."

"That's a reassuring thing to say. A proper reward does wonders for motivation."

Harry walked forward with easy confidence. His composure seemed to settle something in Narcissa. Her voice carried a faint tremor, but she gave him a thorough account of the manor's layout and who remained within it.

As they talked, Narcissa occasionally asked other things.

"What has become of Draco?"

"He'll be in the Slytherin dormitory. He's fine. I've left him with people I trust."

"Who?"
"The ones who follow Carina around will look after him. And I've asked Hermione and Ginny to be kind to him, so he'll be well taken care of."

"…Who?"

"Ah—Granger and the Weasley*s*."

His manner was somewhat different from the usual, but Harry was respectful enough toward his prospective mother-in-law. The ease with which they exchanged words made them look, to any eye, like a mother and daughter with nothing to hide between them.

Then—Harry's brow furrowed at a sudden, foul stench. He turned toward it. A hulking figure with a heavily bearded face and filthy teeth was staring in his direction.

"Heheh… the smell, the smell… A frightened liar's smell…."

"What is that. Can't he smell himself? Has his nose given up entirely?"

Harry's face twisted in distaste. Narcissa moved quickly to shield him.

"Greyback!"

Narcissa's voice cut sharp and clear.

"What are you doing here?"

"Why so tense, Narcissa…. I only followed the smell of a liar…."

Harry recognised him immediately.

Fenrir Greyback. The most savage of Werewolves, a creature who made a hobby of threatening to bite children and turn them into Werewolves if he wasn't obeyed. His favourite thing: the nape of a young woman's neck. He so loved to sink his teeth into that white throat, to turn them, to listen to their howls of despair.

That Fenrir Greyback was now looking at Harry—wearing Carina's body—with eyes full of hunger.

"You killed him? A cowardly little princess like you…? Heh. Doesn't add up. I know. You got scared and ran again. Severus did all the real work, as usual…."

Fenrir circled slowly around Harry. Like a wolf stalking prey, searching for an opening.

Harry watched him with detached calm for a moment—then remembered that he was wearing Carina's body. That reeking criminal was currently looking Carina up and down.

Suddenly he felt unpleasant. No—that was far too mild a word for what surged through him. The shift in Harry's emotions was violent. He had an immediate urge to rip out every one of those teeth and eliminate the source of that breath. The only thing keeping him even marginally rational was the fact that Narcissa was watching. Harry turned to her.

"Mother. Has he always behaved like this?"

"Carina. Bear with it. He is simply this sort of person."

"Oh, is he."

Harry smiled. Not the beautiful, composed smile he wore to conceal himself before enemies—something more like a grin with a screw loose.

"This bastard dares to insult Kari— me and my mother. He must have a death wish."

Fenrir Greyback faltered at the unfamiliar edge in Carina's manner.

This body was Carina's. Harry was borrowing it temporarily through Polyjuice Potion—but the pale skin, the brilliant platinum hair, the soft warmth of her scent. All of it was Carina's.

And this piece of filth had been drinking it in with his eyes. Had even insulted her on occasion?

"Get out of my sight."

Fenrir Greyback was unsettled by the change in Carina, but not frightened. The Carina he had seen before she left for Hogwarts had been keeping herself small because of her father's imprisonment in Azkaban. He had written her off as a coward without the nerve to raise her wand.

"Oh…, getting a bit sharp, are we, Princess?"

"You haven't seen the half of it."

The real Carina would never have let him off either. Harry decided to give him her share as well. He drew his wand instantly, levelled it, and shouted—

"*Relashio!*"

With a crack, Fenrir Greyback's arms flailed as he was hurled across the corridor. He slammed into the wall with a raw scream and slid to the floor.

Harry was considering finishing it there, but Narcissa grabbed his arm quickly. Harry reined himself in and looked down at Greyback with cold eyes.

"Try that again and I'll—"

"Carina has just distinguished herself by killing Harry Potter, Greyback! In future, at least have the self-awareness to remember that you are a guest here!"

Greyback hauled himself upright, seething—but then:

"Touch my daughter one more time and I'll tell Bella."

The moment Narcissa invoked Bellatrix, Greyback flinched visibly. Even he had to tuck his tail between his legs before genuine madness.

Once Greyback had gone, Narcissa pressed a hand to her chest and said:

"You must be careful. There are many violent people here…."

"It's fine. I'll be more violent than any of them."

It was the plain truth without a trace of exaggeration, which made Narcissa look at him with even greater concern.

"…Carina doesn't behave like that."

"I know. But how many people here actually know Carina?"

Harry's words struck true. The Death Eaters, largely indifferent to one another, were less allies than competitors vying for Voldemort's favour. Even if Harry behaved somewhat out of character, it was unlikely anyone would suspect his identity.

If Voldemort himself had no interest in his own followers, there was little chance those beneath him paid each other any real attention.

"Severus will probably notice—"

Narcissa stopped mid-sentence, then murmured as understanding settled over her. The person who had brought Harry—disguised as Carina—here was Severus Snape.

"…This was Severus's idea. He was on Dumbledore's side all along…. Bella was right."

Harry chose to say nothing on that matter. He had no desire to vindicate the man who had put Carina's life at risk, whatever his reasons.

Narcissa led Harry to his room. Standing at the door, she gave him a few final instructions.

"Wear the clothes in the wardrobe. Have the house-elf handle most things, and don't open the door to anyone you can't trust."

"Is this Carina's room?"

"No, this is the holiday house. A place to stay briefly when travelling. But everything you need has been brought in, so use it freely."
"I will."

"Good. Sleep well."

It was the sort of ordinary farewell that belonged in any home. To any observer, they might have looked like a perfectly ordinary family settling in for the night. But Narcissa suddenly broke into tears. Harry looked around in helpless confusion.

"I've only ever… made that child… suffer…."

Before Narcissa stood someone who looked exactly like the daughter she would do anything for. But no matter how warmly she treated him, this was not her daughter Carina Malfoy. This was Harry Potter, who had audaciously slipped into enemy territory wearing her face.

It struck Narcissa anew—Carina and Harry were the same age. And how precarious the lives they had been living were.

"What have we done… to children like you…."

Harry considered how to comfort his prospective mother-in-law. But even with her being his future mother-in-law, it wasn't quite enough to soften the edges of his character.

After all, Narcissa bore her share of responsibility for forcing such a brutal choice on Carina.

"It's all right. If the adults had handled it properly, it wouldn't have fallen to people like us, would it? If you'd sorted it out, we wouldn't be here."

The sight of Harry wearing Carina's face and delivering that barb was so insufferably smug that Narcissa's tears dried up entirely.

It was true, though, so she had nothing to say in reply.

"…I'm counting on you."

Narcissa left those words behind and closed the door. Harry was alone in the room.

The warmth drained from Harry's expression. He surveyed the room first.

A wide carpet. A four-poster bed with a canopy. Pillows and a duvet that looked impossibly soft. The bathroom was en suite, and a wide window looked out over the full sweep of the view.

The furniture seemed somewhat large overall—then Harry remembered he was in Carina's body, and reached into his pocket for the flask. He checked his surroundings, then unscrewed the cap.

Inside was a potion the colour of thin mud.

Polyjuice Potion. Containing a strand of Carina Malfoy's hair.

Severus Snape and Dumbledore had a plan.

Just as Dumbledore used the Fidelius Charm to protect his own hiding places, Voldemort had warded his sanctuary against enemies. But having no friends and trusting no followers, he preferred a different method to the Secret-Keeper arrangement.

Parseltongue Binding. He had placed a curse on every follower—speak the location of the sanctuary aloud, and you die in that instant. It was a convenient piece of magic, but unlike the Fidelius Charm, it had a flaw.

An enemy could still find it directly.

Peter Pettigrew found out where he was.

Voldemort kept snakes. Wherever he dwelt, rats and insects fled in fear. Rather than searching for where he was, one could trace the absence of rats—and find his location by working backwards.

They had searched somewhere in Albania, but Voldemort had regained his body and moved on before they arrived, making it all wasted effort. Peter had despaired and combed all of Britain.

A year and several months of searching. A report of a region with no rats. Days of tracking. Peter Pettigrew had found the location—

And that location had reached Dumbledore. From that point, Dumbledore had begun planning a counter-strike.

Of course, if they spent too long breaking through the magical defences, the enemy would notice and go on alert. That was why Harry and Snape had infiltrated first.

Snape would neutralise the protective enchantments surrounding the manor. Harry would prepare the passage for their allies to come through.

Harry looked in the mirror. Carina's face watched him from within it. A strange sight. Under any other circumstances he might have found it amusing—but Harry could not smile right now.

The mirror held the answer to how Narcissa had seen through him. What Carina would ever wear such a hollow expression? Smile with such graceless carelessness? Only after looking at himself in the mirror did Harry grasp the enormous gulf between the real Carina and the imitation.

It was almost baffling. However little interest they had in one another, surely colleagues who had spent years in each other's company should have felt a difference this vast.

He was so thoroughly unconvincing.

"No wonder you lot keep getting caught, with that kind of eye for detail."

Harry muttered at Carina's reflection. Whatever the case—however well he had concealed himself—the moment had come to prepare for a battle that would cost him everything.

"Carina. Wish me luck."

Harry thought of Carina, lying still as death.

He had followed her memories through the Room of Requirement and destroyed the Ravenclaw diadem—the Horcrux. That alone had been an extraordinary achievement.

And near the Vanishing Cabinet, several potions that Carina had stolen remained. One of them was Polyjuice Potion.

It was there that Harry had conceived the plan.

Transform into Carina and strike at the heart of the enemy.

For that, he'd had to cut her hair. With the reluctance of feeding a high-denomination banknote into a shredder, he had snipped the platinum strand, dropped it into the Polyjuice, and drank. He had transformed into Carina's likeness.

With great care he had changed her into fresh clothes, then dressed himself in garments that still carried her scent, and departed with Snape for Voldemort's sanctuary.

The plan had succeeded, and brought him here.

Harry retrieved the bag he had borrowed from Moody. It was roughly the size of a large pillow, but each time Harry undid a clasp and opened it further, the mouth grew wider. By the time all the clasps were undone, the interior was large enough to hold an entire cabinet.

Harry drew the Vanishing Cabinet from within it.

The portal connected to Hogwarts.

The moment Snape destroyed the protective enchantments, Harry would send the signal—and through this, the Order of the Phoenix would come pouring in. They would drive a blade into the heart of an enemy caught completely off guard.

But first.

"Carina can't be the only one who drinks poison. Can she."

He had been thinking of Carina too much. The emotions he had carefully ordered and set aside stirred all at once. The violent fury that had been holding his Occlumency together like a blown fuse gave way, and the image of Carina coughing blood circled through his mind.

Hatred crashed against Harry's reason like a wave. The small happiness he had found after years of abuse. There were those who wanted to crush even that.

Death Eaters.

"If you love death so much, you can choke on it."

Wearing Carina's face, Harry smiled—cold and dark—and reached for the vial of potion.

Educational Decree Number Twenty-Six. No teacher at Hogwarts is permitted to discuss topics outside the designated curriculum during class hours.

Educational Decree Number Twenty-Seven. All students at Hogwarts are prohibited from forming privately organised groups, teams, societies, or clubs. Only the High Inquisitor may authorise the reformation of any team, society, or club, and any student found participating in an unauthorised group may be expelled at the High Inquisitor's discretion.

When the new Educational Decrees were passed, no one was angrier than Cedric.

"This is completely unacceptable!"

He wore an expression no one had ever seen on him before. That handsome, gentle face was filled today with fierce, undisguised hostility.

"Club activities are a student's right. Even the Ministry of Magic has no business taking that away! That's it. As student body president, I'm going to lodge a formal protest with that woman!"

"Cedric...!"

As Cedric made to stride off somewhere, Cho Chang seized his hand.

"You can't defy her!"

"Cho. Don't try to stop me."

"How could I not? She's the Hogwarts High Inquisitor—she's from the Ministry itself. She could make your school life miserable, and your future along with it! As your girlfriend, I can't just stand by and—"

Cho couldn't finish. She buried her face against Cedric's chest. He looked down at her, torn, and moved to wrap his arms around her trembling shoulders.

But instead of reaching for her, he closed his hand into a fist.

He was the very embodiment of Hufflepuff. His good nature and steadfast character pushed him forward. He could not abandon duty. He could not abandon justice.

"...I'm sorry, Cho."

Cedric tightened his fist and looked toward something farther away.

"I'm the student body president. I have to carry the weight of every student's sweat and tears."

A short, sharp tsk cut through the air, though Cedric didn't seem to hear it. Or perhaps he heard it and chose not to.

What was certain, however, was this: Cedric had to move forward.

The following morning, Cedric walked into the Great Hall carrying a square placard. Students who recognised him called out with grins.

"Cedric, what's that sign for? Going to play softball with it?"

"It's a bit wide for softball, isn't it? Maybe he's staging a protest or something."

"Ha! Come off it."

Ernie Macmillan burst out laughing. Cedric only smiled and said nothing.

With a brief morning greeting, Dumbledore flicked his wand, and breakfast filled the tables. Salads and simple sandwiches, sausages and pickles—all the light fare that sat easily on a morning stomach—appeared in abundance.

The hungry students tucked in, while those who rarely bothered with breakfast focused more on conversation than food.

Then it happened. Cedric shouldered his placard and rose to his feet. With every eye in the hall on him, he walked to the very centre of the Great Hall, set the placard down with a resounding thud, and raised his wand.

Every person in the hall—staff included—turned to look at him. Cedric was a diligent, model student; in the best possible sense, he was someone who rarely drew attention to himself. The sight of him standing there with a placard made everyone stare with undisguised curiosity.

"Respected professors, Headmaster, and High Inquisitor. I am Cedric Diggory, President of the Hogwarts Student Body. I stand here as the representative of every student in this school."

Cedric raised his wand and pointed it at the placard. Black ink bled across the surface, and words rose into view: THE EDUCATIONAL DECREES ARE ANTI-EDUCATIONAL!

Dumbledore smiled. Professor Sprout's eyes went wide before she cast an anxious glance toward Umbridge. Snape looked on with characteristic indifference, but most of the staff wore expressions that mingled pride with worry.

Umbridge, for her part, looked as though her eyes might fall out of her head.

"The formation and operation of clubs, societies, and teams is a student's right! No one—not by rule, not by law—has the authority to suppress students' freedom of assembly! Hogwarts is a school, but it is not merely a place for study. It is a place where witches and wizards meet, where we exchange ideas, where we cultivate compassion and solidarity!"

Cedric swept his wand again. New words appeared: EDUCATIONAL DECREE NUMBER TWENTY-SEVEN IS NULL AND VOID! Umbridge lurched to her feet, pointing at Cedric with a finger that shook violently.

"You—you—you—! Someone stop him—!"

"YEAH!"

But Umbridge's words were swallowed by Harry's roar. Harry, absolutely delighted, leapt up onto the table and bellowed:

"Null and void! Null and void! Null and void!"

And that wasn't all. Fred and George seized the moment and set off fireworks in the air. An impromptu display of pyrotechnics lit up the hall at the crack of dawn.

The atmosphere shifted in an instant. The Gryffindors and Hufflepuffs chanted as one: "Null and void!" Their voices filled the Great Hall, echoing off every wall.

Umbridge, white as a sheet, screamed:

"Headmaster! Do something about these children!"

"I'm afraid I can't, Dolores. I don't believe any of the Educational Decrees contain a provision against students making noise during breakfast."

Dumbledore addressed her with a serene smile.

"Nor, for that matter, do Hogwarts' own rules. You'll have to find another avenue."
Null and void! Null and void! Null and void!

The chanting grew louder. Even the Ravenclaws joined in, tentatively at first; the Hufflepuffs and Gryffindors were now pairing off and dancing as they shouted. As always, only the Slytherins sat scowling at their table. Crabbe muttered a small "...null and void..." under his breath, but fell silent the moment Carina slapped the back of his hand.

Then, from the other end of the Slytherin table, a younger voice rang out.

"Null and void!"

Carina's expression curdled. She turned sharply toward the sound—and found herself, for once, the one caught off guard.

There was Draco, fork in hand, shouting "Null and void!" before looking around in bewilderment.

"Wait—are we not doing this?"

"We are not!"

Carina clapped a hand over his mouth. But whatever she did, the mood in the Great Hall had already run its course.

Around Cedric, standing tall at the centre of it all, the hall had descended into glorious chaos. Harry climbed up onto a table and began conducting the students with his wand like a conductor before an orchestra.

"The Educational Decrees are—!"

"Anti-educational!"

"Decree Twenty-Seven is—!"

"Null and void!"

"Null and void! Null and void! Null and void!"

"QUIET—!"

Umbridge pressed her wand to her own throat and shrieked. A high, piercing, deeply unpleasant sound blanketed the hall. Students flinched, clapping their hands over their ears and sinking into their seats.

Through it all, Cedric stood perfectly upright, placard in hand. Umbridge advanced with bloodshot eyes.

"How dare you—defy a Decree signed personally by the Minister—!"

"Because it is wrong."

Cedric answered with clear, steady eyes. The words on his placard had changed again.

THE MINISTRY OF MAGIC MUST PROTECT STUDENTS' RIGHTS!

"When I came to you before, you told me there was nothing you could do—that the Decree came directly from the Ministry. Very well. Then I will make my demands in this manner. As President of the Hogwarts Student Body!"

Umbridge's face had gone so red she looked like a pomegranate about to burst. Confronted with a form of defiance she had never anticipated, she was incandescent with rage, practically steaming at the ears.

"Detention! My office, six o'clock tonight! How dare you, how dare you—"

Another Hufflepuff student shouted back:

"That's not fair!"

"Cedric hasn't broken a single rule!"

"You're the one changing the rules as you go!"

"SILENCE!"

Umbridge's hair had come half-undone from all her shrieking. She screamed, looking thoroughly dishevelled:

"Educational Decree Twenty-Five! The High Inquisitor has the authority to issue punishments to students! Cedric, you are in detention! How dare you—the Educational Decrees—"

"The Educational Decrees are—?"

"Null and void!"

Harry raised his wand again and called out. The students, who had briefly gone quiet, hammered the tables and roared back.

"Decree Twenty-Five as well!"

"Null and void!"

"The High Inquisitor too!"

"Null and void!"

The students chanted "null and void" with gleeful abandon. Someone's wand produced a thunderous drumbeat. Firework rockets spun lazily through the air above.

It was a festival, and it had started at breakfast. The energy of several hundred students was not something one Dolores Umbridge could ever hope to contain. She opened her mouth to scream something, her face a furious crimson—

Then Dumbledore spoke.

"Now then, everyone. I do apologise for the interruption while you're all enjoying yourselves."

His voice was quiet, yet it reached every ear in the hall with perfect clarity. The students fell silent at once and looked to the front.

"Breakfast is nearly over. You all have classes to attend, so I think we ought to wrap things up here and save the rest of the festivities for another time."
"Yes, Professor Dumbledore."

Cedric nodded and tucked the placard away. The words THANK YOU appeared briefly on its surface before fading without a trace.

Watching this, Harry pocketed his wand and sat down. The other students followed as if on cue, going quiet and beginning to gather their things to leave the hall. The uproar of moments ago might never have happened.

Umbridge, however, could not contain her fury. It appeared to her that the students had defied her at every turn while obeying Dumbledore without question.

That was, in fact, precisely what had happened—but either way, it was more than enough to stoke her rage.

"Cedric Diggory—! Harry Potter—! How dare—!"

Umbridge, furious beyond reason, whipped out her wand and began to cast—

"That won't do, Dolores."

Crack. A chilling force seized Umbridge and held her fast. Her body went rigid.

She couldn't breathe. The air around her felt as though it had turned to solid brick. Umbridge's eyes darted about in barely concealed panic.

"I am obliged to cooperate with the Ministry and provide a proper education. But the use of offensive spells against students is something I cannot permit under any circumstances. It is the Headmaster's duty to protect his students, Dolores."

"I—I speak with the authority of the Minister for Magic—"

"Even if the Minister himself were standing here. No one may attack a student within these walls. I trust that is understood."

The students let out a small cheer. Harry strolled past Umbridge with a broad grin, and as he passed directly in front of her, he made a point of spinning around in a full circle.

The other students did much the same—each one murmuring "null and void" or some other parting jab as they filed past her.

But Umbridge was too gripped by fear to move. Dumbledore had done nothing more than briefly touch his wand, and yet she had been rendered nearly mummified. If he had actually intended to harm her...

"There. I believe you've calmed down."

Thud. The hold released. Umbridge staggered backward. Colin Creevey, who had crept close to taunt her, gave a startled yelp and bolted to the far end of the hall.

But Umbridge had no presence of mind left to punish anyone. She stumbled out of the Great Hall at a near-run.

Laughter erupted from inside. The staff appeared to be in fits as well. But Umbridge could not even summon the anger to respond—she ran, as if trying to outpace the laughter that clung to her heels.

The Gryffindor common room was subdued. Fred and George sat with their heads bowed, speaking in mournful tones.

"Don't call us 'brave Gryffindor' anymore."

"The Gryffindor common room? No. From today, this is the Cowards' Lounge."

The Gryffindors had been deeply moved by Cedric.

His method was not the Gryffindor way. It was the method of someone so earnest they could face injustice head-on without flinching. But the sight of someone standing up with quiet dignity had a way of striking at the heart, regardless.

"We won't be outdone."

"Dumbledore's Army—the D.A.—stands with him."

"Actually, it's Diggory's Army."

"Actually, it's Diggory's Arm."

"Hufflepuff will be his right arm, so we'll settle for being the left."

"What if Cedric's left-handed? Someone go and ask him."

Led by Fred and George, the Gryffindors made their solemn pledge.

At that same hour, Harry and Hermione were explaining the protest to Ron. Ron, who had grown up in a purely wizarding household, was baffled by the whole concept.

"He just... stood there holding a sign? That's it?"

"Do wizards not watch the news? It's a perfectly ordinary protest."

"I've never seen anything like it."

"Well, if you don't have an uncle who curses at the telly every time a Labour demonstration comes on and says they should all be locked up, you might not know about it."

It had been Cedric who wanted to act. Harry had merely suggested the Muggle approach. Having gone to the professors several times and been ignored each time, Cedric had taken an immediate interest in the Muggle method—measured, civilised, and surprisingly effective.

Ron shrugged.

"We use Howlers. A lot of people think this sort of thing is a waste of time. Though I suppose right now there are plenty of people watching and time to spare, so..."

Harry and Hermione looked at each other.

Howlers. The wizarding world's instrument of protest. Bombs that exploded with sound...

This was Hogwarts. A school for witches and wizards.

A place full of parchment, ink, and young students who had been writing essays since they could hold a quill.

Howlers, too, might be quite an effective method.

# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 78: Dark Mark

Much had changed over the summer. The entire wizarding world was now forced to confront a truth it had desperately wished to deny: Voldemort had returned. And with that reckoning came a flood of horrors that had long been looked away from—disappearances, murders, magical catastrophes. All of it was laid at the feet of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named and his Death Eaters.

Cornelius Fudge resigned, bearing the full weight of the fallout. Umbridge was driven out of Hogwarts, and Professor Trelawney was reinstated. Every Educational Decree passed the previous year was struck from the books.

It seemed as though everything had returned to the way it was.

And yet nothing was quite the same as before.

Crack—!

On the first day of term, while students still young enough to be carefree were pouring into Hogwarts and chattering amongst themselves—a sharp slap rang out in front of the Great Hall.

"Malfoy—! How dare you show your face here!"

Carina, struck without warning, turned her head slightly to one side and fixed the girl with a flat, sunken stare.

It was Susan Bones of Hufflepuff. Even as her cheek blazed with heat, Carina's mind was cold as ice water.

Curious. They'd barely crossed paths before last year. Susan had been the one to strike her, yet she stood there with tears welling in her eyes as though she were the one who'd been hit. Her hand was still raised, hovering—ready, it seemed, to come down again.

Carina let the moment settle, then exhaled softly.

"Ah. Bones. So it's you."

Now that she thought of it—hadn't the great witch Amelia Bones been murdered? Even under Ministry protection, even formidable as she was, He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named had seen fit to come himself to finish the job. Her death had been a clear message to anyone who stood against them.

Cross us, and death will find you.

Her beloved aunt was gone. The killer was known. The motive was obvious. And there was nothing to be done about it. That helpless grief and fury had found its outlet the moment Susan laid eyes on a Malfoy.

After all, Lucius Malfoy—locked away in Azkaban—was the living symbol of the Dark Lord's return.

But Susan Bones, this foolish girl.

"I find that rather extraordinary, Bones. Your aunt is dead—why are you taking it up with me?"

"What? You shameless—!"

Susan raised her hand again, but Carina answered by seizing her by the collar and yanking her close. With the distance between them suddenly gone, Susan's arm had nowhere to go.

Silver-grey eyes, cold as metal, fixed on Susan's face. Words just as cold followed.

"Tell me yourself, then—what exactly does her death have to do with me, that you'd slap me out of nowhere?"

"You, you're—you're his—!"

"His?"

Carina tilted her head.

"I'm not sure I follow. Would you care to be more specific?"

"He—He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named! That's who!"

"Ah, yes. The Dark Lord."

She released Susan's collar with a small, deliberate motion. Susan stumbled back. But she glanced around at the gathering crowd, seemed to draw something from their presence, and stepped forward again.

Carina exhaled once—short and quiet—then lifted her chin at a slight angle and stood her ground before them all.

Her father was in prison. Her standing among the Death Eaters had eroded. The eyes that followed her were hostile and unforgiving.

But Carina Malfoy did not bow her head. She would not shrink simply because someone had struck her. Her pride had been built too high over too many years to be broken by something so trivial.

"Very well. Let's say, for the sake of argument, that I—*I*—had some hand in the death of the great and admirable Amelia Bones."

She clothed herself in their hostility, draped contempt and fear across her shoulders like a mantle. Public prejudice was the outermost coat one wore—a brand that never faded. There was no point in trying to shed it. No one would believe otherwise anyway.

Instead, Carina took hold of their fear and wielded it.

"Susan Bones, you don't know your place. Even Amelia Bones—*that* Amelia Bones—ended up dead. What will you do if your parents become the next target?"

For those who still had something left to lose, there was no more fitting weapon than that. Fear flooded Susan's face. Carina smiled pleasantly and leaned in close to her ear.

"Hmm? You said I killed your aunt. If you're standing before your enemy, draw your wand and come at me. Fight like you mean to kill. Don't stoop to something as petty as a slap."

"Stop it!"

Several students moved to separate them. But the attempt came to nothing. Barnette Crabbe and Gray Goyle had already taken up positions at Carina's sides, and other Slytherins were drifting over with their characteristic swagger.

"What's this—I thought it was a Gryffindor being cheeky, but it's a Hufflepuff?"

"Daring to hit Malfoy."

"Brave of you. You don't even have Cedric Diggory anymore. Who exactly are you counting on to back you up?"

The situation reversed itself in moments. The Hufflepuff students, ringed by Slytherins, went pale.
It wasn't simply that the Slytherins were especially menacing. It was that most of them came from families with Death Eater ties. The fear of what might happen if you provoked them was entirely rational.

Then Carina raised one hand, and the Slytherins fell silent and turned to look at her. She had their attention in an instant. She turned to face Susan—her cheek still flushed red—and smiled. A beautiful smile.

"Susan Bones, I know you've been through something terrible. But you can't go around making accusations without evidence. You understand that, don't you?"

Nodding here was a kind of surrender. But Susan, having already spent her anger, had nothing left to fight with—no strength, no defiance. She gave Carina a stiff, reluctant nod.

Carina nodded in return—and swung her hand.

Crack.

The dry sound rang out. Susan staggered from the blow.

An eye for an eye. A tooth for a tooth.

Carina shook out her hand and said, quite calmly:

"Don't think of it as unfair. Not if you have any sense of shame."

She turned on her heel. The Slytherins snickered as they swept past Susan and the Hufflepuffs.

Anyone watching would have called it a flawless victory. But Carina took no pleasure in small wins anymore. This death was not her sin—yet the price of that blood had found her cheek all the same.

There was no way out of this viscous cycle of resentment.

Had Harry seen it? He probably had. The sixth-years had all arrived at Hogwarts together.

Would he be disappointed? Would he feel sorry for her? Or would he take some satisfaction in her misfortune? She had no way of knowing.

At least Draco hadn't arrived yet. That was something. The last thing she wanted was for that poor, already-burdened boy to see his sister being struck.

"I owe you an apology. I was wrong in my thinking."

Harry sat on the sofa, hands clasped tightly together. Dumbledore regarded him with quiet attention.

"That scar of yours—it seems to be something more fundamental than a simple connection. I believe it carries a portion of Voldemort's power, his memories, his nature. That would explain why he was able to send you visions even after you had disciplined your mind through Occlumency. And why, when you were under the Imperius Curse, it actually worked in your favour."

"I suppose it was strange, now that I think about it. Even if Voldemort was particularly interested in me, would he really have observed me closely enough to craft perfectly tailored insults for Ron and the others? No—that was a voice inside me from the beginning. A snake that had grown within me, disguised as my own."

At Harry's reply, Dumbledore shook his head gently.

"Oh, Harry. Every person carries a violent streak within them. Given everything you've endured, it would hardly be surprising to find some of that in you."

"A violent streak wouldn't speak to me and pull me out from under the Imperius Curse, though."

"Not necessarily. A person with a violent nature is actually harder to control. Admittedly, the speaking part is rather unusual."

Dumbledore said it calmly.

"You are like him, Harry. You both lost your parents. You both grew up in difficult circumstances. The people around you both feared you even as they looked down on you. But there was one great difference between you and Riddle. You had—"

"Love? Ha." A short, self-deprecating laugh. Is that all, his tone said—the voice of someone who didn't quite believe it himself.

Dumbledore looked at him with quiet sorrow.

"Have you decided not to see Miss Malfoy anymore?"

At the mention of that name, Harry let out a long breath.

It was a name he missed. Carina—every time he shaped it in his mouth, something sweet spread through him, only to be swallowed back down with a bitter aftertaste.

"I lost Sirius."

The heart follows the body. Just as physical wounds do, wounds of the heart eventually form a scab with time. Over the past months, Harry had turned his grief over and over until a rough, ugly crust had formed across it. It made him raw and uneven in places—but it no longer hurt simply to think of it.

He spoke without inflection.

"Because of my arrogance. Because of Voldemort, who gave the order. And because of Lucius, who devised the plan. But Carina tried to stop me until the very end. She didn't want me to die."

"She made a difficult and admirable choice. Even grown adults are swept along by circumstances—for someone still so young to resist takes real courage. But Harry, doesn't that mean you owe her something in return?"

"In return. I wonder. Is it enough to take her hand and say thank you for standing by me—when it was her father I sent to Azkaban?"

Harry's voice was dry with self-mockery.

"If Carina follows in Lucius's footsteps, she becomes my enemy. And if she betrays her father and chooses to help me again—then like Sirius, I'll lose someone I care about. Again. Staying apart is better, for now."

"Don't you both need a moment to breathe?"

"Breathing is fine, as long as it doesn't stop altogether. There's only one way to end all of this. Finish Voldemort—finish him—and then, once everyone is safe, see her again. That's all."

Harry's position was immovable. Whatever Dumbledore might say, he had no intention of changing his mind. And Dumbledore, for his part, was not the sort to press.

There are two kinds of old men. Those who are certain they are right and insist on it—and those who believe understanding will come in its own time, and watch with patient acceptance.
Regrettably, even the great Dumbledore could not claim the best of both. He was plainly the latter—a man who knew certain things and offered only gentle hints.

Perhaps that was wisdom. Perhaps it was arrogance. Or perhaps it was simply the passing of responsibility onto others, demanding greater sacrifice from those with less power and less knowledge.

"Carina."

Snape's voice was clipped and hard.

"I made a vow to your mother. To protect you. To assist you in what you must do."

"I appreciate that. But I'll decline."

Carina had never been one to refuse help. She considered the favour of teachers and the resources of her family as simply part of her arsenal—tools of capability, nothing more. It would never have occurred to Snape that she might turn him away.

He had been wrong. Carina refused him without hesitation. His eyebrow twitched.

"I don't need your protection or your assistance, Professor. You've finally been given the Defence Against the Dark Arts post you've always wanted—surely you're busy enough. You'd be better served preparing your lessons than concerning yourself with me."

"Ah. You don't trust me."

"And you don't trust me, Professor. This is a task I was given directly. It's mine to carry out."

Carina lied without a flicker of shame. She needed no one's help. Or rather—no one could add anything useful to what she was doing.

Because Carina had not yet made up her mind.

Kill Harry. Kill Dumbledore. Or—betray Voldemort.

She hadn't chosen a direction. Having someone else's weight added to hers before she had would only be a burden. She refused him cleanly, leaving no opening.

Snape spoke slowly.

"…You've received the Mark."

"Yes—quite the honour. I've joined their ranks."

"You would do well to reconsider my offer. If you fail to carry out your task, the serpent on your wrist will coil around you."

Carina flinched and grabbed her own arm. The snake beneath the skin of the tattoo seemed to writhe. Snape watched her without expression.

"Surely you didn't think the Dark Lord's gift—the Dark Mark—amounted to nothing more than a burning sensation? If you neglect your assigned task, or allow yourself to waver even briefly… the serpent will not simply watch. It cannot read your mind, but it can observe your actions. If your conduct strays from the Dark Lord's will, the serpent will report directly to him, and he will know you have abandoned your duty."

Carina bit down on her lip. The faint taste of blood spread through her mouth.

The happiest ending she had sometimes allowed herself to imagine—Carina betraying Voldemort, Harry and Dumbledore defeating him with her help, everyone safe and whole. That rose-coloured dream was now a path she could never take. Because of this Mark.

The one mercy was that she had learned this before acting on anything. Snape may not have known it, but he had already done her a considerable service.

That didn't mean she would accept his help.

Snape asked again.

"Then answer me this much. Do you have a plan?"

"…Yes."

Carina answered in a word.

Last year, when she had sided with Umbridge and moved against that club of Dumbledore's Army or whatever it was called, Montague had been shoved into the Vanishing Cabinet by the Weasley twins and disappeared. But through Montague's account, Carina had learned that the Vanishing Cabinet was a kind of portal. If there existed a portal that could move freely in and out of Hogwarts—where Apparition was forbidden—her options would expand considerably.

The problem was that the cabinet was currently broken and non-functional. With Snape's help, it could likely be repaired quickly. Whatever else one might say about him, he was a capable wizard…

"But I have no intention of telling you what it is. This is entirely my task. My role."

Still—Carina needed time.

If Snape moved quickly, resolved everything, and pressed a lethal poison into her hand along with an opportunity to slip it into Dumbledore's drink—she could carry out the task easily enough, whether she succeeded or failed.

But Carina did not yet have the will to kill. She was not ready to take a life.

If she accepted Snape's help without that readiness—and if Snape grew suspicious watching her hesitate—then she wouldn't need the serpent in the Dark Mark to betray her. Voldemort would sense her wavering himself, and the punishment would follow.

"Don't think about taking the credit from me, Professor. I'm sorry, but as you said yourself—I don't entirely trust you either."

"In that, you take after Lucius… He kept his plans from me for the same reason—afraid I'd steal his glory—and in the end, he failed."

Fortunately, her excuse landed. Snape allowed himself a faint, thin smile and indicated he would stand back for now.

"I'll watch for the time being. But you would do well to make progress. The Dark Mark will be watching you always—unless you are dead, or near death. And even setting that aside, the Dark Lord is not a patient man."

"Thank you for the advice. Though I already knew all of it."

With that, Carina gave Snape a deep, formal nod, turned sharply, and walked away. Her bearing in the corridor was impeccable—composed, unhurried, elegant. Enough self-possession, at least, to keep that fragile interior from showing.

Snape watched her go, and his expression grew darker still. His black eyes held no light—like ash that had burned out more than a decade ago and never rekindled.

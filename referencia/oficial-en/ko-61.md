Saturday morning, herald of a golden weekend. Harry had slept in and made his way to the Great Hall a step behind everyone else. He walked in mid-yawn to find Ron stabbing at a roasted potato with his fork, who called out the moment he spotted him.

"Harry! What took you so long?"

"Bad dreams. I thought I'd sleep like a log after yesterday—"

Harry scrunched his face as though the memory alone was unpleasant.

"Strange dreams?"

"Yeah. I was a snake, crawling through the corridors of the Ministry. Kept slithering toward some closed door at the far end and failing to get through."

"What?"

Ron and Hermione both startled and dropped their forks.

They had spent the summer with Harry and heard enough to know what his recurring dreams meant. Those dreams were real—whenever Voldemort's emotions surged, his memories bled back through the scar, flooding Harry's mind with visions.

"That's definitely him—!"

"Yeah. Must be. He's up to something."

Harry replied with studied indifference and reached for the pile of roasted potatoes on the platter. They were still steaming despite the late hour—a Preservation Charm, by the look of it. He speared a piece, dragged it through a generous dollop of sour cream, and popped it into his mouth, chewing as he spoke.

"Come to think of it, I've been having this dream for a few days now. I can't remember it clearly, but he seems to be after something down in the Ministry's lower levels. What on earth is hidden in the basement of the Ministry?"

"A secret door beneath the Ministry. Now that's intriguing."

Ron mulled it over as he loaded his plate with a well-browned sausage and a boiled egg.

"Maybe that's where they keep their brains. Especially Percy—the only explanation for how he behaves is that his brain's been sitting in a tank somewhere."

Ron's joke lifted Harry's mood a fraction. He snickered.

"Plausible, but I'm not sure anyone would bother stealing it."

"Useful as a weapon, though. Get close enough to Percy's brain and you'd go mad from the nonstop lecturing."

"Ron. Harry. This is hardly the time for jokes."

Hermione gave Ron a mild look of reproach, then turned to Harry.

"Harry. Tell Professor Dumbledore. Now."

"I will. Later."

"Harry, I mean right now! If he's after something, every moment counts!"

"That won't be necessary."

Harry wiped the sauce from his fingers with a napkin, then helped himself to a sausage the same way Ron had, chewing steadily. He'd been losing so much blood lately that meat had never tasted so necessary.

"This dream has been going on for weeks. What I've felt through it is deep frustration. He clearly hasn't managed to get inside yet. Whatever's in there, it's somewhere even he can't easily reach—"

"Harry, what happened to your hand?"

Hermione pointed. Harry turned his hand over, examining the scar.

"Oh, this? Umbridge has been calling me in every evening and carving into me with something sharp."

"What?"

Harry swallowed the last of his sausage and told them everything—what the 'extra study sessions' in Umbridge's office actually were, and how they were conducted.

He left out the part about Carina, of course. That was too private, too singular a thing to share.

Ron and Hermione sat with their mouths open when he finished. Before either could speak, Ginny, who had crept closer during the telling, erupted.

"That's torture! Harry, go to Dumbledore right now!"

"I should, yes—but I'm not sure what he can do about it. She has the Ministry's protection."

"What does that matter? You have scars on the back of your hand!"

"Professor Dumbledore isn't one to bend rules or defy authority. Not that he can't—it's more that he prefers not to, if he can help it."

Ginny knew this well enough to hold back any real criticism, though she grumbled all the same.

"But why? Couldn't Professor Dumbledore just—fix this if he wanted to?"

"He has his reasons, I'm sure."

Harry thought back to the fake Moody incident the previous year. Dumbledore had known Moody wasn't himself, and yet he hadn't stopped Harry or Sirius. It wasn't indifference—it felt more like a deliberate choice to watch and wait, to see what Harry would do on his own.

Perhaps Dumbledore was afraid of changing people. Of intervening too soon.

"Either way, he's busy. He mentioned finding another lead recently, so I'll have to wait on that—"

As Harry murmured this, he caught Draco's gaze from the Slytherin table again. This time it was blatant enough that Draco didn't look away when their eyes met. His searching stare swept slowly over Harry and the space around him.

He clearly wanted something. Harry considered it for a moment, then gave a small nod and rose from his seat.

"It's the weekend, so I should catch Professor Dumbledore before he leaves on his trip. I'll stop by the headmaster's office and then head back to the dormitory."

"Don't dawdle. Information is best exchanged quickly!"

"I know."

Harry grabbed another handful of sausages, feeding them into his mouth one by one as he left the Great Hall.

He passed through the long corridor leading away from the hall, climbed the moving staircases, and arrived at a wide, open landing with nowhere to hide. He spun around.

Across the way, Draco was throwing himself behind a pillar with frantic urgency. He seemed to think he was hidden, but if that had been the plan, he should have left the cloak behind. One shoulder and the trailing hem of his robes were plainly visible.

"Who's there!"

Harry whipped out his wand and called out. Draco flinched.
Harry lowered his voice to something deliberately menacing.

"A little rat has crept in... looking to dig up my secrets, were you? Not a chance. I'll catch you and throw you to the Acromantulas—"

With every slow step Harry took forward, Draco's shoulder trembled more pitifully. He seemed too frightened to form words.

Harry crept closer, then lunged.

"Got you!"

"Aaaaaah!"

Draco let out a piercing shriek, spun on his heel, and bolted. He was quick as a squirrel, but Harry had Seeker's reflexes—he snatched Draco by the scruff of the neck before he'd gone three steps.

"Caught you, you little sneak. Following me around—are you looking to die?"

"N-no! I was just passing through!"

Draco thrashed and shouted.

"I'm a Hogwarts student too! It was a coincidence!"

"Liar."

Harry pressed on.

"You're a first-year Slytherin, aren't you? There are no classrooms on the third-floor corridor for first-years. You followed me."

"I didn't!"

"Tell me the truth. Otherwise I'll take you somewhere no one will ever find you."

Harry's mouth was curling with barely suppressed amusement, but Draco, rigid with terror, couldn't see it. Draco squeezed his eyes shut and screamed.

"Let my sister go!"

Harry blinked, genuinely thrown.

"Let her go? I'm not holding her."

"Liar! I know you're blackmailing her! Yesterday she left the dormitory saying she was going to punish you, and when she came back her face was dark. There was even blood on her hands!"

That blood had been Harry's. Draco, knowing nothing of the real circumstances, had clearly drawn his own conclusions.

"If you don't stop, I'll make your life miserable until you drop dead!"

Harry had no idea how a first-year intended to make his life miserable, but the whole performance was endearing enough that he rubbed his chin and murmured.

"Well, well. You're rather fond of your sister, aren't you."

"I am not!"

Draco seethed.

"Not at all! I don't even like her! She acts like she's so important being a prefect, and she's still getting blackmailed by someone like you!"

"Carina's being blackmailed?"

Blackmailed. Harry's expression went flat.

"Who on earth would— don't tell me—"

"Don't play dumb. It's you. Harry Potter."

"...Me?"

This was genuinely news to him.

Apparently Harry had been blackmailing Carina without knowing it. He stared at Draco, at a loss for words, and Draco fired back.

"There's no other explanation for why my sister would kiss someone like you!"

"Ah."

Harry understood the shape of the misunderstanding now.

He'd wondered from the first day whether Draco had been assigned to investigate him on Lucius's orders—the staring had been too pointed. But that wasn't it at all. Draco had simply witnessed Harry and Carina kissing. And like a child who'd stumbled upon something they weren't meant to see, he'd been wary of Harry ever since.

Harry said, almost to himself.

"That must have been quite a shock. Watching your beloved sister kiss someone else—"

"Th-that's ridiculous! I don't even care about her!"

"I'll take your word for it."

Harry released Draco's collar.

If Draco had been sent to gather information or keep watch, Harry would have found a way to use that. But there was no trace of any such mission in the boy. Which made sense—even the Malfoys wouldn't send a ten-year-old on an assignment like that. Lucius probably wanted his son to have an ordinary school life, whatever else he was.

Harry's tone softened.

"I'm not blackmailing Carina."

"Liar. She'd never kiss someone like you unless you were forcing her."

"...You said you don't even like her."

"I don't!"

"Then what do you want from me?"

He said it with mild exasperation, but turning it over in his mind, Harry had to admit Draco wasn't entirely wrong.

Carina and Harry were enemies, technically. And yet she had never been truly cruel to him. She hadn't finished sorting out her feelings—and neither had he. If anything, Harry might have been using the lingering warmth between them as a kind of leverage without ever meaning to.

"Kid."
"I'm not a kid!"

"You take after your sister. Deny everything on principle."

Draco hissed.

"I do not! She's a girl and I'm a boy!"

"You act exactly the same."

"What would you know!"

"Quite a lot, actually. At least within Hogwarts, I'd wager I know more about how your sister has been living than you do."

Harry said it with deliberate provocation, and Draco reacted exactly as expected.

"How could you! You're not even in the same house!"

"There are ways of knowing things without sharing a common room."

Draco clearly hadn't been told about Harry and Carina's history. And even if he had been, Harry doubted a ten-year-old could have made sense of it.

It was deflating, in a way—and yet, in another way, reassuring. Lucius Malfoy wanted his son to have a normal school life.

'Says who?'

There it was again.

The malicious whisper.

From somewhere deep inside Harry, from the hollow place that never quite closed, a dark and murky feeling rose—black bubbles seething upward. His violent impulses turned toward the small child standing in front of him.

'They slipped the diary into Ginny's bag. Knowing full well it was dangerous enough to throw Hogwarts into chaos.'

For a moment, Draco Malfoy's face blurred and became Lucius Malfoy's—that self-satisfied smirk, that goading confidence, and the hand that had quietly, cowardly, slipped the diary into a little girl's bag. Contemptible. Petty. Stupid.

The snake flicked its tongue again. It was only a sound in his mind, surely—but the hissing seemed to coil around his ears.

'Lucius crossed a line. Your enemies disregard every rule and push wherever they please. Why should you extend them any consideration? If he chose to send his child to a school where Dumbledore is headmaster and Harry Potter walks the halls—he should have been prepared for the same in return.'

Just a child. Simple enough. The Imperius Curse would do. A Confundus Charm wouldn't be bad either. Or perhaps a Memory Charm—reduce him to a blank slate and send him home. Lucius would weep with joy.

Too far?

Ginny had nearly died because of that diary. Her soul had been bound to it, meant to fuel Voldemort's return. The Chamber of Secrets had been opened. Several people had nearly been killed. It was only luck that no one had died—if even one person had, the weight of it might have followed her for the rest of her life.

And yet Lucius, banking on Dumbledore's goodwill and Harry Potter's restraint, had sent his ten-year-old son here without a second thought.

How careless.

"...W-what are you doing?"

Draco must have sensed something. His voice came out small and unsteady.

Harry said nothing. He didn't reach for Draco. He simply stood there, watching the small boy with cold, still eyes.

But Draco couldn't move. Like a mouse before a snake, he was frozen—sweat beading on his skin, heart seized by something invisible.

The snake inside Harry whispered.

'Take the boy by the throat, Harry Potter.'

Harry's hand moved.

'Dumbledore is wrong. Nobility is wasted on enemies this vile and this powerful. Give them the same pain, the same dread. You have earned that right.'

An eye for an eye. A tooth for a tooth. Poison for poison.

The same suffering, returned to those who dealt it.

Harry's fingers curved like a serpent's fangs. He reached out—

And at the last moment, wrenched his hand aside and set it on top of Draco's head.

"Draco."

Harry ruffled his hair. Draco, who had been braced for something terrible, looked up with an expression of pure bewilderment as Harry's hand moved lightly back and forth across his head.

"Go back to your sister. Don't wander around alone from now on. And don't ever think about following someone again. Hogwarts is far more dangerous than you realize."

The trembling stopped. Draco still looked shaken, but after a moment he gave a small nod. Harry exhaled slowly, pressed down on Draco's head, and turned him half a rotation. Draco's body pivoted a full hundred and eighty degrees, following Harry's hand.

"Walk straight back to your dormitory. Go."

Draco began to walk, stiffly, in the direction he'd been pointed. When he'd gone far enough that he must have thought he was safe, he glanced back over his shoulder—then broke into a run and fled.

Harry pushed his hair back and muttered.

"God. That toad of a woman..."

The accumulated toll of Umbridge's extra study sessions, he supposed. He'd told himself he was fine, but having the back of his hand carved open hundreds of times every evening had been eating away at him more than he'd realized. Enough to make him snap at an innocent child.

He'd nearly done it. He'd nearly reached out. Harry pressed down on the surge of aggression still churning through him and murmured.

"I wouldn't have. That's Carina's brother."

One scratch on that boy and Carina would never look at him again. And beyond that—Harry couldn't have hurt a child who reminded him of her. Every time he looked at Draco, he saw a face he missed.

The snake laughed.

'And if that child hadn't been your woman's brother? What would you have done?'

"Shut up."

He was definitely exhausted. Talking to himself out loud—that was proof enough.

Harry, having snapped at no one, turned and made his unsteady way toward the headmaster's office.

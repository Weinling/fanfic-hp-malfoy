After the slug incident, Carina adopted a fiercely hostile attitude toward Gryffindor. Everyone knew why, but no one dared mention it. Even the faintest syllable of the word "slug" was enough to send Carina into a fury that threatened to tear the school apart.

She seemed to harbor a burning, personal hatred for every slug in existence. If a loud pop rang out during Herbology, it was almost certainly Carina Malfoy swinging her wand at some unfortunate gastropod she'd spotted. It had gotten to the point that when all the roosters on school grounds turned up dead, people muttered that if it had been slugs instead, there'd have been no need to look for a culprit at all.

But the incident that followed soon after drove the slug affair entirely from everyone's minds.

The Chamber of Secrets had been opened, and students were beginning to be attacked.

"Hmph. With so many Muggle-borns crawling all over Hogwarts, it's no wonder attacks are happening. I wonder who'll be next."

The bitterness of the slug incident still clung to Carina, and her tongue remained sharp. Crabbe asked in a frightened voice:

"W-we won't be targeted, will we?"

"Don't worry, Crabbe. I've heard the Chamber of Secrets was reserved for the Heir of Slytherin. It won't harm purebloods. None of the people attacked so far have been pureblood, have they?"

"There've only been two victims so far…."

"A Squib's beloved cat, and two Muggle-born idiots. Isn't it obvious? They're targeting the least magical first. Which means we're safe."

Carina answered crisply. But Crabbe still couldn't settle his nerves.

"But we've had plenty of rows with Harry Potter. What if he's holding a grudge and comes after us…?"

"You actually believe that nonsense?"

Carina said, her irritation plain.

After a certain incident at the Dueling Club, people had begun to suspect that Harry Potter might in fact be the Heir of Slytherin. Harry Potter could speak Parseltongue—the language of serpents, the very symbol of Slytherin.

Of course, it was about as plausible as goblins running a charity. How could the Heir of Slytherin end up in Gryffindor?

"I've said it before! Someone like Harry Potter could never be the Heir of Slytherin!"

"Really?"

"Yes! So don't you dare bring up that ridiculous theory about Harry Potter being the Heir of Slytherin in front of me again!"

Speak of the devil—or perhaps Carina had simply been too loud.

Her outburst brought an unwelcome visitor.

"Ahem. Who dares speak the name of the Heir of Slytherin so carelessly?"

Harry Potter strolled over. He had somehow acquired a green banner draped around his shoulders, a crude silver crown perched on his head, and a long cloak trailing behind him along the floor.

The sight of him made Carina's expression crumple with disgust.

"That's the Slytherin banner, Potter! Put it down this instant!"

"Tsk! I'm the Heir of Slytherin—what's wrong with wearing the banner? Old Salsa-sauce Slytherin would be rolling in his grave if he heard you!"

"Get the name right. It's Salazar Slytherin! You could at least show some respect to the founder of Hogwarts!"

"I sent him a personal apology via Parseltongue. He said he forgives me."

Harry replied with a smug grin. Carina ground her teeth.

"Why hasn't Dumbledore hauled you away? There's someone openly claiming to be the Heir of Slytherin standing right here!"

"Oh? Does that mean you're admitting it? That I'm the Heir of Slytherin?"

"As if!"

"Well, it seems they think so too."

Harry gave a casual shrug.

"Of course, if you were to kneel at my feet, press your lips to the back of my hand, and swear your loyalty to the Heir of Slytherin three times over—then I wouldn't complain even if they dragged me off to Azkaban."

"That will never, ever happen!"

Carina's words came out in clipped, furious bursts. She reached out and seized Crabbe by the scruff of his neck as his head began to slowly bow, wrenching it back upright.

"None of you fall for it. If any Slytherin dares bow their head to that creature, I'll curse them so they can never lift it again!"

"Shh. Shhh."

Harry's expression suddenly went rigid, and a strangled, hissing sound escaped his throat. Carina's face froze. What had just come from Harry's mouth was Parseltongue—the language of serpents, said to be the exclusive gift of the Heir of Slytherin.

Every Slytherin student stopped dead. Even Carina went still. Then Harry looked around with an expression of mild puzzlement.

"Hm. I just said 'bow your heads' in Parseltongue and nobody moved. Tsk, tsk. Can you really call yourselves true Slytherins? Parseltongue is to you what Latin is to scholars—a venerable, ancient tongue of prestige."

Harry laughed. The mockery on his face was unmistakable.

Carina ground her teeth and spun on her heel.

"How dare you impersonate a Slytherin. Just you wait. One day you'll pay for this."

"If there's such a thing as 'paying for it' in this world, wouldn't it find whoever opened that Chamber before it finds me? But I'll keep it in mind."

Harry waved a hand and walked away.

Once the Slytherin students had gone, Harry rejoined Ron and Hermione. He said:

"Carina isn't the Heir of Slytherin. She can't speak Parseltongue."

Hermione replied:

"Are you sure?"

"Yes. I asked in Parseltongue whether she'd like to describe what slugs taste like, and she didn't react at all."

"…I see. If she didn't react to that, she definitely didn't understand it."

Harry, apparently stifled by the thing, yanked the banner off and flung it aside. The green Slytherin banner fluttered to the floor and crumpled into a corner of the corridor. He removed the crude crown and unclasped his cloak.

Ron folded the banner with half-hearted effort and said:

"But she clearly knows something. She said it herself when she was vomiting up slugs—'If anyone dies at Hogwarts, it'll all be your fault.'"

Hermione replied, somewhat reluctantly:

"Well… couldn't that have just been said in anger? No one's died yet."

"How can you say that? Colin Creevey, Justin Finch-Fletchley—and even a ghost who'd already died once nearly got finished off for good. Only the most powerful Dark magic could do that. It's a miracle no one's been killed yet!"

Harry listened to the two of them in silence, his expression subdued.

He had his suspicions, but he didn't want to believe that Carina was behind all of this. He knew her softer side. For all her sharp words, if she ever had to watch someone die right in front of her, he was certain it would shatter her.

He believed that—but he wasn't certain. If the Heir of Slytherin wasn't coming from Slytherin, where else would they come from? And the words Carina had let slip still nagged at him.

Harry muttered:
"…I need to get information out of her somehow."

"Professor Sprout is growing Mandrakes. Once the Mandrake Restorative Draught is ready, the Petrified students will recover."

"That's at least two months away. At this rate, Hogwarts will be shut down before then."

"But we don't have any other leads. Except Malfoy."

"…Right. Except Malfoy."

Harry closed his eyes. He wanted to believe in her—but to do that, he needed to be sure.

If any Slytherin student knew the Chamber of Secrets well, it would be Malfoy.

But how was he supposed to get the truth out of her? Ambush her and threaten her? He couldn't do that. Carina's frame was too slight, her face too delicate—there was nowhere to even aim a threat.

"Isn't there some kind of potion we could use?"

"Hold on, let me look… Ah, here. Veritaserum—just three drops can draw out the deepest truths… but it's an advanced-level potion, it takes over a month to mature, and using it without Ministry authorization is illegal. And if it's brewed incorrectly, it can reduce the drinker to a vegetative state."

"That's out."

Harry said flatly. Hermione nodded and flipped rapidly through the pages. Ron, watching from beside her, spotted something and jabbed his finger at it.

"What about Polyjuice Potion? We transform into Crabbe and Goyle and pump Malfoy for information! She treats those two like her personal lackeys—she might let something slip without thinking!"

It wasn't a bad idea. But Harry shook his head, took the book, and leafed through it quickly. After searching for a while, he stopped and pointed to a particular entry.

"Draught of Living Death. Let's use this."

Hermione checked the ingredients and difficulty rating and said:

"This is also an advanced potion. It requires ingredients we can't get our hands on."

"Snape mentioned it in Potions. He said that grinding wormwood-steeped water with powdered asphodel root produces a potion with a similar effect. Drinking it makes the body go rigid and the heart beat so slowly it seems to have stopped. The only problem is that the person remains conscious while they're down."

Hermione gave a reluctant nod at Harry's explanation. She didn't show it, but whenever Harry demonstrated a talent for Potions, a competitive spark quietly ignited in her.

After thinking it over for a moment, Hermione pointed out:

"You're planning to give it to Malfoy? But then you won't be able to hear anything she says. Oh—are you planning to blackmail Mr. Malfoy?"

"No."

Harry set the book down with a sharp thud.

"I'm going to drink it myself."

Perhaps she had always known something like this might happen. The Chamber of Secrets opens. The Heir of Slytherin happens upon Harry Potter wandering the corridors and strikes him down, leaving him cold and still. It was entirely plausible.

But Carina had always forced the thought away. Partly because it gave her less satisfaction than she'd expected, and partly because she couldn't quite picture that insufferably smug Harry Potter going down so easily. The boy who had walked into every conceivable danger and walked back out again—he seemed like the kind of person who simply refused to die.

And yet, here before her eyes, the scene she had only ever imagined had become real.

Harry Potter lay rigid on the floor.

His limbs were bent at grotesque angles. His eyes were wide open, frozen in an expression of terror. His glasses sat askew on his face—the kind of thing she would have mocked mercilessly if she'd seen it while he was upright. Now, with him lying there like a corpse, it was only unsettling.

Unable to find words, Carina opened and closed her mouth several times before she finally managed to speak.

"H-hmph. S-serves you right. Always showing off. Impersonating the Heir of Slytherin. And now look at you."

The words were cold—but Carina's voice trembled so badly they came out sounding almost pitiful.

"You've been struck down too, now. This whole thing will sink even deeper into mystery. Even Dumbledore… won't be able to stay on as Headmaster after this…."

He was only Petrified. Just like Mrs. Norris, Justin Finch-Fletchley, and Colin Creevey—rigid and unable to rise, nothing more.

Carina told herself this as she turned to leave. She needed to find someone passing by, tell them Harry had collapsed, report that another attack had occurred.

But her feet wouldn't move. Even when she turned her head away, the image of Harry lying there lingered at the edge of her vision.

She drew a slow breath, squeezed her eyes shut as if severing something, and wrenched her gaze away from him.

That was when it happened.

"Harry!"

Hermione appeared from around the corner, letting out a scream that sounded almost rehearsed, and came running. Carina's body locked up as though she'd been caught in the act of something terrible.

Meanwhile, Hermione pressed her hand vaguely to Harry's nose and called out in an exaggerated voice:

"Good heavens. He's not breathing and there's no pulse! His pupils aren't responding—Harry must be dead! Where is the culprit? They can't have gone far!"

Ron, lurking behind the corner, pressed a hand to his forehead. She'd said she was good at following a script—but she was reciting it like she'd memorized it word for word and was reading it back aloud.

Regardless, Hermione—having delivered her performance with everything she had—turned her head with a mechanical creak. She spotted Malfoy, covered her mouth with both hands, and cried out:

"At last, you've shown your true colors! I always suspected you, Malfoy, from the very beginning! You attacked Harry. Didn't you?"

"Potter… is dead?"

Carina faltered, her expression blank. Fortunately for the plan, she appeared too shaken to notice how stilted Hermione's acting was.

"How… both his parents were magical. Why would Potter… He hasn't even won a Quidditch match yet…."

"…Er, Malfoy?"

The reaction wasn't what she'd expected. Hermione asked carefully. But Carina kept shaking her head, murmuring, "That can't be right…."

Faced with a situation not covered by the script, Hermione looked frantically toward the corner, at a loss for what to do. But Ron, as it happened, was busy lobbing Dungbombs at someone approaching from the other direction.

Left with no other option, Hermione resorted to the last measure available to her.

"Open!"

At that, the cap on the antidote vial inside Harry's mouth came loose.

Harry sat up and grabbed Carina by the ankle.

"Eek?!"

Carina was too startled to even scream properly. She sucked in a sharp breath and stared down at Harry's green eyes. Focus was slowly returning to them as the potion's effects wore off.

"P-Potter?"

Hic. Carina had even started hiccupping.

Harry rose slowly to his feet. Color was gradually returning to his face, which had been as pale as death.

"Fortunately, I'm not dead. But someone else might be, Carina Malfoy."

He held her gaze, his own reflection caught in her stunned eyes. He kept his grip on her so she couldn't flee, and said:
"Tell me everything. Right now. Everything you know."

"I—hic—I don't know much!"

"You know something. You anticipated that someone might die here. This isn't a game. Lives are at stake."

As the resurrected Harry Potter pressed her, Carina squeezed her eyes shut and let out a strangled cry.

"I really don't know! About the Chamber of Secrets—Father—hic—never told me anything. All I know is that Father sent 'something'—hic—to Hogwarts—"

"What was it?"

Completely overwhelmed by Harry's intensity, Carina answered in a voice barely above a whisper:

"It—it was an object with Dark magic on it. Hic. Things the Ministry was trying to confiscate when the new laws came in… but he didn't want Arthur Weasley seizing them and fining him, so he said he sent them to Dumbledore instead!"

"What kind of object?"

"I don't—hic—know! Father didn't tell me either!"

Her eyes held a desperate, raw sincerity. Harry studied her for a long moment, then gave a slow nod and placed a hand on her shoulder.

"Thank you for telling me the truth. Malfoy."

Harry stood up unsteadily. Then he walked away toward Hermione, calling back to her:

"By the way, your acting was terrible."

"I-it was my first time! I'll do better next time!"

Hermione protested. Their retreating figures disappeared around the corner.

Only then did it dawn on Carina that the whole thing had been their scheme from the start. She let out a sound that was somewhere between a shriek and a wail.

Watching Harry walk ahead of them, Ron lowered his voice and whispered to Hermione:

"…Hermione. I've been thinking, and—I know this sounds strange—but do you think Harry might… Malfoy…."

"You're only just realizing that now?"

Hermione replied, as though the question pained her. Ron's eyes went wide as he glanced around.

"How did you know?"

"It became obvious on its own. Two people who bicker constantly, secretly caring about each other—it's one of the oldest stories there is."

"That's common? I never would have guessed!"

"Girls tend to notice these things. And you're particularly oblivious, even by boys' standards."

Ron muttered:

"It's a bit surprising to hear you of all people claim to be perceptive about interpersonal matters."

"That's exactly what I mean by oblivious!"

Hermione snapped.

"In any case—Malfoy is arrogant and says awful things, but she's innocent in all of this. That's something, at least."

"What's good about it? We didn't find a single real lead."

"At least we know she's not someone who could bring herself to kill anyone. That means we can cheer Harry on without reservation!"

"Cheer him on? We need to catch the culprit before Hogwarts gets shut down."

Up ahead, Harry heard Hermione's words and smiled quietly to himself—wondering what the right moment would be to look back.

Perhaps that had always been true.

Carina Malfoy put on a strong front, but she was also a coward who lacked the courage to watch someone die.

Put another way, she was a decent person who still held to some minimum of conscience.

But the harsh winds of time force choices upon people.

Carina leaned against the sink in the girls' bathroom and prayed.

Please.

I don't want anyone to die. I don't want to kill anyone. But if I don't, he will kill my family.

With Father gone, I am the only one left to protect this family.

To save Mother. To save Draco. I have no choice but to kill someone….

Talk to me. Come to me the way you always do and put me at ease.

I'm so exhausted I can barely hold on. The pain and anguish are so overwhelming I think I might actually break. If no one holds me up. If no one soothes me….

But Harry had lost his godfather. He had lost his family. Unlike Carina, he had been stripped of everything from the very beginning—and then lost even the new family he had found.

The devil had threatened to drag Carina down to hell, but Harry Potter had been submerged in it from the moment he was born.

How utterly selfish.

Carina felt a deep, crushing revulsion toward herself.

She had acted in accordance with the will of the man who had murdered Harry's parents. She had defended that man's ideology to Harry's face. And beyond that, her father had been deeply involved in the murder of Harry's godfather.

After all of that, to ask for comfort.

No—to cry out to be loved still, in spite of everything—that was a vile selfishness. A monstrous arrogance.

Carina Malfoy had no right to it. She was a weakling who had been left behind.

"I'm sorry. I'm so sorry…."

Carina was weak now.

And in the moment she became weak, she understood them—those she had looked down upon.

This was punishment. A stern lesson the world was delivering to her.

If she believed in the right of the strong to rule—then she had to accept Voldemort's rule as well.

And so Voldemort had to be wrong. But he was right. Because Carina was standing here, under his orders, contemplating murder.

Carina wept. For everything she had already done. For everything she was about to do.

On her forearm, inside the tattooed mark, a serpent stared back at her with cold, unblinking eyes.

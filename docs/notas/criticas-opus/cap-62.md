# Crítica externa: capítulo 62, *The List*

*Lectura a ciegas. Continuidad con grep. Nota: 9,5.*

## 1. Veredicto

La escena de la lista es tan fuerte como debe: la mayor victoria pública del libro, construida sobre una página robada, y por eso sabe a podrido. Hopkins levantándose por su cuenta es lo mejor que le pasa a nadie en esta historia. **La escena final de la butaca sí roza**, y hay que retocarla: es el primer sitio del libro donde el lenguaje del deseo se acerca a dos menores. Hay además un choque de horario con Snape y un error de cuenta en la lista.

**Lo que preguntáis:**

- **¿La lista, con la gente levantándose?** Sí. Funciona porque nadie lo ordena y porque Lyra había previsto otra cosa (62:81). *There was a small scraping noise* es el mejor giro del capítulo. Harry se levanta sin decir nada, y *Thomas, D.* y el niño al que hay que ayudar a pasar el banco lo hacen coral.
- **¿Hopkins tiene decisión propia?** Sí, y es la clave: se levanta, dice *Sorry*, lee con la izquierda. *I’d like to be on it, please. It’s just been left off* funciona porque es educado y porque tiene el pulgar en el borde roto.
- **¿La mirada es ambigua?** Sí, pero 62:123 explica de más: cuanto menos se justifique Lyra, mejor. Con dos frases menos queda perfecta (corrección 6).
- **¿Se entiende por qué lo hace?** Sí, sin decirlo: el 61 lo explica todo. *There was no reason why anybody should have to read about Miss M.* es lo justo: es penitencia y es cobardía a la vez.
- **Crabbe.** Sigue siendo un límite. *It’s not going to give him his hand back* y *This isn’t anything* no perdonan nada. 62:59 abre además una inquietud muy buena.
- **«But you’re a good girl».** Pesa muchísimo (50:195), y *Yes, Professor*, con uno de los que están de pie girando la cabeza, es el mejor final de escena del tramo.
- **«Mine», «Yes».** La idea es excelente y sube lo que se pudre: quien dijo *Never own a Malfoy* dice que sí. *It was true. That was the thing that was the matter with it.* Pero la escritura se apoya en el deseo, y no debe.
- **La felicidad contra el papel.** Se lee muy bien: *She had supposed there would be some sort of arrangement.*

**¿Roza algo? Sí.** Sin ser sexual, la suma de 62:157-167 sí lo es en el registro:

- *Nobody in her life had ever wanted her as much as that* y *she liked it more than she had ever liked anything*: en inglés, *wanted her* tiene el sentido que tiene.
- *It went through her from the top of her head to the soles of her feet*.
- *He said it into her hair, very low… His hands had closed.*
- *She wanted it so much.*

Y hay otro en 62:17: **“Show me your wrist action”** es un doble sentido en inglés, y seguido de *She made him do it twice* peor. El lector adulto lo oirá. Fuera.

El impulso de 62:141 (la levanta en brazos y dan vueltas) es juego y puede quedarse. Lo que no debe quedarse es ese vocabulario. La podredumbre debe ser de **posesión y miedo**, no de deseo: que a él le dejen de temblar las manos da mucho más miedo (corrección 1).

## 2. Incoherencias

**Fechas de calendario:** correctas. Lunes 18 (*the day after the crossword*), sábado 23, martes 26, jueves 28. Pascua cae el 7 de abril de 1996, así que *the last day of term before Easter* es verosímil. Los *four and a half months* desde el 10 de noviembre cuadran.

**Prohibido, variante de «a breath she had not been aware of holding»:** 62:103 (*found that she had stopped breathing*) y 62:121 (*She heard somebody breathe out, and it was herself*). Esta última es casi igual que 60:89, y 56:79 dice lo mismo que la primera. Fuera las dos.

## 4. Rastro de IA

Muy poco. El inicio con *She/He* sigue alto: en el 61 era un recurso, y aquí empieza a ser costumbre, sobre todo en 62:33 y 62:49. Ojo en el 63. Ecos bien cobrados: *Somebody had to*, *good girl*, el nudo de la corbata *in full view of everybody* (52). Una leve duda en 62:173: *It might have been a piece of parchment* no añade nada.

## 5. Correcciones listas para aplicar

**1. La butaca, sin vocabulario de deseo.**

- 62:157: “She wanted it so much. She wanted to go on being what he was looking at.” → “She wanted to go on being what he was looking at.”
- 62:161: “He said it into her hair, very low.” → “He said it to the top of her head, very low.”
- 62:161: “His hands had closed. There was something at the bottom of his voice that she had heard once, on a landing, and it did not go with the laughing. It went through her from the top of her head to the soles of her feet.” →

> His hands had stopped shaking. There was something at the bottom of his voice that she had heard once, on a landing, and it did not go with the laughing. It frightened her. She noted that, and she noted that she did not mind.

- 62:167: “Nobody in her life had ever wanted her as much as that; and she sat there in the firelight, in the most comfortable chair in the world, and she liked it more than she had ever liked anything.” →

> Nobody in her life had ever been so sure of her as that; and she sat there in the firelight, in the most comfortable chair in the world, with a page in her pocket, and she let him be.

**2. El doble sentido.** 62:17: “Show me your wrist action,” said Potter. → “Fetch the tea caddy,” said Potter.

**3. El jueves.** 62:141: “It was a quarter past eight, on the last night of term, it was not on any timetable, and he took her round the middle” →

> It was a quarter past ten, on the last night of term. She had come straight up from Professor Snape, who had said nothing whatever about the morning, and had gone through her head twice as hard as usual. It was not on any timetable; and he took her round the middle

**4. La cuenta.** 62:107: “There were fourteen.” → “There were thirteen.”

**5. Las fechas.**

- 62:27: “*Potter, H. Since 2nd Sept.*” → “*Potter, H. Since 6th Sept.*”
- 62:99: “Since the second of September.” → “Since the sixth of September.”
- 62:171: “her father wrote, on the Saturday,” → “her father wrote, by return,”.

**6. La mirada, con menos defensa.** 62:123: →

> She sat with her hands in her lap. He could not know. He could not possibly know. It was in her pocket at this moment. He had only looked. Anybody might look.

**7. Respirar.**

- 62:103: cortar “Lyra found that she had stopped breathing.”
- 62:121: cortar “She heard somebody breathe out, and it was herself.”

**8. Menudencias.**

- 62:41: “in the way that one can get used to a stone in the shoe, until it comes through the stocking” → “in the way that one can get used to a cold room”.
- 62:133, tras “one of them had turned his head.”: “A monocle had turned as well.”

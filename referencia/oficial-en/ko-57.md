Draco had witnessed something he simply could not believe.

"My sister... is being blackmailed."

A clandestine meeting between Carina Malfoy and Harry Potter. The two had exchanged words in a rather sharp tone, and then—for reasons he couldn't fathom—had suddenly seized each other's faces and kissed. For Draco, hidden away in the luggage compartment, it had been a sight shocking enough to stop his heart.

Draco was young, but as befitted a child of a pureblood family, he knew a great many things.

Harry Potter was the Malfoy family's sworn enemy. Kissing was something mothers and fathers did. And the two of them had kissed.

"My sister and Harry Potter... becoming like Mum and Dad?"

What utter nonsense. Draco shook his head vigorously. Sworn enemies were people who fought each other; married couples were people who loved each other. This was a contradiction.

He felt a small flicker of pride at having recalled such a difficult word as contradiction, then shook his head again.

"It must be blackmail. There's no other explanation!"

Draco didn't particularly like his sister.

The age gap between them was barely anything—only four years—yet she treated him like a small child. It also irritated him that she would receive some precious gift and then lend it to Draco as though she were doing him a great favour. And through it all, she was forever putting on airs.

Once he got to Hogwarts, he would crush her utterly—academically, on the Quidditch pitch, any way he could—and wring a full surrender from her. Draco had sworn this to himself long before he ever set foot in the school.

And yet, however much she annoyed him, she was his sister—a Malfoy by name.

If he helped her now, she'd owe him a debt. She might stop looking down on him. She might even be impressed, might see him in a new light.

With a faint swell of self-satisfaction—and just the smallest, most reluctant thread of concern for his sister—Draco made his way toward the Great Hall.

A peculiar undercurrent had settled over Hogwarts' Great Hall.

Several students clutched copies of The Quibbler to their chests, whispering to one another as though the magazine contained some closely guarded secret. Others seemed far more curious about whatever was tucked inside their classmates' robes than about the Sorting ceremony that was about to begin.

The Slytherins, meanwhile, stood apart from this atmosphere entirely. Only a handful of them had read The Quibbler, and those who had knew perfectly well that the contents were rubbish—yet for reasons of their own, they couldn't say so aloud, and their faces were tight with the effort of keeping quiet.

Ginny Weasley slid into the seat beside Harry, looking rather pleased with herself.

"See? I told you people would read it if it was entertaining enough. Not bad, right?"

Over the summer, Harry had done everything he could to get the truth out. But the Daily Prophet operated squarely under the Ministry's thumb, running one unflattering story about Dumbledore after another. And Harry, a minor who couldn't Apparate, had precious little room to manoeuvre.

It was Ginny who had suggested a particular magazine as a means of spreading the truth.

That magazine was The Quibbler.

It was a gossip rag that ran, without a hint of irony, articles claiming that Sirius Black was the lead vocalist of a famous band. Even Harry, who could usually manage a smile at most things, had gone stone-faced the first time he'd seen it.

He had read through the magazine carefully, made Sirius actually sing something, and arrived at the firm conclusion that The Quibbler was rubbish that didn't bother to verify a single fact. If Ginny hadn't been watching, he might well have tossed it into the fireplace as a meal for an Ashwinder.

But Ginny had been relentless.

"It's not the most rigorous publication, I'll grant you that. But no matter how absurd the content—if knowing something makes you feel a little bit special, people will believe it."

And this was the result.

The article had run in a thoroughly unreliable magazine, yet its reach had been remarkable. Harry wasn't even sure whether running the same piece in the Daily Prophet would have produced a similar effect.

Those who wanted to believe, believed. Those who didn't, didn't. That was partly down to The Quibbler's reputation for printing nonsense in the first place.

Either way, it had planted a seed of doubt in people's minds, exactly as intended—so Harry couldn't complain too loudly.

"I feel rather like I've become Professor Trelawney. It's not a pleasant sensation."

He'd felt this way before. In Divination, watching students hang on every word of Trelawney's absurd pronouncements—that was precisely the feeling. How many hours had he suffered through her rambling about Venus this and Mars that, week after week?

If genuine prophecies hadn't occasionally surfaced amid all the fabricated ones, Harry might have stormed out like Hermione. Remarkably, it was Trelawney—intermittent prophet that she was—who kept him sitting through a class he otherwise despised.
Harry muttered with dry contempt.

"Then again, if there are fools out there who worship Professor Trelawney, I suppose this was inevitable."

Ginny chose her words carefully in defence of those 'fools.'

"Lavender and Parvati aren't stupid, exactly. They're just... looking for something interesting. The idea of seeing the future is exciting."

"The moment they actually believe they're seeing the future, that says it all."

Ginny laughed, as though he'd said something genuinely funny.

"Anyway—thank you, Harry."

"Right. Thank you, Ginny. It was all down to you."

"Hehe."

Ginny beamed. It was a warm, unguarded smile—the sort that would have made anyone who knew her well do a double take.

For instance, Ron Weasley, who had lived under the same roof with her his entire life.

Ron, seated beside her, rubbed his eyes once and then fixed her with an expression of profound distaste.

"You were like this at home, and now you're doing it here too? You did one thing. Stop milking it."

"Stay out of it, Ron. Nobody asked you."

Ginny cut him off. The look she turned on him was equal parts irritation and contempt.

"You didn't do anything, did you? You just ate and took up space all holiday. I did far more than you."

"You just introduced Harry to Luna Lovegood!"

"And isn't that something?"

Ginny shot back, utterly unabashed.

"If it weren't for me, Harry wouldn't have known Luna existed. You'd have spent the whole time playing Wizard's Chess with him and wasting everyone's time."

"By that logic, you've got nothing on me either."

"How so?"

"You only know Harry because I was in the same compartment as him on the train. If it weren't for me, would you have ever said a single word to him?"

Ron threw it out flatly, and Ginny replied as though the answer were obvious.

"We're both in Gryffindor. Of course I would have spoken to him eventually."

"Right. Same Gryffindor for four years, and you spent the whole time staring at him without managing a single word. Well done."

At Ron's mutter, Ginny's face flushed as red as her hair. As the two of them looked ready to come to blows, Harry stepped in quickly.

"Ron. You really ought to be kinder to your family."

"And you're far too soft on Ginny, Harry. Keep indulging her and she'll only get worse."

"I'm not being soft. Ginny is your little sister, but I think of her as a little sister too. And when you're that harsh to her, of course she's going to come looking for praise from me."

Harry's measured tone made something shift in Ron's expression. He opened his mouth, closed it, then let out a long breath and turned to Ginny.

"Sorry, Ginny. Want some chocolate?"

"...No, thanks."

The words came out quieter than before, the brightness gone from her voice.
Just then, Harry felt the distinct sensation of being watched. He glanced around, and caught a flash of blond hair on the first-years' side—a boy turning his head away sharply.

Harry knew at once that he was Carina's younger brother. The brilliant platinum hair was the same shade as Carina's, and there was something in the boy's self-assured smile that carried an echo of Carina as a child.

"His name was... Draco, wasn't it?"

A faint, bitter smile touched Harry's lips.

Harry and Carina were now enemies in every sense of the word. Whatever feelings existed between them, however welcome that long-awaited kiss had been—they would each do their utmost to make things difficult for the other. That was simply the position they were in.

Which was why Harry wanted everything finished before he left Hogwarts. He wanted to put an end to the long, tangled war with Voldemort. Within Hogwarts, their rivalry amounted to little more than skirmishing—but outside these walls, it would likely become something else entirely.

Enough of that. Harry wrenched himself out of his thoughts and turned to look at the staff table.

And frowned.

"What is that woman doing here?"

"That woman?"

Hermione followed his gaze toward the staff table. She spotted the unfamiliar face among the returning professors and pointed.

"Her? A stranger arriving at this particular moment would be... the new Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher, as usual, wouldn't it?"

"I know that. What I mean is—why is she the one who's been appointed to Hogwarts?"

Harry's hand tightened into a fist. Hermione studied the woman more carefully, searching for whatever had set Harry off. She was squat and broad, with tightly curled short hair the colour of a brown mouse's coat, and a headband in a shade of pink so aggressively cheerful it was almost painful to look at. She had clearly chosen it to match her outfit, but no amount of generosity could describe the result as anything other than the fashion sense of a woman who had given up on the world.

A pale, toad-like face. Protuberant eyes. A complexion mapped with fine lines. Hermione didn't recognise her—but Harry did.

One of Minister Fudge's aides. Dolores Umbridge. At his hearing, she had been desperate to see him convicted by any means necessary. An enemy, without question.

"Her name is Umbridge. She was at my hearing. But she's supposed to be one of Fudge's aides—the fact that she's here now means—"

Childish as it was, Fudge was apparently intent on interfering directly with Hogwarts. Using Dolores Umbridge as his advance guard.

Hermione, having heard Harry out, murmured in disbelief.

"How can he do that? Hogwarts operates under its own rules. He couldn't possibly do this without changing the law first!"

"Then he must have changed the law. He's the Minister of Magic."

"Surely even the Minister wouldn't stoop to something this petty—sending someone into a school—"

"He seems entirely capable of it. This is the man who dragged a minor into a full courtroom over underage magic use. Wouldn't you say?"

Ron said it as though it were the most obvious thing in the world. Hermione couldn't find a rebuttal, and simply repeated the word outrageous under her breath.

The Sorting concluded and the feast began. Harry reached for a generous drumstick sitting right in front of him.

And felt that gaze again.

He turned toward it. Draco, seated near Carina, snapped his head away.

It wasn't his imagination. Draco had been watching him.

"...What is this?"

Harry tilted his head. No matter how he thought about it, there was no reason for Draco to be staring at him. So why did he keep doing it?

If it were Carina, at least there would be a reason. Though Carina, proud as she was, would never be the first to look his way.

Even now, she sat with her back to him, not sparing him so much as a glance. But her younger brother seemed to have taken quite an interest in Harry.

"I'd rather not involve her family if I can help it..."

But if the other side made the first move—that was a different matter, wasn't it? Harry laced his fingers together and smiled.

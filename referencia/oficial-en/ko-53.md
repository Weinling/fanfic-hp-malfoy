After that day, Malfoy gritted her teeth and threw herself into besting Harry at every turn. She wasn't the type to obsess over grades, but she was determined to stay ahead of him—answering the professor's questions before he could, then looking down at him with a triumphant smile.

At first, Harry had regarded Malfoy's transformation with something close to approval. Then, gradually, his own competitive streak caught fire.

Partly it was the sheer irritation of being looked down upon. Partly it was the simple logic that having ignited this rivalry, he couldn't afford to fall behind. Harry began throwing himself into answering questions with equal ferocity.

Before long, the two of them were locked in a silent contest over who could rack up more correct answers. The professors were thoroughly delighted by their students' newfound enthusiasm.

It should be noted, however, that regardless of what either of them did, the person who answered the most questions remained Hermione Granger.

In any case, Harry distinguished himself in Potions and Defence Against the Dark Arts, while Malfoy pulled ahead in Charms and Herbology—subjects that rewarded accumulated knowledge over raw instinct. Both of them cared about this more than they would ever admit. Harry found it galling enough to drag Hermione into forming a study group to shore up his weaker subjects.

Malfoy, for her part, was quietly furious that she lagged behind Harry in Defence and Potions—subjects where talent counted for more than preparation, and where no amount of prior study could fully close the gap.

"Ugh. Losing to him, of all people. It's a disgrace to Slytherin."

Carina Malfoy muttered the words as she turned the pages of her Potions textbook.

The nature of Potions was unforgiving: no matter how thoroughly you prepared, a single mistake in execution or a misremembered sequence could ruin the result entirely. Even a technically flawless attempt could be undone by the condition of the ingredients on any given day.

Harry had an instinctive feel for it. If one ingredient was missing, he could improvise with another and still produce something that worked. Snape despised that kind of improvisation—but Malfoy understood that it wasn't a rejection of Potions as a discipline. Within that subject, Harry Potter was genuinely gifted.

That was why she had volunteered herself for extra study sessions.

"I'm done."

Goyle, who had been studying beside her, closed her book.

"Studying now won't change the textbook during class. Doing more is pointless. I'm stopping here."

Malfoy didn't particularly want to study alone either. That was why she'd dragged Crabbe and Goyle into it—but neither of them had ever had any natural inclination toward studying, and keeping them at it required constant pressure.

"Goyle, just bear with it a little longer! Can you really stand watching Harry Potter act so smug?"

Goyle replied without hesitation.

"I can, actually."

"How? It's Professor Snape's class—*our* Head of House! Are you going to just let Gryffindor walk all over us?"

Goyle looked at her as though the question were faintly absurd.

"You don't want Gryffindor getting ahead? How have you been tolerating Hermione Granger all this time, then?"

"Ugh."

"That's not really why you're studying, is it?"

Malfoy had no answer for that. She stared down at her book for a moment, then confessed.

"I just—I can't stand Harry Potter!"

"We know. We feel the same way."

Goyle nodded. Beside her, Crabbe, who had been clutching her head in misery, muttered, "Eh, we? Am I included in that?"

Goyle ignored her and pressed on.

"What I want to know is why you're trying to beat him academically. Since when do we compete on those terms?"

Saying it that way made them sound like villains—but Malfoy couldn't entirely disagree.

Slytherins were, broadly speaking, capable and accomplished across many areas—in Malfoy's personal estimation, at least—but the idea of crushing someone through sheer academic performance was more of a Ravenclaw ambition. That wasn't how Slytherin operated.

The reason Carina Malfoy was studying had nothing to do with that. It was simpler, and more absolute: Carina Malfoy could not endure losing.

"I'm not trying to beat him academically. I just refuse to lose to him in anything. I have no intention of falling behind that boy in any area whatsoever."

Malfoy declared it with the gravity of a vow. Crabbe looked up at her with wide, bright eyes.

"Is this what they call a rivalry?"

"A rivalry? With him?"

She denied it immediately—but then paused to consider. The desire to beat him in everything. The need to see him brought to his knees before her. What else would you call it?

Crabbe spoke with an energy entirely absent from her approach to studying.

"What on earth happened between you two? Something definitely changed after the disciplinary hearing. You and Potter both seem... I don't know, it's almost... a healthy competition? Like something worth watching?"

Goyle cut her off flatly.
"Don't be ridiculous. There's no such thing as a healthy competition, Crabbe. There's only whether you knock the other person down or get knocked down yourself."

"But still... it's better than before, isn't it...?"

Listening to them, Malfoy arrived at a conclusion.

It certainly wasn't a healthy competition. She would fight tooth and nail before she accepted defeat.

But if it was Harry Potter—someone who matched her in at least a few areas—then perhaps the word rival wasn't entirely unwarranted.

"Fine."

Malfoy shut her book with a decisive snap.

"I'll acknowledge Harry Potter as a rival. Though naturally, his role is to be the one who loses."

Even words alone have a way of changing things. The moment Malfoy spoke that word aloud, something between them was settled.

They were rivals now.

And this would not end until one of them conceded defeat.

The day after the rivalry was declared.

Malfoy had just borrowed a book and was heading back to class when she spotted Harry making his way somewhere with quiet purpose. On a sudden impulse, she followed him.

The thought that this might constitute stalking crossed her mind briefly—then she dismissed it.

Know your enemy, know yourself, and you need not fear the result of a hundred battles. This is reconnaissance, nothing more.

Harry glanced around, then slipped through a door that stood slightly ajar. Malfoy crept after him and pressed herself against the wall beside it.

She put her ear to the door.

Silence.

She eased the door open and peered inside.

An empty room. Nothing in it but a large mirror—and Harry Potter.

He was standing before the mirror, staring into it with complete absorption.

Is he looking at his own face? Or is there some kind of enchantment on it?

Malfoy stretched her neck to get a better view.

Then she realized, abruptly, that the mirror was a mirror—because the moment her reflection appeared in it, Harry turned around.

Caught. Malfoy froze.

"What—what is it?"

Fortunately, Harry was approximately twice as flustered as she was.

He flailed his arms in a frantic attempt to block her view of the mirror—apparently too rattled to register that the mirror was far too large to be covered by two outstretched arms.

She had him cornered. Malfoy felt the warm flush of victory and stepped into the room with her chin raised.

"What are you doing in this gloomy room, staring at a mirror, Potter?"

"How did you—"

Harry turned to face her, putting his back to the mirror.

"Don't tell me. You followed me."

"I did not follow you. I was passing by and happened to notice. And besides—am I not allowed to be here?"

Malfoy lied without a flicker of shame.

She took a step sideways to see around him. Harry took an identical step in the same direction, blocking her view.

She moved right. He moved right.

Malfoy's eyes narrowed with amusement.

"What exactly are you looking at that you're so desperate to hide?"

"It's none of your concern."
"Hmm. Then you won't mind stepping aside."

"I mind."

Whatever had flustered Harry Potter this thoroughly had to be something significant. A weakness, certainly. Something worth knowing.

Malfoy advanced with the anticipation of someone tearing open a Christmas present.

"Move, Potter."

"Absolutely not."

"You really think you can stop me?"

She feinted left. The instant Harry moved to match her, she grabbed his robes and yanked him sideways.

He stumbled, thrown off balance, and the mirror was exposed.

"No—!"

"Yes."

Malfoy stepped past him and looked into the glass.

What she saw stopped her cold.

In the mirror, Harry Potter was kneeling before her. He was kneeling—head bowed, in an attitude of reverence. Malfoy's voice rang out before she could stop it.

"*Yes!* That's exactly where you belong, Potter!"

She turned, triumphant—and found the real Harry standing awkwardly in the middle of the room, doing nothing of the sort.

Malfoy blinked.

"...Oh."

She looked back at the mirror.

Everyone was looking at her. She stood at the centre of it all, radiant and singular, more luminous than anyone around her. Even the professors had their hands pressed to their chests in a gesture of deference.

And there, at the very heart of the scene, Harry Potter remained on his knees before her.

But the Potter in the mirror was slightly different from the one in reality. His hair was neater, his robes more composed—like a knight paying homage to his queen.

Malfoy's certainty began to waver.

"What... what is this?"

"You're seeing something different from what I saw."

Harry's voice carried unmistakable relief.

Malfoy looked up. Above the mirror's frame, an inscription ran in ornate lettering. Read in reverse, it resolved into a single sentence: I show not your face but your heart's desire.

This was the Mirror of Erised.

The image of Harry kneeling before her—that was her desire.

"Did I appear in your mirror too?"

Harry asked. Malfoy answered without thinking.

"...Yes."

Harry smiled.

And then it hit her—the full weight of the exchange. Did I appear in your mirror? And her answer: yes.

That makes it sound as though Harry Potter is my heart's desire.

The realization arrived like a cold splash of water. Something surged up in her—a feeling like anger, like the urge to grab someone and shake them, like the desperate need to look anywhere but at the person standing in front of her.

Malfoy would never have admitted it, but that feeling had a name.

It was called embarrassment.

"I—don't misunderstand me! Obviously you appeared in the role of kneeling before me. That's all it means!"

Malfoy snapped, her face burning.

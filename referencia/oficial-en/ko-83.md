It was pure chance.

An open cabinet—beyond where the Butterbeer had been—a small jar sat in plain sight. The kind Harry had seen countless times while rummaging through Potions ingredients. He recognized it immediately, summoned it with a quick charm, pried off the lid, and drew it out.

"…A Bezoar."

A small, shriveled stone drawn from a goat's stomach. An antidote capable of saving a life from the most lethal of poisons.

Harry pried open Carina's mouth, pulled back her tongue, and forced the Bezoar deep into her throat.

He exhaled sharply and stared at Carina's ashen face. She was still breathing—shallow, but continuous.

The instinct that had struck him out of nowhere had worked. He'd thought it through during Potions class: if the poison Carina had brought was a compound mixture like the ones used in lessons, then forcing a Bezoar down her throat might be more effective than trying to brew individual antidotes for each component.

As it turned out, he'd been right. Carina was still in critical condition, but she was alive. Harry let out a long, slow breath.

"Thank god…."

What if he hadn't been paying attention in Potions? What if the idea of using a Bezoar had never occurred to him? What if there hadn't been one in this room at all? He would have had to watch Carina die, helpless. He would have held her in his arms and done nothing but scream until her last breath left her, until the candle of her life burned down to nothing.

The thought alone was unbearable. Harry pressed his forehead against hers. Her skin was cold with sweat, but beneath it he could feel a faint warmth, and her thin, shallow breaths tickled against his face.

Carina was still alive. Harry decided that was enough.

"…Of all the places to try dying—right in front of me? I'll never forgive you, Carina."

He wouldn't. Not ever. But the debt she owed him—that would wait until she was well again, until all of this was over. He would collect it. He absolutely would.

Harry gathered her up in his arms. She was as light as one of the battered old Cleansweeps stacked in the broom shed. He carried her to the bed, laid her down, and pulled the blankets over her twice.

His shirt was soaked through with sweat, clinging to his back. He shrugged off the suffocating dress robes and dropped them on the floor.

Even if the worst had passed, Carina's condition was still critical. She needed complete rest and professional treatment. Harry stepped out of the Room of Requirement, pressing a hand to his chest as the tension slowly began to drain.

Truly—what would have happened if the Bezoar hadn't been in this room? He thought again how fortunate it was, and then—

A sudden wrongness.

"Why was there… a Bezoar?"

Why would a Bezoar be in a room meant for just the two of them? It was a reasonable question, but Harry set it aside for now. Carina's treatment came first.

He folded the question neatly and tucked it away, then reached for the door leading out.

The moment he opened it—

"Is it done?"

A cold, hollow voice. A hook-nosed man in a black cloak stood waiting at the door as though he'd been there all along.

Harry's eyes went wide.

"You—how did you get here?"

Snape ignored him and strode into the room. One sweeping glance, and he seemed to understand everything. His gaze moved between the Bezoar rolling on the floor and Carina on the bed.

Harry followed him in slowly. Snape spoke without looking at him.

"A Bezoar. Yes. It has antidotal properties against several poisons. Fortunately, you found the same answer she did."

"…Professor. Did you know a Bezoar would work as an antidote to Professor Slughorn's poison?"

"I did. The curriculum hasn't changed since I was taught it."

The tone of someone who had anticipated all of this. The composed expression of a man whose own House student lay collapsed before him. And above all—the fact that he had been standing outside the Room of Requirement, waiting for Harry to emerge.

Something inside Harry snapped.

A Bezoar—a Potions ingredient—had no business being in a room conjured for two people alone. Someone had placed it there deliberately.

Why? So that if someone drank poison, they could be saved.

Then who had placed it? And how had they known Carina would drink poison herself? Harry's questions stopped there—but only for a moment.

"…No. I need to ask a different question."

With Snape's arrival, every piece fell into place.

Why had Carina drunk the poison herself?

And why had Snape been waiting outside the Room of Requirement as though he'd known exactly when to appear?

It was a very, very simple problem. So simple it barely warranted deliberation.

"…Was it you, Professor?"

Something darker than death rolled off him in waves. A despair as thick and black as molten tar. He had bitten his lip so hard that blood was running from the corner of his mouth, soaking into his collar—but Harry didn't notice. He gripped his wand.

His voice was cold enough to freeze hard liquor.

"I asked you a question. Professor Snape. Was it you?"

"There's something you should confirm first."

Snape gestured to the glass vial on the table. Carina's memories—the ones she had staked her life on delivering—swirled inside it in a small, luminous spiral.

Harry's killing intent turned toward it in an instant. The force of his will alone was magic. The table cracked with a sharp sound. A fracture began at the edge and snaked inward like a serpent, reaching all the way to just beneath the vial. One more moment and it would devour the glass entirely, shatter it to pieces.

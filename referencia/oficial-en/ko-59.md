Hagrid had vanished from Hogwarts.

When a three-metre giant who had always been there to greet students simply disappeared, it couldn't go unnoticed. Many students wondered where he'd gone—whether they'd been fond of him or not.

Harry, of course, knew exactly where Hagrid was.

Over the summer, Hagrid had left for Europe on Dumbledore's orders, tasked with making contact with the giants. The aim was to negotiate a peace of sorts—to keep them from entering into an alliance with Voldemort.

Knowing this, Harry wasn't surprised when Professor Grubbly-Plank took over Care of Magical Creatures in Hagrid's absence.

"Today's lesson is on Bowtruckles. They live exclusively in trees whose wood is used for wand-making. They are highly magical and significant creatures, and you'll need to learn how to interact with them properly."

"Harry, pay attention! These things come up constantly on O.W.L.s—they're important!"

But the more Professor Grubbly-Plank conducted proper lessons—and the better the reviews she received—the harder it became not to feel a pang of something like regret.

"Why did Hagrid spend all of last year teaching us about Blast-Ended Skrewts instead of Bowtruckles and other creatures?"

"He simply thought we'd enjoy them. And we never complained to his face because we didn't want to hurt his feelings."

Hermione replied with characteristic composure. Harry muttered, his expression faintly mournful.

"If Blast-Ended Skrewts had come up on the O.W.L.s, at least you would've enjoyed studying them, Hermione."

"I very much doubt that. I'm genuinely fond of Hagrid, but that doesn't mean I want his favourite creatures appearing on our exams."

Hermione sketched a Bowtruckle on her parchment as efficiently as possible, then added footnotes and meticulously catalogued its characteristics. The small illustration paired with twice as much written description made it look like a page from a textbook.

"Harry—never mind that. What are you going to do about Malfoy?"

Hermione said it as though in passing. Harry turned to look at Carina with an expression even more subdued than before.

Surrounded by her Slytherin companions, Carina was currently facing down Ron. She wore a smile of effortless superiority, her tone imperious as she spoke down to him.

"I heard from my father. Minister Fudge intends to have unqualified professors removed from Hogwarts. The first to go will be—well, it's rather obvious, isn't it?"

"Shut up! Hagrid is not unqualified!"

"Proving that would require a fair evaluation. But until last year, he was bringing dangerous hybrid creatures into class. He has no concept of risk. If he got his hands on a Basilisk egg, he'd probably coddle it until it hatched and then use it for a lesson."

The argument was sound, and the facts were ones even Ron couldn't flatly deny. Even the Gryffindors who were broadly sympathetic to Hagrid found themselves without a ready answer.

Having pressed her point home, Carina added a more pointed barb beneath the surface.

"Perhaps it's the giant blood. His thinking simply isn't like that of ordinary people. Shouldn't someone like that be kept separate from the rest?"

"I said shut up!"

Ron snarled. Professor Grubbly-Plank came striding over.

"Have you all finished your observations of the Bowtruckles? Hurry along! They're patient creatures, but waste their time and make them uncomfortable, and they'll remember it forever!"

Carina and the Slytherins turned away with smiles of quiet satisfaction. Pansy Parkinson fell into step behind Carina, tossing a parting sneer at the still-fuming Ron.

Hermione turned to Harry.

"You ought to decide where you stand, Harry."

As she said it, her gaze moved between Harry and Carina with something like sympathy for both.

"Malfoy seems to have already made her choice."

Harry said nothing.

Just then, the door to the nearby greenhouse swung open and a line of fourth-years filed out, Ginny among them. She spotted Harry and came running over with a bright smile.

"Harry!"
"Ginny."

Harry smiled and greeted her warmly. Ginny approached with her usual easy familiarity, but as she drew close, her eyes dropped slightly and her voice lowered.

"Harry. It was her. She reported The Quibbler to the Ministry—said they were spreading false information."

"Her?"

Ginny glanced back. Luna was walking toward them, shoulders slumped, a smear of dirt on her nose.

Luna was never exactly exuberant, but right now she looked hollowed out. The eyes that usually shone with some peculiar, wandering curiosity were heavy with worry.

Ginny bit her lip and turned back to Harry.

"The Ministry declared The Quibbler a harmful publication and shut it down! Everything we did over the summer—gone. And Luna's father is out of work. All because of her!"

Harry felt the weight of responsibility settle over him. The Quibbler's circulation had grown, yes—but Xenophilius had run the article as a favour to Harry, and now, because the other side had come down hard, he'd been forced to close up shop.

"That's Umbridge, then."

"What? Umbridge? Harry, do you actually believe that?"

Ginny let out a short, scornful laugh.

"Do you think Professor Umbridge even knew The Quibbler existed? Someone tipped her off—that's what happened. Malfoy. It was Malfoy."

It was a bitter observation, and an accurate one. The only person who would have taken the contents of The Quibbler seriously enough to report it to Umbridge—that would have been the Slytherins. Carina.

This wasn't just words anymore. It was a deliberate act of hostility.

"I'm sorry. I didn't think the Ministry would actually send someone directly to Hogwarts. This is on me. I was careless—"

"Stop deflecting! How is any of this your fault? There's a clear culprit here!"

Ginny glared at Carina with burning eyes. Carina, mid-conversation with her housemates, seemed to sense the look and turned her head.

She took in Harry and Ginny, gave a small, dismissive laugh, and returned to her conversation—as though the two of them were beneath her notice entirely.

"Don't get stuck in the past. For your sake too—if they're going to come at us hard, we have to come back harder."

Harry stayed quiet. He'd seen it coming, and she'd given him fair warning before it arrived—but that didn't make the reality of it any easier to accept.

Then Luna, who had been standing still, walked up to Harry and began speaking without preamble.

"I believe you. The Ministry is trying to hide something important. Stopping my father from publishing, Umbridge constantly trying to punish you—it's all because they're afraid the truth will come out."

Luna said it plainly, holding Harry's gaze. The radish-shaped earrings at her ears swayed gently.

It might have looked absurd, but Harry couldn't bring himself to smile. The trust in Luna's eyes was too earnest, too heavy for that.

"But the truth always surfaces eventually. Just like the Blibbering Humdinger and the Crumple-Horned Snorkack! So one day, everyone will believe your story too."

The Blibbering Humdinger and the Crumple-Horned Snorkack almost certainly didn't exist—but Harry found himself moved, just slightly, by the fact that someone was willing to believe in him at all.

And because of that—because of exactly that—the knowledge that he was supposed to treat Carina as an enemy was something he couldn't quite bring himself to accept.

He had a detention with Umbridge at five o'clock, so Harry went straight to the Great Hall without even stopping at the Gryffindor dormitory to drop off his bag. He was already in a foul mood; the last thing he needed was to face her on an empty stomach.

But the moment he stepped through the entrance to the Great Hall, Angelina Johnson's furious voice cut across the noise.

"Oi, Potter!"

"Women keep seeking me out today. Must be my golden era."

Harry muttered tiredly and turned to look at her. Angelina came striding toward him, hair pulled back in a single plait, expression thunderous.

"Don't try to be clever, Potter! I've got a mountain of things to say to you!"
She marched up and jabbed a finger into his chest.

"Detention at five on Friday? What were you thinking?"

"What about it?"

"Don't tell me you forgot."

Angelina growled. Harry let out a long, pained breath.

"Right. The Keeper tryouts."

"You're only remembering now?"

"Sadly, yes."

"You're unbelievable!"

Angelina was incandescent.

Angelina Johnson had taken over as Quidditch captain after Wood graduated, and she was living proof that authority shapes a person. She had spent years lamenting that Wood was a single-minded fanatic with nothing in his head but Quidditch—and then, the moment she became captain herself, she drove her team just as mercilessly as he ever had.

"Did I not tell you I wanted the whole team at the tryouts? Did I not mention I'd booked the pitch especially? And now you're telling me you're not going to be there!"

"I fully intend to be there. But Umbridge won't allow it—what am I supposed to do?"

"Figure something out! Get on your knees if you have to, make up whatever excuse you like—I don't care. You will be at those tryouts."

But Harry had no intention of saying a single conciliatory word to Umbridge. He replied with undisguised reluctance.

"She won't agree. If anything, she'll be delighted to have something to hold over me. I'm sorry, but this time I'm going to have to miss—"

"Why are you so determined to pick a fight with a professor, Potter? Is it because of what The Quibbler said—that Igor Karkaroff is after your life? Is that what's got you rattled?"

Angelina pressed on.

"Fine, yes—if your life were being threatened, you'd be frightened. I don't want you dead either. You're the best Seeker Gryffindor has! But if you're going to stir up conflict with a professor over wild stories about 'his' return and people coming back from the dead, and miss Quidditch practice because of it—"

"So it wouldn't matter much if I died?"

"...Do your best to make sure that doesn't happen! For Gryffindor's sake, for the Quidditch Cup—just swallow it for once!"

Angelina swept away like a storm.

Once inside the Great Hall, Harry dropped into his seat beside Ron and Hermione.

"Apparently Angelina has no use for a Harry Potter who can't ride a broomstick."

"She's still better than Wood. He acted like it didn't much matter whether your broom threw you off or you broke your arm, as long as you caught the Snitch."

Ron muttered. Harry let out a short laugh and sat down, reaching for food.

But unlike a few minutes ago—when he'd felt like he could chew through anything—he now found himself gnawing on a perfectly roasted chicken leg as though it were parchment.

Before Hogwarts, Harry had possessed nothing of value. That had made him free, in its way. His life was the only stake he had to put on the table, so he'd thrown himself into danger without much fear.

But now—Harry Potter, best friend to people who mattered, Gryffindor Seeker, a person woven into the lives of others—he could no longer be entirely free.

"So this is what it feels like."

Everything that bound him pulled him in one direction. And that direction was away from Carina.

He wanted to be close to her. He wanted to be near her. But circumstances—relentless, unyielding circumstances—were forcing them apart.

Carina must feel something similar. And yet she, too, would never be able to cut the things that pulled at her. She had far more to lose than he did.

Harry took a large bite of the chicken leg and forced himself to chew. Even the rich, fatty meat wouldn't go down easily.

Creak.

The gates of Nurmengard swung shut. Carina glanced back once, then slowly turned to face forward.

The method Grindelwald had described was almost insultingly simple. A Horcrux—use the very soul fragment that had tormented them, but in reverse. Cleave that soul and decant it into another vessel.

It was the kind of conclusion that made her wonder why no one had thought of it sooner. So simple, in theory.

No. Simple isn't quite the right word.

The ritual for creating a Horcrux was difficult and intricate—but not impossible, given sufficient preparation.

Killing someone requires no grand apparatus. You can raise a wand and speak the Killing Curse. You can drive a hand's-length dagger into a heart. You can lace a drink with poison, or lure someone to the edge of a cliff and push. Murder is within anyone's reach, provided they have the resolve to cross that line.

Horcruxes were no different. The only real obstacle was obtaining the forbidden knowledge. Once you had it, only one thing remained.

The will to take another life in order to preserve your own.

Carina walked out of Nurmengard with dark, unreadable eyes. Wolfram was waiting for her, leaning against the wall.

"Did you meet the witch?"

Carina nodded.

"What did you discuss?"

"That Dumbledore is dead. A few pleasantries. That sort of thing."

"That's all?"

Wolfram pressed her, clearly fishing for more. Carina ignored him and scanned her surroundings. The only structure on this desolate clifftop was the prison tower itself—no cover, no concealment anywhere outside Nurmengard's walls.

But Carina had sharp eyes. Sharp enough to notice a boulder positioned with suspicious unnaturalness—large enough, she noted, to conceal a person. And that boulder had not been there when she went inside.

Wolfram's expression hardened.

"…How did you know?"

"I might ask you the same thing. Your speech patterns and accent are completely different. Why would you assume I wouldn't notice?"

"Fair enough. I was too hasty."

Wolfram's lips twisted into something unpleasant.

An instant later, both of them drew their wands simultaneously. Carina snapped the incantation out like a whip.

"*Stupefy!*"

Wolfram swung his right arm in a wide arc. There was a sound like metal striking metal, and the red bolt ricocheted away into the distance. He advanced, wand raised, closing the gap with long, deliberate strides.

A Shield Charm? No—the spell had made contact with his arm before bouncing off. Where it had struck, a flash of silver showed beneath the surface, like gilt scraped away to reveal the metal beneath.

A silver right arm, gifted by the Dark Lord. A fighting style that used it as a shield.

Carina recognized him the moment the pieces fell into place.

"Igor Karkaroff!"

"You really are quick, aren't you…!"

Karkaroff dispelled his disguise. The unkempt hair and ragged beard told the story of a man who had been running for a very long time. None of which made him any less dangerous. Carina fired spell after spell, but each one was deflected by the silver arm. With that arm as his vanguard and his left hand moving with a life of its own, Karkaroff pressed forward like a battering ram.

He was nearly on top of her when he shouted.

"*Expelliarmus!*"

"*Prote—*"

Her Shield Charm was still forming when Karkaroff lunged. The silver arm punched straight through it and seized her by the collar. The impact tore the wand from her hand.

"Aah!"

He drove her back into the wall of Nurmengard. The stone slammed into her spine, and a cry escaped her lips.

Karkaroff's voice dropped to something low and menacing.

"Grindelwald. Yes. That was an option I hadn't considered—going to a Dark wizard from the previous generation. My mistake. I never imagined you'd seek out someone like her."

Pinned against the wall, Carina glared at him with undisguised contempt.

"How did you know I'd come here?"

"…Lucius should never have let his daughter leave Britain. I may be hunted by the British Ministry, but my sources abroad are still very much intact. But that's beside the point."

Slam. Karkaroff shoved her harder into the stone. Pain flared through her chest, and Carina's expression tightened.

He leaned in closer, his voice dropping further.

"The information you got from Grindelwald. You're going to tell me everything."

Even with his hand at her throat, Carina answered him with crisp precision.

"And what exactly do you intend to do with it?"

"What do you think? Resurrect the Dark Lord! He will return. By my hand, no less!"

"How devoted of you. It's a pity you couldn't manage that devotion from the start—*aah!*"

The silver arm crushed down on her. She cried out at the searing pain in her throat. Karkaroff snarled.

"You! This is your fault, Malfoy! If not for you—if you hadn't led me to Bartemius, I would never have ended up like this!"

Carina answered through the pain, her voice steady.

"Did I hold a wand to your head? Did I threaten you? Did I tell you that you'd die if you didn't return to Voldemort's side? You came to me because you were frightened. You were already unraveling, and my fear was the last push you needed."

"I didn't think Dumbledore would win! He was too old! And the Dark Lord had conquered death itself! Who could have imagined his defeat?"

It was a wretched, cowardly sight. He had made his own choices—and now he wanted to lay the blame at her feet.

If Carina had deceived him, she might have felt a flicker of guilt. But at the time, she had been his ally, however reluctantly. She could not understand his anger.

"But Dumbledore is dead now, isn't he? The Dark Lord lives on—inside Harry Potter! Once he takes full possession of Potter's body, no one will be able to stop him!"

One thing was certain: Karkaroff was their enemy.

He finished his raving and raised his wand.

"Now. If you won't speak willingly, I'll use the Cruciatus Curse. You will tell me everything Grindelwald told you. Every last word."

She would die before she said a word.

A Horcrux was a method of splitting the soul. If Harry used it, Voldemort's soul could be separated from his—but there was no guarantee that Voldemort, once he seized full control of Harry's body, wouldn't use the same method himself. She could not breathe a word of the Horcrux to Karkaroff. If she did, the remnants of the Death Eaters still in hiding might find new hope and come crawling out of the dark.

But how long could she hold out? Even if she endured the Cruciatus, she was carrying Veritaserum. If Karkaroff found it—

Carina clenched her jaw and forced her eyes upward. And then, almost involuntarily, her gaze fell on the silver arm gripping her collar.

"The silver… arm…"

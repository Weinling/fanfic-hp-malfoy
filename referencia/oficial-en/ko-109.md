The item that arrived by Muggle post was unlike any letter he had ever received. No sender's name—only the recipient's name and address written on a plain envelope. Through the paper, the rigid outline of something solid was already visible. Lucius Malfoy tore open the envelope and extracted its contents.

The first thing his hand closed around was a CD case, roughly the size of his palm. Next came a USB drive, no larger than a finger. Tucked alongside it was a small Post-it note bearing a brief inscription. Lucius read it.

Google Drive

ID : Goldsun

PASSWORD : NTR_f2d

He hadn't the faintest idea what any of it meant. He cast a detection charm on the paper, but found not a trace of magic on it. Lucius set it aside for the moment and placed the note on the table.

He picked up the CD and the USB, but being a wizard through and through, he had absolutely no idea what to do with either of them. One resembled a gleaming disc that might fit over a finger; the other was a rectangular piece of metal vaguely reminiscent of a key. No matter how long he stared, he could detect nothing remotely magical about them.

Some Muggle prank, perhaps. He was on the verge of discarding the rigid contraptions when a half-forgotten memory surfaced.

"Wait. Muggles, they used to..."

Lucius had never had occasion to encounter modern devices such as computers. He had no concept whatsoever of cloud storage or USB drives—but a CD was something he had glimpsed in passing. Recalling that Muggles used round discs to view moving pictures, he took the CD and made his way to the Muggle high street.

What he was actually thinking of was an LP record, but it was fortunate enough that he had arrived at a roughly similar idea. Lucius walked into a shop that dealt in CDs and demanded to know whether the contents of this disc could be viewed. The proprietor found nothing particularly strange about this aristocratic-looking customer. In recent years, as computers and televisions had begun dropping CD players altogether, it was not uncommon for people to come in hoping to play old videotapes or CDs they had unearthed at home.

Lucius settled into a seat in the Muggle shop and had the disc played. A soft crackle of static.

A black screen appeared. He was frowning at it, leaning forward to peer more closely, when he suddenly lurched upright—his torso nearly launching itself out of the chair.

[Do we really have to film this?]

The voice belonged to his daughter—Carina Malfoy, now a fugitive.

[You know why. Magical objects can be traced. The only way to send something without being detected is through Muggle means.]

Harry Potter's voice followed immediately after, grating on his nerves from the very first syllable. The two of them were evidently living together. He had known as much—and yet hearing it confirmed sent a fresh wave of revulsion through him.

Carina's fugitive status was entirely Harry Potter's fault. Voldemort had resided within the boy's soul, and the Ministry had been watching him closely. The Ministry and Harry Potter had agreed to use every resource at their disposal to seal what remained of Voldemort inside him.

And then, on the very day it was to happen—the day Harry Potter came of age—Harry Potter had taken Carina and fled.

Carina had been placed on the wanted list overnight, and Lucius himself had naturally come under investigation. Ministry Aurors had tramped in and out of Malfoy Manor so frequently they had nearly worn through the Floo powder. To avoid suspicion, the Malfoy family had been compelled to disclose the locations of several private retreats.

That had been a painful loss in itself—but not as painful as losing Carina. Because of the Ministry's surveillance, Lucius had not been granted so much as a single opportunity to see her. He and the rest of the Malfoy household had been left to pace and fret, unable to confirm whether she was even alive.

Word had reached him, in roundabout fashion, that Harry Potter had defeated Voldemort's soul alone before fleeing—but could a family's worry truly be so easily extinguished?

Profound anxiety, tangled with fury and grief. And yet at this stage, there was nothing Lucius could do. He fixed his gaze on the black screen and waited for the image to appear.

Carina and Harry were talking, their voices easy and familiar.

[Father won't have any idea how to watch this.]

[Oh, come on. We're sending a CD, a USB, and uploading it to Google Drive? One of them's bound to work.]

[You underestimate my father. He has an absolute aversion to anything Muggle.]

[Still, there's no harm in filming it, is there? You said you wanted to let him know you're all right. Whatever the method, you still need the footage first.]

[That's... well.]

[Let's just do it. We can figure out how to send it after.]

With those words, the screen brightened, and there she was—his daughter, standing against the backdrop of a small room. Lucius had been about to smile at the sight of her when his eyes went wide instead.
After all this time, his daughter looked absolutely dreadful.

[Ahem. Father. Mother. Draco. Can you hear me?]

Skin-tight skinny jeans. A sleeveless shirt that appeared to have been cut from as little fabric as possible. Her robes were nowhere to be seen—her shoulders were entirely bare, a jacket tied loosely around her waist instead of worn on them.

And in her hand, a handbag so small it couldn't have fit a wand. Her face, once pale and unadorned, was made up with colour. Clasped around her wrist like a bracelet was some Muggle contraption utterly devoid of any refinement.

Carina was standing there looking completely Muggle. No wizard alive, seeing her like that, could possibly have connected her to the pureblood Malfoy family.

She set down the smartphone she had been holding with practiced ease, turned to face the camera, and began to speak.

[It's been a while. I hope you're all well. Harry and I are safe. We've been fortunate enough to find a good patron and are managing quite comfortably. We've recently found a small room of our own. We've settled our things there, and we've been spending our free time exploring the area.]

He had been well enough a moment ago. He was no longer. Because he now understood that his daughter had spent too much time in the company of that lowborn Harry Potter and had been thoroughly contaminated by the association. Lucius barely managed to restrain the urge to demand an accounting on the spot.

Even so—his beloved daughter had reached out to let him know she was alive. The least he could do was watch it through. Lucius steadied himself.

Carina twirled a strand of hair around her finger as she spoke.

[Think of it as... a rather extended honeymoon.]

"Gkh—!"

"Sir? Are you all right?"

"Pay me no mind!"

Lucius, who had just coughed up blood, pressed a handkerchief to his mouth and thrust out his other hand urgently. The proprietor shot him an anxious glance.

Nevertheless, Lucius's eyes remained fixed on the screen as though nailed there.

[There's so much I want to ask. Whether the Ministry has been troubling our family. Whether Draco is getting on at school. But it's not easy to say everything that needs saying in a one-sided message like this. We ought to meet in person and talk properly.]

She was right. If they met in person, Lucius intended to demand answers to a great many things. Why she had gone to Hogwarts alongside Harry Potter under the pretence of helping him. Why, when Harry Potter fled, she had not stayed behind but gone with him.

Surely Harry Potter could have managed a fugitive's life on his own. And even if he had to run—now that he possessed that power, couldn't he have found time to visit Carina occasionally?

Lucius was deeply, bitterly dissatisfied that his daughter, barely come of age, had been reduced to a wanted criminal. He glared at the screen with barely contained fury—

[I love you.]

—and felt his anger dissolve at a single word from his daughter, because before he was a wizard, he was a father.

[When the Ministry's grip loosens a little, and people are convinced Harry is no longer a danger... I'll come and see you. Please wait just a little longer until then.]

"Hmph."

Lucius leaned back in his chair, his lips twisting.

There was no shortage of things that displeased him. The Muggle clothing. The cohabitation with Harry Potter. The wretched, wandering life. Not one element of it failed to offend—and yet.

As long as she was alive, they would meet again someday. He could deliver his reprimand then, with all due severity.

He was idly turning the armrest over in his fingers, debating whether to stand, when—

[I love you too, Carina.]

Harry Potter's insufferably smug voice drifted through the speakers. Carina's brow furrowed, and she addressed someone just off-screen with a sharp look.
[You. Why are you interrupting? I'm filming.]

[Hm? Don't you love me?]

[I never said that, you idiot.]

[Heh heh.]

That grating voice. Then the world tilted and went black—someone had flipped the camera face-down. From the darkened screen, the murmur of Harry and Carina's voices continued, low and unhurried.

[Should we send some of our travel photos along with it?]

[Leave out the ones from the beach. If Father sees those... he might actually cough up blood.]

[Ah. Those ones.]

[You were just thinking of sending them, weren't you? Don't. Restrain yourself. I won't forgive you.]

[Tch. Fine...]

[Good. Learn to listen.]

[If I'm being so well-behaved, don't I deserve a reward?]

At that, the tendons in Lucius's hand stood out sharply against the armrest. He fixed the screen—now nothing but sound—with bloodshot eyes.

[Is the camera... off?]

[Yeah. Looks like it.]

[Good... mmm...]

After that, try as he might to think of something else, the sounds that followed could only be described as two people kissing. Lucius yanked out his wand and shot to his feet.

"You insolent wretch, Harry Potteeeeeer!"

At that very moment, there was a sharp crack, and a Ministry official Apparated into the shop. He pressed his hat down and hurried toward Lucius.

"Mr. Malfoy. What is the meaning of this disturbance?"

Lucius rounded on the Ministry official with eyes blazing. The man flinched and threw out words in self-defence.

"P-please, calm yourself! The Ministry still has you under watch. If you start behaving erratically, they'll have no choice but to suspect you of being in contact with Harry Potter—"

"Then arrest him!"

"I beg your pardon?"

"Here. He's just made contact—use this as evidence and throw him in a cell!"

Lucius jabbed a finger at the CD and seized the official by the collar, shaking him vigorously.

"Arrest that arrogant orphan this instant! You call yourselves Ministry Aurors? The boy is barely seventeen years old! And yet not one of you has managed to track him down. Does that strike you as acceptable?"

"I—but why are you—"

"Because there is evidence right here, and I am telling you to use it and bring him in!"

In the end, Lucius's fury had not abated by the time the shop owner threw them both out.

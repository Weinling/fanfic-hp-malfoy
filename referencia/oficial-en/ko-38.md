Hermione spoke with a worried frown.

"Harry, are you really not going to prepare at all?"

"Nope. I have no desire to go to the bottom of a lake."

Harry seemed thoroughly unenthusiastic about the second task of the Triwizard Tournament. He propped his feet up on the desk and yawned extravagantly.

"Someone out there might be looking for a chance to attack me, and I'm supposed to go wandering around the lake floor? That's basically strolling around with a sign that says 'please kill me.' If I use the Bubble-Head Charm to breathe, then a simple Diffindo aimed at the bubble becomes a killing curse. If I get ambushed down there, the lake becomes my grave. And no one would ever know whether I drowned by accident or was murdered."

"Well, aren't you impressive. You're finally listening to the warnings I gave you last year, the year before, and the year before that."

Hermione said it with a dry edge. Harry shrugged and replied.

"Back then, the other side was going to come looking for me whether I moved or not, so I had to go out and intercept them. But there's no need for that now. It's not as though I need the prize money or the glory. More than anything, I want to live a long life. I barely have time to be in a relationship as it is."

Muttering that, Harry tossed a piece of walnut pie into his mouth.

Just then, Neville came through the dormitory common room door. He glanced around, spotted Harry, and trudged over with all the energy of a man walking to his own execution.

"Sorry, Harry... I couldn't get the Gillyweed."

"Why are you apologising? I said I wasn't going in anyway."

"Still... I made such a big deal about having a solution, and then I couldn't find it..."

"It's fine, it's fine. Just knowing something like that exists is enough of a result."

Harry tried to reassure him, but Neville looked so thoroughly crestfallen that no comfort seemed likely to reach him.

"Professor Sprout said she doesn't have any Gillyweed... I looked into ordering it, but apparently it can only be harvested in summer... And on top of that, if you don't keep it in a special potion, it dries out within a few days above ground and becomes useless."

"So it was never really meant to be kept on hand. Who would even have any?"

"...Professor Sprout said Professor Snape probably would."

"Snape?"

Harry's face twisted. And then he understood why Neville had been so apologetic. The thing Neville feared most in the world was Snape, and he simply could not bring himself to go to the man and ask for Gillyweed.

Harry felt the same way. He had no desire to go to Snape with his hat in his hand.

"If it's there, getting it would be impossible short of duelling Snape into the floor and ransacking his office. Snape would make a deal with the devil himself if it meant making my life miserable."

Neville said anxiously.

"H-Harry! You can't attack a professor!"

"I know. I have no intention of picking a fight I'd lose."

"...You say that like you've actually tried it before."

Harry leaned back into the sofa and yawned lazily.

"Well, that settles it. I'm not doing it. I'm not going in. I'm going to live forever in joy and happiness..."

"Hermione!"

Fred and George came striding toward Hermione.

"Have you done something wrong? Professor McGonagall was looking for you."

"She looked absolutely furious, for whatever reason."

"Professor McGonagall? Why? Was there a mistake in the basic Transfiguration essay from last lesson?"

Hermione leapt to her feet in a panic. Harry looked at her with an expression of mild disbelief.

"Hermione, even if it is Professor McGonagall, she wouldn't summon you in the middle of the night over a few essay errors."

"Harry. Basic Transfiguration is extremely important. It changes not just the appearance but the function entirely, so even a small mistake can cause serious problems!"

"How many things we learn here aren't dangerous?"

"Regardless, I'm going. If I'm not back by ten, don't wait up!"

With that, Hermione left the dormitory.

And she did not return even after ten o'clock had passed.

Harry spent the morning of the task searching for Carina. His plan had been to make a great show of complaining before heading to the lake, and collect some sympathy and encouragement in return.

But Carina was nowhere to be found.

If today was the task, she would at least come out to watch—but her shimmering platinum hair was nowhere to be seen.

"Now, all of our Champions are assembled and waiting for the second task. The Champions must... retrieve what they have lost, within one hour!"

Hermione, Cho Chang, and Carina had vanished. The one thing they had in common was that each had been a Champion's partner at the Yule Ball.

The requirement that Champions bring a partner to the ball—that too had probably been designed to identify the hostages.

What elaborate staging. Harry smiled through gritted teeth.

"What they have lost lies below! We trust they have already worked out what that is. On the count of three, we begin!"

Harry had made absolutely no preparations.

He had a rough grasp of the Bubble-Head Charm, but it was far from perfect. His original plan had been to enter the water, pretend the charm had failed, and come back out.

He had no desire to go down to the bottom of that lake—a place where no one could know what anyone might do. He needed to return to the surface, where Carina was.

But Carina was not on the surface. She was waiting for him below.

Then he had to go. Somehow.

"On three! One, two, three!"

"*Accio* Firebolt!"

Whatever the other Champions were doing, he had no time to care.

Far in the distance, the Firebolt cut through the air. Harry swung onto it in one fluid motion and flew toward Hogwarts.

Watching Harry suddenly bolt in the opposite direction, Ludo Bagman shouted after him.

"Harry Potter—where are you flying? That's Hogwarts, not the lake! Oh, wait. That window—"

Harry did not slow down. He aimed straight for the castle wall—toward the large window that faced the lake—and as he hurtled toward it, he raised his wand and cried out.

"*Reducto!*"

The explosion reached them even from that distance. The shattered window burst apart in all directions, and chunks of masonry tumbled down below. Harry drove through the hole he had made, broomstick and all.

While everyone stood speechless at Harry's erratic behaviour, Ron suddenly realised something and shouted.

"That's Snape's office!"

Harry had smashed through the window of Snape's office and broken in. Several people turned to look at Snape with faces drained of colour.

Having just witnessed his own office explode in real time, Snape wore an expression that suggested he would very much like to throttle Harry on the spot.

Meanwhile, Harry was already ransacking Snape's office. He rummaged through a cabinet, muttering to himself.

"He's got an obscene amount of ingredients. He might actually be the wealthiest person in Hogwarts."

Neville had described Gillyweed as looking like slippery aquatic weeds—a magical Mediterranean water plant that, when chewed, caused gills and webbing to grow. Apparently it had been popular among island-dwelling wizards who wished to meet merpeople.

After turning the cabinet completely upside down, Harry finally found the Gillyweed. He shoved it straight into his mouth, climbed back onto his broomstick, and prepared to leave.

Just before departing, he glanced back for a moment. Snape's office looked as though a storm had torn through it.

Still, since he had benefited from the man's stores, it seemed only polite to say goodbye. Harry expressed his gratitude to Snape.

"Thanks for the loan."

And then the Firebolt shot forward like a comet. The backdraft sent parchment and inkbottles scattering across the desk, but that was none of Harry's concern.

Harry chewed the Gillyweed steadily as he flew toward the lake.

"*Impervius.*"

He cast the waterproofing charm once each on his glasses, his robes, and his broomstick, then circled above the lake. He took one deep breath, and accelerated toward the water in the manner of a Wronski Feint.
It was faster than a fall. The difference was that this time, he had no intention of pulling up.

Instead of yanking back on the broomstick, Harry levelled his wand at the surface of the lake and shouted.

"*Reducto!*"

Boom.

The surface shattered like a pane of glass. A column of spray erupted upward, and a passage opened into the blue-dark depths below.

Harry rode the Firebolt straight through the hole in the lake. The passage closed behind him.

Like a child who has swallowed a sweet and is pretending otherwise, the lake shuddered a few more times before settling into perfect stillness, as though nothing had happened at all.

Quidditch was an all-weather sport. Rain, storm, lightning—none of it stopped play until the Snitch was caught.

Naturally, the Firebolt—the finest broomstick in the world—had been built to handle every one of those conditions.

Which meant it functioned perfectly well when thoroughly soaked.

It was noticeably slower than in the air, but faster than swimming. Harry cut across the lake at roughly the speed of a flying fish.

"Just a little longer, Carina..."

Even if she had been taken hostage, this was still a Triwizard Tournament mechanism. Surely Dumbledore wouldn't actually let the hostages go for good just because an hour had passed.

Still, he had to go and find her.

Carina was both an obligation Harry had taken on himself and a right he had claimed. She was the single landmark pointing him toward the place he needed to return to.

He could not give up over a handful of trivial complications. Not even if those complications included someone actively trying to kill him.

Hostage in name or in fact—if she was down here, he was going. Harry rode the Firebolt through the water.

"*Lumos!*"

Harry moved along the lake floor. Aquatic creatures drawn by the light trailed after him, but none could match the Firebolt's pace.

Several minutes of combing the depths passed. Then Harry finally heard a faint, distant singing.

[Come this way...]

He wheeled the broomstick around and went at full speed. In the distance, Harry found the merpeople's village.

The merpeople's dwellings were nothing like human architecture. Where human homes were built to keep out cold and enemies, the merpeople's homes were built to withstand currents and predators.

The flow of water through the lake could not be stopped—only redirected. The stone houses were riddled with small openings, each fitted with a curtain of water-weed that anyone could push aside to peer inside. Cold and warmth were irrelevant; the water was always the same.

The merpeople stared at Harry on his broomstick with wide eyes and pointed fingers. Harry ignored them and headed for the central square, following the sound of singing.

Atop a great stone mermaid statue—there was Carina, eyes tightly shut, suspended in place. Her bare ankles were bound with rope that connected her to the statue.

"Carina!"

Harry went straight to the statue. Carina was motionless, her expression pale as a corpse. She appeared to be in an enchanted sleep.

Harry aimed his wand at the rope binding her ankles.

"*Diffindo!*"

The rope snapped cleanly. Harry untied the knot at her ankle, then pulled Carina against him with one arm and looked around.

Beside her were Cho Chang, Hermione, and a small child who looked almost exactly like Fleur Delacour.

The other Champions' hostages. Cho Chang would be Cedric's; Hermione, Viktor Krum's. The little girl must be Fleur's younger sister.

The other Champions had not yet arrived. Harry stopped himself from heading up and decided to wait a while. He could bring Carina to the surface, but what was the point of going up early only to stand around?

Since he'd eaten the Gillyweed anyway, he might as well check whether anyone was lurking nearby, he thought, scanning his surroundings.

And a date at the bottom of a lake was, admittedly, a novel experience.

"She won't open her eyes, though..."

Dumbledore's enchantment was likely also serving as a protective charm—shielding the hostages from the cold of the lake floor and the lack of breathable air. She would not wake until she was out of the water.

"...Wait. She won't wake up?"

Harry studied Carina quietly.

At rest like this, she looked like a doll crafted by a wizard who had poured every last ounce of skill into the work.

There had been a time when Harry had wished she would simply stop talking. Every time she opened her mouth, something unpleasant came out—he had thought it would be better if she just showed off her face and let her beauty speak for itself.

That was no longer true, of course. The more he learned about her, the more he liked her.

The prim little remarks she delivered with that carefully maintained air of indifference, the expressions she kept tucked behind a single layer of composure, and the small, devastating moments of playfulness that surfaced at the worst possible times—they drove him absolutely mad.

He wanted to reach out right now, and at the same time he wanted to stay exactly like this forever.

Whenever that more primal impulse surged up from somewhere deep inside him, a different kind of feeling got in the way—a stubborn romanticism that insisted the first time should be the most perfect day imaginable.

That was why, even now, he could extend his hand toward Carina and still not quite bring himself to touch her.

"I never know when or how I'm supposed to make a move,"

He had been a little surprised himself when she had followed him to the prefects' bathroom without a second thought. He hadn't expected her to trust him so completely, with so little wariness.

To honour that trust, he had let that perfectly good opportunity slip away. Even so, Harry had not slept that night.

Both he and Carina had changed in many ways as they'd grown. Their faces, their expressions, their heights. And...

Come to think of it, certain other things had changed quite considerably as well.

"Mmm~."

Harry swam down and pulled up a long strand of water-weed.

It was long enough, and it didn't stretch easily even when tugged several times. Harry took the water-weed and drifted back to Carina's side.

"Ahem."

He had no intention of doing anything untoward while she was unconscious. He simply wanted to, well—take a measurement of sorts. Carina was not exactly generous with this kind of physical proximity when she was awake.

Harry looped the water-weed once around Carina's chest. The fact that she wasn't breathing made it considerably easier to get an accurate reading.

He had no ruler, so he couldn't know the number. But he had the template now, and he could find out whenever he liked.

Harry tied a knot at the relevant point to mark the measurement.

"I didn't touch anything. It was the water-weed that made contact. That's just barely acceptable, isn't it?"

It wasn't, really. If it had truly been acceptable, he would have done it when Carina was conscious.

She would probably give in eventually if Harry pushed—with that particular look of resigned tolerance she sometimes wore.

In any case, he had the measurement now. He just needed to keep the marked water-weed and measure it against a ruler once they were back on the surface. Harry swirled the strand of weed around idly, quietly satisfied with the day's findings.

Then his gaze drifted to Cho Chang.

Cedric's girlfriend. Remarkably, it seemed the two had started dating after Cedric's defeat in the Quidditch match last year. Defeat had become the thread that tied them together.

Absurdly, some students apparently considered Cho Chang the most beautiful girl in school. They had clearly never seen Carina. Or perhaps they simply harboured a fascination with exotic looks.

"No matter how I look at it, Carina is better. In every respect..."

Every respect. Harry glanced at the water-weed in his hand.

"Hm."

The loop of water-weed passed through Cho Chang's figure without the slightest resistance.

Harry felt a sudden swell of something almost majestic in his chest.

Cedric had lost again.

"Harry!"

In the distance, Cedric came swimming into view. Harry immediately dropped the water-weed and turned the broomstick away with an expression of perfect innocence.

Cedric had his face encased in the Bubble-Head Charm—the very method Harry had originally planned to use. It kept him breathing well enough, but the thick bubble seemed to be making it difficult to see.
"I got lost. Krum and Fleur are on their way too!"

"Ah. Right."

Harry replied with studied nonchalance. Cedric pulled a knife from his pocket, cut the rope binding Cho Chang, and headed upward. Harry's expression urged him to hurry along.

Next came Viktor Krum. He appeared to have attempted a partial Transfiguration into a shark, with limited success—he was swimming through the water in a human fashion, wearing a swimsuit, but with a shark's head.

The shark-man made straight for Hermione and began gnawing at the rope binding her with his teeth. But a shark's teeth were poorly suited to chewing through a small rope, and there was a real risk of injuring Hermione in the process.

"*Diffindo.*"

Harry cut the rope with his wand. Krum shot him a grateful look and immediately began making his way to the surface.

Now only Fleur remained. If she came and took her sister, Harry and Carina would be alone.

If anyone intended to attack, that would be their last opportunity. Harry kept his hand on his wand and drifted through the merpeople's village.

The merpeople had long, dark green hair like trailing water-weed, and a grey colouring that blended into the water. It would be difficult for a person to disguise themselves convincingly, and besides, the village was a community—if someone had slipped in, the merpeople would have noticed.

He had wondered whether someone might be hiding among the water-weeds or behind rocks, but nothing seemed out of place.

Would whoever it was really pass up the chance to catch Harry alone at the bottom of a lake? Or perhaps they had no intention of attacking at all?

No—more likely they were waiting for Fleur to arrive first. Holding their breath for a cleaner opportunity.

"Fleur's taking her time. At this rate, the date will be over before it's begun."

The Gillyweed was starting to wear off. He hadn't managed to identify any threat, and he hadn't managed a proper date with Carina either.

Harry clicked his tongue in irritation and moved toward where Carina and Fleur's sister were still bound. He cut the rope on Fleur's sister with his wand, then settled both of them onto the Firebolt in front of him.

At that, an adult merperson swam toward him.

"You have already retrieved your hostage. Leave the others..."

"Excuse me. The one-hour time limit ended quite a while ago."

Harry replied with barely concealed impatience.

"If she hasn't come by now, that's a disqualification. Someone was going to come for her eventually—I'll just bring her up with me."

"This is also part of the task..."

"Part of the task? Everyone up there has been watching this whole time. It's not a task, it's a spectacle."

Harry pointed his wand at the watching merpeople. They scattered immediately, darting away from the tip of his wand.

"The spectacle is over. Everyone go home."

He left it at that and rose toward the surface. The merpeople stood about awkwardly, as though they had missed their cue.

On the way up, he felt his gills beginning to disappear. The flow of water through his throat was gone. What had been a cool, briny current until now was suddenly just a suffocating weight pressing against his lungs.

Harry clamped his mouth shut and drove upward. Through the thin membrane of the surface, the sun blazed brilliantly. Harry reached out his hand.

His body broke through the surface of the lake. The Firebolt had managed two passengers, but three was clearly pushing it—the moment they cleared the water, it lurched unsteadily. The waterlogged tail end seemed to be slowing it down as well.

Carina and the little girl opened their eyes. Both of them looked around with expressions of complete bewilderment.

"You're awake?"

Harry said it as he skimmed across the surface of the lake on the broomstick.

"It's been a while. I missed you."

"I... I was told I had to be a hostage, and then..."

Carina was still disoriented, her words coming out in fragments, when she noticed the small girl nestled against her.

Fleur Delacour's younger sister was shaking her cloud-soft hair from side to side, still looking around in wide-eyed confusion.

Carina lowered her voice slightly and asked again.

"Who is this?"

"Ah. Do I have to start from there?"

Harry arrived at the lakeside. Fleur was the first to come running, throwing her arms around the little girl.

"Oh, Gabrielle! Gabrielle... are you all right? A Grindylow attacked me! The Bubble-Head Charm broke and I just couldn't—!"

Fleur exchanged a few words with Gabrielle, then turned to Harry.

"You saved Gabrielle... you could have left her there and come up..."

"Don't worry about it. There was just room on the broomstick, so I brought her along..."

He had brought her out of sheer irritation at Fleur's lateness. That was not something he could say aloud, so Harry was casting about for a more diplomatic version.

Then Fleur took his face in her hands and kissed him on one cheek. Harry moved quickly enough to prevent the second kiss on the other cheek, but Fleur didn't seem particularly bothered. She took Gabrielle by the hand and headed toward the Beauxbatons contingent.

Harry looked up at the sky with a deeply uncomfortable expression. From the corner of his vision, he could feel a gaze burning into him. He let out a long breath and said,

"...Just give me a moment, Carina. I can explain everything."

"Don't bother!"

Carina pulled away from him and spun around.

Harry had learned to read the difference by now. Whether that slightly elevated, clipped tone carried expectation or anger. Whether a movement of her hand was an invitation to be caught or a signal to stay back.

The way she was walking now—wet hair swept back, the set of her shoulders clearly annoyed, but her pace unhurried and slow.

This was a moment to reach out.

"It caught me off guard too, Carina."

He held on and wheedled, like a child making excuses. When he did that—when he acted like a small boy pretending not to notice—Carina would relent, as though she had no choice.

Not a single word had ever been spoken about it, but it was an understanding that had formed on its own over the time they had spent together.

Carina stopped walking and exhaled slowly. That was her signal that the storm had passed.

Harry pressed his advantage, tapping his own cheek lightly with two fingers.

"Get rid of it."

Harry tilted his head down, and Carina rose onto her toes. Carina's kiss covered the spot where Fleur's lips had been.

Carina fixed him with a look and said,

"Just this once."

Carina walked toward where Barnette Crabbe and Gray Goyle were waiting.

Harry reached into his pocket. The strand of water-weed was still there—carrying its forbidden measurements from the lake floor.

He turned it over in his hand for a moment, then tossed it onto the bank.

He didn't need it. If it ever came to that, Harry would find out for himself.

Harry walked away with a thoroughly satisfied expression.

The scores were announced. Harry had exceeded the time limit by so much that he was docked points, finishing with forty. The bonus points had been awarded for bringing up Fleur's sister.

Cedric's interview concluded, and it was Harry's turn. Harry gave a small, modest cough and spoke.

"I'm truly embarrassed to have received such a generous score... I really didn't do anything. The Gillyweed wasn't mine to begin with. I merely received it from someone..."

Those who had watched Snape's office window explode in real time nodded along. It was true that the Gillyweed hadn't been Harry's.

He hadn't exactly received it, either.

Harry spread his arms wide and called out in a carrying voice.

"I dedicate this honour to the Hogwarts professor who so thoughtfully made the Gillyweed available in such a convenient location—Professor Severus Snape!"

Applause erupted from the stands.

Snape's face contorted into something truly dreadful.

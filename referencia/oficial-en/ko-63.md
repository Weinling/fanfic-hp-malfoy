Plans were plans, and nothing more.

No one on the Gryffindor Quidditch team said it aloud, but a silent strategy had taken shape among them: end the game before Ron lets in another goal. They exchanged glances, gave a half-hearted cheer, and filed out onto the pitch. Harry and Ron had not exchanged a single word the entire time.

Until his feet touched the Quidditch pitch, Harry couldn't bring himself to look anyone in the eye. He'd left the dormitory early that morning to avoid Ron's face, yet hadn't gone near the Great Hall either.

He felt like a Basilisk. If he let someone into his gaze, he might wound them—that vague, nagging dread had made him withdraw into himself.

The Slytherin players strode onto the pitch with their usual swagger, and Carina took her position across from him, but Harry couldn't bring himself to look at her face properly either.

"Right then, on my whistle—begin!"

At Madam Hooch's signal, the players launched into the air as one.

Harry immediately swept a circuit of the pitch in search of the Snitch. First match on the Firebolt. The broom cut through the air lighter than ever—and yet Harry couldn't find so much as a glimmer of gold. He was drifting aimlessly around the pitch, going through the motions.

The lack of practice had clearly taken its toll. Harry pressed on, scanning every corner of the stadium with growing unease.

"Oi, Harry! Your Keeper looked just like the portrait outside our common room—give him the password and he opens right up, automatic!"

Montague of Slytherin jeered at him from across the pitch. Harry ignored him. If he responded, he wasn't sure he could stop himself from knocking Montague off his broom.

He was scanning the air for the Snitch when a burst of cheering erupted from the Slytherin stands. Every time someone bellowed "Weasley is our King!", Harry knew Ron had let in another goal. He forced himself not to look and kept searching.

"...And Slytherin extends their lead. One hundred and twenty to forty..."

Harry's jaw tightened. Eighty points already. If this dragged on much longer, Ron might give up a hundred and sixty. It wasn't losing that bothered Harry most—it was the thought of Ron's face afterward. He pushed harder, hunting for the Snitch.

"Ten more points, Weasley! You really are our King!"

Even across the wide pitch, the Slytherin players' taunts carried perfectly. Harry swallowed the surge of anger rising in his chest.

He wanted to knock every last one of them off their brooms right now. One well-aimed spell, one moment of letting go—it would be so easy.

Foals who don't know their place. One simple charm and they'd scatter like autumn leaves.

Do it. Grind them into the fallen leaves and walk right over them...

Harry wrenched his gaze away. And that was when he spotted it—a tiny golden sphere glinting near Montague's ear as the Slytherin player continued his jeering.

Harry drove the Firebolt forward instantly. In his urgency to seize the Snitch, he flew straight at it without a single feint. Carina saw him move and gave chase immediately.

Both rode Firebolts, but Harry's had been fractionally slower ever since it went into the lake during the Triwizard Tournament the previous year. They reached for the Snitch at the same moment. Harry's arm was longer, but Carina's hand swept past his, angling toward the gold.

Shove her. She's light—one push and she'll roll right off that broom...

Harry clenched his teeth and closed his fist. His fingers scraped across the back of Carina's hand, barely nudging her trajectory off course. She closed her fingers around empty air. In that instant, Harry snatched the Snitch cleanly.

Lee Jordan, who had been watching in breathless silence, erupted.

"Harry Potter has caught the Snitch! Gryffindor wins!"

The final score: two hundred and ten to one hundred and eighty, Gryffindor. Had Harry been even a moment slower, they would have lost.

Gryffindor had won, regardless. That would help Ron's mood, at least a little.

While everyone else celebrated, Ron—who had conceded eighteen goals—descended from the pitch looking utterly crushed. Harry was still clutching the Snitch, moving toward him to say something, when—

"Congratulations, Weasley. You won this time."

Carina had landed beside them without Harry noticing. The words were congratulatory; her expression was anything but. Her beautiful face looked down at Ron with undisguised contempt.

"How does it feel, riding in on Harry Potter's coattails—and your twin brothers'? You barely scraped through today because those three carried your weight. Hmm. How long do you think that can last?"

Ron's face crumpled. Harry felt irritation boiling up inside him and shook his head.

Please. Just stop. Watching his closest friend and the person he cared most about tear into each other while he stood helplessly between them—it was its own particular kind of pain.

Harry stepped toward Ron with his arms open.

"Ron. You played well enough."

He deliberately turned his back on Carina, trying to steady Ron. But her voice carried perfectly even with Harry between them.

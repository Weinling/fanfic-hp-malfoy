# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 48: Triwizard Tournament, Third Round

The Lestrange family vault held countless treasures. Gold coins rolled across the floor of a chamber the size of a modest room, and every inch was crammed with gilded goblets, silver armour, dragon hide, and crystal vials of potion.

Finding the golden badger cup among all of it was, in itself, straightforward enough.

Provided one was sufficiently prepared to touch scorching metal.

Sirius, gloved hands braced, exhaled and seized the golden cup. The instant he did, it blazed a furious red and began multiplying—dozens of identical cups spreading in every direction.

He grimaced. Lupin called out a warning.

"Don't lose the real one, Sirius!"

"I know! Bloody hell, it's burning straight through the dragonhide!"

While Sirius struggled, Lupin raised his voice—loud enough to carry to the goblin waiting outside.

"Magnificent gold, this! Madam, you truly mean to let us take some of it?"

Lupin was playing the part of a fugitive who had followed Bellatrix for the money. It wasn't a particularly convincing performance, but it worked well enough on Tonks.

At the word Madam, Tonks squirmed and answered in a simpering voice.

"Yes, hee... please, help yourselves..."

"Er... Madam?"

"Did you call for me, darling?"

"No, wait—"

Sirius shook his head and dropped Hufflepuff's Cup into the enchanted pouch. He grabbed a few extra coins for appearances, then turned to the other two, who were generating an entirely different kind of atmosphere inside the vault.

"I'm giving you the house-elf for the evening, so save the rest for when we're back at the manor."

"What on earth are you saying, Sirius!"

"Thank you..."

"Thank you? Tonks, what are you—"

Regardless, once the cup was secured, the three of them strolled out of the vault as though nothing had happened. Mardnak closed the vault door with a deferential bow and asked,

"Mrs. Lestrange. Is something the matter? It appears the anti-theft enchantment was triggered."

"I told you already. I promised these two I'd let them get their hands on some gold!"

Time to be Bellatrix again. Tonks let out a grating cackle.

"And I kept my promise. They touched the gold without knowing a thing—watched their hands and feet blister right up! Like turkeys in an oven! And still they wouldn't let go!"

An explanation for the anti-theft enchantment. That was precisely why she had brought Lupin and Sirius along.

Dumbledore had known the Gringotts anti-theft spells in extraordinary—and inexplicable—detail, and had helpfully explained that if the enchantment triggered, the goblins would grow suspicious. They needed a plausible reason for why someone had touched gold that didn't belong to them.

Tonks had proposed using Bellatrix's own nature against her.

The plan was to make it appear that the anti-theft magic had been triggered by Bellatrix's sadism—that the foreign visitors had touched the gold on their own, and she had let them suffer for it.

But Mardnak's manner was subtly different from when he had admitted them. The goblin studied Tonks sidelong and probed carefully.

"I—I mean no disrespect to Madam, but we made enquiries, and the Ministry has informed us that... there have been no escapes from Azkaban."

Tonks and Mardnak locked eyes. Tonks stared down at the goblin with Bellatrix's cold face, and the goblin—though his gaze flickered to the wand and a tremor ran through him—did not look away.

The Ministry would be run ragged with the Triwizard Tournament right now. They had miscalculated in assuming Gringotts could reach the Ministry directly even at a time like this.

Sirius and Lupin moved their hands toward their wands.

Tonks snapped irritably,

"Do I really have to spell it out for you? What happened today, of all days? Why today—the day the Ministry is gutted empty because of the Triwizard Tournament—did I choose to leave that wretched place and come here, before attending to a rather more glorious appointment? Do I need to hold your hand and explain it to you?"

"No, that's not—"

Tonks raised her wand. Beyond the passage, she could see several armed goblins flinch.

They're suspicious. That much is clear. To avoid suspicion, she had to act like Bellatrix. But what would Bellatrix actually do in a situation like this?

Easy enough to know. Considerably harder to replicate.

Tonks knew that Bellatrix was a master of the Cruciatus Curse. She tormented others with it whenever the mood struck her, and she preferred driving her enemies to madness through endless suffering rather than simply finishing them off. Mercifully vile.

But right now, to avoid suspicion, Tonks had to cast an Unforgivable. She forced her voice steady and raised her wand.

"And why, exactly—should I have to answer to the likes of you? How dare a lesser creature presume to—*Crucio*—!"

At that moment, Lupin let a handful of gold coins and a necklace slip from his hands—nearly hurling them to the floor. The gold struck the stone with a jarring clatter. Every goblin in the passage instinctively snapped their head toward the sound.

Tonks seized the moment and lowered her wand, shouting,

"You idiot! Do you have any idea what that is?!"

"S-sorry. I'm so sorry."

Lupin ducked his head and scrambled to gather the coins, then stared at them with a slack, vacant expression. It was painfully obvious acting—but fortunately, goblins had little interest in the cultural habits of humans.

Tonks jabbed her wand into Lupin's chest.

"Drop anything again and I'll stretch that tongue of yours out and trim it piece by piece!"

"Madam, please forgive me. I've never held gold like this before—my hands were trembling..."

Tonks looked at Lupin, head bowed before her.

The quiet attentiveness Lupin showed at moments like this always reached her somewhere warm. He was always mindful of her, always adjusting himself so she could do what she needed to do.

Tonks, who could change her appearance at will, was in many ways acutely sensitive. But when she was with Lupin, she could simply be herself.

She smiled faintly and whispered,

"Thank you."

Then she spun on her heel and swept her wand in a wide arc, firing spell after spell. Red beams ricocheted off the walls and ceiling of the passage. Goblins screamed and threw their arms over their heads. Shattered stone dust rained down from the walls and ceiling.

Tonks bellowed,

"Get out of my sight, you filthy little creatures! Every last one of you will answer for this when the Dark Lord returns!"

The goblins waiting around the corner fled in a scramble. The only one who remained was Mardnak, who had escorted them in.

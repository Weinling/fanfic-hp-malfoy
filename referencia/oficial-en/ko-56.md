King's Cross Station, Platform Nine and Three-Quarters.

On the eve of a semester's separation, Narcissa pressed her lips to Carina's cheek and then to Draco's in quick succession. The kisses lingered a beat longer than in years past, as though they were saying what words could not.

Lucius stood composed, but whenever his children looked away, the worry surfaced plainly on his face.

The most animated member of the Malfoy family was Draco. He was so excited at the prospect of boarding the Hogwarts Express that he seemed entirely oblivious to the undercurrent of unease.

Steam began billowing from the train in great white gusts. Time to go. Carina said her farewells alongside Draco and turned toward the platform.

"We'll be off, then."

"Yes. Carina... I'm counting on you."

The words carried more weight than their surface meaning. Carina answered with a faint smile and nothing more.

The two of them boarded the Express. They waved a final goodbye through the carriage window, then went to find their compartment.

Carina, now in her fifth year, had been appointed Slytherin's female prefect. The badge gleamed on her chest as she stowed her trunk and Draco's in the overhead rack of the prefects' compartment. The rack was generous enough that both trunks fit with room to spare.

The other prefects glanced over with interest when they noticed the Malfoy siblings, but Carina had not the slightest intention of making introductions.

She turned to Draco.

"Draco. I have prefect duties, so I can't stay with you."

"I wasn't planning on staying with you anyway."

Draco said it with his usual sideways smirk.

"Besides, why are you a prefect? Maybe Dumbledore really has lost his mind—appointing you of all people!"

"Prefects aren't chosen at Dumbledore's whim, Draco. Professor Snape recommends candidates who have demonstrated the appropriate merit, and Dumbledore gives his approval."

"And that's you? Slytherin is finished this year."

"I swear—"

Carina raised her fist, and Draco darted away like a squirrel. She watched him disappear down the corridor, let out a breath, and gave a small wave.

"See you at the Sorting!" Draco called back.

Then he was gone, swallowed up by the far end of the train. He'd find his friends soon enough.

Carina exhaled quietly and turned the prefect badge over between her fingers.

Only a year ago, she had never doubted she would be made prefect. She had worn the certainty like a second skin, proud that there was no real competition—proof of how singularly capable and exceptional she was.

And yet. Why did the badge feel like a weight pressed against her chest?

She stepped into the prefects' compartment and found a familiar face. The Slytherin male prefect was Blaise Zabini, who had spent the past four years perfecting an air of supreme indifference toward everyone around him. It was already clear that Carina would be shouldering the bulk of the work this year.

The Gryffindor female prefect was, predictably, Hermione Granger—a choice Carina found entirely unremarkable. Her marks were the best in the year, and her compulsive need to look after everyone made her a natural fit.

Which meant the Gryffindor male prefect was probably—

Carina drew a slow, deliberate breath. Not because she was hoping to see Harry. Simply to prepare herself for whoever she was about to face. She turned her head.

A gangly figure with untidy red hair and a face still scattered with freckles came into view. She tilted her head, wondering if she'd misread the situation—but the badge on his chest bore a gleaming 'P', unmistakable.

Carina looked at Ron Weasley.

"Why are you a prefect, Weasley?"

"I was wondering when that was coming. Mind your own business, Malfoy. There must have been some catastrophic error."

Ron muttered it as though he'd been expecting exactly this.

Just as well, Carina thought. They were going to be at each other's throats all year regardless. Better Weasley than Harry—at least he was easier to deal with.

She let herself have the small satisfaction of sharpening her tongue on him.

"Is it hereditary now? Or did they switch to a popular vote? The Weasleys have nothing to boast of except sheer numbers—and of all of them, they had to pick the most useless one."

"Useless?"

Ron bristled, stung.

"Take that back and sit down, Malfoy! I'm a prefect, same as you!"

"Hardly the same. There's a world of difference between me and the runt of a family with nothing to its name."

"Shut your mouth, Malfoy!"

Despite the relentless provocation, Ron was still holding himself together. That irritated Carina.

Ron and Hermione had always treated her with a peculiar, unspoken consideration—solely because she was Harry's girlfriend. But that was over now. It had to be over. And that kind of consideration was no longer needed. Not by Carina, and not by them.

As though severing the last thread of feeling, Carina let the word fall from her lips.

"Ah—trying to balance things out, are they? I suppose if the female prefect is a Mudblood, they'd want the other side to be proper pureblood—"

That was as far as she got. Ron's restraint snapped. He wrenched out his wand and leveled it at her. Hermione cried out in alarm.

"Ron, don't!"

"Let go, Hermione!"

The compartment erupted. Carina's fingers found her own wand in her pocket, ready.

As it happened, Ron did not fire. Somehow, remarkably, he swallowed his fury and put his wand away.

Instead he fixed her with a hard stare and said flatly:

"What's the matter—miss Harry? Wish you could sit together?"

Carina's expression didn't flicker.

"How childish. That's exactly why you never grow up."

"You're the one who can't let go. You call yourself pure and noble and all the rest of it, but you're just his lackey, aren't you? No—worse than that. You're a wallet he pulls out when he needs money, and a human shield when he needs one."

Now it was Carina's turn to glare, fury rising in her chest. Ron glared back, unyielding, shaking off Hermione's attempts to intervene.

"You lot serve him, don't you? So what happens when the war comes and your little brother ends up dead? There'll be no one left to carry on that precious Malfoy name—"

Carina was reaching for her wand when the compartment door swung open and Cedric Diggory walked in.

"Hello! I'm Cedric Diggory, this year's Head Boy! I have a few things to pass along to the prefects—"

He trailed off, taking in the scene. Ron, barely restrained by Hermione. Carina, furious to her roots. Blaise, feigning disinterest while watching everything from the corner of his eye.

Cedric frowned, working through it, and ultimately gave up. He offered an uncertain smile.

"Er—sorry to interrupt. Could everyone calm down and take a seat?"

Carina and Ron dropped into their seats with matching scowls. Cedric stepped into the middle of the compartment, his handsome face creased with visible discomfort.

"Right. Lively group—that's good, that's good. Prefects should have some spirit... though I'd prefer if you kept personal disputes personal. The risk of it spilling into house rivalries is rather high."

The two of them were still glowering. Cedric had a feeling this was going to be a very long year.

Prefects were responsible for supervising students, escorting them to the Great Hall at lunch and dinner, and maintaining order. They could deduct points or assign detentions to students who broke the rules. They were required to patrol on a regular schedule, and they had the right to use the prefects' bathroom.

Once Cedric had finished the briefing, he assigned the prefects their first duty: patrolling the Express. Carina, who had no desire to remain in that compartment a moment longer, was the first one out.

She walked the corridor, and the anger still hadn't left her.

The blood-traitor scum—the only thing the Weasleys had more of than the Malfoys was the number of headstones they'd soon be adding. How dare he.

Yes. Carina had started it. She knew that. But hearing that her brother might die—she had not been able to hold herself together.

Because.

"...Who would ever want... a war...!"

It was a future that could actually come.

Carina was biting her lip, about to move on to the next carriage, when a voice reached her.

"Why the long face?"

Familiar. Achingly familiar. A voice she had heard so often, for so long, that she would have known it without turning around.

A piece of herself—lodged in memory, in the quiet corner of her chest—was speaking to her.

Carina didn't turn.

"This section is for prefects only. What brings you here, Potter?"

Her voice came out cold enough to raise goosebumps. Even she was surprised she could sound like that.

Harry approached anyway, as though the temperature hadn't changed at all.

"Came to see Ron, Hermione, and Cedric. I told them I'd come find them, but they never showed up no matter how long I waited. Prefect duties keeping them busy, I suppose?"

"Extremely busy. For instance—"

Carina stepped into his path and fixed him with a look.

"Dealing with ordinary students who have no business loitering in the prefects' section."

"A punishment? If it's from you, I'll take it gladly."

Despite the flat refusal, Harry kept coming. He had no sense of personal space whatsoever. One step away now.

He's not going to back off. The moment she reached that conclusion, Carina drew her wand and pointed it at him.

Even Harry's easy expression shifted slightly—his eyes widened just a fraction.

Carina spoke with deliberate precision.

"Go back to your compartment, Potter. This is your last warning. If you don't follow the rules, I will take 'appropriate' action."

Points, detention, or a hex. Whatever it took to make him leave.
She said it in a voice stripped of every last trace of warmth. When she pressed the wand closer to emphasize the point, Harry raised both hands in a gesture of surrender.

A group of students coming from the next carriage spotted them and flinched, then huddled together whispering and retreated in the other direction. Neither Harry nor Carina paid them any attention. They only looked at each other.

About a minute passed. Then Carina said, still staring him down:

"If you understand, then kindly step aside."

"All right. Oh—Carina. One thing before I go."

She had been about to tell him not to use her first name. Instead she held back and listened.

"Parkinson and Montague are making quite a scene a few carriages down. Thought you, as prefect, might want to handle it."

"You came all the way here to complain about students being loud on a train? Potter, do you really go around tattling about every little thing?"

Even if she went, she'd sooner take their side than reprimand them. Carina was Slytherin's prefect. She intended to use every right and responsibility that came with the role in a thoroughly Slytherin fashion.

But the next words made her stomach tighten.

"You sure about that? They were cheerfully telling a group of fourth-year Ravenclaws—'Karkaroff? You don't know the truth!'"

"What? The truth?"

"Yes, the truth. Curious now?"

The truth. Were they about to let slip that Voldemort had returned?

The word they were using might mean something else entirely—but Carina couldn't shake the dread. Montague and Pansy both knew Voldemort had returned, and they were widely regarded as the least discreet people in all of Slytherin.

She had to stop them before they said something irreversible.

Carina turned and moved quickly toward the rear carriages.

"It had better be something worth hearing, Potter. If it's nothing, I'm taking five points from you."

"Make it a detention instead. Preferably Potions—I've got a recipe for Butterbeer I've been meaning to try."

"If you'd like a drink that will kill you the moment it touches your lips, by all means."

She left him behind and went to find them.

Five carriages from the prefects' section, and still no sign of them. Carina moved methodically, checking each compartment as she passed—and noticed something odd about the atmosphere.

Students had shut themselves in with the doors firmly closed, clustered in small groups, reading something together.

Some were reading the Daily Prophet. But a surprising number were passing around a small magazine, handling it with a kind of furtive reverence. They kept poking their heads out into the corridor as though afraid of being caught. Carina nearly collided with two of them.

What on earth is that? She was curious, but finding Montague and Pansy came first. She pressed on.

It didn't take long. She stepped into the next carriage and heard Pansy's grating, carrying voice immediately.

"Ha! Seriously? Someone actually paid money for this rubbish? Must be idiots with Galleons to burn!"

Carina strode to the compartment and threw the door open. Inside: Pansy, Montague, a Ravenclaw girl she didn't recognize, and Ginny Weasley. All eyes turned to her.

Carina planted a hand on her hip.

"Pansy. What are you saying that's loud enough to be heard through the walls?"

"Oh, Carina! Look at this!"

Pansy was still giggling as she thrust a magazine forward. The same one the other students had been passing around.

Carina checked the title first.

[The Quibbler]

She'd never paid it much attention, but the lurid cover and the headline—SHOCKING! SPONTANEOUS EMERGENCE OF THE SELF-MULTIPLYING STIGLANDROW?!—told her everything she needed to know. A third-rate gossip rag with nothing of substance inside.

What could possibly be in here. Curiosity got the better of her. She read the section Pansy was pointing to.

And stopped.

[THE DARK WIZARD WHO KILLED BARTY CROUCH—WHAT IS KARKAROFF'S TRUE PURPOSE?!

In the interest of public safety and the common good, this reporter has been tracking Igor Karkaroff, a dangerous Dark wizard who has resurfaced after years of silence—and has uncovered a staggering truth. Karkaroff has been searching for a means to resurrect the dead. According to an anonymous source known only as 'H,' his appointment as Durmstrang's headmaster was motivated entirely by access to forbidden dark arts available nowhere else, and the murder of Barty Crouch was carried out to obtain a necessary sacrifice.

'He spent the entire Triwizard Tournament hunting for prey. His target was a champion who might perish in an unfortunate accident during the competition.' H implied that Harry Potter was that intended prey.

'Harry Potter is the sworn enemy of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named. Offering up one's enemy as a sacrifice for resurrection... it's a cliché, but it works.'

The sharp-eyed reader will grasp what lies beneath these words. Yes—Karkaroff has been working in the shadows to bring about the return of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, the very figure who once plunged our world into terror.]

Carina's hands trembled as she read on. This was deeply, deliberately misleading.

Voldemort had already returned. Karkaroff had rejoined him in the final moments before the resurrection, offering something in exchange for his pardon.

But this article had twisted the sequence of events just enough—framing Voldemort's return as something imminent, something Karkaroff was still working toward, with Karkaroff cast as the architect.

Not the truth. But close enough to be dangerous. Carina was still staring at the page when Pansy cried out, oblivious to everything churning beneath the surface.

"It says Karkaroff's been conducting a secret ritual! And do you know what the ingredients are? Bone dust and blood and flesh weighing the same as a human body, all combined with alchemical transmutation! These people clearly don't know the first thing about alchemy!"

"Be quiet, Pansy."

How could Pansy not grasp the gravity of this? She had been told to keep Voldemort's return secret. They all had.

As long as the Ministry refused to acknowledge it, perhaps the damage was limited. But that was precisely the problem. Someone out there was willing to distort the truth just enough to get it out—and they were doing it.

Carina held up The Quibbler and looked around the compartment.

"Whose is this?"

"Mine."

The answer came from a Ravenclaw girl with tangled, dirty-blonde hair that fell to her waist. Her eyebrows were faint, and her slightly protuberant eyes gave her a vaguely dreamy look. She seemed mildly annoyed, and her voice carried a thin edge of hostility.

"Give it back. I was still reading the part about the Crumple-Horned Snorkack."

Crumple-Horned Snorkack. Carina had never heard of such a creature in her life, and felt confident she never would again. She snapped the magazine shut.

"Your name?"

"Luna. Luna Lovegood."

"Why would you bring something like this on the train?"

"My father is the editor."

The dreamy quality in Luna's voice shifted abruptly. Carina didn't so much as blink.

"How convenient. Saves me the trouble of writing a letter. Tell your father to stop printing this sort of rubbish and come back to reality—before his daughter loses all grip on it too."

Luna was clearly angry. She fixed Carina with an unblinking stare, raised one finger, and began sweeping it back and forth through the air.

Some kind of self-invented hex, Carina supposed. She responded with a faint snort.

"Don't talk about someone's parents like that, Malfoy!"

Ginny Weasley, sitting beside Luna, turned on Carina sharply.

"You have no right to dismiss someone else's work!"

"I stated a fact. Some people find reality too painful to face and prefer to bury themselves in delusion."

Carina said it coolly, but Ginny didn't back down. She shot back without hesitation.

"That's probably why Harry dropped you."

"...What?"

"You're all surface and no substance, but you act so particular—no wonder Harry got fed up. A girl with a mediocre personality and an oversized ego gets old fast. Play hard to get long enough and you end up discarded."

Ginny's tone was light with contempt. Carina, already battered by one Weasley after another, opened her mouth and found nothing came out. Pansy erupted on her behalf.

"Discarded! Harry Potter was Carina's plaything, not the other way around!"

Pansy's voice rose sharply.

"Who in their right mind takes Potter seriously? He's on borrowed time! That was just a bit of fun! When the Dark Lord returns, Potter will be the first to—"

"Pansy Parkinson!"

Carina's voice rang out before Pansy could finish. It was sharp enough that heads appeared in the corridor outside, craning to see what was happening.

Pansy and Montague went rigid. The realization of how close they'd come to a serious mistake crossed both their faces.

Carina looked at them steadily and pointed toward the door.

"Stop talking nonsense and get back to your compartment. Now."

They nodded and went. Carina watched them go, and when she turned back, Ginny was wearing a smile of quiet satisfaction.

"You've got your hands full, haven't you. Harry's ex-girlfriend—prefect duties, keeping your lot in line. How exhausting."

Ginny looked as though she was enjoying every second of it.

"Look on the bright side—you'll be so busy you won't have time to think about your love life. Convenient excuse for getting dumped, really."

Carina was quiet for a moment. She looked at Ginny.

Ginny Weasley. The girl who had been deceived by a diary and opened the Chamber of Secrets. Harry Potter had fought a Basilisk to save her life, and from that moment on, she had looked up to him the way Slytherin first-years looked up to Carina—reaching toward something just out of reach.

But now Harry and Carina were over, and the Weasley household had been drawing closer to Harry. The distant admiration had curdled into something more immediate. More presumptuous.

If Carina had to sum Ginny Weasley up in a single word.

Jackal.

A small, scavenging creature. Nothing more.

"Are you angling for what he left behind?"

Carina's voice had recovered its ease. She let the words come out almost lazily.
Of course, Carina could not be with Harry. Voldemort had returned, and that made them enemies—people who must never be close again. That was simply the reality.

Even so. Carina was still far ahead. She let out a small, quiet sound of amusement.

"Go ahead, Miss Weasley. I'll even cheer you on."

The tone of someone looking down at a challenger from an unassailable height. Carina smiled pleasantly and offered the words like a gift.

Ginny's eyes narrowed.

"Still acting superior after being dumped. That's exactly why you—"

"But you'd do well to remember something, Miss Weasley,"

Carina cut her off and let the words unspool deliberately.

"Wherever you go with him, it's somewhere he and I have already walked. Whatever part of him you touch, my hands have been there first. Even the moment that might be your first kiss—for him, it will be one kiss among dozens. And afterward, I imagine he'll find himself making comparisons."

Harry Potter's first love was Carina Malfoy.

Even if he were to be with Ginny Weasley, everything between them would be a second.

"Whatever you do together—it will all be something we did first. You are second."

Carina let the word land. Love, she implied, had an order—and that order created a kind of arithmetic that could not be undone.

The shock that moved through Ginny's eyes was visible. Her lips trembled. A mean, low satisfaction rose in Carina's chest at the sight.

She knew she shouldn't. And yet the feeling was almost intoxicating.

"Still, I do wish you well, Miss Weasley. Work hard. Make him forget his first with your second." She paused. "When you're actually together, of course."

Carina turned with a clean, bright smile and pulled the compartment door shut behind her with a decisive click.

Something thudded against the inside of the door. Not her concern. Carina walked the corridor between carriages, riding the cheap, sharp thrill of it.

But a petty victory was like cheap liquor—pleasant while it lasted, and hollow the moment it wore off.

With each carriage she passed through, the mood settled heavier on her.

Ginny had an after. A second chance to eclipse the first, and if not that, a third or a fourth—a future she could keep layering over the past.

Carina had nothing.

It was over.

They were enemies now. Enemies, and that was the end of it.

She had provoked Ron and Hermione. She had all but declared war on Luna and Ginny. Better to make a clean break of it—cut everything off completely.

She was on the opposite side now, in every sense that mattered.

Carina was making her way back toward the prefects' compartment when she nearly walked into someone.

"It's been a while. Have you been well?"

Harry Potter. He was standing there with a copy of The Quibbler in one hand, giving her a small wave.

Anonymous source 'H.' A pseudonym he hadn't even bothered to disguise. The identity of the person who had tipped off the magazine required no particular effort to deduce.

Carina tilted her chin toward the magazine.

"You're the one who gave them the story."

"Yes."

"What do you think you're doing?"

"You already know. A small act of defiance. Mm—this isn't the best place to talk. Want to step inside?"

Harry gestured toward an empty compartment. Carina had things to say. She fixed him with a look and went in.

Alone together in the empty compartment, Harry cast a Mirror Charm on the glass panel of the door, then added a No-Entry Jinx and a Locking Charm. The room became completely sealed.

Nothing that happened here would reach the outside.

Harry pocketed his wand.

"I'm going to keep doing things like this. Outmaneuver the Ministry, raise awareness, let people know he's back."

"That ridiculous magazine is your plan? How remarkably sophisticated."

Carina's voice was dry. Harry answered without any change in expression.

"That article was published in Xenophilius Lovegood's personal magazine—Luna Lovegood's father. The piece was written by Rita Skeeter. People will pick it up thinking it's gossip, but they'll read it out of curiosity. And each time, the content shifts—becomes more serious. That he's about to return. That he's already back and gathering followers. Slowly, like rain soaking through cloth, people come to understand the truth."

"Impressive strategy, Potter. I'll give you that."

Carina's tone was still sardonic.

"But why are you telling me?"

"Because I wanted to show off. And because I didn't want you to be caught off guard."

Harry answered with his usual easy candor. Carina looked at him and made a dismissive sound.

"It's a fool's errand, Potter. People don't learn the truth from gossip. Gossip is gossip no matter what you put in it. As long as the Ministry refuses to acknowledge his return, you'll be backed into a corner eventually."

"Maybe. But it's better than doing nothing, isn't it? The more options, the better."

Harry shrugged, unbothered.

"We clashed for a long time before that, but in the end the diary brought us into an alliance. And then something more, after that."

At the mention of the diary, Carina went very still. The diary—the Malfoy family's most dangerous liability, the lever that had first pulled her into Harry's orbit. There had been a time when it had kept her tethered to him.

But she had no intention of being tethered by it any longer. The Horcrux was destroyed. Whether Harry held the remains or not, the danger was the same.

"I'll say it again, Potter."

He probably wouldn't try it, but Carina intended to be clear.

"Don't think about using that to threaten me. You can't reveal that the Horcrux was destroyed either—the moment you do, he'll hide the rest somewhere no one will ever find them."

Harry looked at her. He read the resolve in her clear eyes—and the emotion beneath it.

Eyes that were working hard to see him as an enemy. They were honest eyes. Startlingly honest.

He said, out of nowhere:

"Can I kiss you?"

"What?"

"I heard you called Hermione a Mudblood."

"...She told you already? Honestly, of all the—"

Carina clicked her tongue softly. Harry took a step toward her.

"I know you were trying to draw a line. But you still shouldn't use that word. You know what happened last time. Or—does this mean you want to be kissed again?"

"Rubbish. Get out."

Carina said it and moved to push past him. Harry caught her wrist and turned her around.

It was a slender wrist. Thinner than he remembered. She'd lost weight—when he pulled her, barely any force at all, her whole body swung toward him.

Carina's eyes went wide. Harry took her chin in his hand and kissed her.

"Mm—"

She froze, eyes wide, too startled to react.

Two breaths passed. Then Carina came back to herself and pushed him away. It wasn't a forceful push, but Harry stepped back.

The distance opened between them. Carina's face was flushed—anger or embarrassment, or both—and she was pressing her sleeve against her lips.

Resenting what he'd done. Resenting herself for not pulling away sooner. She looked up at him with eyes that held the full weight of that contradiction. The faint glimmer of tears made the storm inside her legible.

After a long silence, Carina spoke.

"...This is the last time."

"I hope not."

"If you ever touch me without permission again... I'll kill you."

"You should threaten never to see me again. That's far more frightening than dying."

Carina turned sharply away from his easy smile. One sleeve still pressed to her mouth, she walked out of the compartment without looking back.

Harry stood alone for a moment, fingertips brushing his lips, savoring the aftermath. Brief and yet not brief at all. The kiss had carried too many feelings to be simple, and it had tasted different for it—but it was still sweet.

He left the compartment with a quiet, settled feeling in his chest.

With both of them gone, the space that had been their private meeting place became an empty compartment again. Still and quiet, as though holding its breath long after they had left—jolting faintly at intervals, keeping its silence.

Some time passed.

The overhead rack rattled.

A space no adult—no child of any reasonable size—could have fit inside. Its door swung open on its own. A blond boy climbed out, face contorted in an expression of profound suffering, legs numb enough that he half-collapsed onto the seat below. He sat there, visibly shaken in multiple ways.

"...S... sis...?"

Draco had slipped away from his friends to come find Carina—meaning to surprise her in an empty compartment. But Harry and Carina had walked in before he could do anything, and in a panic he had crammed himself into the overhead rack.

A thoughtless bit of mischief. And yet, by sheer, impossible coincidence—through a feat of endurance his own parents would never have expected of him—he had witnessed everything.

"That can't be right..."

Draco murmured to himself.

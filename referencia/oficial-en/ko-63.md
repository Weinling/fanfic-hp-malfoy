Plans were plans, and nothing more.

No one on the Gryffindor Quidditch team said it aloud, but a silent strategy had taken shape among them: end the game before Ron lets in another goal. They exchanged glances, gave a half-hearted cheer, and filed out onto the pitch. Harry and Ron had not exchanged a single word the entire time.

Until his feet touched the Quidditch pitch, Harry couldn't bring himself to look anyone in the eye. He'd left the dormitory early that morning to avoid Ron's face, yet hadn't gone near the Great Hall either.

He felt like a Basilisk. If he let someone into his gaze, he might wound them—that vague, nagging dread had made him withdraw into himself.

The Slytherin players strode onto the pitch with their usual swagger, and Carina took her position across from him, but Harry couldn't bring himself to look at her face properly either.

"Right then, on my whistle—begin!"

At Madam Hooch's signal, the players launched into the air as one.

Harry immediately swept a circuit of the pitch in search of the Snitch. First match on the Firebolt. The broom cut through the air lighter than ever—and yet Harry couldn't find so much as a glimmer of gold. He was drifting aimlessly around the pitch, going through the motions.

The lack of practice had clearly taken its toll. Harry pressed on, scanning every corner of the stadium with growing unease.

"Oi, Harry! Your Keeper looked just like the portrait outside our common room—give him the password and he opens right up, automatic!"

Montague of Slytherin jeered at him from across the pitch. Harry ignored him. If he responded, he wasn't sure he could stop himself from knocking Montague off his broom.

He was scanning the air for the Snitch when a burst of cheering erupted from the Slytherin stands. Every time someone bellowed "Weasley is our King!", Harry knew Ron had let in another goal. He forced himself not to look and kept searching.

"...And Slytherin extends their lead. One hundred and twenty to forty..."

Harry's jaw tightened. Eighty points already. If this dragged on much longer, Ron might give up a hundred and sixty. It wasn't losing that bothered Harry most—it was the thought of Ron's face afterward. He pushed harder, hunting for the Snitch.

"Ten more points, Weasley! You really are our King!"

Even across the wide pitch, the Slytherin players' taunts carried perfectly. Harry swallowed the surge of anger rising in his chest.

He wanted to knock every last one of them off their brooms right now. One well-aimed spell, one moment of letting go—it would be so easy.

Foals who don't know their place. One simple charm and they'd scatter like autumn leaves.

Do it. Grind them into the fallen leaves and walk right over them...

Harry wrenched his gaze away. And that was when he spotted it—a tiny golden sphere glinting near Montague's ear as the Slytherin player continued his jeering.

Harry drove the Firebolt forward instantly. In his urgency to seize the Snitch, he flew straight at it without a single feint. Carina saw him move and gave chase immediately.

Both rode Firebolts, but Harry's had been fractionally slower ever since it went into the lake during the Triwizard Tournament the previous year. They reached for the Snitch at the same moment. Harry's arm was longer, but Carina's hand swept past his, angling toward the gold.

Shove her. She's light—one push and she'll roll right off that broom...

Harry clenched his teeth and closed his fist. His fingers scraped across the back of Carina's hand, barely nudging her trajectory off course. She closed her fingers around empty air. In that instant, Harry snatched the Snitch cleanly.

Lee Jordan, who had been watching in breathless silence, erupted.

"Harry Potter has caught the Snitch! Gryffindor wins!"

The final score: two hundred and ten to one hundred and eighty, Gryffindor. Had Harry been even a moment slower, they would have lost.

Gryffindor had won, regardless. That would help Ron's mood, at least a little.

While everyone else celebrated, Ron—who had conceded eighteen goals—descended from the pitch looking utterly crushed. Harry was still clutching the Snitch, moving toward him to say something, when—

"Congratulations, Weasley. You won this time."

Carina had landed beside them without Harry noticing. The words were congratulatory; her expression was anything but. Her beautiful face looked down at Ron with undisguised contempt.

"How does it feel, riding in on Harry Potter's coattails—and your twin brothers'? You barely scraped through today because those three carried your weight. Hmm. How long do you think that can last?"

Ron's face crumpled. Harry felt irritation boiling up inside him and shook his head.

Please. Just stop. Watching his closest friend and the person he cared most about tear into each other while he stood helplessly between them—it was its own particular kind of pain.

Harry stepped toward Ron with his arms open.

"Ron. You played well enough."

He deliberately turned his back on Carina, trying to steady Ron. But her voice carried perfectly even with Harry between them.
"I can only imagine how hard your parents worked to raise you, Weasley—blood traitors that they are. You're rather like tea that's been steeped too many times. Even pure-blood stock, brewed over and over, apparently produces something as watery as you."

That was too far. Even Fred and George, who had made an art form of winding up their younger brother, curled their fists at that. This had crossed a line they didn't cross.

Why say that? Was losing the match really so unbearable?

Don't look for reasons, Harry. She was always like this. A spoiled girl who looks down on everyone and digs her fingers into wounds. You've been staring at her good side so long you forgot.

His temple throbbed. Harry willed someone—anyone—to step in and end this. But Madam Hooch was occupied berating a player for an earlier foul and seemed entirely oblivious.

Carina kept going.

"The limitations of unplanned children. Then again, what plans could their parents have had? If they'd had any sense, they wouldn't have earned the name blood traitors."

She's lost her mind.

Look around you. Three Weasleys standing right here, and she's come alone to say this? Did she actually think they'd just stand there and take it?

Arrogant. And stupid.

Bite her, Potter. Sink your teeth into that white throat, tear it open, leave her bloody. Lose her voice and she'll be the most beautiful doll in the world.

Wouldn't she be worth more as a doll, Potter...?

Katie and Angelina had both grabbed Fred to keep him from charging at Carina. Harry caught George by the arm and stepped forward ahead of him.

Pure hostility moved him. The urge to tear and claw at something overwhelmed him. Harry reached out toward Carina's white throat, the way he'd reached for the Snitch—

Wait. I'm—I'm about to strangle Carina?

Harry finally looked at her face properly.

Beneath the faint fear in her eyes, there was something else: a braced, deliberate set to her lips, as though she had already accepted the pain coming her way. The smile she wore was a mask, and behind it she was calculating—accounting for Harry, for Ron, for something beyond them both.

When Harry hesitated, the voice pressed him.

Go for the throat—

That's strange. That can't be right. You're not me, are you?

Harry ground his teeth and pulled his outstretched hand back. Instead of closing it around Carina's throat, he swung his arm—and slapped her across the cheek.

Crack.

The dry sound rang out, and Carina's face snapped to the side.

The Quidditch pitch went silent. It was probably the first time in Hogwarts history.

The Gryffindors who had been celebrating, the Slytherins who had been jeering—every last one of them clamped their mouths shut and looked on.

Harry, who had delivered the single clean slap, said flatly:

"Shut your mouth, Malfoy."

That was the signal. Shouts and curses, cheers and jeers—a wall of noise somewhere between uproar and chaos—flooded the pitch.

Madam Hooch blew her whistle so hard she nearly dropped it, flung her broom aside, and bore down on Harry with long, furious strides.

"What on earth do you think you're doing!"

Harry saw it through the pale gold hair—a flush of red spreading across Carina's cheek. His doing. He was probably the first person ever to have struck her.

Carina hadn't moved. Her face was hidden behind her hair. He was curious what expression she wore, but he couldn't find the nerve to lift that curtain of blonde and look at the mark he'd made. He stayed still.

Madam Hooch's voice rose.

"Good heavens. Striking the opposing Seeker with physical violence! You—go to your Head of House this instant. Now!"

Harry left the Quidditch pitch without a word. The cheers and jeers faded behind him as he walked back toward the castle.

He arrived outside Professor McGonagall's office and waited. A short while later, McGonagall swept in behind him. She looked at Harry with an expression that was more searching than angry.

"What possessed you, Potter? Striking the opposing Seeker on the pitch? In front of everyone?"
Rather than simply reprimanding him, McGonagall wanted to understand why. Harry said nothing. She exhaled slowly and pressed him.

"Was it because you thought the Weasley twins were about to do something first?"

Partly, yes. But even at their most furious, Fred and George wouldn't have ganged up on Carina together.

The real reason Harry had slapped Carina was that Carina had wanted him to.

She had goaded Ron precisely because she wanted someone to strike her. He didn't yet know why—but if someone was going to lay a hand on her, better him than the Weasley brothers.

He would understand the reason in time. But before that—

Hey. What are you, exactly? You've been steering me toward nothing but things I'll regret.

Harry addressed the voice inside him. He had begun to suspect that what he'd taken for his own impulses was something alien and malevolent. Now he was certain. And that left him with one thing to do.

Snape. Dumbledore told me to learn how to protect my mind from him.

He had absolutely no desire to. But there was no choice. Someone was going to get hurt if this continued.

Educational Decree Number Twenty-Five.

Dolores Umbridge is hereby appointed Hogwarts High Inquisitor.

The Hogwarts High Inquisitor shall hold the following authority:

1. The right to observe, evaluate, and adjust the lessons of other professors.

2. The right to strip any professor of their position upon appropriate review if found lacking in competence.

3. The right to modify, at independent discretion, the punishments, permissions, and privileges applicable to students.

Issued by Minister for Magic Cornelius Fudge.

---

"Thank you, Miss Malfoy."

Umbridge accepted the decree with a smile that stretched nearly to her ears. New authority, and the paperwork to prove it—these things never failed to make Umbridge happy.

She heaped praise on her hidden collaborator without restraint.

"Things have gone so much more smoothly with your assistance, Miss Malfoy. A violent incident on Hogwarts grounds, and no appropriate disciplinary action taken... Thanks to your father's vigorous representations to the Ministry, I've now secured the authority to revise disciplinary measures myself. It's all down to you, Miss Malfoy, for stepping forward in such an unpleasant business."

"Think nothing of it. It served my interests as well."

Carina tilted her face deliberately, letting the red mark show.

"Harry Potter pointed the finger at Igor Karkaroff as the mastermind, and my father—who had dealings with the man—has been on the receiving end of suspicious looks ever since. Tsk. When baseless rumours like that spread, the world becomes unstable. The source needs to be stopped."

"Quite right, Miss Malfoy!"

Umbridge looked as though she might leap for joy.

"Pure-blood families are always so much easier to reason with. Compared to those irrational, violent, authority-defying children, you are an absolute angel, Miss Malfoy."

Carina answered with a smile and nothing more. Umbridge treated her with every warmth and kindness at her disposal and pledged her full cooperation.

On the walk to the hospital wing to have the mark seen to, Carina touched her cheek absently as she went.

This was what she had wanted. What she had planned for. Every step of it had been calculated. But it was a well-known fact that reason and feeling run on separate tracks. The blow had been far gentler than she'd expected—and yet, having been struck by Harry, an unbearable wave of grief washed over her that she couldn't hold back.

Carina muttered under her breath.

"...That hurt, you idiot."

Still. Her father now had grounds to lodge a formal complaint with the Ministry—his daughter had been struck, after all. Under the guise of outrage, under the pretence of protest, he would move in and out of the Ministry building, using the visits to investigate the Department of Mysteries hidden in its depths.

That was the real reason Carina had provoked the Weasley brothers. To give her father, entrusted with a critical mission by the Dark Lord, whatever small advantage she could.

Carina touched her cheek once more and walked on toward the dormitory.

First year ended in disaster. Not just any disaster—the kind with no floor beneath it, the kind Carina had never experienced before and hoped never to experience again.

Slytherin's victory had been all but certain. The Great Hall draped in silver and green, the students sitting tall with their chins lifted, not even bothering to look down at their plates—that was how it had felt.

Then that cowardly Dumbledore opened his mouth and, without any context or justification whatsoever, handed Gryffindor a hundred and sixty points. Like some deranged Father Christmas, scattering points with no logic and no basis.

The banners that had covered every surface vanished, replaced by red and gold—and the loss that came with that transformation was something no one could understand unless they had lived it. Some summer camp somewhere apparently pulled similar stunts, but even they didn't run their summer camp for an entire year.

The deflation alone was enough to leave the Slytherin students feeling genuinely defeated.

And then there was what Dumbledore actually said.

A chess match? A test of knowledge? And what else—standing against the darkness? Protecting the Philosopher's Stone from an intruder? Fifty points each to Weasley, Granger, and Potter?

What an absurd story. It was a lie. A fabrication. Ten-year-olds couldn't have done any of that, and even if they had, handing out points like party favors made no sense whatsoever.

But Carina's anger ran deeper than that, for a different reason entirely.

What if they really had done it? What if they had genuinely faced Professor Quirrell and defeated him? That would mean Carina was far, far behind them.

That was what bothered her most.

"I could have done it too, if I'd had the chance!"

Carina fumed. On the Hogwarts Express, Crabbe sat across from her in the same compartment and said, with some reluctance:

"That was, uh, three months ago now…"

"A grudge must be remembered forever, Crabbe. That's the only way they'll know to fear us and watch their step."

"Except the one we're talking about is Dumbledore… Fear us? He'd probably just find us adorable."

Carina fixed Crabbe with her sharpest glare until he shrank back into his seat. Then she crossed her arms and gave a contemptuous sniff.

"Hmph. Well, he'll have reason to fear us soon enough. Because his position as Headmaster is about to become very precarious."

"What? Is that true?"

Goyle leaned forward with sudden interest. Carina shrugged.

"You know my father is the chairman of the Hogwarts Board of Governors? He told me something is going to happen at Hogwarts this year. Something significant enough to bring Dumbledore down."

"Like what?"

"I don't know the details. He didn't tell me everything. But it won't touch us—I can promise you that."

Goyle grinned from ear to ear, nodding vigorously.

"Dumbledore deserves to be thrown out! A foolish Headmaster who does nothing but coddle Gryffindor has no place near us!"

"Exactly. The humiliation, the shame… Slytherin has endured it for far too long. Eight consecutive years, and he throws it all away with that ridiculous stunt… It's time for revenge, Dumbledore!"

The two Slytherin girls laughed with sharp, unpleasant satisfaction. Between them, Crabbe alone wore a worried expression, quietly unwrapping a Chocolate Frog.

In first year, Carina had competed with Harry Potter in everything. Classes, school life, house points—she and Harry had clashed at every turn.

But there was one area where Carina could never win.

Not because of her skill, her character, or her qualifications—but because Hogwarts rules made it impossible. And because Harry Potter alone had been granted an exception that placed him beyond those rules.

Quidditch. The sport of the wizarding world.

Carina was a witch, and her passion for Quidditch was anything but ordinary. True to her nature—she couldn't stand being anything less than the protagonist—she had been seriously eyeing the Seeker position.

The Seeker was the most critical role in the game, the one who decided victory or defeat. The fact that Harry Potter had gotten there before her, and not just gotten there but been recognized for genuine talent and made the youngest Seeker in a century as a special exception—that had stung.

If Harry had been mediocre, she might have laughed it off and waited for the following year. But watching Harry Potter, Carina had felt something she would never admit aloud: a wall.

He had handled a broomstick for the first time in his life as though it were an extension of his own body.

He had caught up to her with both hands off the handle.

And then he had reached out toward her as she fell—

If he had been some hopeless Weasley type, she would have dismissed him with a sniff. But whenever she thought of Harry, something knotted itself inside her—gratitude and guilt, resentment and envy, all tangled together.

Carina shook her head. There was no point getting sentimental.

"But this year will be different."

She said it as she pulled on her Slytherin Quidditch robes.

She had spent the entire holiday practicing, even buying her own personal Snitch to train with every day. While Harry Potter had been rotting away in some Muggle house for months, Carina had elevated her game considerably.

And with a small measure of her father's influence, she had secured the Seeker position on the Slytherin team.
It was perfect. The Quidditch robes fit as though they had been made for her from the start, and the sky itself seemed to bless her debut practice session—cloudless and impossibly clear. Carina tossed her platinum hair, which seemed to shine with particular brilliance today, and declared with supreme satisfaction:

"Just you wait, Harry Potter. I'm going to crush you fair and square!"

She picked up her new Nimbus 2001. The grip felt exactly right in her hand.

The Slytherin team's practice session happened to overlap with Gryffindor's, and the two teams came face to face.

Carina, immaculate in her Slytherin robes, stepped forward and stood directly in front of Harry Potter. His expression was deeply satisfying. He stared at her with his mouth open, as though he couldn't quite believe what he was seeing.

Carina smiled with cool confidence and lifted her chin.

"My father made a gift of broomsticks to the Slytherin Quidditch team."

"Oh. Er. Good for you."

"You don't understand what I'm saying, Potter. The team you'll be facing is the strongest it's ever been. The latest Nimbus 2001—better than yours—and me as their Seeker."

Carina tossed her platinum hair and squared her shoulders. Harry still said nothing. He was clearly intimidated by the Slytherin team's display of strength, Carina decided.

Then Fred stepped forward, his face twisted in distaste.

"Your father's Lucius Malfoy, isn't he? Must've sold off all those Dark Arts objects at the pawnshop. Sudden windfall, was it?"

Marcus Flint moved to intercept him.

"Didn't expect you to be throwing Carina's father's name around, Weasley. Oh, but now that you mention it—wasn't it your father who raided Mr. Malfoy's home this summer on some baseless charge? Found nothing illegal, as it turned out, and got himself reprimanded for overstepping his authority? Bit desperate, really, when you're that strapped for cash."

Flint watched Fred and George bristle with visible amusement, then rolled his shoulders with easy contempt.

"Now, would you mind clearing the pitch? Our team needs to test our new Seeker with our new brooms. As for your lot and those ancient Cleansweeps—well. Better suited for sweeping desks, I'd say."

Gryffindor and Slytherin squared off over the Quidditch pitch, neither side yielding. Then Ron and Hermione, curious about the commotion, came walking over from the far side.

"What's going on?"

Ron asked Harry.

"And why is she here making a scene?"

"I'm the Slytherin team's Seeker, Weasley."

Carina said it with insufferable smugness.

"And I was showing off the broomsticks my father gave the Slytherin team. Things your family could never dream of affording."

Ron's jaw dropped at the sight of seven brand-new broomsticks. Carina's mood improved further, and she added with a smirk:

"Save up enough Galleons and maybe you could buy one too. If the museum agrees to take that old wreck off your hands first."

The Slytherin players burst into loud, raucous laughter. Hermione, unable to hold herself back any longer, shot back sharply:

"At least no one on the Gryffindor team bought their way in."

"Shouldn't you count how many people on your team can actually afford anything first, Granger? Though looking at those Cleansweeps—museum pieces, the lot of them—I suppose there's no need to bother."

Carina sneered at Gryffindor. Hermione's expression darkened.

"Even if they could afford it, the others wouldn't have accepted. They want to be judged on their own ability, not on how well they flatter you."

Several Slytherin players flinched. It was a sore point they had all been quietly aware of but had chosen not to voice—which made the jab land all the harder.

Marcus Flint's face contorted with fury.

"Shut your mouth, Mudblood. No one gave you permission to speak."

The word landed like a stone thrown into still water. A great uproar followed—Gryffindor students erupted, and even Carina turned to stare at Flint.

The Slytherin players responded as though they had anticipated this, but Carina herself stood motionless, momentarily blank.

In first year, she had used that word and ended up in a spectacular fight with Harry—and ever since, the word had sat uneasily with her. Saying it felt like inviting back the memory of that day: the humiliation of sobbing in front of everyone.

And there was the other image she couldn't shake loose—Harry Potter, covered in blood, walking toward her one step at a time, his eyes fixed on her.

But regardless of Carina's own reluctance, it was a word someone else could always say. Fred and George lunged at Flint immediately; Montague shoved his way between them. Then Ron pulled out his wand and shouted:

"You'll pay for that!"

Flint sneered and sidestepped the spell. A flash of green light crackled through the air, and the hex, deflected at an odd angle, shot sideways—and disappeared into the space where Carina was standing.

"Aaagh!"

Carina was knocked flat onto the pitch. Every head turned. Montague and Flint, still furious, raised their fists toward the nearest Gryffindor players. The two teams were on the verge of an all-out brawl.

Then Harry moved—faster than anyone—and crouched down beside Carina.
"Are you all right?"

Carina tried to say something. Nothing came out. Then, with a small belch, she deposited several slugs onto the ground beneath her.

The pitch went utterly silent. Only the slugs moved, writhing slowly beneath her.

Carina herself seemed unable to believe what was happening. She stared at the slugs she had just produced, wide-eyed—and then felt something rising again and gagged.

"Don't—look! Get—away—urgh!"

She waved her hand frantically. Absolutely not. Under no circumstances could she allow anyone to watch her vomit slugs.

Beyond dignity, beyond pride—the mere fact of having produced slugs was an affront Carina could not accept. She tried to clap her hand over her mouth, as though denying it hard enough could unmake it.

But the sensation of slugs squirming inside her mouth was not something she could endure. The nausea surged, and she opened her mouth again and expelled another wave of them.

"I said don't look! Turn around! All of you!"

People crowded toward her but couldn't bring themselves to touch her. Then Harry put his arm around her to steady her.

"Let—go—of—"

"You need somewhere to hide first."

He said it without any apparent concern for the fact that whatever was soaking into his sleeve was either saliva or slug mucus.

Carina felt tears threatening. Today was supposed to have been the best day of her life. Instead she was retching slugs in front of an audience. And the sensation of them moving on their way up was so vivid and so immediate that she couldn't shake it loose.

She was so wretched, so miserable, she wanted to cry. The tears didn't come—partly out of pride, and partly because every time her eyes welled up, her mouth produced another slug instead.

Thud. A few more slugs hit the ground. Carina screamed in outrage:

"I'll kill you—I'll kill all of you—urgh—every last one—I'm telling my father—no—urgh—ugh!"

"Where should we go—the hospital wing?"

"No!"

Carina refused with vehemence. Sitting in a hospital bed while everyone watched her produce slugs under Madam Pomfrey's attentive care was absolutely not what she wanted.

What Carina needed, above all else, was to be unseen. She needed to get through this somewhere no one's eyes could reach her.

As if reading her thoughts, Harry offered:

"If—if you're looking for somewhere to hide, Hagrid's hut is just over there. What about that?"

"Take me—there!"

"I'll bring you. I know Hagrid well. The rest of you Slytherins—someone go to the hospital wing and ask Madam Pomfrey if she can come by for a moment."

Harry steered Carina toward the forest. As they neared the gamekeeper's hut, the door swung open and Gilderoy Lockhart stepped out, resplendent in a violet cloak.

Carina's eyes went wide. Harry quickly pulled his cloak around her and pressed them both against the stone wall.

"…Do come and find me if you ever need my assistance. I'll even give you a copy of one of my books. Well, then."

Harry held his breath until Lockhart was out of sight, then moved to Hagrid's front door.

He decided not to think about the three slugs squirming at the base of the stone wall.

Once inside the hut, Carina wept. She cried properly—tears and snot, slugs at intervals, hiccupping curses in every direction.

"Hic—sob. All of you. I'm telling my father—he'll make you—regret—every last—"

"Yes. Yes, of course."

Harry rubbed her back with complete naturalness. Occasionally he repositioned the bucket and smoothed her hair back from her face; occasionally he wiped her face with a handkerchief.

Carina expelled her shame and her fury along with the slugs. By rights she should have been exhausted by now, but every time she looked at the bucket filling up, the anger surged back fresh.

She wiped her mouth and shouted:

"Just you wait. If anyone dies at Hogwarts this year—it's all your fault! Every single one of you brought this on yourselves!"

Ron and Hermione arrived just then, Madam Pomfrey in tow. Ron took one look at Carina's murderous expression and muttered:

"Still pretty furious, by the look of it."

"Wouldn't you be? Even if it's Malfoy—she's a girl, and she's been vomiting slugs!"

"Well, I mean, I didn't think she'd be the one to get hit."

Hermione elbowed him in the ribs. Ron yelped.

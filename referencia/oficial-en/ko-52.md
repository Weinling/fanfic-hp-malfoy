--(Interlude, continued from Age Ten)--

"Harry Potter. Your punishment has been decided."

Professor McGonagall delivered the news to Harry. For all that he had been acting as though he'd lost his capacity for fear, the word punishment made his stomach drop.

A deduction of nearly a hundred points, and a formal punishment. It would end there for now—but if something similar happened again, expulsion was not out of the question. It was that serious.

Up until this moment, Harry had been throwing himself headlong into everything as though he had nothing to lose. But the moment he imagined leaving Hogwarts and returning to Privet Drive, he felt fear for the first time.

"Tonight at nine o'clock. Report to the front gates. Rubeus will be there to guide you."

Professor McGonagall said it coldly. Harry asked:

"Guide us? Are other students being punished too?"

"I intended to punish all of you who were involved in that brawl, but Professor Dumbledore advised against it. He thought it better to make a clear example of the ringleaders instead… So it will only be the two of you—you and Malfoy, the ones who started it."

Professor McGonagall said her piece and strode away. Harry tilted his head at the words just the two of you.

The punishment itself was frightening, of course. If he didn't take it seriously, he could be expelled, and then he'd have to go back to that wretched cupboard on Privet Drive.

But if it was just him and Malfoy… that wasn't so bad, was it?

Harry arrived at the front gates at half past eight and waited. Hagrid turned up about ten minutes later. Malfoy, however, didn't appear until just past nine, and she lingered at a distance, clearly reluctant to draw any closer to Harry and Hagrid.

Hagrid called out in his great booming voice:

"A bit late, aren't you? Right then—let's be off!"

"…Off where?"

Malfoy muttered something under her breath, and Hagrid replied:

"Speak up! Your mouths don't even come up to my waist—if you don't shout, I can't hear you!"

"I said, where are we going?"

"The forest."

"Which forest?"

"Which forest? The one near Hogwarts, of course."

Hagrid said it as though it were obvious. Malfoy's brow furrowed.

"The Forbidden Forest? They say all manner of beasts and magical creatures live there—wolves, even. Why on earth should we have to go into some filthy, dangerous forest?"

"Because it's filthy and dangerous—that's what makes it a punishment, isn't it?"

Hagrid replied. Malfoy had no answer to that.

"If a beast does come at you… you're welcome to fight back with your wands. You've clearly both got energy to spare. If you're going to cast offensive curses, better to aim them at wild animals than at fellow students."

Malfoy looked thoroughly displeased. Harry drifted quietly around to her back and said:

"No creature stupid enough to attack with Hagrid around."

"Eek!"

He had apparently been a little too quiet about it.

Malfoy lurched forward with a startled yelp. She stood there trembling, then pressed her lips together and turned to glare at Harry.

She'd been frightened out of her wits, yet she couldn't seem to find the words to snap at him. Her mouth opened and closed. She looked as though she were about to say something cutting, then caught sight of Harry's face, swallowed, and deliberately turned away.

Harry understood what Malfoy was afraid of.

It wasn't the forest. It was him.

The dark wood was alive with sound—rustling undergrowth that suggested something might burst out at any moment, the low growl of predators stalking prey, a distant howl of pain, the frantic crash of something fleeing through the brush.

Harry walked close to Hagrid's side, every nerve on edge.

Malfoy was just as frightened, that much was obvious. But for some reason she kept her distance from Hagrid, shuffling along with a stiff, uncertain gait.

"Not so bad, is it? There are a great many creatures living in this forest, but most of them—Centaurs, unicorns—are either harmless or simply indifferent. The ones that are hostile to humans are few and far between, and they keep to the deep parts…"

Hagrid seemed to think Malfoy disliked him. But Harry, watching her footsteps carefully, noticed that she was moving away from Harry rather than from Hagrid.

He'd suspected as much, but it seemed she had come to truly fear him. Had he frightened her too badly? Or had she sensed something dark lurking inside him that day?

Either way, the fact that someone he liked was putting distance between them left Harry feeling rather bleak.

"Oh, you two…? Hmm. Doesn't look like you're having much fun."

One who was frightened, and one who was gloomy because of it. Hagrid, caught between them, wore an uncomfortable expression.

Even when it came to punishments, Hagrid could never bring himself to do something he didn't want to do. He made an effort to lift the heavy atmosphere.

"Is it because no interesting animals have shown up? Hmm. A unicorn passed by just a little while ago, actually. You two wait here with Fang for a moment—I'll go and fetch it…"

Hagrid left his hound behind and pushed off alone through the undergrowth. Malfoy started and reached out a hand, then seemed to think better of asking Hagrid for help and curled her fingers into a fist.

Before long, Harry could see her clutching her skirt in a white-knuckled grip.

He couldn't imagine why she was this frightened. He'd had enough—he spoke up.

"You're absolutely terrified. Malfoy. What is it you're so afraid of?"

The sudden question made Malfoy react sharply.

"Wh—what? Me, afraid? Don't be absurd."
Malfoy pressed a hand to her chest and lifted her chin. She was doing her best to project composure, but she would have been better served steadying her voice rather than her posture. It was shaking so badly that Harry half-wondered if someone had hold of her and was rattling her.

"You think I'd be frightened just because I walked into some forest?"

"If it's not the forest—is it me?"

"What?"

The question hit its mark. Malfoy faltered.

The trembling shoulders and the gaze that couldn't quite meet his were proof enough that she was afraid of him.

Carina was enduring the urge to put as much distance between herself and Harry as possible through sheer stubbornness. The sight of her—like a small, terrified herbivore—was almost endearing enough to make him smile, but the fact that he was the source of that terror left a bitter taste in his mouth.

"D-don't be ridiculous. As if I'd be afraid of someone like you."

Her voice broke apart in fits and starts, almost like crying. So this is what it feels like when someone fears you, Harry thought grimly. Not pleasant at all.

He found himself wondering why Voldemort had gone to such lengths to spread terror—to make people tremble at the mere sound of his name. If that was the result, no one would ever be glad to see him.

Harry raised his head.

"Carina Malfoy."

Malfoy flinched and looked at him.

Harry squared his shoulders the way he'd once seen someone do—with confidence. He curved his lips into the easy smile he'd practiced in Diagon Alley, and stepped forward.

"I'm not afraid of this forest. I have my wand. I know plenty of spells. I've even fought a troll alongside my friend Ron and won."

He didn't want her to be afraid of him. Being treated like Voldemort was unbearable.

He'd rather she resented him. Envied him. Competed with him.

Like the first time they'd both been on broomsticks—he wanted her to look at him with clear, defiant eyes. To come at him with that insufferable confidence, determined to win.

"You're the one who's scared, aren't you? How amusing. I never would have thought the great Malfoy—always going on about pure blood—could be such a coward."

So Harry provoked her. He pressed on her pride and her stubbornness.

He wanted to turn her fear of him into indignation. Into rivalry.

"What? Amusing? A coward?"

Exactly as expected. Malfoy bristled and fixed him with a glare.

"Me, frightened? How dare you! You're the one strutting around because you knocked over one troll, Potter! I could take down a troll with a Sneezing Charm!"

"Oh? Then trolls don't scare you?"

"Obviously not. Trolls are nothing but thick skulls with a talent for being durable! Although I suppose being too stupid to feel boredom could count as a virtue—if you're being generous!"

She was back to her usual self—that tone that seemed to look down on everyone who wasn't her.

Harry smiled.

He preferred this. Even when she went too far, even when she made him want to give her a good shove—this was so much better than watching her cower. The arrogance suited her.

Harry grinned and started walking.

"I'm going deeper in. If you're scared, stay here. Fire off a spark and Hagrid will come galloping to your rescue like a knight in shining armour."

"I don't need any knight, least of all a filthy one!"

Malfoy huffed and fell into step behind him. She still wasn't entirely free of her fear—of Harry, and of the Forbidden Forest—a faint unease still clung to her movements.

But Carina Malfoy had always been the sort of person whose stubbornness and pride outweighed everything else. The way she stuck close behind him, refusing to fall behind, made that perfectly clear.

The forest at night had its own quiet charm. A cool breeze tangled through the undergrowth; a nocturnal bird blinked at them and let out a hiccup. Crickets serenaded from every direction, and moonlight filtered through the lattice of branches, casting a faint, uncertain glow on the path ahead.

The moonlight wasn't quite enough. Not to see where he was stepping, and not to see Malfoy's face.

"*Lumos.*"

Harry murmured the spell. Light spilled from the tip of his wand.

They walked together, guided by that single wavering glow at the end of his wand.

"The stars are beautiful out here. Not much air pollution, I suppose."

"Air… pollution? Did someone scatter Ashwinder ash into the atmosphere?"

"What? Is that what causes air pollution? Is that something wizards did?"

"What even is air pollution? Stop talking nonsense and say something I can understand."

They talked about nothing in particular as they walked through the night. In the darkness, the two of them existed inside a small world of light. Around them, only the singing insects and the dense trees. From somewhere deeper in the forest came the distant sound of hooves, but whatever it was kept its distance, watching from afar.

Harry glanced sideways. Malfoy was walking with careful, deliberate steps, still tense—too focused to notice him looking.

This is enough, Harry thought, and was just beginning to feel satisfied—

"Woof!"

Hagrid's hound Fang erupted into frantic barking.

There were bloodstains on a tree stump. Spatters of it, flung in every direction, as though something had writhed in agony. More of it, everywhere.

Harry's expression hardened. He reached out and grabbed Malfoy's arm, pulling her toward him.

"What do you think you're—"

Malfoy snapped at him in a sharp voice, suddenly pressed against his side.
"Don't touch me without permission, Potter!"

"Shh. Just a moment."

Through the tangled branches of an ancient tree, something pale and faintly luminous gleamed. Nearby, amid vast quantities of blood, silver hairs clung to bark and branch.

Malfoy seemed to sense that something was wrong. She went quiet. Harry took a step forward.

And then, in the centre of a small clearing, a white shape came into view.

A unicorn.

Lying still.

Dead.

Harry had never felt a sense of dread so acute. Such a beautiful creature, destroyed so utterly. Its legs were twisted at grotesque angles; its mane lay limp and lifeless against the ground. Something immensely powerful and immensely evil had done this—

And that something was right in front of them. Cloaked and hooded, it had its mouth pressed to the wound in the unicorn's side, drinking.

"*Aaaaah!*"

Malfoy screamed—a raw, horrified sound—and stumbled backward. The hooded figure raised its head and turned toward Harry. Silver blood gleamed on the front of its robes.

His scar ignited. The hooded figure slowly rose to its feet. Fear closed around Harry like a fist.

He shouldn't have brought Malfoy here. Not to a place like this.

"…Run."

Harry stepped in front of her. If he was the one who had led her here, then the responsibility was his.

He didn't know how long he could hold that thing off. But he had to try.

The figure drifted closer. The pain in his scar swelled. Harry gritted his teeth and stepped back—and felt something warm against his hand.

Malfoy. She was frozen solid, too terrified to move.

Too late. The moment to run had passed. The hooded figure advanced, trailing silver blood. It reached out a hand. Harry's head was screaming—he could barely grip his wand. Through the burning agony in his scar, he pulled Malfoy close and held her.

Then—hoofbeats, thundering from behind. Something leapt over Harry and struck the figure hard. The figure dissolved like a shadow, twisting away, and was gone.

Harry pressed a hand to his forehead and looked up. A Centaur stood over them, gazing down at Harry and Malfoy.

"Are you all right?"

This Centaur had pale golden hair that caught the faint light—at least from the waist up. Below, he had the body of a horse: palomino coat, white mane. Powerfully built, but not threatening. Not to them.

Harry let his guard down and looked at Malfoy, still trembling in his arms.

"Are you all right, Malfoy?"

Malfoy shook violently as she spoke.

"Wh—what was that—"

"What was that thing?"

The Centaur did not answer. He studied Harry with quiet intensity, his gaze settling on the scar.

"You are the son of Potter."

He said it in a low voice.

"You should return to Hagrid. The forest at night is dangerous. Especially for you—"

Harry didn't respond. He was looking at Malfoy, scanning her carefully, his eyes moving over her from head to foot with open concern.

She didn't appear to be hurt. Harry let out a slow breath of relief.

"Why didn't you run when I told you to? You should have bolted straight away!"

"I—I was going to—fight—like the troll—"

"You mean you were going to stand and fight, the way I fought the troll?"

Harry stared at her in disbelief.

"That thing was clearly more dangerous than any troll! If you could see you didn't stand a chance, you should have run immediately! Are you out of your mind?"

"What? Out of my mind?"

That did it. Malfoy's eyes flashed. She shoved Harry back and scrambled to her feet, her voice rising sharp and high.

"And what about you? Why were you standing there trying to fight it instead of running?"

"So you could get away! Someone had to run and warn Hagrid!"

"Oh, and you fighting it was going to buy me time, was it? If you wanted even a second's head start, you should have run the other way and drawn it off!"

"That's what I was trying to do, but a certain Malfoy was too frightened to move her feet!"

"I could move my feet perfectly well! I was facing it—I was going to fight! You're the one who was backing away and crashed into me!"

"I was measuring the distance—"

"You were retreating!"

Harry and Malfoy bickered furiously. Above them, the Centaur—Firenze—turned his gaze toward the sky.

Above the glittering constellation of Aries, Venus burned with unusual brilliance.

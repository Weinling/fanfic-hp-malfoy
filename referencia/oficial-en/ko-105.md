# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 105: Decision

The bonfire burns. Flames feed on the logs and grow. A gesture meant to blaze more brilliantly—to carry light and warmth to the whole world—yet it pays no mind to the logs pinned beneath, groaning under its weight.

Neither the fire, nor the people warming themselves by it.

What Carina had brought was a sweetly poisoned temptation. If he simply looked away for a moment, the matter would resolve itself cleanly. There was a man who deserved to die, a method at hand, and someone ready to push him over the edge.

A Horcrux. The dark art of splitting one's own soul—through murder.

Harry's instincts, and the dim knowledge wrested from Voldemort, both cried out that it was possible.

But the reason he had never consciously reached for that method—it wasn't simply because Harry was a stubborn man.

His lips had gone bone dry. Even magical fire, it seemed, could not drive out that particular dryness. Harry forced the words out.

"Carina. That method—"

"What more is there to think about? Right here, right now—is there any better alternative?"

This must be how the devil whispers. The person you love most, in desperation, bearing nothing but pure goodwill and affection—a sweet, sweet temptation.

Just as Adam could not bring himself to refuse the fruit Eve offered, Harry was on the verge of being unable to refuse the forbidden fruit she had brought him.

Harry raised his wand with a trembling hand. Warmth enveloped him. The heat born from the phoenix feather at the wand's core pressed into him.

"Sorry, Fawkes."

A phoenix burns itself to ash and is reborn—but that is a path only creatures born to it can walk.

Mortal beings have no choice but to sustain themselves on the lives of others.

He was sorry to Fawkes, but with this wand made from that feather, today… he would have to commit murder.

"There's no reason not to…."

That was right. There was no other way. To survive, he had to try everything. And besides, the target was an enemy, a traitor, a criminal. If there was any life that could be taken without consequence, it was his—Karkaroff's….

Having steeled himself, Harry looked at Carina once more.

Beside the fireplace, her face half-swallowed by shadow—

Fear was written across it.

"Ah."

It was as though cold water had been poured over his head. Harry looked back and forth between his own hand, the wand in it, and Carina's face.

She had tilted her head slightly downward, as if to keep her expression from showing. Her shoulders were drawn in so tightly he felt he could wrap one arm around them entirely. The confident smile was nowhere to be found. What remained was a girl wearing an expression so dim and unsettled it bordered on something wretched.

The complete opposite of the girl he had met in Diagon Alley, all those years ago. Still lovely—but not the way Harry wanted to see her.

And yet the one who had made Carina look like this.

Was Harry.

"…Let's stop, Carina."

The decision came quickly. Saying it aloud even brought a kind of relief. Harry arrived at his conclusion with clarity.

"Not like this. Murder—splitting a soul by taking someone's life—I can't do something that monstrous."

At Harry's answer, Carina's face crumbled completely. She replied in a trembling voice.

"Why?"

"I'm truly grateful you thought of me. But if I chose this path, it would drag you into it as well. You have no reason to commit any crime—if not for me, you would never have touched Dark Magic at all. I can't dirty your hands because of my circumstances."

Harry gently took Carina's hand and spoke with a quiet, steady smile.

"I want to hold my head up high. I want to face the world with a confident smile. I want to stand with my shoulders back before everyone."

Words he had heard once in Diagon Alley. The force that had driven him forward all this time.

To return to the beginning—to honour what was said then.

"I don't want to disappoint you. Or myself."

Harry confessed what was in his heart.

The flames in the fireplace surged with a sudden roar. In their light, the shadows fell away from Carina's face.
She was looking at him with an expression on the verge of tears—utterly, devastatingly undone.

"…I just wanted you to be alive, no matter what…."

It was precarious. The emotions she had forced high—built up to keep her eyes turned from sin and conscience—collapsed in an instant. If eyes are glass that reflects the soul, then Carina's soul was bleeding. Her silver-grey irises were red with it.

"Whatever I say… you won't do it, will you?"

A single tear fell. A truth serum more potent than Veritaserum. One that compelled honesty simply by being witnessed—

Harry nodded.

"No."

He saw the grief that crossed Carina's eyes, and the relief. And then, trailing after it, the self-loathing. She had sought out the method to create a Horcrux, had even brought Karkaroff to serve as the sacrifice—

And yet she was relieved that Harry had not killed him.

Harry Potter had never studied Legilimency. But in this moment, standing before Carina, he was more perceptive than any Legilimens alive. He reached deep into her emotions and traced each one, one by one.

Carina had urged him to commit murder—and now, finding herself relieved that he hadn't, she despised herself for it. At the same time, she resented Harry for not valuing his own life above all else, and was sickened by the ugliness of that resentment.

But beneath all of it, surpassing all of it, she wanted Harry to survive. Wholly. Completely.

"…I'm sorry. I just…"

And so, no matter how right a choice Harry had made—

"I'm tired."

Some things simply cannot be helped.

Carina pulled her hand free and rose to her feet. Wiping her tears on her sleeve, she crossed the common room and walked out. Not toward the boys' dormitory where she had been lying moments before—but out of the dormitory entirely.

When she was gone, the light and warmth lost their meaning. The roaring fire in the hearth was no longer warm, and though there were no shadows, the light did not reach. Only darkness and silence remained in the Slytherin common room.

The flames burned on, purposelessly….

…And yet.

You two can still find your way back.

If you are still alive, if you have not yet fallen, if you have not yet given up.

…If you had not quarrelled before a young girl and ultimately caused her death. If you had no shackle to bind yourself with for the rest of your life. If only you had acted with a little more wisdom in that moment.

You and I might have become something different.

Albus, you were wrong. I still deny you.

But even as I deny you, I could never change you. I could not even attempt to.

Because it was I who made you carry that guilt, that regret, that chain.

If I had not quarrelled in front of your sister. If I had not, in my jealousy over your family—which I had come to resent as a burden—attacked your brother. You would never have come to think the way you did.

And so I could only be judged by your hand. And you judged me.

…Congratulations, Albus. The legacy you left behind passed my test magnificently. For them… you were worth staking your life on.

But your test is of a far crueller quality than mine. I used to say it sometimes—that you had a far worse character than I did…. It was half in jest at the time, but looking back now, it seems it wasn't entirely wrong.

Those two will find the right path in the end. The path that you and I could never walk….

The moon is bright tonight. There was a time when we looked at the same moon and laid bare our ambitions to each other.

…I find myself missing you tonight.

After that, Carina did not leave the Slytherin dormitory. Harry visited several times, but no matter how hard he tried, every attempt to enter the girls' dormitory sent him bouncing back. Not even Voldemort's power and knowledge could undo the enchantment barring men from Hogwarts' girls' quarters.

And of course, even if he had managed to undo it, it would have made no difference.

With no other option, Harry stood at the dormitory entrance and raised his voice, talking about one thing and another. Stories of his time on Privet Drive, the first spell he had ever cast, the Weasley twins' joke shop.

But no answer came from Carina on the other side.

In the meantime, many visitors came to Hogwarts. Harry handed Karkaroff over to Cedric and informed him that the method had, in fact, not been found—though for Carina's sake, he offered a vague explanation about some misunderstanding.

Naturally, Cedric—who had believed Harry had found a solution—was horrified.
"Wait, hold on! Then what happens? The Ministry is absolutely convinced it exists!"

"I'm counting on you."

"What? Counting on what? On me? Why?"

No one spits in a smiling face. Someone with a particularly disagreeable character might do so in defiance, but Cedric was not that sort of person. Harry smiled and gave him a gentle push, and Cedric, for the time being, took Karkaroff and headed for the Ministry.

He would likely receive a life sentence in Azkaban—but at any rate, he would go on living.

News travels at different speeds depending on who receives it. Moody, who had joined the Aurors in hunting down the remaining Death Eaters, carved out time from his busy schedule to come find Harry.

"Hmph. I hear you didn't find a method."

"Well. That's how it turned out."

"Your nerve is something, given how close the day is…. If you ever need someone to stand as witness for an Unbreakable Vow, say the word. Without a witness, or if someone else kills you first, the Vow weakens. I won't be easy to kill—so I'd be the right choice for the job…."

"Ah. Wouldn't it be better to just do something about me directly, rather than that?"

Moody gave a short, derisive snort.

"The war is over. You've all paid more than enough. We pushed our problems onto you—the cleaning up is ours to do…."

His eye spun in its socket before coming to rest squarely on Harry's face.

"…Though it's a far sight less than what you'll have to pay. Hmph!"

The grotesque, oversized eye that had earned him the name Mad-Eye could not quite meet Harry's gaze—guilt keeping it from doing so. Harry let out a quiet laugh.

Lupin arrived in a hurry. He was still wearing his threadbare suit. He was earning some money, he said, but with a second child on the way, there was nothing to spare on things like new clothes.

After a brief exchange of pleasantries, he glanced at Harry cautiously and spoke with care.

"Harry, this is a method I truly, truly would not recommend, but… have you ever considered becoming a werewolf?"

"Pardon?"

Lupin, who knew better than anyone the dangers and suffering of being a werewolf, averted his eyes as he spoke—as though even suggesting it pained him.

"The werewolf curse is a curse that inflicts severe damage on the soul. It would be an enormous and terrible agony for you—but the soul inside you would suffer far more by comparison. And since you'd be able to brew Wolfsbane Potion yourself, managing it would be easier…."

"Would that still be possible after my birthday? Even with Voldemort's soul outside?"

"I'm not entirely certain…. But at the very least, during the full moon, he wouldn't be able to emerge either."

"The wolf would come out instead."

"That's right."

After a brief discussion, they agreed: if Harry showed signs of instability after his birthday, Lupin would willingly bite him. On a full moon night, of course.

Ron and Hermione had tried to visit, but the Ministry refused their request—on the grounds that Harry's condition was dangerous.

Of course, there was no chance they would do anything to harm Harry. But the Ministry was deeply wary of him. They seemed to believe that Harry, having grown attached to his own life, might flee their surveillance.

Instead, the two sent letters to comfort him.

[I searched through every book I could find, Harry, and I couldn't find any method for splitting a soul. It seems that magic involving souls is generally classified as Dark Magic. No matter how hard I tried to research it, I kept hitting walls….]

Remarkably, Hermione's conclusions were quite close to what Grindelwald had told them.

[If it's a very deep and very dark form of magic, it might be possible. Harry, could you look through the Restricted Section at Hogwarts? There might be something there…]

But Harry was under surveillance, and he had decided not to use Dark Magic—so he accepted her advice in spirit only.

Next came Ron's letter. Ron had a gift for making even the gravest things seem lighter, and his letter was extraordinary from the very first line.

[I heard you nearly found a way to split Voldemort's soul? Dad told me. Well—more precisely, Dad was telling Mum about what he'd heard, and Ginny eavesdropped using Extendable Ears, and I found out from her. Oh, don't worry. I gave Ginny a proper telling-off for listening in on our parents.]

Harry let out a quiet laugh. Even in a letter like this, his friend's warmth came through.

[I tried to come to Hogwarts, but the Ministry said they couldn't grant permission. Fred, George, and Ginny are all gutted about it. Ginny especially… well, let's not get into that. Anyway, mate. I should have been there with you.]

Reading the letter, heavy with longing, Harry steadied himself once more. He chided himself for nearly straying down the wrong path—and resolved that he would face his friends again with his head held high.

And so time passed.

His birthday was one day away.

"The theme of the third task is... a maze!"

Ludo Bagman announced, gesturing toward the Quidditch pitch. Where there should have been open grass, low hedges had grown up to waist height. It looked like a complicated garden for now, but once those hedges grew taller than a person, they would form a labyrinth with walls on every side.

"We'll be filling it with all manner of magical creatures, jinxes, and traps. The Champions must navigate through the maze and find the Triwizard Cup. The Champion with the highest score enters first, and the rest follow in order of their scores. But don't let your guard down—if luck and skill are on your side, even the last Champion to enter could win, so give it everything you've got right to the end!"

"May I ask a question, Mr. Bagman?"

"Of course! Ask away!"

Ludo Bagman flashed a broad white smile in Harry's direction.

"I'll answer whatever I can—within the rules, naturally!"

He gave Harry a conspiratorial wink.

He was supposed to be the impartial judge of the Triwizard Tournament, yet Mr. Bagman had always shown a particular warmth toward Harry. Whether he thought it unfair that Harry was so much younger than the other Champions, or whether he simply had a soft spot for The Boy Who Lived, was hard to say.

Then again, he might simply have wagered a great deal of money on Harry winning.

"We won't be able to see anything from inside, but the spectators watching from above will be able to see everything we're going through in there, won't they?"

"Naturally! There's a reason we chose the Quidditch pitch for the third task!"

Quidditch was a sport played on broomsticks high in the air. The stands, accordingly, were built at a considerable height.

However tall the maze grew—two, perhaps three metres at most—anyone looking down from the stands would have a clear bird's-eye view of the entire thing.

"If the audience couldn't see anything during the Triwizard tasks, they'd be terribly bored—especially after we've gone to the trouble of building a maze like this. Every spectator will be able to see exactly where their Champion is and what they're up against!"

"What if someone in the crowd tries to direct a Champion through the maze?"

"Any Champion who receives outside assistance will face appropriate consequences, naturally. Inside the maze, you'll see only a magically conjured sky overhead, and no one will be able to pass above it. I'm afraid flying over the maze on a broomstick won't be an option this time, Harry!"

The task would unfold in full view of every professor and every student at Hogwarts. Anyone who wanted to interfere would find it impossible.

The maze would be packed with magical creatures and traps designed to test the Champions—but the Ministry of Magic would never permit anything genuinely lethal inside. And even if a dangerous situation arose, Dumbledore would be watching; rescue would come swiftly.

Harry was in no real danger.

In that case.

Harry murmured to himself, a thick fog settling over his thoughts.

Then why was my name put in the Goblet of Fire?

——

Carina crossed paths with Sirius entirely by chance. That neither of them had wanted this encounter was plain enough—he was in the middle of climbing out of the one-eyed witch statue, Fred and George in tow.

"Weasley*s*. You can't spend all your time finding secret passages. You ought to think about making one named after yourselves. If you truly wish to explore the legends of Hogwarts, you must first become a legend. That is the only—"

Sirius straightened up and found himself face to face with Carina. A brief silence fell. He gave her the slightly sheepish smile of a student caught out by a professor.

From somewhere behind him, the twins' voices drifted out.

"Ow. Padfoot! What are you doing? Are you stuck?"

"Sorry. Coming now."

Sirius stepped aside, and Fred and George tumbled forward out of the passage, arms full of sweets from Honeydukes.

Fred spotted Carina and greeted her cheerfully.

"Malfoy! What a coincidence."

"We're throwing a banquet in honour of Gryffindor's Champion, Harry Potter—care to join us?"

"We don't usually extend invitations to other houses, but we'll make a special exception for you."

"No, thank you."

Carina's refusal was immediate and absolute.

"Go and enjoy yourselves. I have something to settle with Black."

Sirius himself had no idea what she was talking about. What on earth could he possibly have to settle with Carina Malfoy?

Fred and George exchanged a glance.

"Padfoot was supposed to come to our banquet..."

"He's our second exception, you see."

"It won't take long."

"Well, in that case."

"We'll go on ahead, Padfoot."

Fred and George nodded and headed off toward Gryffindor Tower, leaving Sirius standing alone.

"R-right, then. What is it?"

Sirius found Carina difficult.

He despised pure-blood supremacy with every fibre of his being, and by extension loathed the House of Black. He had fled that house, thrown himself into the Order of the Phoenix, and fought against Voldemort.

His cousin Narcissa Malfoy was a remnant of everything the Black family stood for.

Haughty, self-important, indifferent to others' suffering, concerned only with her own circle—a selfish woman he had always despised.

Setting aside Bellatrix, whom he regarded as an outright enemy, Narcissa had been the living woman he disliked most.

Her daughter, Carina Malfoy, had inherited that imperious manner in full. The way she carried herself as though the world revolved around her reminded him uncomfortably of the old Black family, and he found it grating.

That had been his first impression, at any rate. Watching her quietly from her third year onward, his opinion had gradually shifted.

Now there was only a mild awkwardness between them.

The awkwardness of knowing that if those two ever married, he would have to face Lucius and Narcissa at the ceremony.

"Black. Have you found out who put Harry's name in the Goblet of Fire?"

"Ah. That. Not yet. But don't worry."

Carina, for her part, seemed to hold no particular fondness for Sirius.

He pressed on.

"We've taken every precaution we can for Harry. The third task, especially, will be conducted in full view of everyone. If anyone tries to enter the maze, they'll be caught immediately—even under an Invisibility Cloak."

"So you haven't found anything."

Carina fixed him with a sharp look.

"And yet here you are, enjoying an outing with a pair of troublemakers. You seem remarkably relaxed given the circumstances."

"I—I've been investigating too. Spending time with the twins was part of gathering information!"

Sirius protested hastily. Carina folded her arms and tilted her chin, her expression saying: go on, then, let's hear it.

Sirius felt, for the first time in years, like a student waiting to have his homework marked.

"The twins placed a bet with Ludo Bagman at the Quidditch World Cup—seventeen Galleons on Ireland winning but Viktor Krum catching the Snitch. They were owed roughly three hundred Galleons. But Bagman paid them in Leprechaun gold. They've become his youngest and most tenacious creditors, following him everywhere."
"And?"

"Well, a man who heads a department at the Ministry of Magic, reduced to cheating children out of their winnings—doesn't that strike you as odd? So I looked into it, using Rita Skeeter's files as a starting point, and discovered that he's lost a great deal of money betting against house-elves. The man is drowning in debt."

"And?"

"..."

Carina's gaze remained cold. Sirius deflated slightly.

"I just... thought it was worth knowing."

"There ought to be a conclusion. That someone offered him money, or that he sold information. You gather intelligence—do you not then draw conclusions from it?"

"W-wait, there's more. It seems he's placed a new bet—staked everything he has left on Harry winning the tournament. So he has every reason to want the Triwizard tasks to proceed without incident..."

It was, in truth, information of almost no value whatsoever. Carina's gaze grew sharper by the second, and Sirius waited for her verdict like a student awaiting a grade.

The corners of her eyes softened—just slightly.

"I see. So in the end, you've found out nothing."

"Don't worry. At the very least, Harry is safe for this task."

Sirius said it with conviction.

"They'll put magical creatures and traps inside, but nothing life-threatening. And if anyone tries to interfere, we'll know at once. No one can harm Harry while Dumbledore is watching. That much, at least, you can count on."

"...I'll keep it in mind. Though I don't find it particularly reassuring."

"So you won't come to the banquet? Harry would want to see you."

"No. Not yet."

If this was all needless worry—if the world remained peaceful and nothing came to pass—there would be time enough to mend things then.

But if what she feared did happen, it would be too late to undo.

...It might already be too late, in truth.

"If I learn anything, I'll tell you. In the meantime... let me know how Harry is, from time to time."

Carina said it with a quietly desolate expression.

That expression was part of why Sirius found her so difficult to read.

She claimed Harry had pursued her relentlessly and she'd had no choice but to go along with it—yet a look that anguished could never appear on someone's face without genuine feeling behind it.

Back in Gryffindor Tower, the celebration in Harry's honour was in full swing.

Harry had insisted, repeatedly, that he had not entered his own name. But after scoring the highest marks in two consecutive tasks, a few of the others had begun to harbour doubts.

"Come on, Harry. Be honest. Did you take some kind of age-altering potion?"

Lee Jordan asked, with a sly edge to his voice. Harry shrugged.

"No. I folded a paper aeroplane and threw it in. Apparently the Age Line couldn't measure the age of the wind."

"That's rubbish. I tried throwing paper in there myself. The Goblet has a Deflection Jinx on it—you can't get anything in that way!"

"You should have thrown it more precisely."

"I'm telling you, it doesn't matter how well you throw it—it won't go in."

"Lee, haven't you worked it out yet? The trick to throwing a paper aeroplane properly isn't throwing it well. It's making your arm longer before you throw."

"What?"

Lee Jordan tilted his head, unsure whether Harry was joking. At that moment, Fred and George came through the portrait hole, arms laden with Honeydukes sweets.

"Right, then. Father Christmas has arrived, a little early and a little ginger. Everyone bow before us and Padfoot, and take what you're given."

"Harry's exempt, of course—he's Padfoot's godson. Help yourself to whatever you like! It's all bought with his money anyway!"

Harry laughed and picked out a Butterbeer and a packet of Mice Biscuits. Before he could return to his seat, Fred leaned in and murmured in his ear.

"Harry. Padfoot's going to be a bit late. He ran into Malfoy on the way. Says he'll be along after a quick word with her."

"Carina?"

Gladness, resignation, and a quiet ache passed across Harry's face in quick succession. He arranged his expression into something carefully neutral.

"...Right. Hope he's not long."

Fred and George, who had sharper eyes than most, had already noticed the strain between Harry and Carina. They clapped him on the shoulders.

"That's just how it goes sometimes. Even the closest people have a falling-out now and then."

"Fred and I have had our rows too. But you don't make a big thing of it—you let it pass."

"Though that's easier said than done on your own... which is why we came prepared."

George reached into a deep inner pocket and produced a bottle of mead, pressing it discreetly into Harry's hands.

"There are things worth learning from the adult world."

"Drink it alone, by moonlight."

Harry accepted their kindness with genuine gratitude.

Once the sweets and Butterbeers had been distributed, everyone raised their cups and called out together.

"To Gryffindor's rightful Champion!"

"Well—rightful is debatable, but undeniably the finest Champion we've got!"

"Who defied the Age Line, outwitted the Goblet of Fire, and claimed his place by his own hand!"

"To Harry Potter—cheers!"

Gryffindor was not a house that troubled itself over minor infractions.

Whether Harry had bent the rules or lied about his age, the fact remained that he had tried and succeeded—and that was Harry's achievement. They might envy it, but they would never question the morality of it.

In that respect, Gryffindor and Slytherin were polar opposites—Slytherin bent rules to their advantage while Gryffindor simply ignored them—yet somewhere the two houses touched.

With the mood running this high, clearing up the misunderstanding about the Goblet seemed a distant prospect. Harry smiled and raised his cup in answer.

"Oh, it's already started."

Sirius arrived at that moment. All of Gryffindor welcomed him, Fred and George leading the charge to meet him at the door.

"Padfoot! Get in here—we've already done the toast!"

"Come on. Harry's third task!"

Sirius was, in every way that mattered, a Gryffindor—and the students had always loved spending time with him. Young, handsome, easy-going, and entirely unbothered by petty rules: there was not a Gryffindor alive who disliked him.

The next task's theme—the maze. What traps might be hidden inside? Would Hagrid's Blast-Ended Skrewts make an appearance?

Everyone offered their theories, and several students volunteered to teach Harry useful spells. Neville suggested that eating a Canary Cream might allow someone to float upward, and was promptly shot down by Ginny.

As the evening warmed, Sirius said:
"Oh, that's right. Harry—I ran into Malfoy on the way here."

The moment Sirius mentioned Carina, Harry's face went still. Fred and George let out a quiet oh, and Ron and Hermione both hesitated, glancing around the room.

"Why haven't you two been seeing each other lately?"

Carina Malfoy was a somewhat delicate subject in Gryffindor.

Slytherin and Gryffindor had been rivals for generations. A certain couple had done something to ease the tension in recent years, but old feelings didn't simply vanish.

More than that, Harry was Gryffindor's pride. More than a few students in the tower had never been entirely comfortable with the idea of Harry dating a Malfoy—the very emblem of pure-blood supremacy.

Neville, for instance, who had been made to suffer by Carina in years past.

Or Ginny Weasley, who harboured a deep and lasting grudge against the Malfoy family over the Chamber of Secrets.

"She won't see you? How ungrateful."

Ginny took a sip of Butterbeer.

"She's being difficult again, I expect. *I've grown bored of this.* *You're beneath my station.* I've found a reason to end things. I don't even need to hear it—that's exactly what she'd say. Honestly. The nerve of her, thinking she's better than everyone."

"Oi, Ginny!"

Ron moved to stop her, but Ginny didn't so much as flinch.

The freckles that were something of a Weasley family trademark seemed to have vacated her face early. Since the Yule Ball, she had taken to spending a great deal of time with Hermione, learning how to present herself—and somewhere along the way, she had become the sort of girl who drew second glances in the corridor.

The only people who refused to acknowledge it were her own brothers, and since those three happened to be the loudest voices in Gryffindor, the fact tended to go unremarked. It may have done nothing for her reputation, but it had done nothing to diminish her looks either.

"Why should I? Just because she's Harry's girlfriend doesn't mean I have to like her."

"Have you had too much Butterbeer?"

"I'm perfectly sober, Ron."

Ginny held up her cup. She hadn't taken a single sip.

Ron let out a hollow laugh.

"You'd be better off blaming the drink. Harry's already got enough on his mind—don't make it worse."

"That's rich, coming from someone who made a very public point of calling Hermione a traitor when she was seeing Viktor Krum."

"Oi!"

Ron's face cycled through several shades of red. Ginny gave a small, contemptuous sniff.

"As I was saying. It was always an odd match, wasn't it? Different houses, different loyalties. If anything surprised me, it's that I thought Harry would be the one to end it."

"Ginny?"

"Yes, Harry!"

Harry's voice made Ginny switch registers in an instant. Ron caught the way her voice jumped a full octave in that brief moment.

Ginny Weasley had idolised Harry Potter since before she ever set foot in Hogwarts.

The Chosen One. The youngest Seeker in a century. The boy who had faced Voldemort alone.

She had barely been able to believe that her own insufferable brothers were actually his friends. She had arrived at Hogwarts with her heart full of hope.

And Harry had been everything she'd imagined—more, even. But...

"Sorry. Carina—let's not talk about her tonight."

"...If that's what you want, Harry."

The one who fell short was Ginny.

Harry was popular and perpetually busy. He had little attention to spare for a freckled first-year girl.

Or perhaps he had given her more attention than she realised—but for Ginny, it was never enough.

That was why she had clung to the diary. It offered the right words at the right moments, and whenever Ginny seemed to be hurting, Harry worried about her all the more.

She had known, even then, that it was wrong—and yet she had been glad for his concern.

"I'll leave it alone, for your sake. But Harry—"

Then, at some point, Ginny had understood.

She had seen where Harry's gaze always went.

The diary had found the cracks in her and widened them, and Ginny had opened the Chamber of Secrets. The disaster had been too great, and the realisation of Harry's heart too clear—and so Ginny had spent the years since keeping very quiet.

She had watched in silence when Harry made his public declaration to Carina at the Yule Ball.

Whether the world had taken pity on her, she couldn't say.

But not long after the two of them began dating, the cracks between them had started to show—and a crack, for Ginny, was an opening.

"Couples with too many secrets never last. Remember that."

Ginny murmured it with her head bowed, then straightened up again.

Harry had always had a soft spot for Ginny. She was, after all, the victim of the Chamber of Secrets—a girl who had lost her first and second years to a Horcrux of Voldemort's making and the reckless cruelty of Lucius Malfoy, all before she was old enough to understand what had happened to her.

Even setting that aside, Harry thought of Ginny as a younger sister.

"Thanks for the advice. I'll think about it."

Harry gave a wry smile and raised his cup. Ginny looked faintly disappointed, but managed a show of breezy confidence as she clinked her cup against his.

Of course, the Weasleys who thought of Ginny as an actual younger sister were considerably less forgiving than Harry.

"You're getting more like Mum every day."

"What!"

Ginny's head snapped around. Ron, Fred, and George grabbed her by the arms.

"Give it a rest. This is a party for Harry, not for you."

"You're wasting your time. Go sort out those freckles first."

"I've nearly got rid of them! I just need a bit more of that skin potion!"

"Right. Fine. But wouldn't it be better to work on your actual abilities rather than talking about other people? Honestly, Malfoy may have a terrible personality, but you have to admit she's—"

"And you, on the other hand, have a terrible personality and your face is... well. I feel awful saying this, but we do look alike."

Fred said it with what appeared to be genuine sympathy. The room dissolved into laughter. Ginny had undeniably grown into her looks—but genetics, as ever, had the final word.

Ginny's face went as red as her hair as she swung her arms wildly.

"I'll kill you all! Nothing ever works out because of you lot!"

"How gratifying."

"High praise, coming from us... wait. Don't reach for your wand!"

Harry watched the warm chaos of their family and smiled.

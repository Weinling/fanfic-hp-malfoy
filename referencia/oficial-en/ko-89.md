The first thing that greeted Carina was the sensation of something flowing into her mouth. Only then did she realize she was thirsty—and that she needed to drink more of whatever this was. The liquid didn't go down easily, so she worked it with her tongue like a small animal nursing, coaxing it past her parched throat. Like water seeping into cracked earth, it spread through her throat, into her stomach, and along her veins until it had soaked through every part of her.

Some time later, Carina Malfoy opened her eyes. There was something hard in her mouth. She traced its shape with her tongue and teeth—round, hollowed out, capable of holding liquid. A spoon.

Someone was feeding her, one careful spoonful at a time. With extraordinary tenderness.

It was only natural to want to see who. Carina turned her head slowly, and found herself looking into the eyes of a boy holding a spoon.

She called his name in a cracked voice.

"Har… ry?"

"You're awake."

Harry smiled. Beside him, Professor Slughorn stood holding a potion vial, his expression characteristically sour.

Still not fully oriented, Carina lay there for a moment in a daze before gathering herself and pushing upright. However many days she'd been lying there, her body had gone rigid. Every time she moved her limbs, her muscles ached in protest—as though lodging a formal complaint.

Still, Carina was alive. That was remarkable in itself. Just before she'd lost consciousness, the poison had been so agonizing…

"Ugh—wait, just—"

She forced herself upright through the pain, a low groan escaping her. Her platinum hair hung limp and dull, but she had no attention to spare for that. Panic seized her, and she grabbed Harry's arm.

"Wait, wait. The Dark Mark—Harry, the snake—He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named—"

She was murmuring in confusion and fear when Harry set down the spoon and wrapped an arm around her shoulders. He held her against his chest as she trembled in uneven intervals, and whispered close to her ear.

"It's all right, Carina. It's over."

The trembling stopped. Harry's voice was cold like deep lake water—and it carried some quality that stilled her, smoothed her out. Her ragged breathing began to ease, and reason returned like cold water poured over her head.

Carina steadied herself, then looked at Harry with eyes still full of questions. The look demanded an explanation.

Harry read it and answered.

"Your poison had an antidote. I worked it out too late—it nearly went badly—but fortunately I found it and got it into you in time. You barely kept your life."

"Wait, then—"

The reason Carina had drunk the poison was to suppress the Dark Mark on her wrist. But now that she was conscious, the snake within the Mark would not stay quiet.

She moved to push up her sleeve. With Harry's arm still around her, it wasn't easy.

"Oh—the tattoo on your wrist, that?"

Harry understood before she could manage it. He drew back and calmly folded her sleeve up, layer by careful layer. As the fabric fell away, there was the ghastly skull—and a snake lying utterly still, as though dead.

Carina's eyes went wide. The snake that had watched her, monitored her—it was doing nothing, even with Harry right there. Showing this Mark to anyone else was unambiguously betrayal, the kind you couldn't argue your way out of—

Then Harry raised his wand and spoke.

"That's rather rude, you know. We had a falling-out for a bit, yes—but getting a delinquent tattoo without my consent? On this pretty wrist, no less."

He brought his wand to her wrist. In that instant, Carina felt something strange.

It was exactly like the moment Voldemort had given her the Dark Mark. That tingling sensation, as though a channel had opened in her wrist. Back then, Voldemort had pushed a snake through that channel. She had shuddered at the wrongness of it every time she thought of it.

But Harry, remarkably, was pulling the snake back out.

The tattoo dissolved like ink washed away by rain, streaming toward his wand. The snake went with it. It writhed a few times as though resisting, but couldn't fight the current. Hissing all the while, it was drawn inexorably into the wand.

"Ha… hah…"

When the last trace vanished, Carina exhaled—a breathless, exhilarated sound. With the magical snake extracted from inside her, she felt a clarity she had no words for. She was free now. Free from that magic, from that contract.

Harry, for his part, looked entirely unmoved, as though he'd done nothing of consequence.

"The information you gave us let us find all of his Horcruxes. And then the opportunity to kill him presented itself. So we decided to turn your plan against him—used Polyjuice to disguise someone as you, followed Snape into their stronghold. We struck from inside and out simultaneously, and they collapsed without putting up much resistance…"

Now that calm had returned, Carina finally had the space to look at Harry properly. His face was as composed as ever, but beneath it sat deep exhaustion—and something else. A darkness she couldn't quite name.

The easy confidence she knew in him was gone. Something had been taken out of him. What had happened?

"There were losses. Professor Snape and Professor Dumbledore both died… But through their sacrifice, we were finally able to defeat He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, and most of his followers have been arrested and sent to Azkaban."

Was it the losses? But was that truly the source of the darkness in his face?

No—there was something else. Something more fundamental.

Voldemort was gone. So how had Harry removed a Dark Mark that had persisted even after Voldemort's death?

"Your father… will stand as a key witness at trial. His release on bail was agreed in exchange for information about the Death Eaters. He'll be back at the Manor before long."

"Re—really?"

That was good news. The news that her father would be out of prison and back with his family pushed the swelling questions aside, at least for a moment. Harry smiled faintly at her unguarded joy.

That afternoon, in the Great Hall, the atmosphere pressed down like a great stone weight. Students who hadn't yet heard the full story whispered among themselves, eyes drawn to the empty seats at the staff table.

Most conspicuous of all was Dumbledore's chair at the head. The seat where he should have been sitting—absurd hat and all, laughing his unhurried laugh—stood empty. The professors on either side of it were pressing handkerchiefs to their eyes every ten seconds.

Even the most oblivious students understood that something had happened. The Slytherin table was no different.

"Dumbledore must be dead!"

Pansy Parkinson said it with barely concealed delight.

"He must have killed him—who else could have killed Dumbledore?"
"Maybe he just died of old age? I hadn't heard anything was happening last night."

Goyle offered this, and Pansy waved it away. She lowered her voice and leaned in, glancing around as she whispered.

"You clearly don't know—my uncle sent a letter! He said the Dark Mark flared up! Something enormous happened, I'm certain of it!"

At that moment, the doors to the Great Hall swung open. The Minister for Magic entered, flanked by Aurors and aides. Cedric Diggory was among them, his face so sunken with grief that he didn't notice the Hufflepuffs waving at him as he passed.

Rufus Scrimgeour mounted the dais, pressed his wand to his throat, and spoke so that his voice filled every corner of the Hall.

"I am Rufus Scrimgeour, Minister for Magic. I have come today to share with the students of Hogwarts one piece of good news, and one piece of bad."

"The bad news is that Dumbledore's dead—or wait, maybe that's the good news!"

Pansy sniggered—then went rigid as the next words landed.

"The good news is that last night, Professor Dumbledore and our Aurors defeated He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named. The wizarding world is now free from terror. The darkness that threatened us has retreated. We have apprehended his remaining followers, and they will be sent to Azkaban as soon as their trials conclude. The Ministry of Magic hereby officially declares our complete liberation from He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named…"

On those words, owls began pouring through the windows. Dozens of them—as though every owlery in the country had been emptied at once—dropping scrolls indiscriminately across the Hall. The Daily Prophet special editions they carried announced his death in enormous letters.

THE DARK LORD: GONE FOR GOOD.

Cheers erupted from the other tables. Students pounded the tables, broke into wide grins; some were crying.

The Slytherin table was the precise opposite.

The faces of students with Death Eater parents had gone white. Several leapt up and made for the owlery at once. Others sat blank-faced, as though the situation still hadn't reached them.

Scrimgeour continued.

"The bad news is that in the course of these events, two wizards lost their lives—Professor Dumbledore and Professor Snape. The Ministry honours their courage with the Order of Merlin, First Class, and their funerals will be held here at Hogwarts. They were the finest wizards of this age, the most devoted of adults, and the finest of teachers."

A sob broke from the staff table. The celebrating voices cut off as though by agreement. Death has a way of stopping people cold. In the hush that followed, joy felt impossible.

After that, the rest was procedural. How things would proceed, that everything else would continue as normal, that Professor McGonagall would be appointed interim Headmistress… At the end of it all, Scrimgeour replaced his hat and concluded.

"Their purpose was to protect you and bring peace to the wizarding world. So honour them by focusing on your studies within the peace they secured. Nothing will threaten or hinder you now."

He stepped down from the dais. The students weren't sure whether to applaud or grieve.

Slytherin, at least, had no such confusion. Neither the Dark Lord's death nor Snape's was welcome news to them.

It was inevitable that the students would converge on Carina. She had vanished without warning, and two days later everything had changed—of course they would look for a connection. Above all, she had been closer to the Dark Lord and to Harry Potter than anyone.

She answered their questions plainly.

"I don't know the details either. I was asleep for two days straight, and when I woke up it was all over."

But that wasn't enough to satisfy anyone. Several families were deeply entangled with Voldemort, and for their heirs, this was a matter of their family's survival.

"You can't have had nothing to do with it. Why did you go outside that night?"

"Were you lured out? Did they take you prisoner and use you somehow?"

Questions that were really accusations. They were desperate, and they wanted answers that made sense—even if the person they were pressing was Carina.

Carina closed her eyes for a moment.

What should she say? Even she hadn't known what would happen when she drank the poison. She had been prepared to die. There had been no room to think about what came after. All she had done was pass her memories to Harry and Dumbledore, hoping they would be of use. How much they had actually helped, she still didn't know.

She could deflect. That would be the sensible thing. After all, her betrayal had gone undetected—by Voldemort himself, and by everyone else.

But.

"I met with Harry Potter alone and gave him information."

"…What?"

Did she really need to hide? Did Carina Malfoy need to be afraid of these people—afraid enough to lie about who she was?

What a ridiculous thought. She had betrayed Voldemort when he was still alive and at full power, with death as the price.

So why should she now, of all times, be frightened of her own housemates' opinions? If anything, now that the outcome was settled, standing her ground openly was the better way to protect Slytherin's position—and her own.

Carina lifted her chin. She met the stares directed at her without flinching, looked steadily back at the wide, furious eyes, and spoke with her usual composure.

"The Dark Lord was holding my family hostage. But… it's something of a family tradition not to play along with hostage-takers. Even when the hostage-taker is the Dark Lord. So I passed along what I knew and lay low for a while."

"You—you dared—you betrayed him?!"

"I don't particularly think of it as betrayal. Someone who holds your family hostage and then claims to have trusted you—isn't that a bit… ironic?"

A shout went up from somewhere—no telling whose. Pansy and Montague reached for their wands. Crabbe and Goyle blinked at each other. In the midst of the outrage, Carina's hand moved to her own wand, ready to fight if it came to that.

"What is going on here?"

The common room door opened, and Professor Slughorn waddled in. The students clicked their tongues and dropped their hands.

Horace Slughorn entered the dormitory with Harry Potter at his side. He cleared his throat several times before getting to the point.

"Ahem. Forgive the interruption. But I have something to inform you all. With Professor Snape's passing, the position of Head of Slytherin House is vacant. I will be filling that role on a temporary basis—if anything arises concerning the house, please come to me."

Slughorn exhaled a long, heavy breath and continued.

"Snape… he was an extraordinarily talented student. Gifted across the board, but with a particular genius for Potions. I always thought he would become a great scholar… I never imagined he'd become a teacher, frankly. His temperament didn't suggest he'd take to children. And yet—to think that he gave his life to protect Harry, to protect his students…"

"Professor."

Pansy cut him off, her expression sour.
"Understood. But why have you brought a Gryffindor in here?"

"Ah, young Potter? He said he had business here."

"That doesn't mean you bring him into another house's common room. This is Slytherin's space."

Harry, who had been listening quietly, gave a small shrug.

"Sorry. I just need to take Carina somewhere for a moment."

"Says who?"

"Says Carina. She has the right to choose where she goes. Isn't that right, Professor?"

"Quite right."

Slughorn nodded, and Pansy found she had nothing to say to that. Harry made his way through the crowd with easy steps and took Carina's hand as naturally as breathing.

"Come on, then."

Carina hesitated briefly. She didn't want to look as though she were running away.

But there were things she still needed to hear from Harry. She still didn't know what had happened while she was unconscious—what had happened to him.

The hesitation was brief. Carina raised the back of her hand slightly and said, with perfect composure:

"Lead the way."

She was a noble receiving an escort; he was her partner. Harry bowed deeply and guided her forward. Pansy and several other Slytherins looked as though they'd bitten into something rotten, but with Slughorn watching, none of them moved.

"Off you go, Harry. I'll stay and have a word with them…"

Slughorn turned to open the door for them.

And Pansy Parkinson, who had been waiting for her moment, reached for her wand.

She was the daughter of a Death Eater family. A follower of Voldemort in her own right. And once, she had trailed after Carina like a shadow.

Carina had betrayed Voldemort. She was going to send Pansy's family to Azkaban. And now she was abandoning Slytherin itself to walk out behind Harry Potter.

Unacceptable. At the very least, she could not be allowed to simply leave. She had to pay for her betrayal here and now.

Unable to contain her rage, Pansy raised her wand and took aim at Carina's back.

Harry Potter opened his mouth.

"Ah."

Thoom.

The air took on weight and pressed against their limbs. The oppressive atmosphere of the Slytherin common room became something physical, something that bore down on them. An immense magical force poured outward. Their arms and legs screamed with fear and refused to move. Pansy and the two students beside her stood frozen, wands clutched in trembling hands.

Furious magic surged through the room. At its centre stood Harry—one hand around his wand, his eyes a strange reddish-brown as they swept across every face in the room.

"Do you all want Voldemort back that badly?"

Under normal circumstances, that name would have made them flinch, or flare with anger. But in this moment, hearing Harry say Voldemort—there was something deeply, disturbingly natural about it, as though he were saying his own name.

"If that's what you want, I can be a second Voldemort. But I'll need to make an example first. You won't enjoy it."

Thud.

The moment the words ended, all three of them crumpled to the floor, gasping. Only now did they realize they hadn't been breathing properly at all.

If even the residual force of his magic could do this—if Harry had actually intended to—

"Harry!"

Slughorn's voice rang out. His beard was trembling—whether from fear or fury, it was impossible to say.

"Out! Right now!"

"Ah—sorry…"

Harry took Carina's hand and pulled her out of the Slytherin common room. Until the very last moment before the door closed, his eyes stayed on Pansy, watchful.

The lock clicked shut, and time resumed. The Slytherin students, who had been as motionless as mice before a snake, began murmuring uneasily.

"What… what was that. Was that really Harry Potter?"

No one had an answer. Slughorn, who knew more of the truth than any of them, only stared at the place where they had disappeared, his face creased with worry.

The moment they were out of the common room, Harry turned to face Carina. Her silver-grey eyes were fixed directly on him. He let out a breath and spoke with careful concern.

"Carina. You need to be careful. It hasn't been long since he died. Until the ones who are left come to terms with reality, provoking them isn't—"

"What are you hiding from me?"

Harry stopped. He gave a vague smile and began to turn his head away—and Carina grabbed him by the collar.

Half a head shorter than him, she pulled him down to her level and held his gaze. Her silver-grey eyes took him in completely. The Harry reflected in them wore a pleasant expression, but occasionally—very occasionally—something strange flickered through his eyes. A brief, wrong flash of red.

Others might not have noticed. But not Carina. She had watched him since their first year at Hogwarts, studied him for weaknesses, met his eyes, held him in her gaze and been held in his.

She had seen the foreign thing living inside Harry.

"Tell me everything. Right now."

Harry smiled—a sad, quiet smile.

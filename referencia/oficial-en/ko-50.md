In Albania, there was a wealthy man's villa.

The man was a businessman who had come over from America—a back-room elder who had handed the day-to-day running of his affairs to professional managers and stepped away from the front lines. He still kept his title on his business card, but for all practical purposes he had retired; he himself considered it a half-retirement, unwilling only to surrender the designation.

Plenty of money, plenty of time. As wealthy men commonly do, he combined those two surplus quantities and made a habit of spending one on the other.

His hobby of collecting old castles was part of that. He would travel across Europe, inspect castles that had come up for auction, appraise their asset value, and then, once he had won the bid, have them fitted out as private retreats.

Buying an ancient castle and turning it into one's own villa. It was a hobby that demanded a great deal of both money and time.

He was a reasonably well-known rich man, though not among the wealthiest in any country, and the castles he owned numbered perhaps five—bargain lots that had come up cheap. The annual upkeep was, of course, a sum ordinary people could not dream of, but for him it was a cost well within his means.

He was an ordinary rich man, living an ordinarily wealthy life. Had he never come to Albania for a holiday, his end, too, would have been ordinary.

But.

"Don't you find it laughable, Karkaroff?"

After wandering deep into the forest on what he called a stroll, and encountering some terrible presence there—his life had been turned entirely upside down.

Vertical pupils gleamed. The man who had once been described as a slightly eccentric old fellow now possessed a voice of boundless cold and cruelty.

"Even this one is little better than a beggar compared to the Muggle millionaires. Muggles revel in every manner of wealth and power, living with brazen ostentation. They make no effort to conceal their lives from anyone. On the contrary, they proclaim it to the world at the top of their voices—I have this much money, I have this much power. How laughable, when all of it is nothing more than a spring dream that could vanish in a single night."

He turned his head sharply. On that wrinkled face, the sinister presence that had once clung to the back of Quirrell's skull flickered briefly into view. Scarlet pupils, a flattened ruin of a nose, and a mouth worn away as though it had been erased—something that had once been human was lodged within his body.

As a child, he had wished to become a wizard, just like every other child. But this, one could be certain, was not the manner he had wished for.

"Blinded by the money and power before their eyes, they cannot see what truly matters. How foolish. Is that not so, Karkaroff?"

Igor Karkaroff, Headmaster of Durmstrang, bowed his head toward his former master.

"Quite right, quite right... Master..."

He panted with fear.

Voldemort's red eyes turned toward his former subordinate—and informant—with something like curiosity.

"Oh, Karkaroff. My foremost 'former' friend."

"M-Master..."

"You were at Hogwarts today. How did you find this place?"

"Malfoy... told me."

"Lucius?"

Voldemort's voice, calling the name of his old servant, was thick with interest.

"Was he more loyal than I thought, or more capable than I thought? I know this much: after I disappeared, Lucius had no interest in me whatsoever. He never so much as thought to search for me. I certainly never left him any clues... How did he know?"

A sound like a serpent dragging itself across stone reached his ears. Karkaroff watched a great snake glide past his feet and swallowed hard.

The snake was behaving as though Karkaroff were not there—but should Voldemort give the order, it would make a meal of him without hesitation.

"Lucius's daughter... She informed him from Hogwarts. I heard what she said and followed Barty Crouch... Sure enough. The great one... he who once punished us from the high bench of the court had become your instrument, Master... And there I found... a former colleague."

"A daughter? Lucius's?"

Voldemort spoke.

"You're saying it was Lucius's daughter who detected the Imperius Curse? Clever—very clever indeed. Then again, Lucius was always clever in his way. Though his inclinations ran in a somewhat different direction... The moment he believed me dead, he renounced me without hesitation and extricated himself from the courtroom..."

Voldemort murmured, as though recalling something fondly, then turned back to Karkaroff.

"Interesting. I had intended to simply kill you, but I find my mind has changed. What can you offer me?"

"Everything! Master, you have proven your immortality by returning. There can be no further doubt about—!"

Karkaroff broke off mid-sentence, seized with terror, and clutched at his own throat. He choked and retched, crawling across the floor.

Voldemort's cold voice rang through the castle.

"Must I prove myself? Must I exert effort to dispel your doubts? Is your belief something I must earn?"

"For-give, gkh—!"

Click. Voldemort snapped his fingers, and the force crushing Karkaroff dissolved.

Karkaroff drew a ragged breath. Voldemort withdrew his gaze and murmured.

"I am power. I am fear. I am immortal, and I am invincible. All must fear me. They must hesitate to let my name pass their lips. I will transcend mere existence and become fear itself—I will return to punish those who have forgotten to be afraid! With the blood of my nemesis!"

A young man with stiff blond hair walked slowly in, carrying a glass vial. He cast a look of contempt at Karkaroff, then raised the vial—filled with a swirling crimson liquid—in both hands and presented it before Voldemort.

"Master. The blood of Harry Potter."

"Well done, Bartemius. My faithful servant."

"I only did what was required."

Bartemius Crouch Jr. answered with composed deference.

Barty Crouch Sr. had a son. While the father had been fighting dark wizards at the height of his career, the son had been seduced by Voldemort's ideology—had killed, had tortured the Aurors, the Longbottoms. Because of it, Barty Crouch Sr. had been forced to sentence his own son to a life term in Azkaban.

That decision had been the right one. The son was the sort of wretch who would cast the Imperius Curse on his own father for Voldemort's sake.

Voldemort extended his hand. The fat, wrinkled hand of the old merchant grasped the crystal vial. He muttered with profound distaste.

"I dislike this method. No mystery, no reverence—action undertaken purely for its end. A vulgarity fit for Muggles... How many precious words did I have to spend merely to obtain this—Harry Potter's blood..."

"Forgive me, Master. My abilities were insufficient..."

"Bartemius. I am not blaming you. How could I blame you? I have not forgotten. You—controlled for ten years beneath your father because of his twisted notion of family love, living in suppression all that time—and yet the loyalty you showed me never wavered..."

As Voldemort murmured, Bartemius bowed his head deeply and replied.

"Master, with respect, that was not family love. He could not bring himself to kill his only blood with his own hands, and so he kept me close and controlled me instead—a horrific captivity maintained through the Imperius Curse. He intended, at some suitable point, to use me as a stud to continue his line. Dreadfully, he planned to... graft me with a Muggle!"

Violent fury blazed in his eyes. He spat out the rage and hatred directed at his father—a man no longer present, and who would never be present again.

"That is not a father. That is not family! My only true home is at your side, Master, and my only family is my comrades! I will give everything I have to you—you who delivered me from those terrible years under my father's dominion!"

"It was not I who delivered you. It was the hatred within you. Had you not seized upon that moment of weakened control to send up the Dark Mark, I would never have known you were there."

Karkaroff reflexively clenched his own wrist.

The Dark Mark is no mere tattoo. It is a brand that carries a specific magical covenant. Every Death Eater is bound to answer when Voldemort issues a summons, and can also send a signal to Voldemort through the Dark Mark.

There was no certainty that Voldemort would respond to any given signal—but at the very least, he had responded to Bartemius's.

"He would not have known. That when the Imperius Curse is used for a prolonged period, a channel forms between the caster and the subject... He would have done well to think a little harder about why I do not simply use the Imperius Curse to rule the wizarding world. Thanks to that channel, you—controlled for ten years—slowly drained your father of his strength and his reason. And even... his position."

"I am only glad to have been able to serve you with my father's legacy."

"Indeed. It was your best effort. I know the lengths you went to. I merely lamented that this stage was so squalid, so Muggle-like. If I had my way, I would have used a Portkey to invite Harry Potter to my palace—I wished for him to witness my resurrection..."

Voldemort tightened his grip on the vial. The borrowed hand of the old man was so feeble it could barely hold even a glass vial without exerting its full strength.

Voldemort gave voice to his contempt for his own condition.

"With Dumbledore watching, I could hardly arrange for Harry Potter to grasp it at a fixed time. I have never felt the lack of power so acutely as this. That I—I—should have had to scrape blood from inside a filthy beast... It is humiliating."

Bartemius had manipulated his father to prepare countless traps. Barty Crouch Sr., as the head of the organizing committee, had held the authority to decide what obstacles would be placed inside the maze.

Plants with steel teeth, blood-sucking leeches, thorn-trap snares. Whatever trap Harry Potter stumbled into, it would draw blood.

All of it to obtain blood—that blood through which the protective magic flowed—and present it to his master.

"But the humiliation ends here. The curse that woman placed upon me, the one that stopped me... If I am reborn through this blood as the medium, I will be free of her curse. Harry Potter will no longer be able to evade my magic. Once more I will reign as the fear of all!"

Voldemort cried out.

A pall of darkness settled over the castle. An ominous surge of magical energy wrapped itself around the old stone walls, as though heralding the Dark Lord's resurrection.

And this was not even his full restoration—yet the power was already this great. Karkaroff trembled, but inwardly felt a flicker of relief.

Even if he had fled, a Voldemort restored to full strength would have killed him.

Perhaps bowing his head now and begging forgiveness was the better choice after all.
"Karkaroff."

Karkaroff startled and dropped to his knees.

"Yes! Yes... Master..."

"I will give you a chance. Offer me your flesh. Do that, and though you are a coward... I will count you among my servants."

A servant.

No rank, no authority—merely someone to be used.

It was a far diminished station compared to what he had once held, yet Karkaroff was grateful even for that.

At least he would be alive.

"I offer it gladly!"

Losing an entire hand had certainly not been part of his plans—but he would survive, at any rate.

As he had wanted.

Barty Crouch Sr. was found dead in his own home. Mad-Eye Moody and several others, following their own independent investigation, had sensed something amiss and forced their way into Crouch's residence—but he was already dead by the time they arrived, so it was said.

Igor Karkaroff, Headmaster of Durmstrang, was named as the prime suspect. He had reportedly complained openly about the Triwizard Tournament being held at Hogwarts, and when Viktor Krum was eliminated in the final task, he had reportedly shouted that both Barty Crouch and Dumbledore were frauds. And immediately after the Tournament ended, he had left Hogwarts without a word to anyone.

Every circumstance pointed to Karkaroff.

Most people found the explanation satisfactory.

Except for the few who knew the truth.

"Cornelius. The one who killed Barty Crouch was not Karkaroff."

In the Minister for Magic's office, Dumbledore met with Cornelius Fudge in private. Dumbledore pressed his case.

"Barty Crouch was being controlled under the Imperius Curse. There were many suspicious aspects to his behaviour..."

"So? If Karkaroff isn't the culprit, then who is?"

"Surely you can already guess. The very one I have spoken of repeatedly. He who must not be named—"

"Absolute nonsense, Dumbledore!"

Cornelius erupted.

"He was doing his utmost in his duties. Thanks to Barty's efforts, the Triwizard Tournament concluded with Hogwarts Champion Cedric Diggory taking the victory. Everyone was satisfied with the outcome. Everyone except that dark wizard Igor Karkaroff! Igor—a former Death Eater from years past—never managed to rid himself of that foul, petty nature of his. When Durmstrang lost the Triwizard Tournament, he nursed a grudge and killed Barty!"

"That is not the truth, Cornelius. Severus—come here, if you would."

Severus Snape strode forward and rolled up his sleeve. On his forearm, the Dark Mark writhed and pulsed with a sinister light.

"The Dark Lord is summoning everyone."

Snape said it with a face carved from stone.

"This mark is what is transmitted to the Death Eaters. It is sending its signal more intensely than at any time before. Those who receive this signal must assemble at the Dark Lord's side immediately."

"Utter nonsense! Has everyone gone mad?"

Minister Fudge shot to his feet. He stared at Dumbledore with a face full of disbelief.

"Everyone is enjoying the peace! Thanks to the International Magical Cooperation Department's year of hard work, the tournament concluded successfully. There was an unfortunate incident, yes, but that is simply a criminal matter! Everyone believes the present moment is peaceful!"

"The fact that everyone believes it does not make it true."

"The fact that it comes from your mouth doesn't make it true either!"

Cornelius jabbed a finger and shouted.

"He has returned? Without a shred of evidence, you expect me to take your word for it! The explanation that you and everyone around you have collectively succumbed to paranoia is far more plausible than the claim that a man who vanished without a trace has returned after fourteen years!"

"Cornelius. Even so—he will return one day. He will work in secret, in the shadows, and bare his fangs at the most lethal moment. When that happens, the entire world will be plunged into chaos and terror."

"The one plunging the wizarding world into chaos and terror is you, Dumbledore! Get out!"

Minister Fudge issued his dismissal. Dumbledore left the office with Snape.

"I trust you will move quickly to apprehend Karkaroff."

"That, too, we will handle ourselves. Do not interfere."

"Then I shall see you again later. I would hope we might meet under somewhat better circumstances."

Bang. The door closed behind them.

Dumbledore and Snape returned to Hogwarts. He concluded that, given Minister Fudge's refusal to believe, it would be best not to expect any support from the Ministry.

Instead, Dumbledore moved independently. Former students, friends, comrades who had fought alongside him fourteen years ago, those with whom he had maintained various ties over the years—he sent word to those he could trust. Most replied in the affirmative.

And so, while preparations for the coming fight were well underway, Moody came to his office.

"I'm resigning my teaching post, Dumbledore."

"Why is that?"

"I'm old. I've fallen behind the times. That alone would be no great problem—but I've come to realize my mind has gone rigid."

Moody looked profoundly exhausted and aged. He spoke with effort.

"The man who preached constant suspicion and vigilance—and yet he failed to notice the very person he should have suspected most. Barty Crouch: a man who never compromised with dark wizards. Of course he would be on our side, I assumed... It never occurred to me that a wizard as formidable as he could have fallen under the Imperius Curse."

"It was not your fault. Neither I nor the Ministry staff noticed either."

"Malfoy noticed."

Moody mentioned Carina Malfoy. She had been the first to detect something wrong with Barty Crouch and had sent word by letter—a beat too late, perhaps, but she had done it nonetheless.

Dumbledore spoke.

"Alastor. That is a matter of Miss Malfoy's position. You and I knew Barty too well—that familiarity was precisely what prevented us from suspecting him. Miss Malfoy, standing at one remove, was able to see him more objectively than we could."

"And that is precisely the proof that I am outdated. I was so busy suspecting Miss Malfoy that I put the very people on guard against a valuable informant who was cooperating with us. When in fact they..."

Moody thought of several Slytherin students.

Carina had openly dated Harry.

Crabbe had dismissed the great war as something out of old stories.

Even the Slytherins, who of all people might have been expected to revere him, gave no thought to a war from the past.

Even a sincere voice needs a shared experience to find resonance. Moody's warnings had been nothing but hollow echoes, spreading without anything to strike against.

"...Not even knowing that they had no particular feeling about He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named or the old war. All I did was raise the enemy's guard. I accomplished nothing of worth."

"I do not diminish your intentions. You simply did not want to see the tragedies of the past repeated."

"Once you've failed, intentions are just excuses."

Professor Moody took a long pull from his hip flask.

He had a habit of always carrying his own drink, wary that someone might poison his food—but today, the flask smelled strongly of hard liquor.

"There were signs everywhere. They never did find Bertha Jorkins... That would have been impossible for the Barty I knew. What sort of man leaves a missing employee unaccounted for an entire year? Who has the authority to simply let that go? I should have suspected him before Ludo Bagman..."

"That is enough regret for now. What matters is what we do next."

"What is there to do. I'm old, as I keep saying. I'll do what I've always done. Suspect, track down suspicious characters, bring them in... Teaching was never the right fit for me."

"It has been peaceful until now, but war will come again. Your knowledge will prove invaluable."

Moody drained the flask to the last drop. He wiped his mouth roughly with his sleeve and said,

"You took Snape back in despite knowing his past. Malfoy only told us Barty Crouch was under the Imperius Curse after he was already dead—and yet Harry hasn't even thought to press her on it. If it had been me, I'd have extracted the information by rougher means... but you lot wouldn't stand for that."

That was simply the truth of it. Snape was still Potions Master, and Harry had not once confronted Carina about why she had told them so late.

It was a sight Moody could not understand. And he had decided not to push back simply because he couldn't understand it.

"Knowing my methods are outdated and persisting in them anyway—that's just stubbornness. It's time for me to step aside."
"...You've worked hard, Alastor. We've lost a fine Defence Against the Dark Arts professor."

"The only thing I did right as a Defence teacher was knowing when to quit."

Moody turned his back and trudged away. Dumbledore watched him go in silence.

The news that he had returned was not made public.

Yet a handful of Slytherin students came to know the truth a step ahead of everyone else.

"..."

They all kept their mouths shut and said nothing.

It was not a situation that permitted careless speech. Even knowing the truth, it was not something one could easily voice aloud—and no one could decide whether to smile and rejoice or to shrink back in fear.

He had returned.

The Dark Lord had been resurrected.

A past that predated their own births, something they had only ever held as a vague dread—and now he had come back alive from hell itself.

The children who knew the truth fell silent or grew anxious, trying to distinguish between the Slytherins who could be trusted and those who could not. Those who hadn't heard, or whose parents were not Death Eaters, kept their mouths shut in turn, reading the room.

It was then that Carina Malfoy walked into the common room.

"Huddled in your little corners, whispering amongst yourselves. What are you, chicks? Why are you making such a nuisance of yourselves in the Slytherin common room?"

Carina tossed her still-beautiful platinum hair back over her shoulder. Both the children who had been worrying in the know and those who had been anxious without knowing looked up at Carina, standing squarely in the centre of the room.

Carina looked down at all of them, alone, and spoke.

"I have no interest in whatever troubles you're carrying. But always conduct yourselves with the dignity of Slytherins. Children of pure-blood families do not cower and make themselves small out of fear!"

Carina emphasized it once more.

"Don't forget. Each of you is the heir of a great house. No one gets to look down on you!"

At Carina's words, the Slytherins slowly began to recover their composure.

Whatever else might be said, Slytherin was more favourably disposed toward Voldemort than the other houses—and while they feared him, that fear was closer to reverence.

Right. There had never been any reason for Slytherin to despair.

Whether their families supported Voldemort or not, they were at least half a step removed from the immediate threat of his wrath.

Carina turned sharply. Crabbe, who had been sitting across the room, came trotting over.

"Thank you, Carina. But..."

Crabbe glanced around at the others and asked in a very small voice.

"You and Harry Potter... it's over, isn't it?"

"Obviously. It was just a brief romance. I'm not foolish enough to let some trivial romantic feeling take precedence."

Carina said it as though cutting something off cleanly.

"R-right."

Carina shot Crabbe a sideways glance, then turned on her heel.

"Where are you going?"

"The bathroom."

Carina answered curtly and turned her back on the common room. The atmosphere behind her was noticeably easier than before.

But Carina was not.

With every step she took down the corridor, her breathing grew shorter.

The soothing green of the dungeon walls, the cosy atmosphere of the dormitory—none of it helped. Her footsteps quickened, and with that quickening came a desperate urgency, as though something were chasing her. She half-ran into the bathroom and locked the door behind her.

Click. The moment the lock turned, she staggered as though every ounce of strength had left her body. She caught herself on the edge of the sink and looked into the mirror.

A stranger stared back at her. The bravado she had performed was gone; the gilt of pretence had been stripped away, leaving a plain, unadorned face—a small girl with nothing left to hide behind.

Carina Malfoy drew out a letter with trembling hands.

A letter from her father. From Lucius.

⟨Carina. The thing that opened the Chamber of Secrets two years ago. The one destroyed by basilisk venom. Its remains should be somewhere in Hogwarts—find them if you can, and bring them to me.

You must say nothing of this to anyone. It is not a matter that can come to light. Not under any circumstances.⟩

The ink splattered at the edges, the hurried handwriting—both bled anxiety and desperation.

The diary that Voldemort had entrusted to him for safekeeping—the one Lucius had slipped into Ginny Weasley's bag and sent off to Hogwarts.

Only belatedly had he realized his mistake, and now he was trying urgently to retrieve it through Carina.

But there were several things he did not know.

It was a Horcrux. A vessel containing a fragment of Voldemort's soul. It had already been destroyed—and because Dumbledore had realized what it was, the enemy was now searching for the other Horcruxes.

"Ugh."

The thought was so horrifying that her stomach heaved involuntarily. Carina gripped the edge of the sink.

She would die. If it came out, she would certainly die.

It was too great, too catastrophic a mistake. While no one knew, it didn't matter—but if he ever thought to take stock of his Horcruxes even once...

It would not end with Lucius's death alone.

"Hh—ha. Haah..."

Carina drew a ragged breath. One hand pressed to her chest, she whispered to herself—as though trying to deceive her own mind.

"As long as I—as long as I do my part. Then everyone can be safe."

You'll fail again, just like always. The plain, unadorned Carina in the mirror murmured back.

All those times she had tried every trick she could think of to beat Harry Potter. Most of her attempts had ended in failure. Carina had never once managed to see something through properly.

And with that level of ability, she dared to hope that everyone would come through unscathed—what a wish entirely beyond her station.

"I am Carina Malfoy."

Carina Malfoy—what a thoroughly wretched name.

What was the Malfoy family, really. People whose lives hung by a thread over a single diary, nothing more than one among Voldemort's countless followers.

The pride that had sustained her, the confidence that the Malfoy name had given her—it crumbled. The worth of a family name was like a price tag fixed to a sandcastle. No matter how beautifully it was praised, no matter how much it seemed worth a king's ransom in words—when a great wave finally came and swept it away, no one would spare it a second glance.

Had the name Malfoy always been this light, this worthless.

"I am..."

Carina stared into the mirror.

A pale face, hair damp with cold sweat. Fear and tension had ravaged her features. In those wide eyes there was nothing but dread.

A weak and foolish girl. Insignificant, incapable of anything. A stupid child with nothing left ahead of her but to be swept along—that was who stared back from inside the mirror. She looked so wretched she wanted to smash the glass.

She wanted to give up and sink to the floor. She wanted to crawl into somewhere warm and sheltered and rest. She wanted to set aside all the complicated things and lie in bed—and spend time with her friends, and with the person she loved.

But a single remaining thread of pride—everything she had done, everything she had built—held her in place.

She had to protect the Malfoys. Even if the Malfoys could not protect her.

"...I have to see it through."

You won't be able to.

The Carina in the mirror murmured back, without strength.

Carina turned her face away. Even showing weakness was no longer something she was permitted. She had to lift her chin and smile.

To deceive others—and to deceive herself.

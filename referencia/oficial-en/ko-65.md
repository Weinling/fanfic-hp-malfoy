Umbridge's power grew stronger with each passing day. New decrees issued from the Ministry in a steady stream; the Educational Decrees multiplied their numbers daily, each new clause rendering the previous ones obsolete.

Everyone sensed that something was off, yet they kept their mouths shut, telling themselves it couldn't be that bad. They dismissed the vague unease with the reassurance that daily life wouldn't change any further than this.

Then it happened.

"Th—this is tyranny!"

Professor Trelawney stood in the middle of the corridor, clutching an empty bottle, utterly dazed. Two trunks lay tumbled across the floor—by the look of them, someone had hurled them down from the top of the staircase.

"Impossible! This can't be happening! How dare you do this to me—!"

"Even for someone who can't predict tomorrow's weather, it's rather astonishing that you failed to foresee your own dismissal after conducting such abysmal classes."

A voice pitched high as a schoolgirl's rang out. Professor Umbridge addressed Professor Trelawney with a smile of deep self-satisfaction, as though she had just accomplished something momentous.

"You are no longer a professor. As Hogwarts High Inquisitor, I submitted the request for your dismissal—and the Minister signed it."

"This is outrageous! Hogwarts is my home!"

"It was, until an hour ago. Now, please leave promptly. You're making the students uncomfortable."

Umbridge appeared to take enormous pride and pleasure in what she had done. She would probably return to the Ministry and boast to anyone who would listen about how she had personally driven out one of Hogwarts' incompetent professors.

Carina had no particular fondness for Umbridge, but regardless, dismissing a professor had been an unavoidable step in consolidating her influence within Hogwarts.

"…And it was better if the one dismissed was the least competent."

Setting Umbridge herself aside, there were two candidates for that distinction: Sybill Trelawney and Rubeus Hagrid. One had to be chosen, and Carina had recommended Trelawney.

Just as the occasional oddity like Dobby emerged from among house-elves, Hagrid sometimes produced something genuinely remarkable from among his bizarre menagerie of creatures. Trelawney, by contrast, was a charlatan who taught nothing of substance.

There was a world of difference between a broken clock that was right twice a day and one whose hands had fallen off entirely. All things considered, Trelawney's removal suited Carina far better.

Crabbe wore an expression that stood in stark contrast to Carina's composure.

"B—but there's still a chance Professor Trelawney is a real Seer. She's gotten my mood right sometimes…"

"Don't talk nonsense, Crabbe. My brother Draco, who hasn't a shred of sensitivity, gets your mood right sometimes too."

Carina cut him down flatly.

"That's… that's too harsh…"

"What's too harsh is your brain. If even one percent of what that woman says were genuine prophecy, Harry Potter should have died fifteen times over between his third year and now. She's nothing more than a self-indulgent drunk—no more, no less."

It was work done in Umbridge's service, but in truth it was also something Hogwarts genuinely needed. Carina disliked Dumbledore and his haphazard way of running things, but she did not dislike Hogwarts itself.

If she was going to play the role of someone's instrument, she wanted to put this otherwise wasted power to slightly better use. That was Carina's thinking.

"Um… well…"

Crabbe glanced back and forth between Trelawney and Carina. He seemed to have something to say but couldn't bring himself to say it. Carina, who had been waiting, lost patience.

"If you have something to say, say it."

"…Malfoy. It's not that… you were bothered because Trelawney kept prophesying Potter's death, is it?"

"That again? I'm sick of hearing it."

Again and again and again. It had been months since they'd parted ways—why were these two so determined to keep linking her to Harry? Carina fixed Crabbe with a look of undisguised displeasure.

"I'd rather she were a real Seer, actually. That way, according to prophecy, Harry Potter would die in an accident in the not-too-distant future, and we'd be better off—"

Harry lying on a stone floor, his heart stopped. A thin line of blood trickling from the scar on his forehead. His glasses askew on his nose. His wand rolling away from his limp, outstretched hand. The red that had seeped from his ravaged body soaking into the floor. Empty, vacant eyes meeting hers…

Carina barely managed to suppress the wave of nausea that surged up without warning.

A fleeting image. Just one. But if Voldemort were to win, it was a future that would come to pass. A future that required no prophecy to imagine—one that was, in fact, entirely plausible…

Carina drew a sharp breath and murmured.

"It can't be helped… it can't be helped. Better that he…"

Should die.

The mere thought of it was agony. The words would not come easily to her lips.

Crabbe watched her in silence, then placed a hand on her shoulder.

The shouting from the entrance hall continued to grow louder.

Thanks to Dumbledore's intervention, Trelawney was stripped of her professorship but not expelled from Hogwarts. Umbridge was incensed at having her authority overridden.

"To dismiss me in front of everyone! This is Dumbledore showing contempt for the Ministry! How dare he—! A High Inquisitor appointed directly by the Minister exercises her rightful authority, and he makes a mockery of it!"

Umbridge slammed her fist on the desk in fury. Carina waited for her anger to run its course.

After venting at length, Umbridge steadied her breathing and spoke.

"…I'm sorry, Miss Malfoy. I lost my composure in front of a guest."

Carina inclined her head with graceful composure.

"I understand. Headmaster Dumbledore operates without any standards whatsoever. It's hardly surprising."

"Quite. Not a shred of common sense or proper hierarchy to be found. And the new professor he's brought in—what? A centaur? A half-giant and a centaur walking the halls as though it were perfectly normal. Is Dumbledore trying to turn Hogwarts into a zoo?"

This too, setting aside the manner of its expression, was not without merit. Take Hagrid alone—being half-giant himself, he seemed to assume the students were equally robust, and kept bringing in the most outlandish creatures. One ought to engage with other species through exchange, not instruction. It was better to first understand what one had in common before gradually learning to appreciate the differences.

But what was done was done. Carina probed Umbridge carefully.

"If Headmaster Dumbledore hadn't brought in a centaur, that position might have been filled by someone of your own choosing, Professor Umbridge."

"Exactly! When there are far more suitable, knowledgeable, and proven candidates available—and he drags in some horse-boned creature from the forest!"

Umbridge's plan was straightforward. Dismiss professors with obvious grounds for removal, publicise their incompetence, and fill their posts with Ministry-approved personnel.

That was precisely why she had chosen Trelawney over Hagrid, whom she despised. Hagrid had an excellent replacement in Grubbly-Plank; dismissing him would only result in someone else filling the vacancy.
Umbridge muttered to herself.

"…I'll need to ask Minister Fudge whether he can put a temporary hold on Broderick Bode's transfer."

An offhand remark, spoken without thinking. But Carina caught the information embedded in it.

Deep beneath the Ministry of Magic lay the Department of Mysteries—a place dedicated to researching phenomena so arcane and inexplicable that even magic could not account for them.

One such mystery was prophecy. The art of glimpsing the future, closely aligned with divination.

Broderick Bode must be an employee assigned to the prophecy division—

Having spent several days as Umbridge's subordinate, Carina had finally obtained the information her father wanted. She gave Umbridge a small, composed nod.

"You've achieved your primary objective, regardless. No professor will be able to dismiss you so easily again."

"I suppose I must be satisfied with that… Thank you, Miss Malfoy. Having someone as sensible and cooperative with the Ministry as yourself makes things considerably easier."

"It's simply my duty."

Carina offered a carefully constructed smile and took her leave of Umbridge's office. There was much to be done. She needed to write to her father, and there was a backlog of work and assignments that had piled up while she attended to Umbridge.

Absorbing Umbridge's hysteria was a draining business. The sharp edge of her nerves refused to settle easily. Even after retreating to the Slytherin common room and sinking into one of the fine sofas, her composure would not return.

"Malfoy, you're back?"

Crabbe approached. Carina returned a perfunctory greeting and glanced around the room looking for Draco. She could see a cluster of first-years making noise, but Draco was not among them.

"Has Draco not come back to the dormitory yet?"

"I think I saw him heading out a little while ago."

Goodness knows who he was off with. Carina pressed a hand to her forehead. Draco had a fondness for wandering, and with a boldness that suggested nothing frightened him, he had taken to exploring various corners of Hogwarts. It was behaviour more befitting a Gryffindor than a Slytherin.

He hasn't been at Hogwarts long—everything must still feel new and exciting to him.

Carina nodded to herself and rose from her seat. It was time to write to her father.

At that same hour.

"*Impedimenta*!"

The moment Draco had mastered the Impediment Jinx, he fired it squarely at Harry Potter's back. It was a perfect ambush. Draco was wearing the smile of someone who had just pulled off a masterstroke.

But children rarely consider how they appear to others when they're busy doing something to someone else.

He had crept around in a manner that was entirely obvious to everyone watching, and in his excitement before striking, he had swung his wand in a wide, theatrical arc. Draco had announced his attack with his entire body before he'd uttered a single word. By the time he cast the spell, every D.A. member in the room had already been watching him.

"*Protego*."

Harry, who had read every move, deflected it effortlessly with a Shield Charm. Draco went sprawling. Laughter broke out, though several people were actually cheering, fists clenched.

"Get up, Slytherin!"

"Don't give up—knock Harry Potter down!"

However fierce the rivalry between Gryffindor and Slytherin, no one directed real anger at a small child. These days, they had even taken to cheering Draco on—a boy who hit the floor every single time and still refused to quit.

It would have been different if Draco had been openly hostile to Harry, but Harry himself was perfectly warm towards him.

"Right, watch. This is the Shield Charm. Simple, low-level jinxes bounce right off. Pretty useful, isn't it?"

Draco scrambled to his feet, fuming.

"That's cheating! I cast my spell first!"

"Cheating? Is my wand some legendary wand or something? We both had wands, we both cast spells—what exactly is unfair about that?"

"I hadn't learned the Shield Charm yet!"

"Well, now you know what to do about that. Learn it."

"I was already planning to!"

Draco shouted this and then made a beeline for Hermione. The reason was simple: Granger was the best teacher.

"Granger! Teach me the Shield Charm!"

"Honestly. Harry's been winding him up again…"

"Quickly!"

"*Protego*. It's the incantation for the Shield Charm. The size of the shield changes depending on how you flick your wrist, so you need to decide on that before you cast…"

For what it was worth, the female members of the D.A. found Draco absolutely endearing. Even Marietta and Cho Chang drifted over on the pretext of helping him.

Once a general atmosphere of independent practice had settled over the room, Ron grumbled.

"Harry. How long are you going to keep teaching that kid?"

"Same as everyone else—until he decides to stop coming."

"He's a Slytherin. The moment he decides to quit, he'll probably go straight to Umbridge and report the whole thing."

"He won't. Kids that age are obsessed with secret clubs like this."

Harry dismissed Ron's concern. Ron clicked his tongue and watched with a distinctly unenthusiastic expression as Hermione ruffled Draco's hair. Draco stood perfectly still and let her. He seemed entirely accustomed to it…

"Ron, what does Mr Weasley say?"

"You were right. According to the Ministry visitor logs, Lucius Malfoy has been showing up at the Ministry almost every day for the past week."

Ron sat down beside Harry and lowered his voice.

"At first Dad thought it might be reconnaissance, but he's not so sure anymore. If Malfoy had contacts inside the Ministry, there'd be no reason to keep showing up in person… Dad thinks he's after something in the Department of Mysteries."

Harry recalled the time he had visited the Ministry—riding the lift down, passing floor after floor of different departments.

The Department of International Magical Cooperation. The Department of Magical Law Enforcement. The Department for the Regulation and Control of Magical Creatures. The Department of Magical Transportation. The Department of Magical Games and Sports. There had been departments that seemed genuinely important, and others so obscure you had to wonder whether the wizarding world really needed them.
And then the ninth floor underground. Described in the briefest possible terms, with no explanation whatsoever: simply, the Department of Mysteries.

"…That was it."

The memory surfaced. He had caught a glimpse when the lift doors opened—a wide corridor, and at its far end, a firmly shut door. The layout had been so bare that he'd forgotten it entirely.

Thinking about it now, it was identical to what he had seen in his dreams.

"The Department of Mysteries. That's what they're after—something inside it. But why? What's in there?"

"I don't know. Dad probably doesn't either. The people who work in the Department of Mysteries are called Unspeakables—they're bound to reveal nothing… Oh."

Ron spoke as though something had just occurred to him.

"Wouldn't Professor Dumbledore know?"

"He seems to be one of them too, in a manner of speaking. He says he won't tell me until I can successfully defend my mind."

Harry pressed down hard on the tangle of dark feelings rising inside him. He kept his voice light, but Dumbledore's secretiveness genuinely frustrated him.

What was in the Department of Mysteries that Dumbledore was so reluctant to tell him?

Just then, Draco detached himself from Hermione and began creeping towards Harry. He moved with such exaggerated stealth that Ron and Harry had to make a deliberate effort to look elsewhere.

Draco drew close, wearing the smile of someone about to deliver a decisive blow.

"*Protego*!"

He cast the Shield Charm he had just learned directly at Harry.

The spell worked better than it ever had before—but naturally, a Shield Charm did nothing to harm Harry. It was a spell for blocking incoming attacks, not launching them.

Harry counted silently to three, then the moment the shield dissolved, he flicked his wand.

"*Expelliarmus*."

"Ah!"

Draco, disarmed, stamped his feet in outrage.

"That's dirty!"

"The Shield Charm is a useful defensive spell, but it leaves you exposed to attacks from the side or behind, and if your opponent is holding back, you'll lose the initiative. Don't let nerves make you cast it before your opponent does."

"Ugh! Give it back! I want another go!"

"No. That's enough for today."

Harry jumped to his feet and called out.

"Right, everyone—good work! That's all for today!"

The students filed out in small groups. Several paused on their way out to cheer Draco on or ruffle his hair. Draco batted the hands away with an air of irritation.

Harry held up Draco's wand and said,

"Make sure you practise that Shield Charm. It won't do much when you're trying to bring someone down, but you'll need it when you're trying to protect yourself—or someone else."

"It's useless for beating you, though!"

"But it might stop me when I go after your sister."

Harry said this and handed the wand back.

Draco gripped his hawthorn wand tightly. As always, Harry had never retaliated when Draco attacked him—he simply disarmed him and returned the wand before they parted. He had never once raised his voice at him or shown any anger.

It was clear he didn't dislike Draco. And that was precisely what confused Draco most.

In the end, his curiosity got the better of him.

"…Why did you hit my sister?"

Even a thoughtless child could tell the difference between goodwill and hostility. Draco knew well enough that the D.A. members didn't dislike him. Harry Potter included.

Harry paused at the question, considering. What would happen if he ruffled his hair and called him 'brother-in-law'? Would he get angry, or just look put out?

But still—

Lucius Malfoy was clearly moving with some purpose, and he would almost certainly come into conflict with the Order of the Phoenix.

It was nearly time to start preparing. Harry had no desire to burden a ten-year-old with a difficult truth.

Harry arranged his features into an arrogant smirk.

"Because she was getting above herself."

"What?"

"She lost the Quidditch match and still walked around like she owned the place. It was insufferable. I acted before I thought."

As expected, Harry's words were more than enough to make Draco furious. Draco glared at him and issued his warning.

"You won once! Enjoy your gloating while it lasts. My sister—no, I'm going to be the one to bring you down someday!"

With that, Draco turned and ran off down the corridor at full tilt. It was impossible to tell whether he was fleeing or whether this was simply how he always walked.

There was something about Draco—the particular carelessness of a child who had grown up loved. He moved through the world as though it revolved around him, as though nothing could ever stand in his way.

Lucius Malfoy was a Death Eater. But to his own children, he had surely been the most devoted of fathers.

"…This isn't the time to be worrying about other people. I need to sort out my own problems first."

If it came to a confrontation, Harry would have to bring Lucius down. Barring something truly extraordinary, Azkaban awaited him.

When that happened—how would Carina react? How would Draco?

And could Harry bear it?

It was a genuinely difficult thing. Harry let out a long breath and ran a hand through his hair.

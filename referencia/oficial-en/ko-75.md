The children who had gone to the Ministry of Magic returned. So much had happened in the span of a single day that even among those who had gone themselves, few knew the full story.

With so many witnesses, each with their own partial view, only fragmented rumours drifted lightly between the students.

Rumour had it that the Death Eaters, caught off guard by the students' sudden appearance, had fled.

Rumour had it that the Death Eaters hadn't been particularly startled at all—it was Dumbledore's unexpected arrival that had scattered them in every direction.

Rumour had it that Dumbledore and He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named had duelled.

Rumour had it that half the Ministry had been destroyed, that London itself had trembled, and that senior Ministry officials, drawn back by the chaos, had witnessed him with their own eyes.

There was only one thing that was not a rumour.

That Harry Potter had walked out drenched in blood. That beside him, Sirius Black had lain utterly still, as though dead.

Because Carina had seen it herself.

A brief funeral was held.

Dumbledore read the eulogy with solemn gravity. Those gathered learned of a side of Sirius they had never known—how he had broken from the House of Black, how he had fought against Voldemort, and how a false accusation had ultimately landed him in Azkaban.

Carina learned of Black's life only after his death, a life she had known nothing of.

She felt a strange, involuntary solemnity settle over her, and when she considered the circumstances of his existence, something close to desolation followed.

A man who had lived with such ferocity. A man whose life had gone unrewarded.

All the things he had never been able to say, all the secrets no one would ever know—buried in his chest, condensed into a few words, and lowered into the ground.

Death still felt distant and foreign to Carina. Remote. Unfamiliar.

And having now experienced it once, she hoped it would remain that way. She could not easily accept someone's death—nor did she wish to.

Harry sat in the very front row. Faced with his godfather's death, he sat motionless, watching Sirius's coffin as though every emotion had been stripped from him entirely.

Carina reached toward him—then stopped, her hand curling into a fist before it could fully extend.

She had no right. Her father had devised the plan to lure Harry there, and Carina had not stopped him.

The attempt to stop him was no excuse. If she had told Harry everything instead of simply standing in his way, none of this would have happened.

All she could do was watch from a distance as Harry swallowed his grief in silence.

And she had no capacity to comfort anyone else, either.

Because a few days later, word came that Lucius Malfoy had been arrested and imprisoned in Azkaban.

The holiday began. A very long and very dark holiday.

"Your father made a grave mistake."

The Dark Lord.

He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named. He, that man, the one whose name must not be spoken.

A being with one name, yet called by countless titles precisely because that name could never be uttered.

Voldemort.

He sat upon his throne and spoke in a slow, unhurried drawl.

"He failed a plan he was so supremely confident in, announced our existence to the entire world, shattered the prophecy in the process… and lost something of mine."

But Voldemort's slowness was like a serpent closing in on its prey. It approached in a way that left no room for escape, tightening around Carina by degrees.

"With Lucius locked away in Azkaban, I find I cannot interrogate him directly… so I shall have to ask you instead."

"…He is my father, but he was never a man of flawless execution. Problems arose constantly—small oversights, a subtle overconfidence in his own abilities. Had you entrusted the matter to me, I could have handled it with considerably more care."

No expression. No emotion. The same beautiful mask she always wore.

Even standing before Voldemort, Carina did not lose her composure as she inclined her head.

Her manner drew a flicker of interest from him.

"You are saying you could have done better."

"With respect, if you were to grant me the opportunity to prove it, I would not disappoint."

"Hmm…"

The eyebrowless skin around his eyes shifted. His eroded nose and blurred features turned toward her.
Beyond any question of beauty or ugliness, his appearance was inhuman to the point of terror. What depths of dark magic one would have to plumb to arrive at such a form—Carina could not begin to imagine.

But concealing her true feelings was Carina's particular gift. She forced down the urge to look away and held his gaze steadily, her head tilted just slightly downward.

How long she waited, she could not say. At last, Voldemort's ruined lips parted.

"Your grandfather, Abraxas Malfoy, was a man of exceptional ability and great ambition. If there is anyone I can say with certainty gave me genuine aid, it is only him."

She did not know what he meant by it, but it did not sound like a condemnation. Carina lowered her head further without a word. Voldemort seemed to expect no reply; he simply extended his hand.

A soft, sliding sound. A great serpent crossed the floor past Carina's feet. Nagini, Voldemort's pet, coiled herself around the throne and settled beside him, as though she were his favoured consort.

Voldemort stroked Nagini and spoke.

"Kill someone. Dumbledore, or Harry Potter—either will do."

He did not bother to add the words or else. It was a kind of blank cheque. How well Carina performed would determine the measure of 'punishment' Voldemort chose to levy upon the Malfoy family.

Understanding his meaning, Carina bent low at the waist and answered.

"I am grateful for the charge. I will return having consumed their deaths, as you command."

There was not a moment's hesitation in her reply. Voldemort expressed his satisfaction.

"Ah, yes. I nearly forgot."

As though the thought had only just occurred to him, Voldemort raised his wand.

"Roll up your sleeve, Carina Malfoy."

The moment had come. Carina complied without resistance. The tip of Voldemort's yew wand drew close to her white hand and the slender wrist above it.

And the instant wand met skin—

A serpent burst from the tip of Voldemort's wand and burrowed into the inside of Carina's wrist.

It was a profoundly revolting and horrifying experience. Whenever the snake tattoo crawled across her skin, Carina was made to feel a creeping, skin-prickling dread.

Then, at some point, the serpent raised its head and began to writhe through her body.

Terrible pain tore through her. It was as though something were boring holes through her bones and crawling inside them. The wound blazed red, and her hand trembled violently. The nauseating agony—like maggots eating through her flesh—made her want to wrench her arm away and run.

But.

Carina clenched her teeth and endured it. Without a single cry, she watched what the serpent was doing with clear, unflinching eyes. As though she refused to be broken by something as trivial as pain, her expression did not waver.

And at last.

The Dark Mark was fully branded onto Carina's wrist. A serpent coiled through a human skull stared back at her.

Only then did Voldemort withdraw his wand. He spoke in a voice devoid of feeling.

"Congratulations, Carina Malfoy."

Nagini turned toward Carina and hissed. Carina did not speak Parseltongue, but from the way Nagini looked at her—from the quality of the attention—she understood well enough what was being said.

Nagini was welcoming a new companion.

Or mocking a foolish child ensnared by an unfortunate fate.

There was little need to distinguish between the two. The expression differed; the meaning was the same.

"You are now a Death Eater."

Carina would have to consume someone's death.

Whether she wished to or not.

Spinner's End.

Narcissa Malfoy sank to the floor and wept. Clutching Severus Snape's arm as though it were the only thing keeping her from drowning, she pleaded.

"Severus… you are the only one I have left to turn to. Please—my daughter, my proud, dear daughter…"

"That child has been given a mission by the Great One! You should be proud!"

Bellatrix's words were nowhere near enough to comfort Narcissa. Narcissa turned sharply, her tear-streaked eyes blazing.

"Carina will fail…! How could anyone carry out such a mission at Hogwarts, with not a single ally…! And even if—even if she somehow managed…!"

Even if she succeeded in killing someone, the likelihood of escaping unscathed was effectively zero. What could be the reason for assigning a task that even seasoned Death Eaters could not accomplish?
Narcissa did not find the answer difficult to guess.

"This is retribution. It is no different from ordering Carina to die somewhere far from home, as punishment for her husband's failure. Severus, please. Ask the Dark Lord…"

"If you wish to ask that the mission be rescinded, I can only tell you it is impossible. No one can bend his will."

Narcissa began to sob openly. But Snape continued, his manner as composed as ever.

"The Dark Lord is deeply furious. Lucius was the one responsible for that operation. Yet he failed to obtain the prophecy, was arrested alongside several others, and is now imprisoned. That failure was the most devastating setback in recent memory…"

"He means to kill her!"

Narcissa tore at her own hair as she cried out. Snape poured a glass of wine and held it out to her.

"Calm yourself. There is no certainty she will fail, and even if she does, that need not be the end. Dumbledore and Harry Potter are intoxicated by their own virtue—they will not bring themselves to cause Carina any visible harm."

"But if she fails, the Dark Lord will not forgive it!"

"What decision he would make in that event, I cannot say."

Snape cut the matter short. At that moment, Narcissa seized his arm. She knelt on the floor, her face desperate, and looked up at him.

"Severus—you, you are the only one. Lucius is in prison. Draco is too young. Carina is isolated at Hogwarts. Only you—only you can help her…"

"You are asking me to take the mission the Dark Lord assigned and claim it for myself?"

"If she fails, the task will fall to you next anyway… Oh, Severus. In the end, it is only a matter of order. Please, watch over Carina. And if it truly seems she cannot manage it… finish what she could not in her place."

"I will try."

"If you would protect her… Severus, will you swear it? An Unbreakable Vow?"

"An Unbreakable Vow?"

Snape's expression was so composed it was impossible to read. But Bellatrix let out a triumphant, sneering laugh.

"Cissy, you believe him? He said he'll try. He's planning to wriggle out of it again, same as always—he'll claim he needs to stay safe because he's a spy… tch!"

"Very well. I will swear it."

Snape said it quietly. Bellatrix's mouth fell open.

"Your sister will serve as witness. Come—move closer."

Snape spoke.

Bellatrix stepped forward and stood over them both, placing the tip of her wand upon their clasped hands.

Narcissa began.

"Severus, will you watch over my daughter Carina while she fulfils the mission the Dark Lord has set before her?"

"I will."

Snape answered.

From the tip of the wand, a bright tongue of flame flickered to life and wound itself around their joined hands.

"And will you use every ability at your disposal to aid her?"

"I will."

Snape answered, his voice steady.

A second flame stretched long from the wand's tip and entwined with the first, forming a chain that shone with a cold, beautiful light.

Two vows already bound between them, yet Narcissa did not release his hand. She whispered, her voice trembling.

"And if… if it seems Carina is going to fail……"

Snape's hand shifted. But he did not pull it free from Narcissa's grip.

"Will you complete what she could not?"

A brief silence fell. Bellatrix watched them both with wide eyes, her wand resting upon their clasped hands, perfectly still.

Snape opened his mouth slowly.

"I will."

A third flame rose. It wound itself around the other two, binding the hands that held each other fast—like two serpents locked in a struggle to the death.

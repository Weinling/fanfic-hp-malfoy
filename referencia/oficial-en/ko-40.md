# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 40: Mad-Eye Moody

Carina had been bound hand and foot and shoved into a dark, cheerless room.

It was a grim, unlived-in space. The only furnishings were a bed, a wardrobe, and a desk buried under stacks of documents and magical instruments. The room had all the warmth of a utility closet, and with Rita Skeeter and Moody both crammed inside, it felt ready to burst at the seams.

Moody dragged a chair over and sat Carina down. Resistance was pointless at this stage—and pointless struggling was simply undignified—so she settled into the chair without protest.

Moody's base of operations was a trunk concealed in the undergrowth. Like the tents at the Triwizard Tournament, the trunk contained a habitable space folded away inside it.

Carina kept her voice carefully level.

"This trunk has an Undetectable Extension Charm on it. So this is where you've been hiding."

"That's right... everything I have is in here. After they cleared out, I slipped back and retrieved it. None of it would have been possible without the Marauder's Map, of course..."

Moody said this with a faint smile and drew a folded sheet of parchment from his pocket. It was the Marauder's Map—the one Harry had shown her once. His eye swept rapidly across it.

"Good. There's still time."

He muttered this to himself, then rummaged in his coat and produced a crystal vial. A clear liquid sloshed inside the transparent bottle, indistinguishable from water.

The corner of Carina's mouth gave an involuntary twitch.

Colourless. Odourless. Unless Mad-Eye Moody had developed a habit of carrying plain water in glass vials, that could only be—

"Veritaserum. Truth Potion. Extraordinarily rare." He turned the vial in his fingers. "I would prefer that you answer my questions honestly. If you lie, or attempt to—I will use it. And once I've gone to the trouble, I may find myself asking rather more than I originally intended."

Carina knew too much. From Voldemort and his Horcruxes to her father's role in releasing the diary into the world.

She made herself a promise: avoid outright lies as much as possible. Give away only the smallest, most inconsequential details—but do not drink the Veritaserum. If anything about the Horcruxes slipped out, the family would be in danger.

Carina gave a single nod. Moody turned his head. Rita Skeeter fixed her with a venomous stare, shoving her glasses up her nose.

"You'd do well to answer properly, little girl... I know considerably more than you think!"

"Save the threats. Just take your notes."

Moody waited until Rita Skeeter had produced her Quick-Quotes Quill, then loomed over Carina with deliberate menace.

"What did you discuss with Karkaroff?"

"I exchanged pleasantries. I wanted to ask whether anything unusual had been happening lately."

Not a lie. It simply happened that the unusual matter in question concerned the Dark Mark.

Moody's magical eye spun in its socket. Carina swallowed—but to her surprise, he showed no particular reaction.

He scowled and pressed on.

"Pleasantries? You boarded the Durmstrang ship for pleasantries? You weren't acting on Lucius's instructions?"

"My father was the connection, yes—that much is true. He suggested I make use of Karkaroff. I agreed because having one more contact is never a disadvantage."

Every word of that was the truth, start to finish.

Moody's eye spun again. Carina found herself wondering whether that eye was some kind of lie detector.

"When did you first learn the Triwizard Tournament was coming?"

"Last summer. Around the time of the Quidditch World Cup."

"Do you know how the Goblet of Fire came to Hogwarts?"

"Only that it was held at the Ministry and then transported here."

Carina answered without hesitation, and each time she did, Moody clicked his tongue and rolled his eye—literally—as though her answers disappointed him. None of them, she gathered, were what he was looking for.

"Then why did you approach Potter? What were you trying to find out?"

"Approach him?"

The suggestion was absurd. Carina answered at once.

"Harry approached me. He confessed his feelings first. I simply... entertained the idea, to pass the time."

Harry Potter had been the one to lay his heart bare. Carina had merely relented, moved by the sheer desperate sincerity of it. Approach him? Carina Malfoy making the first move, going to someone and confessing? The very idea was preposterous.

She said it without a shadow of doubt.

At which point the golden antenna behind Moody vibrated sharply. His magical eye swivelled backward, lingered for a moment, then rotated back to Carina.

"That was a lie."

Carina felt a jolt of alarm but kept her expression composed.

Strange. Every word had been the absolute truth—or so she'd thought.

"I have a Sneakoscope that detects falsehoods."

Moody pointed at the golden antenna with one finger. It was still trembling, having caught her out.

She'd let her guard down. So preoccupied with the Veritaserum, she hadn't considered that other means might exist, and she'd let something slip.

She needed to think fast—but Moody was clearly practiced at interrogation. He gave her no time to collect herself.

"Lying won't work. Take what you just said and replace the false parts with the truth. Every word. Don't change anything else."

Harry had approached her first.

Harry had confessed first.

She had entertained it to pass the time.

Three sentences. Only one of them contained even the smallest possible misrepresentation.

Carina pressed her lips together and repeated what she'd said.

"Harry approached me first... he confessed his feelings to me first... and..."
She squeezed her eyes shut and forced the words out.

"I—I accepted because I wanted to!"

The moment the words left her mouth, her face flooded scarlet with mortification.

Of all people to hear this for the first time—not Harry, not Barnette or Gray, but two old strangers.

It was unbearable. But Carina Malfoy did not bow her head before an enemy. She lifted her chin instead and brazened it out.

Moody turned sharply. His gaze landed on a second golden antenna resting on the desk. He watched it, clearly willing it to move—but it sat perfectly still.

He muttered, sounding dissatisfied.

"One shouldn't place too much faith in a Sneakoscope... but there it is."

Then he raised his wand. Carina flinched and shut her eyes—and felt the ropes binding her fall away.

"That'll do. Wait a moment. I'll let you out shortly..."

He tossed her wand back to her. She caught it on reflex. Moody turned his back to her, completely unguarded—but Carina found she couldn't bring herself to attack.

Partly because she'd already learned her lesson once. But also because she felt no hostility from him whatsoever.

Rita Skeeter shot to her feet.

"Moody! That's it? You're just letting her go?!"

"Have to, don't I. I might make an exception for Lucius himself, but I won't lay hands on a Hogwarts student."

Moody said it bluntly and began arranging his bottles and instruments into neat rows.

Rita Skeeter's lined face crumpled into something uglier.

"Do I look like someone who deserves to rot in a hole like this just because the Prophet stopped running my articles? Not a chance! I'm helping you for revenge—revenge on the Prophet, which sold its journalism for a handful of Galleons, and on the Malfoys, who think money can buy anything!"

A tabloid journalist invoking the principles of journalism. Had she been hit with a Memory Charm recently, or was she simply without conscience?

Carina decided it must be a Memory Charm. No human being could be that brazen and that shameless.

Moody remained unmoved by Rita Skeeter's outburst.

"There'll be plenty of opportunity for that. Lucius is next. Save your revenge for then."

"Why not use this girl right now?"

"Dumbledore won't allow it. And neither will I."

Dumbledore?

Only now did Carina realise she had been operating under a fundamental misapprehension.

She had taken Harry's word for it and assumed that Professor Moody was an enemy in disguise. She hadn't questioned it, hadn't thought to verify it.

Because from the very beginning, Alastor Moody and Carina Malfoy had been on opposite sides.

The Malfoys—a wizarding aristocracy of wealth and influence. The most useful allies a Death Eater could ask for. Even after the war, when Lucius had wriggled free by claiming innocence, he had continued to support the surviving Death Eaters from the shadows.

The real Moody would despise the Malfoys.

If the man before her were a fake, he would be wary of Carina as Harry's companion.

But the one who actually had reason to abduct her here was—

"Wait. Moody... Professor?"

"What?"

Moody's expression remained thoroughly unimpressed. Carina's mind worked quickly.

How could she determine whether this was the real Moody or an impostor? She thought for a brief moment, then spoke.

"Harry suspects you might be someone disguised as Professor Moody. What do you have to say to—"

She got no further.

Moody burst out laughing. It was remarkable, really, that a man with a twisted mouth and only one eye could laugh with such abandon.

When he finally recovered, he bellowed:

"Potter! That boy has talent!"

His face was alight with genuine delight.

"Always suspect your surroundings! Judge trust with reason, not feeling! Potter is the only one who's ever actually done it properly! He has the makings of an Auror. No—he already is one, and a fine one at that! I see hope in him!"

It was the last reaction she'd expected.

A fake Moody would have been alarmed.

The real Moody, she'd assumed, would have been furious at Harry's unfounded suspicion.

Instead, Moody was laughing with evident pleasure.

"Albus introduced me. But Potter—he didn't simply take my word for it! He trusted Dumbledore, and extended to me exactly as much trust as that warranted. The moment he noticed I wasn't cooperating, he dropped me without hesitation. He moved immediately to verify my true intentions, my true worth! That is exactly the right attitude! There is nothing more dangerous than an ally who won't cooperate!"

Hope.

Moody had been driven out by Harry—and he was still placing his hopes in him.

"Potter is truly the Chosen One... His abilities are equal to, or greater than, He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named!"

"Don't say that!"

Carina snapped, glaring at him. Moody let the laughter drain from his face and looked at her steadily.

"There is only one thing that concerns me. And that is you. Malfoy."
Mad-Eye Moody was every bit as mad as his name suggested.

Overjoyed that someone had suspected him. Placing his hopes in that very suspicion. And now, on top of all that—

He was worried about Carina, whom he'd been so eager to catch.

"You'd do well to think carefully. Your actions are rational enough. Dumbledore is endlessly principled, and Potter would tear out his own heart for you if you asked. Setting your feelings aside entirely—aligning yourself with this side is sound judgement..."

Carina hadn't sided with Harry out of strategic calculation. That kind of cold reasoning was only ever something she used to explain away her own feelings to herself.

But she fell silent at what Moody said next.

"—Nevertheless, you need to understand this. His return is a certainty. No amount of effort will prevent it. The only question is whether he can be killed once he does return... And when he returns, either you or Potter will die."

The vague unease she'd been carrying took on a definite, ominous shape.

Carina had known. She'd always known.

The Dark Mark blazing in the sky over the summer. The missing persons reports, growing longer by the week. The dark letters arriving from her father.

She was too close to the shadow falling over the wizarding world to pretend she couldn't see it.

"It would have been simpler if you were acting on Lucius's orders. But if you're not... can you navigate this on feeling alone? In the war between He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named and his Death Eaters, and the Order of the Phoenix—can you keep your heart intact?"

His spinning eye held something like pity.

The scars covering his face spoke of pain endured.

And the mouth that delivered these facts without flinching carried a cold, clear warning.

Carina could not find an answer.

At that moment, the Sneakoscope on the desk began spinning furiously. Moody's eyes swept the room. His gaze visited the Sneakoscope, the mirror on the wall, and the Marauder's Map in quick succession, then returned.

"Potter and Black are coming. Remarkably fast response... I find I approve."

Moody raised his wand. His posture said he intended to fight.

Carina spoke quickly.

"Wait. If you're the real Professor Moody, there's no reason to fight Harry!"

"On the contrary."

Moody smiled—a crooked, satisfied thing.

"Opportunities for a genuine duel, no quarter given, are rare. It will be good experience for Potter..."

"You're completely mad!"

The words had barely left her mouth when the trunk's lid flew open. A square of sky appeared overhead, and framed against it were the faces of Harry and Sirius.

Harry's expression was colder than she had ever seen it. And yet the moment Carina saw his face, she felt an involuntary smile rise to her lips.

Harry's gaze found her. He confirmed she was unharmed, and said in a voice stripped of warmth:

"You're surrounded. Let Carina go."

"Potter—a word of advice. If something is most important to you, keep it hidden until the last. Mentioning it this readily is the same as showing your enemy your weakness..."

Harry's jaw tightened, but Moody made no move to use Carina as a hostage. He swung his wand with unhurried ease.

"Why don't you come down and take her?"

The interior was Moody's territory.

Plenty of cover. Room to manoeuvre. And Carina as a potential hostage, which made reckless spellfire impossible.

Harry and Sirius, by contrast, would have to descend through a narrow opening.

Even at two against one, the advantage was Moody's. Whatever his intentions about giving Harry real experience, the risk of Harry being hurt was real.

This fight had to be stopped.

"Everyone stop!"

Carina raised her wand.

At her own temple.

Harry froze. Sirius froze. Even Moody, with his all-seeing magical eye, went still. All three stared at her with identical expressions of bewilderment.

"...What are you doing?"

She'd originally meant to point it at Moody. But the last time she'd done that, she'd ended up as a ferret. Carina had hesitated mid-motion.

It wasn't the transformation itself that frightened her. It was the thought of what it would do to Harry's state of mind.

So she'd redirected the wand.

To her own head.

"Nobody move. If anyone does... I'll—to my own head, I'll—"

Pointing her wand at her own head hadn't been part of any plan. Her hand had simply lost its direction and aimed at the nearest available target.

Carina was so thoroughly flustered that she shouted the most terrible thing she could imagine.

"I'll stick bubblegum in my hair!"

Carina Malfoy, you absolute idiot. What on earth are you saying.

Carina's face went the colour of a ripe apple. Everyone else fell into a stunned, wordless silence.

Only Rita Skeeter's Quick-Quotes Quill went on scratching, faithfully recording every word.

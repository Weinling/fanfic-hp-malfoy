Diagon Alley.

The street built for wizards alone was filled with wondrous magic and mystery. A ladle stirred itself through a cauldron packed with a mud-coloured potion; each time a bubble burst, iridescent smoke drifted upward on a faint, sweet scent before dissolving into nothing.

A witch seated at the roadside yawned at length. The shops were cluttered beyond measure with magical ingredients and reagents, yet the street itself was cleaner than it had any right to be.

Today, Diagon Alley had no customers.

There were never many wizards across all of Britain, so a quiet day outside the busy season was hardly surprising—but the particular emptiness today was not a matter of imagination.

The Triwizard Tournament at Hogwarts had been drawing people away for days. Ministry employees had poured every effort into ensuring the Tournament concluded without a hitch, and a considerable number had been dispatched to Hogwarts. Beyond them, anyone with even a passing connection to the Ministry had found some excuse to attend and watch.

As a result, Diagon Alley today was quieter than anywhere else in London.

"Which is precisely why we came."

Gringotts, the bank built by goblins, was open for business as always.

An elderly goblin found himself facing a customer he hadn't seen in a very long time. He looked up with a sour expression at the sharp-featured woman standing before him.

"And so."

Lustrous black hair. Heavy-lidded eyes. A witch who had grown somewhat gaunt but still possessed a brilliant, imperious beauty stared down at the goblin with undisguised arrogance.

Bellatrix Lestrange. Imprisoned in Azkaban, she had aged only the very slightest fraction beyond her youth as she pressed the goblin with her demands.

"When can I access my vault?"

"The vault has been unused for the past seventeen years, and locating the key is taking a little time. If you would be so kind as to wait just a moment."

The elderly goblin answered with practiced courtesy. But Bellatrix Lestrange suddenly cried out.

"I am in a hurry! I need to retrieve it this very instant!"

"It will only be a moment. That said, Mrs. Lestrange—I had understood you to be confined in Azkaban."

The elderly goblin ventured the remark as a test. Bellatrix responded with a high, grating laugh.

"Kyahaha! Curious, are you? Shall I tell you? Would you like me to explain, very kindly, exactly how I escaped from that place?"

"...No. That will not be necessary."

Anyone who assumed that goblins, working only in a bank, gathered no intelligence would be making a dangerously costly mistake. Goblins were a people who employed every means available to protect themselves.

They, too, had been reading the ominous signs in their own way, drawing information from every quarter they could reach.

Where gold flows, information follows.

The goblins had obtained certain pieces of intelligence unknown to wizardkind, and so, while Bellatrix's appearance startled them, it did not entirely confound them.

"May I see your identification?"

"Don't hold it long. If you leave so much as a fingerprint on my wand, I may find myself wanting to kill you."

Bellatrix extended her wand with the same imperious, overbearing manner. The elderly goblin received it in both hands without any change of expression and examined it carefully.

Walnut wood. Dragon heartstring. Twelve and three-quarter inches.

Unmistakably the wand of Bellatrix Lestrange.

Beyond that, Bellatrix drew a necklace from inside her robes and dangled it briefly.

"If you still don't believe me, I have the Black family seal as well. But if you allow your grubby hands to touch even this, I will be taking one of those hands with me."

That cruelty, that contempt—fourteen years in Azkaban had done very little to change her.

"That will not be necessary. The Black family seal is unmistakable."

The elderly goblin bowed his head and produced a golden key. He called over a younger goblin and passed it to him.

"Mardnak. Escort Mrs. Lestrange to her vault."

"Understood. This way, if you please."

Bellatrix wheeled around. The two shaggy-haired men who had been standing behind her shuffled nervously after her.

Bellatrix and the two men followed Mardnak. When Mardnak opened the door, a long passage of rough-hewn stone appeared before them.

Before stepping through, Mardnak turned to Bellatrix.

"Mrs. Lestrange, these gentlemen are...?"

"They escaped Azkaban with me."

Bellatrix glanced at them sidelong. One man wore his long hair hanging loose over his face; the other was a shabby-looking fellow with a flat cap pulled low. For just a moment, something like affection flickered in her eyes as she looked at the shabby one.

It passed in an instant. Bellatrix gave a contemptuous sniff.

"We shared a prison, so I agreed to give them a bit of gold. The more worthless a person is, the easier they are to use as a servant..."

"Ah, I see. Will they be entering the vault with you?"

"I promised them as much gold as they could carry in both hands. Greedy creatures..."

Mardnak was seized by a cold, crawling dread.

Ancient magic had been laid upon Gringotts: any gold lifted from another's vault would grow scorching hot to the touch, and simultaneously a Gemino Curse would trigger, burying the thief beneath an avalanche of burning false gold. A thief who helped themselves to something that wasn't theirs could be drowned, crushed beneath a cascade of searing metal.

Bellatrix had planned all of this to make an example of these unsuspecting escapees.

How sadistic. How utterly merciless. Surely the heavens had simply forgotten to include compassion when they made her.

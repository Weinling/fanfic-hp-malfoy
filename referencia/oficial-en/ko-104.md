# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 104: The Devil's Trial

My dearest Albus.

You decided not to judge. Not to evaluate. To inform without instructing, to share what you had learned while keeping the conclusions you had drawn buried deep, to the very end.

To give others time to think, the right to seek truth, the chance to find their own morality.

Without acting—only watching. Looking down upon the children from your seat as Headmaster of Hogwarts.

But Albus.

That is not goodness.

It isn't even kindness.

The conclusion you arrived at through your own deliberation is not a choice available to ordinary human beings.

You, who know all the answers, looking down upon people infinitely more foolish and fragile than yourself, watching to see what choices they will make.

Whether things go well or badly. Whether they choose right or wrong—simply watching in silence until the decision is made.

And then sitting in solemn judgment, observing that choice… and assigning it a score.

Do you understand now, Albus?

What people call your method.

A test.

A trial handed down by some lofty, transcendent being to grant enlightenment to insignificant creatures.

I rejected your way. That remains true even now, imprisoned in this tower after my defeat at your hands.

Your way was wrong, Albus.

However brilliant you may be, you are still human in the end. Capable of being right, capable of being wrong.

And so you must hunger for the wrong answers. You must not abandon action after wrestling with questions of good and evil. If your choices prove correct, posterity will revere you; if you chose wrongly, they will look upon that and build new morality from the wreckage.

If you still don't understand, allow me to explain.

Tom Marvolo Riddle is the worst failure of your life, and Harry Potter is the life your sins brought into being. That is precisely why Harry Potter could only ever become your heir.

Albus. I intend to test him.

Your final creation—left behind by you, who spent a lifetime administering tests—

can he, I wonder, pass mine?

The witch atop the prison tower murmured softly. Remembering her old friend, who had died before her.

Cedric Diggory was a close confidant of the Minister for Magic, and his standing grew more secure with each passing day. When Cedric made his wishes known with such force, the Ministry had no choice but to defer to him—even knowing of his friendship with Harry. He had come to Hogwarts to resolve the matter concerning Harry Potter.

"Harry!"

The moment Cedric arrived at Hogwarts, he went looking for Harry. Granted access to Gryffindor Tower with Professor McGonagall's permission, he found Harry shuffling down the stairs in the middle of a long, lazy yawn.

Harry straightened his rumpled pyjamas and greeted him.

"It's been a while, Cedric."

"Where's Malfoy?"

"Asleep."

Harry answered briefly, then shut his mouth and studied Cedric.

He couldn't quite put his finger on it, but something felt off. Showing up without warning and asking for Carina the moment he laid eyes on Harry? It rather looked as though Cedric had business with Carina specifically.

Voldemort's soul resided in Harry. Carina had simply come along with him.

Harry shook the drowsiness from his body and asked.

"Why are you looking for Carina?"

Cedric, oblivious to Harry's suspicion, answered plainly.

"Everything Malfoy asked for is ready. I came to tell her."

"Asked for?"

"Right. Anyway, I'm so relieved. That you're going to be all right!"

"What?"

There were still problems left to resolve before anyone could call him all right. As Harry frowned in confusion, Cedric set down the large trunk he'd brought with him. It was a distinctive piece of luggage—seven keyholes lined across its face.

After a moment's thought, Harry recognized it.

"That's Professor Moody's trunk."

"Borrowed it for a bit. Better if no one sees me bringing him in."

"Him?"

Leaving Harry none the wiser, Cedric pressed ahead on his own. He produced a thick key and fitted it into the largest keyhole. A click—the lock gave way. The seventh compartment opened.

Cedric said:

"I heard the story. That you've found a way to separate the soul inside you?"

Harry had pieced together enough of the picture to hold his tongue and wait for Cedric to continue.

Cedric was too busy keeping a wary eye on the trunk's interior to notice Harry's expression. He glanced toward it and went on.
"To think there was a way to transfer a master's soul onto a servant who bears the Dark Mark. I never would have dreamed such a thing was possible."

This was news to Harry as well. Transfer a master's soul onto a servant? Searching through his own memories and the full breadth of Voldemort's knowledge combined, it was simply impossible. If souls could be passed between people so easily, why would such magic have been forbidden as Dark in the first place?

It was absurd. Someone had fed Cedric false information.

But since he could already guess who that someone was, Harry said nothing to correct it. He kept his silence.

"It's a bit grim, forcing another person's soul into someone—but if Azkaban were still standing, Karkaroff would have received the Dementor's Kiss anyway, so perhaps it's not so different… And he was his master, after all."

"Karkaroff."

It was a name he'd heard from Carina. Harry turned it over in his mind for a moment, then said, as if testing the waters:

"Impressive, catching Karkaroff. How did you manage it?"

"How did we manage it? He attacked Malfoy during her visit to Austria and got overpowered for his trouble. You should have seen our faces when the Austrian Department of International Cooperation brought him in—"

Cedric, who had been lowering a long ladder into the trunk, gave a sudden start and turned his head. His eyes met Harry's.

Cedric said, with an uncomfortable look:

"…Did you not know?"

"Until about a minute ago?"

"Wait—Malfoy didn't tell you? Good grief."

Harry didn't wait to hear more. He dropped straight into the trunk. No broomstick, no ladder needed. Just before he hit the bottom, he cast Arresto Momentum to cushion the impact and landed lightly on his feet.

Inside: dim and close. A space stripped of any sense of habitation, containing only what was necessary. More tools than furniture—it had the look of a workroom.

Seated in a hard wooden chair fitted with iron chains was a face Harry had encountered once, long ago.

Harry gave a small wave toward the man drowning in fear and dread.

"Hello, Mr. Karkaroff."

In the Gryffindor common room, scarlet flames crackled and spat in the fireplace. The fireplaces at Hogwarts ran cool in summer and warm in winter; no matter how fiercely they burned, neither smoke nor ash ever drifted into the room. They gave off steady light and a comfortable heat at all times—the ideal magical hearth, in every sense.

If magic were available to him, he supposed a fireplace would be preferable to an electric heater. Harry caught himself thinking it and laughed, a little bitterly. He hadn't even decided what was going to happen yet, and here he was contemplating what kind of heating to install.

"Harry?"

Just then, Carina came stumbling down the stairs from the Gryffindor dormitory. She had probably woken to find him gone and come looking.

Any other day, he would have been on his feet at once, grinning broadly. He would have walked out to meet her, pulled her close—warmer and brighter than any fireplace—and stretched out on the sofa with her. Or simply scooped her up and carried her back upstairs.

But not today.

"The conversation we didn't finish on the Hogwarts Express. It's a bit late, but I think we need to continue it."

The fact that Carina's expression didn't change told him she had been expecting this. Like Cassandra accepting what she had already foreseen, Carina came toward him without a word.

Dressed in pyjamas like Harry, she pulled her loose collar together and settled onto the sofa.

"You went to Nurmengard?"

"Yes."

"And you met Grindelwald there?"

"I'm sorry, but could you get to the point? I'd rather not sit here being cross-examined about things we both already know."

Carina answered with a sideways tilt of her chin. She made no effort to hide that she had no desire to dwell on this subject. Harry exhaled and went straight to it.

"I won't say a word about the fact that you went alone, without any protection, and nearly came to grief when you ran into Karkaroff."

"Ah, right. I survived Karkaroff thanks to the protective charm you placed on me. But since you said you won't mention that… I suppose I won't say thank you."

"But what came after isn't something I can simply let go. Transferring Voldemort's soul onto Karkaroff? Why did you tell that lie—to Cedric, and apparently to others at the Ministry as well?"

Carina answered with cool composure.

"It isn't exactly a lie."

"Grindelwald told you a method like that exists?"

"Something like it."

"What do you mean, some—no."

Harry pressed a hand to his forehead.

"Carina. Grindelwald is a Dark wizard. One of the most powerful and dangerous Dark wizards who ever lived—powerful enough that Professor Dumbledore himself had to be the one to stop him. And you're trusting a method he gave you?"

"What other choice did I have?"

Carina fixed him with a sharp look.

"If I hadn't found something, you would have taken your own life."

"That's not—I was only going to bind myself with an Unbreakable Vow, to contain it—"

"To me, that's the same thing!"

Carina leaned forward sharply. In her wide, silver-grey eyes, the firelight blazed and danced.

"I looked at what the Unbreakable Vow would bind you to. Touch a wand—you die. Use magic to harm anyone—you die. To prevent you from breaking the Vow, seek out a witness or enter any wizarding library—you die. And more, and more, and more."

Death wasn't listed as a separate condition, but the Unbreakable Vow had no price other than death. And without binding Harry this severely, Voldemort might find a way to break free. The Ministry had feared that. So had Harry.

But a handful of people who valued Harry's life had refused to accept it and were still searching for another way. The most desperate among them was Carina.
"Is this what you call living? Locking yourself in a cage and sinking to the bottom of a lake—is that what you mean when you say you want to live?"

The same argument. The same ending, every time.

Harry had nothing to say to her accusation. Every word he spoke was another wound he was inflicting on her.

But today, Carina held one more card.

"I'd rather follow the method Grindelwald gave me than listen to any more of your nonsense."

He couldn't refute it. Harry closed his eyes and steadied himself.

Perhaps, without realizing it, he had been clinging to Dumbledore's instructions out of guilt. Snape, Dumbledore, Sirius—the weight of those who had died in his place had made him resistant to any other path.

There was no reason to refuse what Grindelwald had offered. Harry talked himself into it.

"All right. What is it?"

"It's quite simple. You already know it, in a way. But once you hear it and agree, we have to act immediately. If we wait, the soul inside you might sense something and prepare itself."

"No reason not to."

"I heard that clearly. No taking it back later."

Her expression brightened. Carina pointed to Moody's trunk sitting in the middle of the common room.

"Do you know what's inside?"

"I said hello to him earlier."

"Then you'll know he's the villain who attacked me, and a traitor who deserves what's coming to him."

"Yes."

"And on top of that—I told the Ministry a few things that weren't entirely true. That there's a deep bond between Karkaroff and Voldemort, and that with the right method, a soul can be transferred between them. That we'd need Karkaroff to perform it, so once his interrogation was finished, they should hand him over to us."

That was why the Ministry Aurors had lowered their guard around Harry. And with their attention consumed by interrogating Karkaroff and rounding up the remaining Death Eaters, their surveillance of Harry had grown lax.

That was also, no doubt, why Harry and Carina had been able to spend the past few days alone together.

"But Carina—the Dark Mark isn't that deep a bond. Voldemort designed it so that he could exert influence through it, but he never allowed the reverse. Even to his own servants, from Voldemort's perspective, they were no different from ants he happened to pass on the road."

"I know. I was actually one of his followers, once."

Carina mentioned the uncomfortable past without any change in expression, then smiled—bright and triumphant.

"But Harry—you know this too, don't you? That there were things he prized far more than any of his servants."

"Things?"

"Yes."

The things he had collected—the treasures of Hogwarts' founders, among others. And they all shared one thing in common.

Horcruxes. Objects that had held fragments of his soul.

The moment that thought struck him, it hit Harry like a bolt of lightning. The objects that had been his symbol—the dark artefacts Harry had been meant to find and destroy. Their defining characteristic was—

That a soul could be torn away and stored within them.

The fire crackled on. The pop and hiss of sparks rang out, low and eerie. Light carved shadows from the dark, and in those shadowed faces, every kind of feeling lived.

"Don't tell me. Carina, you—"

"You've got it, Harry."

Carina looked at him as though nothing were the matter—open, hopeful, almost serene.

"Karkaroff… was a criminal who was going to receive the Dementor's Kiss regardless. If we say he died from complications during the ritual, no one will question it."

A life that deserved to be forfeit. No need for excessive guilt. She had removed 'guilt' from the equation entirely.

"Everything else is already prepared. Grindelwald told me how the ritual is performed, so I know what to do."

All the preparations were in place. All that remained was the will to act.

"Come on, then. Harry. Before the soul inside you senses something is wrong and starts to defend itself."

Voldemort was the master of Horcruxes—she pressed that point, her voice sharpening with urgency.

"Kill him, and tear the soul free."

Carina—who wanted Harry to live more than anyone in the world—whispered it to him.

The witch in the prison tower laughed.

It would come wearing the face of the one he loved most. And the more desperate that love, the more meticulous and intricate the web it would weave around him.

And yet. And yet.

There is no paradise at the end of a road walked in flight.

One who falls from the very beginning may not know better—but one who has walked the right path and strays from it can only end in ruin.

Little one. If you would claim to be Albus's heir, then show yourself worthy of it.

If, after devouring even Albus's life to sustain yourself, you fall at the last—

This is a test. And at the same time, it is spite.

Against the child who killed the dearest friend she ever had, a little cruelty seems only fair.

Grindelwald smiled, quietly.

Despite the various complications along the way, Harry and Carina finally arrived at Malfoy Manor.

A picturesque estate perched atop a hill, encircled by a stone wall. The front gate was wrought from silver iron, and as the Rolls-Royce drew near, the iron doors swung open as if to welcome them.

The moment they passed through, Harry felt the brush of magic against him—a faint but unmistakable trace.

Surveillance, detection, identity-revealing, misdirection, confusion, evasion of trackers, protection and defence. Every variety of protective enchantment imaginable wrapped itself around the estate. The old Harry would have registered only a vague sense of wrongness and moved on, but the Harry who now carried Voldemort's power and knowledge could enumerate each spell by type and effect. Warding a home was common enough, but the protective magic layered over Malfoy Manor went far beyond the ordinary. Had he not arrived in Lucius's car, Harry would have been forced to break through a gauntlet of enchantments that would have identified him as an intruder.

Not that the current Harry couldn't have managed it—but that sort of show of force would have served no purpose. These wards had been cast not against him, but to protect a family from Death Eaters.

The Rolls-Royce rolled to a stop before the manor. The Malfoy family and Harry stepped out, and with a flick of Lucius's wand, the car glided off on its own to find the garage. The luxury vehicle moved away without a sound, smooth and unhurried.

The beautiful stone manor looked like nothing so much as a refined, miniaturised Hogwarts. In place of the deep lake harbouring a giant squid, a lush pond bloomed with lotus flowers; in place of the forest teeming with monsters and magical creatures, a garden of exquisite blossoms. The elegant white-brick building seemed to have been designed with a craftsman's painstaking care from the very first stone—every window, every soaring tower, composed with an eye for form. It was what you might get if you stripped Hogwarts of everything superfluous and kept only the beautiful.

While Harry was taking in his surroundings, the Malfoy family had already opened the door and gone inside. Carina noticed him lagging behind and reached back to pull him along.

"Come on, Harry."

"Hmm. I'd planned to visit my girlfriend's house when her parents weren't home. This is considerably less appealing."

Lucius, who had somehow caught that remark, turned to fix Harry with a withering look. Before he could say anything, Carina glanced at Harry with an expression of weary contempt.

"Hmph. As if you'd know what to do with it anyway, you idiot."

From Harry's perspective, that was deeply unfair. He'd picked up on every signal with uncanny precision—had deliberated, held himself back, exercised extraordinary restraint for a variety of reasons, chief among them the soul of Riddle. If anything, he deserved commendation, not insults. He was nearly indignant enough to say so.

But whatever Harry felt was nothing compared to Lucius. The man turned to his daughter with a bewildered, slightly strangled voice.

"Wh—what do you mean by that, Carina? You mean he—he hasn't—"

Carina tossed her hair back and replied with cool irritation.

"Exactly what it sounds like. You heard me perfectly well."

"Carina—Carina?"

Carina did not answer her father. She simply strode down the corridor at a brisk pace. Lucius followed in her wake, his expression that of a man who desperately did not want to believe what he'd just heard.

Harry was about to follow when Narcissa placed a hand on his shoulder and spoke in a gentle tone.

"Potter. Is what Carina said true? You haven't laid a hand on her—not once?"

Harry felt, for the first time in a long while, the sensation of being tested. What was the correct answer here? Confirmation? Denial? A carefully calibrated half-truth?

Even Harry understood that he needed to earn the goodwill of at least one of his prospective in-laws. Better to make a favourable impression where he could.

"I've laid hands on her. The problem is that's where it stopped."

He said it with a grumbling tone, but the subtext was unmistakable—a quiet appeal to his own restraint. Emphasising care and consideration for a child tended to go over well with parents, regardless of which one. When Harry said it, Narcissa's expression filled with something warm and quietly proud.

"I'd been anxious about entrusting Carina to you, given your reputation… But this is a relief. It seems you care for her very much."

A contented smile spread across her pale face. In that moment, Harry found himself thinking of all the time he and Carina had spent together. The hours side by side beneath the Invisibility Cloak. The two of them alone in the prefects' bathroom. The steady, accumulating closeness between them. To call it 'caring for her' felt like a generous understatement—he'd come very close to the end of the road. He simply hadn't crossed the final line.

Harry looked away from Narcissa's gaze. The goodwill and trust she directed at him were too heavy to hold for long. He hesitated for a moment, then exhaled and answered honestly.

"To be truthful, I wasn't always so careful. There were circumstances each time that made me hesitate—it wasn't restraint so much as bad timing. If it had been purely a matter of inclination, I'd have done something about it long ago. As you know, things between us haven't exactly been straightforward."

The feeling of a sinner in the confessional suited Harry rather well just then. He braced himself for Narcissa's anger, or her sense of betrayal, or some sharp rebuke. But contrary to his expectations, she continued to smile with the same warmth.

"That is precisely what I mean. If you cared for each other through all the time that wasn't straightforward… then there is reason to hope for what comes next."

Narcissa said this, then reached out and took Draco's hand.

"Draco, I think we ought to give them some space."

"What? Why?"

"Because what they have to discuss won't be of much interest to you. Come along. You said you'd made the Quidditch team as a Seeker? I found a practice Snitch for you…"

"Really?! Where? Show me, show me!"

The moment they were home, Draco came alive like a fish returned to water, practically bouncing on his heels. Narcissa and Draco disappeared up the staircase.

Harry shrugged. He'd secured one ally. That would do nicely for whatever further skirmishes lay ahead with Lucius.

Beyond the entrance hall, a wide corridor stretched out, its floor laid with deep red carpet. Enchanted torches along the walls lit themselves in sequence, welcoming their master home. At the far end stood a door of dark chestnut wood, which swung open of its own accord as Carina approached, revealing a sitting room furnished with elegant chairs and a solid oak table.

Harry, Carina, and Lucius made their way to the sitting room without exchanging a word. Whenever Harry and Lucius happened to meet each other's eyes, they locked into a staring contest immediately, neither willing to look away first. When both men stood rooted to the spot, glaring at each other, Carina had no choice but to clap her hands.

"Dobby!"

At her call, there was a sharp crack, and the house-elf Dobby appeared. The small creature, dressed in a pillowcase, clasped his hands together and squeaked at the top of his voice.

"Miss called for Dobby, miss!"

"Bring drinks and biscuits. We'll be talking in here."

"Leave it to Dobby!"

Dobby announced this and vanished with another crack. Carina stepped between Harry and Lucius, who were still engaged in their silent war of nerves.

"Harry. Sit here."

"Ah, we're finally sitting down. I was starting to wonder whether those four-legged things were actually fanged beasts in disguise. You've been keeping your guest standing this whole time."

Carina shot Harry a cool look, then physically took Lucius by the arm and steered him toward a chair. Even Lucius, who had been standing as rigid as a statue, moved—slowly—when Carina guided him.

"…Father, sit here as well."

"A fine idea, Potter… I've been thinking, in fact, that I ought to have a chair made that bites unwelcome guests on the backside, for future occasions such as this…"

How long was she expected to stand between these two? And one of them was her father, whom she respected, and the other was the man she loved. Both of them were scraping the bottom of the barrel simultaneously, and Carina was so mortified on her own behalf that she could have died.

Oblivious to her feelings, the two men pressed on with their petty campaign.

"The manor is grand, but the man inside it seems rather small."

"The fact that I permitted you to set foot in this manor at all is a generosity beyond measure. Though I suppose it's rather pointless to discuss breadth of character with someone who hasn't even a home to invite guests to."

"Oh, I'm enormously broad-minded. I've got a whole Voldemort living inside me—shall I show you—"

"Miss! Dobby has brought drinks and biscuits!"

If Dobby hadn't appeared at precisely that moment and set down the grape juice and biscuits, Carina might have fled the room in sheer embarrassment. She snatched the glasses and plate from Dobby and set them down on the table with a decisive clatter. Then, wearing an expression of undisguised displeasure, she opened the conversation. If she didn't start it, it was clear no one would.
"Father. You may or may not be aware, but Voldemort's soul is currently residing inside Harry."

"I am well aware. And furthermore…"

Lucius fixed Harry with a sunken, measured gaze. He spoke with cold, undisguised hostility.

"…he—or rather, the Dark Lord within Potter—killed Albus Dumbledore. I know that as well."

Carina flinched, but on reflection, it was hardly surprising. Narcissa Malfoy had witnessed the scene herself; of course Lucius knew. And the fact that Harry would have to take his own life before his next birthday had been a significant part of what convinced Lucius to sever his ties with the Death Eaters.

And yet—even as Lucius acknowledged the nobility of Harry Potter's choice—the way the boy conducted himself in the face of death grated on him enormously.

"Harry Potter. You are a young man who has resolved to end his own life before his birthday arrives. So why are you still clinging to my daughter's side? If you've truly made that decision, you ought to force yourself away from Carina, however difficult. Instead you attach yourself to her without a shred of shame."

Tap. Lucius brought his cane down sharply against the floor. He stared at Harry for a long moment with an expression of profound dissatisfaction.

"My daughter is not an ornament to adorn your final days. Her precious time is not grave goods to be buried alongside you! If you genuinely care for Carina, then go somewhere quiet this instant and prepare for your end in peace!"

Harry, naturally, did not yield. He crossed his legs with casual insolence, lifted his chin, and looked down at Lucius.

"It seems you haven't received the latest news. I've decided not to die."

"I beg your pardon?"

Harry smiled without apology.

"Carina made me realise something. Why on earth should I do all the hard work and then die for someone else's benefit? I've decided to survive this and be happy. That's my position now."

"…Hm. And you have a method?"

"That's why I'm here—to find one. You're a man of considerable knowledge and connections, Mr Malfoy. You might know something useful."

There was no misunderstanding him. Lucius Malfoy understood the logic of self-interest better than almost anyone. Hearing Harry's resolution, he leaned back in his chair and ran his fingers along the handle of his cane.

"A method to separate two souls that have merged… I'm afraid I can't help you there."

"Meaning?"

"I don't know of one. Magic that deals with souls is extraordinarily complex to begin with—and frankly, rather useless. Souls? That's the sort of subject that would only interest a ghost. Who in their right mind would research it?"

"I wasn't expecting you to know, Mr Malfoy. I came to ask whether anyone in your circle might."

"Hm…"

Lucius drew the word out at length. Whether he was trying to hold the reins of the conversation or simply thinking, he let his gaze travel between Harry and Carina for a long, unhurried moment before he spoke.

"I don't know."

He concluded with those three words.

Harry bristled.

"Really. Does a search take that long?"

"I know a great many people, and they do not share all of their information with me. Their deepest secrets they keep even from me. The most powerful wizard of my acquaintance might well know something about the matter, but…"

Lucius's gaze shifted to Harry. He meant Voldemort. But Voldemort's power and knowledge were not fully Harry's to access, and even approaching that territory was dangerous. Harry muttered with a disgruntled expression.

"Right. Fine. What about an Unbreakable Vow, then, or a binding enchantment of comparable strength?"

"You intend to wrap yourself in seals before your birthday arrives. So that he cannot wake—and if he does, so that he cannot act."

"You catch on quickly."

"Your thinking is transparent enough. And there aren't many other options to begin with…"

Tap. Tap. Lucius struck his cane against the floor in a steady rhythm. He furrowed his brow deeply, like a man wrestling with a difficult problem, and answered slowly.

"As for that—I could provide you with enchanted objects from our vaults, or supply you with relevant magic directly. However, no seal, however powerful and systematic, can be trusted absolutely. Particularly when what is contained within it is formidable…"

"I'll have a look at everything first. We can decide after."

"Very well. I'll open the vault in the cellar. Find what you need there. But as I said—nothing can guarantee safety. Not yours, and not the seal's."

"Well. You're being rather cooperative, all things considered."

Lucius gave a dismissive sniff.

"Like it or not, we are in the same boat, you and I."

"Well, thank you. I suppose even a father-in-law comes through when it counts—"

"I have not given you permission to use that title!"

Harry snickered at Lucius's indignation.

For the entirety of the Easter holiday, Harry and Carina worked in the cellar vault. Day after day—except for meals, washing up, and the occasional hour spent flying broomsticks with Draco—they devoted themselves to studying the curses and binding enchantments held in the Malfoy family's collection.

Seals were a form of dark magic used to imprison powerful individuals or diminish their strength—and, perhaps surprisingly, they were spells that dark wizards themselves employed with some frequency. The Malfoy family, with their deep-rooted Slytherin heritage, had accumulated a remarkable array of such magic. The vault held ancient binding arts, curses designed to erode magical ability, Unbreakable Vows, and a host of other dangerous enchantments.

In the course of their research, Harry came across something promising and handed it to Carina.

"What about this?"

Carina took it and examined it carefully.

"Basilisk-hide Rope… It weakens magical ability and paralyses anyone who resists. The drawback is that the marks it leaves don't fade easily."

"I'll just call it a tattoo. Here—try it on me."

"Hold out your hands. I'll bind you."

Carina wound the rope around Harry's wrists and ankles, tied it off with a knot, and stepped back to observe. Harry stood still, raised his bound hands, tapped the air a few times experimentally—then shook his head and shrugged.

"No good. It's not working."

Harry gave his hands a shake, and the Basilisk-hide Rope unravelled and slid to the floor. Even while being restrained, he had cast a nonverbal Untying Charm.

Carina's irritation was immediate.

"Useless rope. It can't even stop a wandless wizard?"

"No, it did have some effect. I could feel the spells catching and stuttering."

"And that's the problem! If you'd had your wand, fine—but you broke free without it!"
"As you know, resistance to magical objects has nothing to do with whether you're holding a wand."

"Even so!"

Carina's frustration had a reason behind it. Every object and spell they'd found so far that was meant to weaken or suppress magic had produced no meaningful effect on Harry. He dismantled or escaped each one with contemptuous ease. Which meant that to restrain Harry's magic, they would need something considerably more powerful—and considerably more dangerous.

Carina found that fact deeply unsatisfying.

"The Unbreakable Vow is a last resort. It can't be broken, and it puts his life at risk. Binding enchantments are useless. The Schadenfreude Charm? That requires a constant watcher—I could do it, but the moment he's out of my sight while I'm asleep, it fails. What's the right answer? What should we use?"

Carina was flipping through a book she'd propped up nearby when she felt a gaze on her and looked up with a frown.

Harry wasn't reading. He was studying her face with quiet, unhurried attention—as though her face were the only thing in the room worth looking at. Carina reproached him.

"Focus, Harry. This concerns your life—and mine along with it."

"Wow, you're staking your life on me? I'm honoured. To hear you say that directly."

"Read one more line instead of talking nonsense. Unless you'd like that line to be the last one of your life."

Carina snapped at him, turning pages briskly.

If laughter were something you could carry in a pocket, someone had clearly put a hole in Harry's. No matter how serious Carina was, how desperate, how disappointed, how hard she worked—Harry kept smiling that easy, unhurried smile. He teased her, reached out to touch her, seemed perfectly content just to look at her face.

Carina's heart was racing against the clock, but their paces were entirely mismatched. Somehow, inexplicably, it was Harry—the one in the greater danger—who moved the slowest.

"Here. Read this next one. The Vow Contract. It says that upon paying the corresponding price stipulated within—"

"When it comes to the Unbreakable Vow."

Carina clenched her fist and fixed Harry with a hard look. Harry met her gaze with a calm smile.

"I want to make the vow on your wand. Will you do it?"

The Unbreakable Vow could not be broken—and breaking it meant death, without exception. Carina had no desire to go down that road. She knew that a vow of that severity carried terrible consequences. But Harry Potter was insisting on it.

If there was one point of genuine disagreement between them, it was this. Carina slammed the book shut.

"That is a last resort!"

"It's the best option. Nothing short of that will keep you and your family safe."

But Harry had his own reasoning.

The danger of the Unbreakable Vow was death. A simple, absolute consequence.

But the moment Harry broke an Unbreakable Vow would be the moment Tom Riddle's consciousness surfaced.

In that case, the danger wasn't a punishment at all—it was a safeguard.

That was why Harry wanted the Unbreakable Vow. It was the most straightforward way to kill a Harry Potter who carried Voldemort's power and knowledge.

"When should we do the Unbreakable Vow? We'll need a witness—since we're on the subject, why not now? Your mother has experience with it; she could help us."

"Don't decide things on your own! Nothing has been settled yet. There may be another way we haven't found yet!"

"You can see it as well as I can, Carina. The more powerful the magic, the greater the risk it carries. Weaker magic can't contain me. Even if we find another method, it'll be something powerful enough to threaten my life."

Bang.

Carina shoved back her chair and stood. Harry, who had been reclining at his ease, slowly raised his head. The difference in their paces, the distance between their positions—it was exactly this.

In Carina's large silver-grey eyes, tears gathered. But they did not fall. They were swallowed back down along with her fury.

"There will be another way. There has to be. There must."

Harry said nothing. He had already reached his conclusion, and there was no hesitation left in him, no lingering doubt. He would wrap the rope of the Unbreakable Vow around his own throat and call it done.

Carina, burning with anger, left Harry where he was and walked out of the dark cellar with long, deliberate strides.

Tomorrow. When the short holiday ended and they returned to Hogwarts—another day would begin to pass.

As she walked, Carina turned over the words Lucius had said to her.

On the first day at the manor. Lucius had called Carina aside and spoken to her in a lowered voice. He had told her something he had kept from Harry.

'…I said I didn't know anything about souls. But there is one person among my acquaintances who might. Carina, before I tell you—promise me something. Promise me you will not tell Harry Potter.'

'Why not?'

'The Dark Lord resides within Harry Potter. Whatever method we devise, if he becomes aware of it in advance, it may be turned against us. Your opponent is the most powerful dark wizard in history—a man who reached the forbidden depths that no one else has touched, a Dark Lord so formidable that even Dumbledore could not defeat him by ordinary means and had to resort to cunning. Your efforts are admirable, but searching for a solution together with Harry Potter is not wise.'

Carina paused in thought.

Was this why Harry had been so passive about finding a solution? Perhaps Harry had understood it instinctively, there in that cellar. The moment he found a way, the Voldemort inside him would find a way around it.

If that was true, then in the end, there was only one path left. The final method Lucius had passed to her in secret.

'But is there truly a wizard who knows magic even he does not?'

'Exactly one. Still alive in this world. A dark wizard who held dominion in an age before the Dark Lord, and who once stood as Dumbledore's equal.'

Grindelwald.

As he spoke the name of the dark wizard who had been defeated by Dumbledore half a century ago and imprisoned in Nurmengard, Lucius had emphasised it once more.

'Carina. I say this again. If the Dark Lord returns by reclaiming that boy's body—we are finished. All of us. So if you cannot extract a method from Grindelwald either…'

Lucius's conclusion was the same as Harry's.

Kill Harry. Or bind him in a yoke so severe it was tantamount to death.

They had been at each other's throats this entire time, and yet here they were, in perfect agreement on this one thing. Infuriating men, both of them.

But.

"I won't allow that, Father."

Bartemius, encountered at the seaside villa. Bellatrix and Karkaroff, reported to have fled. And the other Death Eaters still uncaptured, still in hiding.

If Harry Potter disappeared—

They would come for the Malfoys.

When the holiday ended, Carina would have to go to Austria. To find a way. To Nurmengard—the great prison that Grindelwald had built and in which Grindelwald was now imprisoned. She would go there to find an answer.

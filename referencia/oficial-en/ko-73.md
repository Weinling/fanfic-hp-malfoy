The Ministry of Magic, swallowed by darkness, carried the same desolate chill one might feel in an empty school after hours.

A space that should have been bustling with people now echoed only with Harry's footsteps beneath dim, failing light. Darkness and silence had claimed what ought to have been loud and alive—and that dissonance made Harry's body draw inward, almost involuntarily.

Or it could be Voldemort.

Harry knew Voldemort was somewhere in the Ministry. He would be making his way to the Department of Mysteries to retrieve the prophecy—but he might just as easily change his mind and step around the next corner. If they came face to face, Harry would be in very serious trouble.

Harry had talent in Defence Against the Dark Arts, and he'd worked hard at it. But that didn't mean he rated himself highly enough to fight Voldemort and win.

Priori Incantatem could hold him off once—but it was a last resort, usable only once. A wizard of Voldemort's calibre would never allow it a second time.

My role isn't to fight him, anyway.

His purpose was simply to draw people to the Ministry. To let the world know that the Department of Mysteries had been breached, that a prophecy had been stolen—to announce his return to everyone.

A place like the Minister's office would have magical alarms. If I can just set one off and bring people running...

Harry was thinking this as he passed the great fountain in the atrium. He stopped short.

He couldn't have said, for a moment, what had stopped him. But his instincts had seized his ankles and refused to let go. He drew his wand and swept his gaze around the atrium.

"Good instincts, little boy?"

A sharp voice rang through the atrium.

From behind one of the pillars lining the space, someone stepped slowly into the open. A witch—gaunt, with a look of perpetual irritation etched into her features. Her face bore a strange resemblance to Sirius's, and might once have been beautiful; now, even the most wicked witch from a fairy tale would have struggled to match it. Cruelty and madness had eaten away at whatever she'd once been.

Harry spoke her name in a low voice.

"...Bellatrix Lestrange."

"Kehaha! The Boy Who Lived, gracing me with my own name! I don't know where to put myself!"

Bellatrix laughed savagely, her tattered robes flaring around her. Harry gave her a thin smile in return.

Until just a few months ago she'd been locked in Azkaban—yet she had continued her activities even after Voldemort's disappearance, one of his most extreme and devoted followers. She was also the woman who had tortured Neville's parents into madness. Among all the Death Eaters, she was known as the most brutal and the most powerful.

A formidable opponent. Harry smiled instead of worrying.

"If his most devoted servant is here, that means Voldemort is here too."

"Don't say his name!"

Bellatrix shrieked. Fanatics were always easy to handle—just say the name and they did the work for you.

"Am I wrong?"

"Of course you are! The Ministry and Dumbledore are tearing each other apart out there—why would the Dark Lord bother coming here himself?"

But the information Bellatrix had just given him didn't match Harry's assumptions. Voldemort would surely want to confirm the prophecy's contents himself.

"...Why would he come here? Obviously for the prophecy. Only the subject of it can take it."

Harry said it plainly. Bellatrix threw her head back and laughed until the atrium rang with it—as though Harry's failure to think beyond that was the funniest thing she'd ever heard. She stared at him for a long moment with undisguised contempt.

The laughter finally died. Bellatrix hiccupped and said:

"Kehah—that's exactly it! He came here himself! The person who'd bring it to him walked right in on his own two feet!"

"...What?"

"Kehahaha! You idiot, you still don't understand? You really are insufferably arrogant! Even the Dark Lord acknowledged it! Still don't see it? Khihihit!"

Harry's brow drew tighter and tighter.

"That means you've walked into a trap, Potter."

A wizard stepped out from behind Bellatrix. Stiff blond hair, an expression utterly devoid of feeling—he looked less like a person than a puppet being worked by an Imperius Curse. Harry's wand came up on instinct.

"Bartemius Crouch... Junior?"

Bartemius answered without inflection.

"You know my name. Have we met before?"

"I saw you in a Pensieve. You were begging your father for mercy. Pathetically. You seem to have recovered."

Bartemius's brow twitched slightly, but he didn't give Harry the anger he was fishing for. He kept his composure.

"Naturally. I overcame him."

"These days people call it 'overcoming' when they had someone else do the killing for them?"

"You're even more insolent than I was told. I suppose that's why you walked straight into a trap."

Harry had been keeping his voice steady, but the word trap made him flinch.

Bartemius Crouch Junior was another of Voldemort's most extreme followers. He had placed his own father under the Imperius Curse and rigged the Triwizard Tournament to ensure Voldemort's resurrection—and it had worked. Voldemort had returned at full strength. By any measure, Crouch was the one most responsible.

And now both he and Bellatrix were here, together, in the same atrium. If they were meant to cover Voldemort's back, there was no reason to concentrate this much firepower in one place...

In an instant, Carina's face surfaced in Harry's mind. She had been waiting in Umbridge's office as though she'd anticipated Harry would come to the Ministry—and she had attacked him, actively and without hesitation, to stop him from going any further.

Harry had broken through her. He'd assumed she was on Voldemort's side.

But what if the reason Carina had tried to stop him wasn't because she was following orders—but because she was defying them, to keep him safe?

"...A trap. Don't tell me—"

The realisation hit Harry like a hammer blow. Bellatrix sneered at him standing there, dazed.

"Finally worked it out? Yes. The memory of his you saw—it was fabricated. He noticed you were watching, and he used your arrogance and your hero complex against you. He lured you here."

"Why? Why go to all that trouble—"

"Why? Because, as you said yourself, only the subject of the prophecy can take it!"

A single hypothesis cut through Harry's mind.

Professor Trelawney's prophecy concerned 'the one with the power to vanquish the Dark Lord.' And so Harry had reflexively assumed it was a prophecy binding him and Voldemort together by fate.

But there was a blind spot in that assumption.

This prophecy had foretold Harry's birth—not Voldemort's. When it had first been spoken into the world, Voldemort was already the Dark Lord.

Which meant: the prophecy bound Harry and Voldemort together, but the actual subject of the prophecy was—

"Me. Only I... can take it."

Voldemort had gone to such elaborate lengths to set this trap precisely because only Harry could retrieve the prophecy. Rather than go for it himself, he had given his followers a single instruction:

Have Harry Potter bring it to me with his own hands.

As if to confirm it, another figure emerged from behind a pillar. A bearded, middle-aged man walked out with the uneven gait of someone whose body didn't quite balance properly.

"What are you wasting time for! Let's deal with this quickly and go get the prophecy!"

Harry knew him. He'd seen more than enough of him during the Triwizard Tournament—Igor Karkaroff, Headmaster of Durmstrang.

Harry looked at him with a thin, needling smile.

"Mr. Karkaroff. I heard you'd switched sides—you do look considerably more cheerful."

Harry's eyes flicked to Karkaroff's left arm, which he was gripping with his right hand. Beneath the sleeve, something silver and metallic caught the light.

"And you've upgraded the arm as well. I'm almost envious. I used to dream about having a mechanical arm when I was younger."

"Still as cocky as ever, Potter... but just as presumptuous. A mere student, crawling in here without knowing his place... Didn't your parents teach you not to wander about at night?"

"Sorry. As you well know, the parents who should have taught me were killed by your master. At least my parents died—what did yours teach you, exactly, that you managed to betray people twice?"

Karkaroff's face cycled through several shades of red and purple. Harry kept pushing.

"Durmstrang really found themselves a fine headmaster. A wizard who sold himself to the Dark Lord to save his own skin! Isn't that the perfect cautionary tale? Your portrait will hang in the toilets. Not on the wall—on the cistern."

"You... you'll regret not dying easily—"

"Easily? I won't die at all. Unlike some people, I have no intention of dying for a wizard who can't even hold onto his own wand."

"*Avada*—"

Karkaroff, incensed, had begun the Killing Curse when someone rushed forward and seized his arm. Harry's eyes narrowed at the sight of silver-blond hair.

"Don't be rash, Igor! Have you forgotten our objective? We must bring the prophecy to the Dark Lord!"

Lucius Malfoy had grabbed Karkaroff's good arm. Karkaroff glared at Harry, his body coiled as though he might lunge at any moment.

"Let go, Lucius. I won't be satisfied until I've dealt with that insolent little brat—"

"I am in command of this operation. You will follow my orders."
Lucius's voice carried the weight of a man who expected to be obeyed. From across the atrium, Bartemius said flatly:

"In command? Amusing."

Lucius clearly commanded little respect among the Death Eaters. He wheeled on Bartemius.

"Bartemius—let me be perfectly clear. This entire operation was my design. Don't forget that. None of this would be happening without me!"

Bartemius's reply was indifferent.

"Hmph. Achieved by selling your own daughter."

"Bartemius!"

Lucius raised his wand in fury, but Bartemius didn't spare him a glance. Lucius stared at him for a long moment, clicked his tongue, and turned back to Harry.

"We've wasted enough time... Come now, Potter. Don't resist. Accept your fate... and at least it won't hurt."

Lucius looked like a man whose body was itching to finish the job. His eagerness to deliver Harry and claim the credit was written plainly across his face.

Did he know? That the daughter he trusted so completely had been working against his plan—trying to keep Harry alive?

What would happen if Harry told him? Would he grieve? Rage?

A cruel impulse stirred. Harry wanted to throw Lucius into a spiral of doubt and conflict.

But that would put Carina in danger. Whatever the circumstances, that was never an option.

There was only one answer. Whether it worked or not, he had to fight.

The moment Lucius raised his wand, Harry thrust his own forward.

"*Imperio*!"

"*Protego*!"

The Shield Charm deflected the Imperius Curse. The rebound staggered Lucius.

"You'd actually resist? In this situation?"

The Death Eaters' eyes went cold. The look said: surely this helpless child doesn't think he can fight us.

Harry met them with a cool, contemptuous smile.

"What else? Should I just hold my arms out and let you have me? Unlike you lot, I'm the type who keeps throwing himself at the wall until something breaks."

"Khihihit! Yes! That's the spirit a man should have!"

Bellatrix laughed like someone unhinged and closed the distance between them. She cackled for a long moment, then fixed Harry with a smile that was vicious and mean and entirely genuine.

She was enjoying herself. The harder Harry fought, the more satisfying it would be to break him.

"Now then, little boy. The lesson's gone on long enough, hasn't it? Shall we get moving?"

"Where? Hell? Sorry—I prefer pretty women. You can go on ahead without me."

"Kehaha. Such a rude child. But what exactly can you do, all on your own?"

The gap in power was enormous. Four senior Death Eaters—all seasoned, all formidable practitioners of the Dark Arts.

Against them: Harry Potter, fifteen years old.

He'd walked into a trap. He'd been careless. He had to admit it.

He hadn't even tried to understand why Carina had stood in his way—he'd hit her with a Stunning Spell and pushed past her and come all the way here regardless.

Which meant he had to leave in one piece. For Carina's sake—so she wouldn't have to live with regret.

"What I can do."

Harry murmured it to himself, raised his wand, and snapped it forward like a bolt of lightning.

"*Stupefy*!"

Naturally, Bellatrix—directly in front of him—threw up a Shield Charm on instinct. But Harry's wand wasn't aimed at her. The Stunning Spell shot sideways and struck Lucius Malfoy squarely across the chest. He hadn't expected to be the target. He crumpled.

Bellatrix looked at Lucius and burst out laughing.

"Kehahaha! You stupid brother-in-law! You're not bad, Harry Potter!"

Harry had no time to respond. Karkaroff and Bartemius gave him no room to breathe. Spells came pouring from both of them at once.

Harry ran. If he had any advantage over them, it was stamina and reflexes—not the breadth of his spell repertoire, not their years of experience.

"Running like a rat!"

He was doing exactly that—ducking, rolling, changing direction every time light flashed. Spells that missed him detonated against the walls in bursts of multicoloured fire.

But he wasn't only running. Even as he rolled across the floor, he angled his wand beneath his robes.

It was a technique Moody had taught him—attacking from an angle the opponent couldn't track.

"*Stupefy*! Impedimenta!"

His opponents were Death Eaters hardened by years of real combat.

Bartemius deflected both spells with a single flick of his wand. Karkaroff batted them aside with his silver arm. Neither of them lowered their guard against someone half their age.

Karkaroff watched Harry sprint across the atrium and muttered:

"Tiresome... Expulso!"

His wand shuddered. Harry felt the danger before he understood it, and threw himself sideways.

The floor exploded behind him. A concussive roar rolled through the atrium.

It was a devastating blast. Loud enough that anyone still in the Ministry would have heard it. Shards of stone flew upward; dust billowed in thick clouds. The shockwave lifted Harry off his feet. Broken fragments drummed against his back—he'd have bruises, several of them.

Harry gritted his teeth and twisted in the air. He'd fallen off a broomstick enough times to know how to land. He turned himself mid-flight and came down on his feet.

Through the dust, Bellatrix's venomous voice cut across the atrium.

"You idiot! That'll be heard outside the Ministry!"

"If we don't catch him fast, this gets worse! Stop standing there and help!"

While they bickered, Harry used the cover of the dust cloud to sweep his wand across the floor. Broken tiles and shattered stone rose into the air.

Harry wound his arm back as though preparing to throw. His wand cut through the air with a low hiss.

He thought of Quidditch—of the times he'd practised as a Beater to understand the position. Tracking an incoming Bludger, timing the swing precisely. Above all else: weight distribution. The point where your force meets the club's centre of gravity, meeting the Bludger's trajectory at exactly the right moment.

He held that feeling in his body and swung.

"*Depulso*!"

His wand struck the floating marble tile. The wand was infinitely thinner than a Beater's club, but the Banishing Charm at its tip was something no club could match.

The broken tiles fanned outward in a wide arc, spinning like discuses, hurtling toward the Death Eaters.

Tiles that had borne the weight of hundreds of footsteps every day were not light things. If they connected, even a wizard wouldn't walk away unscathed.

"Tch—cheap tricks!"

"Oh. Not bad."

Neither of them stood still for it.

Karkaroff thrust his left arm forward like a shield. It moved with uncanny speed, snatching tiles from the air. The sound of stone grinding against metal filled the atrium—and tiles that had seemed capable of smashing through anything went docile in Karkaroff's silver grip. He tossed them aside and advanced.

Bartemius flicked his wand. The marble tiles aimed at him curved away like boomerangs, arcing wide around him—and then reversed course, flying back toward Harry. Harry's eyes went wide at the elegant counter.

"Damn—"

Harry was scrambling to dodge the boomeranging tiles when a red beam from Karkaroff's wand struck the ground at his feet.

His legs tangled. Harry went down, caught himself on one hand, rolled, and threw himself behind the base of the fountain's statue. A volley of violet light rained down behind him.

Karkaroff clicked his tongue.

"You run well, Potter... but how long do you think you can keep it up?"

Harry answered between ragged breaths.

"All day, if I have to. Unlike you and your master, I'm young and healthy."

"All mouth. But there's no Dumbledore here to save you!"

Karkaroff was right. Harry's situation was genuinely dire.

Bartemius Crouch was simply powerful—no other word for it. His orthodox responses were laced with unpredictable variations that were nearly impossible to anticipate.
Karkaroff, by contrast, was like a medieval knight with a thick shield and a heavy lance. His left arm neutralised every threat that came near him, and the spells he cast with his wand were strong enough to shatter Shield Charms outright.

And on top of that...

Bellatrix Lestrange hadn't moved. She was watching Harry fight and laughing at him. Harry would have loved to send a curse at her face, but he was too busy with the other two to find the opening.

Even if he had found one, his spells would likely have missed. Unlike Lucius, she showed no gaps. And if she decided to join in—the fragile balance Harry was barely maintaining would collapse instantly. He needed to reduce their numbers before Bellatrix grew bored.

That wouldn't be easy. But it was the only option he had.

"Resistance is meaningless. The Dark Lord's will shall be done..."

Bartemius advanced on the statue, wand raised. Now. Harry tapped the legs, shoulders, and head of the witch statue with his wand in quick succession.

"*Locomotor*!"

The statue straightened its rigid spine and stepped down from its plinth. The tiles cracked under its weight. The stone witch opened her eyes, heavy and deliberate.

Something shifted in Bartemius's expression.

"The Locomotion Charm?"

The statue answered for Harry. It let out a grinding metallic shriek and raised the broomstick in its hands high overhead. A metal broomstick, aimed at his skull.

The intent was unmistakable. Bartemius stepped back sharply—the right call. The golden broomstick came down and drove itself into the floor, splitting the tiles and burying itself up to the bristles.

Bartemius regarded Harry with a new wariness.

"Impressive for your age. Even Aurors struggle with high-level Animation Charms like this without a pre-enchanted object..."

A single step backward—Harry's first real hit. He had no intention of letting it be his last.

"*Locomotor*!"

Harry slid across the floor and tapped the ankle of the centaur statue with his wand. The centaur stepped down from its base and turned to face the direction Harry's wand was pointing.

Karkaroff was at the end of that line. The centaur charged.

Karkaroff's wand came up in alarm.

"*Impedimenta*!"

The Impediment Jinx—slowing living things and objects alike. Harry threw up a Shield Charm to cover the centaur.

"*Protego*!"

The jinx glanced off the shield. By the time Karkaroff readied his next spell, the centaur was nearly on top of him.

"Aaaargh!"

His wand was useless at this range. In desperation, Karkaroff thrust out his silver left arm.

The arm moved on its own. Its fingers curled into a fist and swung—striking the centaur statue squarely in the midsection.

Both Harry and Karkaroff stared.

A centaur statue over two metres tall. A single silver arm. By any rational measure, there was no contest.

But the silver arm prioritised destroying the threat over protecting its owner.

Clang.

Lightning struck. Sparks flew. The sound of metal on metal shook the atrium.

The silver arm won.

The golden statue crumpled grotesquely. The centaur that had been bearing down with enough force to reduce a person to pulp lurched violently sideways from a single human-sized punch. The vibration that ran through its entire body sounded like a death rattle.

Karkaroff was thrown backward as well—but at least he hadn't been crushed. The magical arm Voldemort had given him was terrifyingly powerful. Karkaroff smiled faintly. Satisfying performance. In this moment, he didn't regret sacrificing his left arm for the Dark Lord.

But he let his guard down.

"Here—!"

An instant before the fist connected, Harry had anticipated the worst and started running. This was the opening he'd been waiting for. He had to reduce their numbers now, while he had the chance. Driven by that single thought, he sprinted toward Karkaroff.

He vaulted nimbly over the toppling centaur statue, reversed his grip on his wand like a dagger, and dropped onto Karkaroff from above—as though he genuinely intended to stab him with it.

The silver arm registered the attack and moved to intercept. Any spell he cast would be caught by that arm.

But there was only one arm to worry about.

"*Stupefy*!"

As expected, the Stunning Spell was swallowed by the silver fist. Karkaroff smiled and raised his wand.

Harry drove his knee into Karkaroff's stomach with his full body weight behind it.

"*Ugh*—!"

Karkaroff's body buckled. His eyes bulged with the shock of it.

One arm. It couldn't block two simultaneous attacks from different directions. And the silver arm seemed slow to register non-magical strikes.

Harry followed with a clean punch to the jaw. Karkaroff's eyes rolled back and he went down.

Perhaps if he'd been in better shape it might have gone differently. But a gaunt, hollow-cheeked man had no answer for someone young and fit and fighting with everything he had.

"Kehahaha! What an idiot! A wizard, beaten senseless and foaming at the mouth!"

Bellatrix was howling with laughter. Bartemius had only just finished blasting the witch statue apart.

The odds had shifted.

Two against one again. It would be harder this time—but there was no rule that said he couldn't do twice what he'd already done once. If he could put those two down, he'd be safe.

More than that—he'd have leverage against the Ministry. Even without Voldemort present, four fallen Death Eaters would be impossible for the Ministry to ignore.

There was hope. Harry was pushing himself upright, wand in hand, when a powerful grip closed around his ankle.

"Ah—!"

Harry gasped and tried to turn.

The silver arm threw him.

Voldemort's gift moved independently of its owner's will—even when its owner was unconscious. The arm attacked Harry of its own accord.

His body skipped across the stone floor like a flat stone on water. The skin scraped raw left a long smear of blood; the impact hammered through him from head to toe, leaving everything aching.

In the moment he lost focus, a spell from across the atrium tore his wand from his hand.

Bellatrix twirled Harry's wand between her fingers and crowed. She looked as though the sheer delight of the moment was almost too much to contain.

"Kehaha! Igor! You're more useful unconscious than you ever were awake!"

"Bella. Hold onto that wand."

Lucius, recovered, stepped forward past Bellatrix with his wand raised. Bellatrix made a disgruntled noise.

"Lucius. Just a moment—can't I torture him a little? He hurt our dear colleague."

"No. Potter must take the prophecy with his own hands."

"His hands are all you need, aren't they. There's a special pleasure in using someone else's wand for it, you know—watching them bounce off the walls."

"Your Cruciatus is too powerful. And the Imperius Curse does not animate corpses—it controls a living mind. If his mind breaks, he won't function properly."

Leaving Bellatrix to grumble behind him, Lucius approached Harry.

Harry slid his hand inside his robes and waited. One last chance remained. If Lucius came close enough, Harry would trip him and take his wand.

Harry let out a low groan—performing—and measured the distance from the corner of his eye. Two steps. If Lucius came carelessly closer, Harry would bring him down in an instant...

"*Impedimenta*!"

Bellatrix cast the spell. An invisible force struck Harry's body, and for a moment he felt as though he'd been submerged in water.

Everything slowed. He couldn't move a single finger the way he wanted to. The Impediment Jinx—a weak curse, one that merely made movement sluggish and difficult.

But for Harry, waiting for the moment to strike, it was devastating. Even if Lucius Malfoy were ten years old, he wouldn't fall for Harry's trap now.

"Bella!"

"Tch—that much is fine, isn't it? Call it affection."

"...Don't act without my orders again. Imperio!"

Consciousness dropped away like a stone. The world seemed to pull on a thin, translucent veil. In a dreamy, drifting haze, Harry heard a voice—and followed it, walking somewhere, anywhere, without knowing where he was going...

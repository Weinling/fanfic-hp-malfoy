The Ministry of Magic, swallowed by darkness, carried the same desolate chill one might feel in an empty school after hours.

A space that should have been bustling with people now echoed only with Harry's footsteps beneath dim, failing light. Darkness and silence had claimed what ought to have been loud and alive—and that dissonance made Harry's body draw inward, almost involuntarily.

Or it could be Voldemort.

Harry knew Voldemort was somewhere in the Ministry. He would be making his way to the Department of Mysteries to retrieve the prophecy—but he might just as easily change his mind and step around the next corner. If they came face to face, Harry would be in very serious trouble.

Harry had talent in Defence Against the Dark Arts, and he'd worked hard at it. But that didn't mean he rated himself highly enough to fight Voldemort and win.

Priori Incantatem could hold him off once—but it was a last resort, usable only once. A wizard of Voldemort's calibre would never allow it a second time.

My role isn't to fight him, anyway.

His purpose was simply to draw people to the Ministry. To let the world know that the Department of Mysteries had been breached, that a prophecy had been stolen—to announce his return to everyone.

A place like the Minister's office would have magical alarms. If I can just set one off and bring people running...

Harry was thinking this as he passed the great fountain in the atrium. He stopped short.

He couldn't have said, for a moment, what had stopped him. But his instincts had seized his ankles and refused to let go. He drew his wand and swept his gaze around the atrium.

"Good instincts, little boy?"

A sharp voice rang through the atrium.

From behind one of the pillars lining the space, someone stepped slowly into the open. A witch—gaunt, with a look of perpetual irritation etched into her features. Her face bore a strange resemblance to Sirius's, and might once have been beautiful; now, even the most wicked witch from a fairy tale would have struggled to match it. Cruelty and madness had eaten away at whatever she'd once been.

Harry spoke her name in a low voice.

"...Bellatrix Lestrange."

"Kehaha! The Boy Who Lived, gracing me with my own name! I don't know where to put myself!"

Bellatrix laughed savagely, her tattered robes flaring around her. Harry gave her a thin smile in return.

Until just a few months ago she'd been locked in Azkaban—yet she had continued her activities even after Voldemort's disappearance, one of his most extreme and devoted followers. She was also the woman who had tortured Neville's parents into madness. Among all the Death Eaters, she was known as the most brutal and the most powerful.

A formidable opponent. Harry smiled instead of worrying.

"If his most devoted servant is here, that means Voldemort is here too."

"Don't say his name!"

Bellatrix shrieked. Fanatics were always easy to handle—just say the name and they did the work for you.

"Am I wrong?"

"Of course you are! The Ministry and Dumbledore are tearing each other apart out there—why would the Dark Lord bother coming here himself?"

But the information Bellatrix had just given him didn't match Harry's assumptions. Voldemort would surely want to confirm the prophecy's contents himself.

"...Why would he come here? Obviously for the prophecy. Only the subject of it can take it."

Harry said it plainly. Bellatrix threw her head back and laughed until the atrium rang with it—as though Harry's failure to think beyond that was the funniest thing she'd ever heard. She stared at him for a long moment with undisguised contempt.

The laughter finally died. Bellatrix hiccupped and said:

"Kehah—that's exactly it! He came here himself! The person who'd bring it to him walked right in on his own two feet!"

"...What?"

"Kehahaha! You idiot, you still don't understand? You really are insufferably arrogant! Even the Dark Lord acknowledged it! Still don't see it? Khihihit!"

Harry's brow drew tighter and tighter.

"That means you've walked into a trap, Potter."

A wizard stepped out from behind Bellatrix. Stiff blond hair, an expression utterly devoid of feeling—he looked less like a person than a puppet being worked by an Imperius Curse. Harry's wand came up on instinct.

"Bartemius Crouch... Junior?"

Bartemius answered without inflection.

"You know my name. Have we met before?"

"I saw you in a Pensieve. You were begging your father for mercy. Pathetically. You seem to have recovered."

Bartemius's brow twitched slightly, but he didn't give Harry the anger he was fishing for. He kept his composure.

"Naturally. I overcame him."

"These days people call it 'overcoming' when they had someone else do the killing for them?"

"You're even more insolent than I was told. I suppose that's why you walked straight into a trap."

Harry had been keeping his voice steady, but the word trap made him flinch.

Bartemius Crouch Junior was another of Voldemort's most extreme followers. He had placed his own father under the Imperius Curse and rigged the Triwizard Tournament to ensure Voldemort's resurrection—and it had worked. Voldemort had returned at full strength. By any measure, Crouch was the one most responsible.

And now both he and Bellatrix were here, together, in the same atrium. If they were meant to cover Voldemort's back, there was no reason to concentrate this much firepower in one place...

In an instant, Carina's face surfaced in Harry's mind. She had been waiting in Umbridge's office as though she'd anticipated Harry would come to the Ministry—and she had attacked him, actively and without hesitation, to stop him from going any further.

Harry had broken through her. He'd assumed she was on Voldemort's side.

But what if the reason Carina had tried to stop him wasn't because she was following orders—but because she was defying them, to keep him safe?

"...A trap. Don't tell me—"

The realisation hit Harry like a hammer blow. Bellatrix sneered at him standing there, dazed.

"Finally worked it out? Yes. The memory of his you saw—it was fabricated. He noticed you were watching, and he used your arrogance and your hero complex against you. He lured you here."

"Why? Why go to all that trouble—"

"Why? Because, as you said yourself, only the subject of the prophecy can take it!"

A single hypothesis cut through Harry's mind.

Professor Trelawney's prophecy concerned 'the one with the power to vanquish the Dark Lord.' And so Harry had reflexively assumed it was a prophecy binding him and Voldemort together by fate.

But there was a blind spot in that assumption.

This prophecy had foretold Harry's birth—not Voldemort's. When it had first been spoken into the world, Voldemort was already the Dark Lord.

Which meant: the prophecy bound Harry and Voldemort together, but the actual subject of the prophecy was—

"Me. Only I... can take it."

Voldemort had gone to such elaborate lengths to set this trap precisely because only Harry could retrieve the prophecy. Rather than go for it himself, he had given his followers a single instruction:

Have Harry Potter bring it to me with his own hands.

As if to confirm it, another figure emerged from behind a pillar. A bearded, middle-aged man walked out with the uneven gait of someone whose body didn't quite balance properly.

"What are you wasting time for! Let's deal with this quickly and go get the prophecy!"

Harry knew him. He'd seen more than enough of him during the Triwizard Tournament—Igor Karkaroff, Headmaster of Durmstrang.

Harry looked at him with a thin, needling smile.

"Mr. Karkaroff. I heard you'd switched sides—you do look considerably more cheerful."

Harry's eyes flicked to Karkaroff's left arm, which he was gripping with his right hand. Beneath the sleeve, something silver and metallic caught the light.

"And you've upgraded the arm as well. I'm almost envious. I used to dream about having a mechanical arm when I was younger."

"Still as cocky as ever, Potter... but just as presumptuous. A mere student, crawling in here without knowing his place... Didn't your parents teach you not to wander about at night?"

"Sorry. As you well know, the parents who should have taught me were killed by your master. At least my parents died—what did yours teach you, exactly, that you managed to betray people twice?"

Karkaroff's face cycled through several shades of red and purple. Harry kept pushing.

"Durmstrang really found themselves a fine headmaster. A wizard who sold himself to the Dark Lord to save his own skin! Isn't that the perfect cautionary tale? Your portrait will hang in the toilets. Not on the wall—on the cistern."

"You... you'll regret not dying easily—"

"Easily? I won't die at all. Unlike some people, I have no intention of dying for a wizard who can't even hold onto his own wand."

"*Avada*—"

Karkaroff, incensed, had begun the Killing Curse when someone rushed forward and seized his arm. Harry's eyes narrowed at the sight of silver-blond hair.

"Don't be rash, Igor! Have you forgotten our objective? We must bring the prophecy to the Dark Lord!"

Lucius Malfoy had grabbed Karkaroff's good arm. Karkaroff glared at Harry, his body coiled as though he might lunge at any moment.

"Let go, Lucius. I won't be satisfied until I've dealt with that insolent little brat—"

"I am in command of this operation. You will follow my orders."

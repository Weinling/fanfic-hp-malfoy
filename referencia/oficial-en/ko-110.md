# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 110: Epilogue. Muggle Corruption

Rita Skeeter had always been a tabloid journalist.

It had started in a bathroom—overhearing friends exchange whispered secrets by chance—and from that moment, gossip became both her hobby and her life. She was forever hunting it down, never letting a juicy detail slip past her. Looking back, it seemed like destiny, and luck had followed her accordingly. She'd exposed the fact that several teachers who'd been notoriously strict about student romance were themselves carrying on affairs, and everyone had praised Rita Skeeter for it. Gossip had ceased to be a pastime and become her very purpose.

The trouble began when someone shouted at her, "You're exactly like a journalist!" From that day on, Rita Skeeter's ambition was to become one, and the moment she graduated, she walked straight through the doors of the Daily Prophet. She intended to breathe new life into that stiff, fusty institution. Young and magnetic, she roamed everywhere, digging up fascinating truths.

But when the editor took the story she'd worked so hard to uncover and tossed it into the rubbish bin right in front of her, she felt as though the world had shattered. As Rita raged, the editor intoned in his dusty voice:

"We are in the business of telling people what they want to know."

What Rita had brought him was a story about an improper relationship between a senior Ministry official and his mistress. Interesting enough—but on the grounds that it would stir public anxiety, or more precisely, that it would irritate the Ministry, her article became Ashwinder fodder in the fireplace.

An ordinary journalist might have broken her quill or simply fallen into line. Rita was not ordinary. She rewrote the piece to be even more incendiary and sold it to Witch Weekly. The provocative, sensational article caused a tremendous stir, and for a brief period the magazine's circulation surpassed that of the Daily Prophet. Rita Skeeter rode the wave and kept the articles coming. Witch Weekly became the number-one favourite magazine among witches in Britain.

The publisher was furious. The old, fusty editor was sacked, and Rita stood before his replacement with her chin held high and graciously bestowed her articles upon the Daily Prophet as well. It was the moment popular appetite dismantled tired authority.

Rita Skeeter felt a thrill run through her. This was her life. Seizing people's attention with provocative stories, then wielding that attention as power. She went looking for even more incendiary truths.

But the world is a conservative place. Genuinely compelling material doesn't come along every day. Chasing ever more exciting stories, she hit a wall quickly. No matter how much legwork she put in, the results were the same dull, unremarkable pieces she'd seen in the Daily Prophet every morning.

Yet Rita was already addicted to gossip. Craving a sharper thrill, she made a choice no journalist should ever make.

When the material wasn't there, she manufactured it inside her own head.

She twisted ordinary information into something malicious and stitched together a shambling monster.

Her method of writing changed too. She would craft an article bloated with intrigue and insinuation, and when the subject pushed back, she'd whip up public opinion against them—then quietly slip away once interest cooled. Rita had drawn eyes to herself that way more than once.

It was around that time that she became an Animagus. The supply of material obtainable through legitimate means had nearly run dry. Rita deployed every tool available in her pursuit of gossip, of stories.

It worked well enough at first, but having stumbled upon a few secrets she was never meant to know, Rita earned the wariness of the world. She walked a tightrope between truth and gossip, and kept on living the life of a journalist.

And so, and so, and so.

Growing ever more sensational—yet even that sensation turning routine.

On a day that had been threatening to become truly dreadful for Rita.

While covering the Triwizard Tournament, she stumbled upon something that read exactly like a romance novel.

And that became the second turning point of Rita Skeeter's life.

Carina walked toward the mountain of stacked books.

The cover bore an illustration of a man and a woman, each holding an oversized staff, standing back to back. They were dressed like ancient sorcerers—wide-brimmed hats and robes that swept from collar to floor. A bright yellow band across the cover carried a promotional tagline in large, bold letters.

[Medieval. Magic. Academy. And a Forbidden Love! The Final Volume of Leta Sticker's Romance Series!]

That Leta Sticker was Rita Skeeter's pen name was obvious without a second glance.

Carina opened the book with trembling hands.

As it happened, the page she landed on showed an illustration of a girl lying with blood at the corner of her lips. The girl in the picture wore a heartbroken expression as she clutched someone's hand.

[Kill him... in my place...]

[No. Absolutely not!]

The man bent his head over the woman's lips as she coughed up black blood. He kissed her with fierce urgency, as though trying to draw every drop of poison out of her.

And then, a miracle unfolded. A burst of brilliant golden light gathered around them as if in blessing...]

"Aaaargh!"

Carina flung the book away as though it were something monstrous. It struck the pile beside it, and the whole stack came crashing down. The people standing nearby snapped their eyes toward her and glared.

"Excuse me, young lady!"

A middle-aged woman stepped out of the queue and scolded her.

"You can't go throwing books around like that! Do you not see the queue?"

"...Ugh."

She was right. A long line of people stood waiting for something. These books had been prepared for today's visitors, and no matter who Carina was—even if she happened to be the model for this book—she had no right to damage them.

Carina pressed her lips together and began gathering the fallen books. The woman grumbled but helped her tidy them up.

As they worked in silence, the woman spoke to Carina in a gentle, reproving tone.

"Today is Leta Sticker's signing event. For those people out there, this book will be the greatest book of their lives. Today is going to be a truly magical day! So imagine how heartbroken they'd be to receive a crumpled copy."

The middle-aged woman spoke with the devotion of someone utterly lost in the story. And so Carina found she absolutely could not say it.

That she herself was the model for this romance novel. That she was a real witch.

"Carina!"

Harry came jogging over from a distance, a coffee in each hand. He set them down in front of Carina, who was crouched on the floor gathering books, and said:

"Did you actually read it?"

"...Yes."

"Oh. Impressive! Even I couldn't get through it without burying my face in my hands."

"Only one page."

"One page is more than enough to make our little shrinking violet curl up with embarrassment."

"You make me curl up with embarrassment just as much..."

While she grumbled and finished tidying the books, the queue of people suddenly erupted into noise. Carina looked up to find everyone facing the same direction, whistling and waving.

She followed their gaze.

There was Rita Skeeter, drawing every eye in the room as she strolled forward with breezy delight. She looked like an enormous peacock—draped in every glittering ornament imaginable, her billowing fur and shawl trailing so low they nearly brushed the floor.

Rita was returning the crowd's greetings when she spotted Harry and Carina and came toward them with a warm smile.

"Oh, my muses! Come here, come here!"

Harry sprang to his feet and extended his hand.

"Good to see you, benefactor."

"My goodness. I'm genuinely moved. To think I could become the patron of the Malfoy and Potter heirs—the two who stand to inherit the greatest fortunes in the wizarding world. Who could ever have imagined it?"

Carina looked Rita up and down. The expensive violet suit, the gleaming spectacles, the fur coat draped over her shoulders—all of it announced her vanity without apology.

She must have earned enough to satisfy even herself. By selling Carina and Harry's story.

Carina muttered with barely concealed resentment.

"...Congratulations. You've become the world's greatest tabloid journalist."

"Ho ho! All thanks to the two of you! Oh, look at me—I've left my honoured guests standing!"

Rita clapped her hands with theatrical fuss. From somewhere behind her, Peter Pettigrew came scurrying forward in an oversized suit and began holding back the crowd pressing in around Rita.

"One moment, please! One moment! Leta Sticker is currently receiving an important guest! The signing begins in ten minutes—please wait until then!"

While Peter squeaked at the crowd in his thin, reedy voice, Harry and Carina followed Rita deeper into the building.

The event hall was large and bustling, as though determined to advertise how much money had been spent on it. Rita swept through the crowd, waving and smiling at everyone she passed.

Then, in the inner room prepared for her, Rita let the smile drop, exhaled, and sank into a chair.

"Sit down, both of you. Goodness. I'm exhausted."
"You looked like you were enjoying yourself."

"...I used to think that once I was famous, the world would revolve around me. That people would recognise me everywhere, that I'd earn a fortune, that I'd have everything. But now that I am famous..."

"What—do you have regrets?"

Carina's tone was dry. Rita's head snapped up and she cried out:

"Not in the slightest!"

Her face was radiant with happiness. Rita leapt to her feet, threw her arms wide, and declared:

"It's perfect! An absolutely perfect life. The spotlight pouring down on me, more money than I can spend, countless people who know my name! I finally understand. I never wanted to be a gossip journalist. I wanted to be famous!"

The Rita Skeeter who shouted this looked considerably younger than she had before. Part of it was the career change—from tabloid hack to beloved romance author—but the lavish skincare treatments her overflowing income now funded had surely played their part as well.

In any case, Rita Skeeter was happier and wealthier than she had ever been. She had even grown noticeably more relaxed because of it.

Carina, however, muttered sourly:

"...All you did was take someone else's story and twist it beyond recognition. What on earth was that?"

"I had no choice. If I'd written the wizarding world as it actually is, the Ministry would have sent investigators. And to protect the identities of my muses, I had to change certain things."

"Then change the events too! Drinking poison of your own free will—that's lifted directly from our story!"

"Oh, don't fuss over the small details."

"Small details! You wrote it off so casually, but we were desperate at the time!"

The poison she had drunk herself. The blood running down. Harry had scooped her up in a panic, crying out, and then—remembering the bezoar just in time—shoved it down her throat.

In that moment it had been truly desperate, heart-rending, agonising enough to drive her to swallow poison of her own accord. But now that everything was over...

Harry, who had been turning it over in his mind, remarked flatly:

"Looking back, it's a bit of an embarrassing memory, isn't it?"

"You be quiet too!"

"It wasn't meaningless, though, was it? We confirmed how we felt about each other. I can say this now—I was absolutely terrified at the time, but when I think back on it these days, I find myself smiling a little."

"I said be quiet!"

Carina glared at him, fuming. Harry closed his mouth and looked away with studied innocence. Watching Carina's indignation, Rita remarked with mild disinterest:

"What's the fuss about? I also included the scene where you two went for a walk under the Invisibility Cloak."

"How do you know about that...? You saw it at the Triwizard Tournament!"

"That, yes. And some of it came from digging. Like the first kiss instead of the Dementor—and the underwater date in the lake, for instance."

"Underwater... in the lake?"

What had happened beneath the lake during the Triwizard Tournament held no memory for Carina at all. She had been put to sleep by Dumbledore's magic and sunk to the bottom, only waking once she'd surfaced again.

But if someone knew about that, then who on earth...

Only one person could have known. Carina turned her head sharply. Harry was scratching the corner of his mouth and avoiding her eyes.

"Harry! You told her all of that?"

"She asked."

"You tell a tabloid journalist everything just because she asks?"

"Sorry—I wanted to show off too badly..."

"Show off? Explain yourself this instant, Harry Potter!"

Carina put on her most convincingly outraged expression. Harry grinned sheepishly and said:

"When we were keeping it secret. You were genuinely adorable back then. The way you'd be so aware of other people watching, and then quietly try to help me while pretending you weren't."

"What? When did I ever—"

"Don't you remember? Without the Invisibility Cloak, you wouldn't even meet my eyes."

Carina recalled that time and pressed her lips firmly together.

"But when we were keeping it secret, there was nowhere to boast about it at all. She approached me first. Said she was interested in our story, and that if I told her, she'd give us her full cooperation."

"So—so you told that beetle everything?"

"At the time it genuinely seemed like a brilliant idea. I got to brag about my memories with you and gain the services of Britain's finest tabloid journalist in one go."

"Don't even start! Do you have any idea how insufferably smug she was over one little lakeside walk?"

Rita Skeeter mimicked Harry's voice with relish:

"Carina will kill me if she finds out. Carina will use me as target practice for Professor Moody's curses if she finds out. Carina will twist her face like a dragon and breathe fire at me if she finds out... I half thought you were some sort of monster! I was so caught up listening to the story that I even promised to track down You-Know-Who's hideout!"

It was only then that Carina understood why Peter Pettigrew was with Rita Skeeter. He had gone out on investigations with her, and that connection had led to them staying together. Peter had always needed someone to look after him, and Rita Skeeter loved to be fawned upon—they were, in their way, a remarkably well-matched pair.

Harry scratched the back of his head.

"I was just so caught up in the moment that I thought everything would be fine. I never imagined she'd write a novel and sell it to Muggles. By the time I realised, it had already spread across all of Britain."

Now that she understood the full picture, Carina fixed Harry with a withering look. He flinched and glanced away.

"And you're all right with this? Our story has become everyone's entertainment."

Harry raised both hands under the weight of Carina's pointed stare.

"...I'm sorry, Carina. I wasn't thinking."

"Hmph."

Carina gave a small, dismissive sniff, then crooked a finger at him. When Harry stepped closer, she rested her forehead lightly against his chest.

Her voice carried through the warmth of him.

"Idiot. It doesn't matter if you show off."

"Hm?"

"Fool. Why hide it if there's nothing to be ashamed of? Show off as much as you like. Flaunt your far-too-good-for-you girlfriend to your heart's content."

Carina lifted her head. The anger had vanished from her face as though it had never been there, replaced by a self-assured smile at the corner of her lips. Harry, who had been completely taken in by the performance, let out a short, helpless laugh.

"You've gotten better at teasing."

"Wit is the mark of composure. I have always been composed."

"The tongue is as sharp as ever."

They looked at each other and laughed—quietly, helplessly, as though they'd both inhaled Amortentia at the same moment. They laughed for a long while, and without a word passing between them, arrived at the same impulse at the same instant.

Harry's reflection appeared in Carina's eyes. In the same way, Harry was watching his own reflection in hers. Once they had only been able to look at each other from a distance. Now there was nothing standing between them.

If there was nothing to hold them back, there was no reason to hold back. As though by agreement, or by the law of gravity itself, they drew slowly toward each other. At this rate, a historic and entirely ordinary collision was about to occur...

"Ahem! Ahem!"

If only there had been no interruption.

Rita Skeeter cleared her throat and inserted herself between them. Carina startled and pulled back. Harry's lips, deprived of their destination, swept through empty air.
Harry opened his eyes and grumbled:

"That's deflating. Like someone offering you a gift and snatching it back."

Carina offered a defence that wasn't quite a defence.

"I forgot. That Rita Skeeter was watching."

"You said to show off. You said to flaunt it."

"That doesn't mean doing whatever you like in front of other people!"

"So it means doing it somewhere no one can see? Fine. I'm absolutely collecting on that when we get home."

"Is that really what matters right now?"

"Nothing else matters."

Harry's expression was utterly grave, as though he were facing the end of the world. Carina's face went briefly pink before she turned sharply away.

Rita Skeeter, who had been watching the two of them with the air of someone witnessing a particularly saccharine display, clicked her tongue.

"You're absolutely insufferable, the pair of you. Charming, but shall we get to the actual business?"

Harry said:

"Wasn't that just now a perfectly good scene to write about? Since when do you pass up material?"

"I've already written the ending."

Rita answered briefly, then held out a card with the air of someone bestowing a great favour. Harry took the square debit card and turned it over in his hands.

"What's this?"

"A card with your royalties loaded onto it. Now that the series is finished, I wanted to change how I pay you."

Rita then picked up a signed copy of the book, opened it to the last page, and held it out. Harry and Carina read the text written there in an elegant hand on the back page.

"'If you donate to the account below, the funds will be passed on to the couple who inspired this novel'...? What is this?"

"Exactly what it says. Someone donated a rather generous sum a while back, and it gave me the idea."

Below the text was an account number, with a note emphasising that donors could include a short message of encouragement and urging readers to give generously.

"From now on, whatever comes into this account is yours. It'll be Muggle money, but so what? It's not illegal. With this, you could live like royalty in the Muggle world."

The wizarding world was entirely separate from the Muggle world—but that didn't mean witches and wizards were entirely separate from Muggles. They too built ordinary homes, ate ordinary food, and lived ordinary lives. A handful of wizards had even ventured into what Muggles called the "economy."

The difficulty, however, was roughly equivalent to a Muggle dropped into Diagon Alley trying to scoop up Galleons barehanded—a wizard without magic trying to earn Muggle money was that kind of challenge. Most who had set out boldly returned having lost every Muggle coin they'd brought, and so the failures went largely unnoticed.

But the world always has its lucky ones, and that lucky one was Rita Skeeter. Now a celebrated author in the Muggle world, she spoke with an almost rapturous gleam in her eye.

"A spacious house, a car with a driver, a cleaning service that's slower than a house-elf but far more attentive. Every delicacy imaginable, sweet cocktails, a plush bed and a swimming pool! With this money, you could enjoy luxuries beyond anything you've ever dreamed of! A different kind of indulgence entirely from what wizards know!"

The fervour in her voice had something almost evangelical about it, and Carina felt a conditioned resistance rise up in her. The Malfoy pureblood upbringing hadn't quite washed out of her yet—she was aware of it even as she couldn't stop herself from being cutting.

"Hmph. Muggle money. It's worthless—there's not a scrap of magic in it."

Rita's eyes lit up and she leaned forward.

"Worthless? This is real! This money moves Muggles. If you have enough of it, they'll serve you as though they're under the Imperius Curse—without a single spell cast. This money is magic. It's no different from magic!"

Watching a once-famous witch advance with that manic gleam in her eyes, Carina felt a faint chill. Not because Rita frightened her. Because she was afraid that Rita's current state might be a glimpse of her own future.

"I can see you two still don't know how to spend money properly—that's why you're talking like this. Here."

Rita opened a notebook on the desk, pulled out a ticket tucked between the pages, and handed it over.

"I was going to use this myself after finishing all my engagements today—it's a top-tier spa. I'm giving it to you instead. Go and experience it for yourselves."

Carina's pride would not permit her to accept it—but Carina was not the only one present. Harry's eyes lit up and he took the ticket without hesitation.

"Thank you. We'll make good use of it."

"Good. I've given you what I had to give—off you go."

"Brilliant! Come on, Carina!"

Carina rose without a word. Without so much as a farewell, she turned and walked out after Harry.

Was this what became of a witch who had fled the wizarding world? She was clearly enjoying herself, living in her own brand of luxury—but to Carina's eyes, it was a counterfeit kind of wealth. Shallow pleasures with no trace of magic's wonder. Low-grade indulgence.

Even if she was living in the Muggle world now, Carina would return to the wizarding world one day. She would live as a proud pureblood, enjoying everything she was rightfully entitled to.

She would never, under any circumstances, grow accustomed to a Muggle life.

Carina made herself that promise.

"Haah..."

Carina made her way to the room, her body heavy with pleasant exhaustion.

The bath strewn with flower petals had been deeply satisfying. The soap bubbles burst a little too easily—a Muggle bath, after all—but there was a certain charm to washing surrounded by a soft, lingering floral scent. Through the enormous glass window, a mountain capped with permanent snow filled the view. Magic could conjure something similar, but when the real thing was right there, why bother? Carina washed herself at leisure, enjoying the cool, sweeping vista.

She returned to the room wrapped in a warmed robe. The neatly turned-down bed spoke of careful, considered attention. Not as convenient as magic—or rather, convenient in a different way. There was something in it that suggested someone had thought about her comfort and taken pains over it.

Carina lay down on the soft bed. The yielding duvet received her body gently. She felt as though she were sinking into cloud, drifting through open air...

"You look completely melted."

Harry lay down beside her and said it. Carina turned her head the other way and murmured primly:

"Hmph. Not at all."

"With that expression on your face?"

"What expression?"

"Completely melted. Soft all the way through. If I pressed a finger in, I think it would just sink."

"Go ahead and try."

Harry smiled with quiet mischief and pressed gently against her back. Through the damp fabric of the robe, the soft warmth of her reached him.

"Mmh..."

Incidentally, the sound that came from the other side pleased him considerably.

"I thought so. You're going to melt away entirely. Whatever happened to 'Muggle amusements, of all things'?"

"I'm right here. I'm not satisfied yet."

She was clearly still trying to preserve her dignity—but Harry knew Carina far too well by now. I'm not satisfied yet meant: satisfy me.

How to satisfy Carina—that, too, Harry knew far too well.

He bent his head and pressed his nose into the robe. A faint floral scent, a clean hint of vanilla, tickled at him. His mouth watered, inexplicably. Whether it was hunger or something else entirely, he wasn't sure.

As though to find out, Harry lifted the edge of the robe and touched the tip of his tongue lightly to her skin. A single droplet hiding at the nape of her neck disappeared onto his tongue. Carina's shoulder gave a sharp, involuntary jolt.

It tasted good—so apparently it was hunger after all. Harry leaned close to Carina's slightly flushed ear and whispered:

"Where did the cool composure go? You're melting away by the second. What are you—ice cream?"

Carina did not answer. She could not say ice cream.

Thoroughly melted, thoroughly dishevelled, Carina had to wash herself all over again.

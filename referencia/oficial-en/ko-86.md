Harry Potter had gathered every potion Carina left behind. Unfortunately, there was no poison remaining—she had already used it all.

Even if there had been poison, it would have been difficult to deploy. Poison was easy for the target to detect, and given the sheer number of Death Eaters, incapacitating one or two through poisoning would have accomplished very little.

So Harry had taken the other potions instead.

Colorless, tasteless, odorless. A potion that could extract the truth from any person with just three drops.

Veritaserum.

At Harry's request, Narcissa Malfoy had slipped Veritaserum into the drinks prepared for the Death Eaters.

Of course, it was a truth potion—not a paralytic. Drinking it wouldn't send someone rolling helplessly across the floor. There was a side effect of dazed disorientation, not unlike intoxication, but as the long history of wine and alcohol demonstrated, people tended to dismiss such minor symptoms without a second thought.

Narcissa had added the Veritaserum to the wine, which was precisely why those who drank it were slow to notice anything amiss.

By the time they realized, it was already too late.

"Hey, you! What is your master's name!"

Fred shouted at the Death Eater crouched behind a pillar.

It was a baffling question, and there was no obligation to answer. The Death Eaters sneered at it and began preparing lethal curses in response.

But the Veritaserum in their stomachs had other ideas.

"Volde— gkh!"

"The D-Dark Lord, ah, ah. Vol, Voldemort—!"

The Death Eaters who spoke Voldemort's name went white as sheets, their faces contorting as though invisible hands were closing around their throats, and they crumpled one after another. Thanks to that, several of the Order of the Phoenix members were able to bypass entire skirmishes entirely.

Stepping over the fallen bodies, Fred said with undisguised delight:

"Wow. Bringing someone down with a single question. I feel like a grand sorcerer from a fairy tale!"

"Harry, what exactly did you do?"

"Nothing. I simply helped them be a little more honest."

Harry shrugged.

"It's just that He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, in his obsession with his own mystique, put a curse on his own name. His followers bear the brunt of it more than anyone. Idiotic, really. What was he planning to do if someone fed them Veritaserum and forced them to say it?"

"You're probably the only person in the world who would ever think of that."

"Where did the Veritaserum come from?"

"Carina's doing."

She hadn't left it for this purpose, but it had served one well enough. Harry said it briefly and moved on.

The manor was divided across four floors, and Voldemort's quarters were at the very top. Carina's room was on the third floor—already deep in the heart of the building, which meant there were few obstacles between there and the uppermost level.

By now, Dumbledore and the Aurors he'd brought would be making their way through the front entrance. The Death Eaters on the first and second floors would have their attention fixed in that direction. All that remained was to push through to the fourth floor.

That said, complacency was not an option. Their group was elite but small. If they were surrounded, they would be isolated—and that would be dangerous.

Fred and George stopped at a door and turned back.

"Right then—as planned, we'll hold this door. Getting flanked from behind would be bad."

"Transforming barricade. We've been saving this for exactly this moment."

They reached into a large sack and pulled out a set of toothed clamps, which they wedged into the door frame. The door seemed to stretch and yawn—and then sharp steel teeth began sprouting along its edges. Any Death Eater careless enough to approach would find themselves caught in those steel jaws like a bear trap.

The Weasley twins pressed on, pulling item after item from their pockets: Peruvian Instant Darkness Powder, a slippery cleaning solution that sent anyone who stepped on it skidding, protective cloaks, protective gloves, and more. A full arsenal of tricks designed to confuse and obstruct.

Once they'd finished their preparations, the twins called out:

"Go on ahead! We'll hold the rear!"

"And when you defeat You-Know-Who, put in a good word for our joke shop!"

"Sorry. I'll be calling Carina's name at that moment. I'll try if there's time left over."

The Weasley twins cackled and waved them off. The rest of the group pressed forward, leaving them behind. Molly Weasley glanced back at her sons several times with barely concealed anxiety—but with Dumbledore entering from the ground floor, staying behind might actually be the safer position.

Of course, no one could say what would happen. This was a battlefield, and no one could guarantee anyone's safety. All they could do was make the best choices available to them.

Ron and Hermione, armed with the gadgets and protective gear the twins had handed out, stayed close to Harry's side as they advanced. Ron looked down at the heavy sack of tricks and said:

"I honestly didn't think my brothers would give us any of this before a fight. They haven't given me a single thing for free all year. But I suppose blood is blood—they'll hand things over when lives are on the line."

Ron looked genuinely moved. Hermione gave him a withering look.

"They're planning to bill us for it afterward."

"Don't say things like that right before a battle—"

"And you shouldn't be talking nonsense right before a battle either. Review your Shield Charms. Have you mastered nonverbal spells? When two wizards of equal skill face each other, nonverbal casting is absolutely essential—"

"Do I really have to listen to your lectures even now?"

They both fell silent and walked on. The long corridor ended, and the next door drew steadily closer. With it, Ron and Hermione's strides grew shorter and shorter.

Tension, anticipation, and the dread of not knowing what lay beyond that door—it had made them both go quiet. They approached it carefully.

While Mad-Eye scanned the other side of the door with his magical eye, Ron looked at Hermione and drew a long breath.

"Hermione, I'm only saying this now because there won't be another chance… When this fight is over—"

"Everyone down!"

At that moment, Moody shoved Ron and Hermione aside and bellowed. An instant later, a thunderous crash rang out and the door was blown clean off its hinges. Screams erupted around them, and several people who hadn't managed to get clear were pinned beneath the door, groaning.

Footsteps followed. A young man with swept-back blond hair and a face drained of all expression—Bartemius Crouch—stood in the doorway, spine straight, wand raised. Behind him, a cluster of tense Death Eaters waited.

Bartemius spoke.

"How did you get this far? Dumbledore should still be on the ground floor."

"Curious, are you?"

The fallen door rose into the air, spinning furiously, and hurtled toward the Death Eaters. They scrambled for cover—but Bartemius himself didn't move. He simply flicked his wand.

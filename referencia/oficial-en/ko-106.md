That morning began in chaos. Aurors from the Ministry of Magic descended on Hogwarts in force, flooding the Great Hall and surrounding Harry. Among them were Mad-Eye and Lupin—both present not merely as Aurors, but as members of the Order of the Phoenix, whose role in defeating the Dark Lord had earned them a place in these proceedings.

Moody, moreover, carried considerable standing in his own right. He had taken command of the Auror contingent.

"I will now give a brief explanation of the Unbreakable Vow."

Given its singular nature and brutal consequences, few Aurors had ever encountered an Unbreakable Vow firsthand. They listened to Moody with careful attention.

"First: the Unbreakable Vow is a contract between two parties. A bonder increases its reliability. However, in all cases, if one party dies, the Vow is dissolved. In the past, victims forced into unwanted contracts against their will invariably sought to kill the other party. The opposing side did everything in their power to prevent them."

At the word kill, the Aurors flinched and glanced toward Harry.

Harry Potter himself had never taken a life—a decent young man by any measure. But the soul residing within him belonged to Voldemort, the darkest wizard of the age, who had killed more people than anyone alive could count. To enter an Unbreakable Vow with Harry was to risk having Voldemort, lurking inside him, make an attempt on your life.

Moody let that warning hang in the air, his sharp gaze sweeping the room.

"For that reason, when an Unbreakable Vow is made, it is customary to impose restrictions preventing either party from killing by any means. But the methods of killing are as numerous as the crimes in this world—endless. I am warning you now. Anyone who participates in the binding of Harry Potter must be prepared for that."

"If there's that kind of danger, who in their right mind would volunteer?"

One Auror grumbled, and Moody brought his leg down hard against the floor.

"I would! I will be the first."

Even to a fellow Auror, Mad-Eye's gaze carried a weight that pressed down on the chest. The room fell silent under the force of it.

"The rest of you needn't step forward. This is a Vow that can only be made one at a time—adding more bodies to the count serves little purpose. Better to have one or two representatives take the Vow on everyone's behalf. Remus and I have already volunteered, so we'll go first."

"Is all of this truly necessary? Every Auror here has other duties, and a Vow that powerful is ultimately a noose around your own neck. Confiscating his wand and placing him under surveillance in a secure room would be far more—"

"Dawlish!"

A short-haired, grey-cropped Auror had been muttering his objections. Moody rounded on him with a ferocious expression.

"He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named was defeated because Harry Potter threw himself into it at the risk of his own life! The wizarding world owes Harry a debt it cannot repay! And you would cast him aside over a minor inconvenience?"

"He hasn't disappeared yet!"

But this time, Dawlish refused to back down. He rose to his feet and shot back defiantly.

"You speak as though everything is settled and peace has returned—that is a profound delusion! As long as He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named remains inside that boy, the threat is not over!"

"So. An Auror is saying he'd be willing to harm an innocent child?"

"I am speaking about reality!"

The argument that had never quite reached a conclusion flared up again. Harry sat quietly in his chair and listened. Kill him, spare him, there's no need to waste valuable manpower and time on him—Dawlish's case was met blow for blow by Moody's rebukes.

Lupin, his expression pained, crossed the room and placed a hand on Harry's shoulder.

"I'm sorry. You shouldn't have to witness this."

Harry answered without any particular change in his face.

"Don't be. I've had worse. This is hardly new."

"The Ministry has already reached a decision. They intend to implement the highest level of security measures on your behalf. Don't let those men trouble you."

"I'm not troubled."

Harry tilted his chin toward a cluster of Aurors across the room. Even in the midst of the argument, they had been stealing anxious glances at him—and the moment Harry looked their way, they seized their wands in a reflex that was less caution than panic.

Sensitive to the point of convulsion. As though Harry were Voldemort himself.

Harry smiled, though it didn't reach his eyes.

"They seem to be, though."

Lupin exhaled slowly.

When He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named had returned and clashed with the Order, some of those Aurors had witnessed firsthand the power Voldemort wielded. Those who had survived that encounter lived in fear that he might yet rise again through Harry.

But how to explain it? How do you tell a boy who turns seventeen tomorrow that the adults around him are afraid of him, and ask him to understand?

Lupin couldn't bring himself to say it. He simply rested his hand on Harry's shoulder in silence.

It was then that the doors of the Great Hall swung open.

Every head in the room turned at once, nerves already wound tight. The latecomer was Carina Malfoy of Slytherin—a girl who had ended the war in a manner quite different from Harry's, the daughter of the Malfoy family, first among the decisive defectors.

She was immaculate in her school uniform. Even McGonagall would have found nothing to fault. Her platinum hair streamed behind her as she walked straight into the middle of a room crawling with Aurors.

"If you'll forgive me, everyone."

Carina passed through the Aurors without a glance and came to stand beside Harry. Harry looked up, and his face did something it hadn't done in some time—it opened, like a man receiving an unexpected gift. This was the first time they had seen each other since Harry had declared he would not make a Horcrux.

He had half-expected never to see her again, and had been quietly making his peace with that. But the body is honest. The moment their eyes met, a smile found its way to Harry's lips without his permission.

Carina smiled back—a small, bright thing—and slipped her arm through his.

"It may well be our last day together," she said pleasantly. "Would you allow Harry and me a little time alone?"

The people who had been arguing so heatedly over Harry's fate stared at her as though she had said something incomprehensible. Dawlish, still flushed from his row with Moody, frowned.

"Miss Malfoy. That's rather difficult to accommodate. We are here to protect Harry Potter, to monitor him, and to bind him as a precaution against any contingency. To allow Harry Potter to—"

"Let them, Malfoy."

Moody's easy permission sent Dawlish into an immediate uproar.

"Moody! Again, doing exactly as you please—"

"Hmm. What's the problem? They're a couple who want a moment together. Can't we spare them that much?"

"Harry Potter requires surveillance!"

"Strictly speaking, that applies from midnight tonight. Dumbledore's enchantment is still in effect. Look at the boy."

Moody gestured toward Harry, who sat watching them all with quiet eyes.

"Does he look to you like a man possessed by that creature's soul? No. What I see is a young man calmly accepting the fate that's coming for him. Are you truly unwilling to grant him even a brief moment of peace?"

"Hmm…"

Even Dawlish couldn't bring himself to be that cruel. He gave a reluctant nod and attached a condition.

"But they are not to leave our line of sight. He requires monitoring."

"You can't be serious. What couple could spend time together in here, with everyone staring at us like that?"

Carina turned in a slow, graceful circle, drawing every eye in the room. With that single movement—half pirouette, half performance—she had the room's full attention. She clapped her hands lightly and pointed to the trunk Moody had brought with him.

"In that case—would it be acceptable to spend a short while alone inside that? If we're in there, there's at least no risk of escape."

Moody's trunk, enchanted with a Space-Enlarging Charm, was packed with tools for countering Dark Magic and various potions. It was an isolated space, used on occasion to detain and hold Death Eaters when the situation called for it.

Dawlish, unable to find a reasonable objection, trailed off.

"That's true, but…"

"Thank you so much for your understanding. We'll borrow it for just a moment."

Carina walked toward the trunk with complete self-assurance. She released the seventh lock with practiced ease and threw the lid open. What appeared from the outside to be a trunk barely large enough for one person concealed, within, a hidden space that descended deep underground.

Carina wound her arm around Harry's neck and whispered.

"I'm going to jump in, Harry."

"What?"

"You'll catch me, won't you?"

"I will, but—wait, Carina—!"

Before he could finish, she leapt straight into the trunk. Harry yanked out his wand, cast Arresto Momentum, and went after her.

The two of them were swallowed into the trunk as though pulled by a current.

With a soft thud, the lid fell shut.

—

The inside of the trunk was as dark and lived-in as ever—which is to say, not at all. The cramped, cluttered space was packed with instruments for countering Dark Magic and a variety of potions. Harry picked his way carefully around the chairs, following Carina deeper in.

"What was that about—jumping in like that, you could have—"

"Harry."

Carina turned sharply.

"What?"

"I have a favour to ask. Will you hear it?"

"Anything you ask."

Harry said it lightly, with a hint of a smile. Carina's expression did not soften.

"Kill me."

There was no way to mishear it. It was too vicious to be a joke.

She was still holding onto what had happened with Karkaroff—that much was obvious. Harry tried to ease the tension.

"Carina. It's all right. Did you see all those Aurors upstairs? That many Aurors, gathered here for me alone. I'm safe."

He'd thought it was rather a good line. Carina didn't laugh. She looked at him with an expression of absolute seriousness.
"Harry Potter. This is not a crime."

She moved slowly toward the desk. Among the clutter covering its surface, she picked up a small black vial.

"Snape dying to protect you is not your fault."

Harry's expression hardened. There are things you can joke about, and things you cannot. Harry had disliked Snape and mocked him often enough—but before the fact of his death, Harry had always been reverent.

And yet.

"Just as Dumbledore being struck by the Killing Curse is not your fault."

Carina pressed into the wound. It felt like something that had closed was tearing open again. Harry swallowed.

"Carina. They sacrificed themselves to save me."

"Yes. They sacrificed themselves for you."

She said it with a bitter edge, and uncorked the black vial. Only then did Harry register what it was—an ordinary glass bottle filled with a liquid that moved like darkness itself, viscous and slow. From the open mouth of the bottle, black smoke curled and swayed like something alive.

Carina scattered it into the air. Tar-black darkness clung to her brilliant platinum hair. The dark smoke merged with the shadows of the room and coiled around them both.

Wrapped in that black haze, Carina murmured.

"It doesn't have to be only them."

"…Carina, what is this?"

"The Horcrux ritual."

Harry's eyes went wide.

The Horcrux ritual. You scatter a prepared potion, stand somewhere sunlight cannot reach, and speak a foul, ancient incantation. Dark magic latches onto the soul—cackling as though it might tear free at any moment.

"The preparations are complete. Harry. You can begin now."

Carina, the ritual finished, looked at him with a composed face and made her demand.

"Cast the Killing Curse on me."

Harry answered with difficulty.

"…There's no way I could do that just because you're asking. I couldn't kill Karkaroff. How could I do that to you?"

"You'll have to. Because if you don't, I'll kill myself."

"Don't be—"

Carina's eyes snapped open.

"I've drunk poison before. Does that look like a joke to you?"

Harry pressed his lips together. Any ordinary protest would only fan the flames of her fury.

"I heard everything they said up there! They claim they'll help and support your binding—but the moment it becomes inconvenient, the moment the cost seems too high, they'll dispose of you and wash their hands of it. That's the cleaner outcome for them!"

Carina's voice cracked with force.

"Don't look away! That isn't even a second-best option. It's the lesser of two evils. You cannot survive on something this vague! Face it. To live, you have to kill someone!"

The black smoke churned. Harry had lived inside one trial or another for as long as he could remember, but lately they had been coming too fast, too close together.

Death and life. He had been saved by Snape, a man he had treated as an enemy. He had killed Dumbledore, the man who had been his greatest guide. And Dumbledore, dying, had asked something of Harry.

Before your birthday comes, take your own life.

He hadn't sworn to follow that instruction. He had thought—if there was a way to survive without dying, that would be acceptable too.

But survival on those terms would not be a whole life. And his lover was urging him toward life—urging him to live even if it meant killing her, the person he held more precious than his own existence.

Harry said, exhausted.

"Carina. This kind of fight doesn't help anyone. I can't let you sacrifice yourself too."

"Don't flatter yourself, Harry Potter. Do you think I'm doing this for you?"

Her voice came sharp and cutting. Carina raised a finger and pressed it into his chest.

"Voldemort is sleeping inside you. If you die, is there any guarantee he dies with you? If your body is destroyed—burned to nothing—if an Unbreakable Vow is used to bind your throat—is there any guarantee that he'll die alongside you, hand in hand?"

"…"

"His body is not that of an ordinary human. It was a grotesque thing, stitched together from scraps of flesh. And the Unbreakable Vow was made with you. If the Vow is broken, you die—not him. He could simply take possession of your body the moment you're gone!"

"…?"

The world lurched.

In Harry's consciousness—in the hollow space that had always been there—something shifted. A darkness that seemed deliberately concealed revealed itself.

Carina's words had been meant to hide her own feelings. She had been piling on reasons as fast as they came to her, trying to bury what she actually meant.

But by chance—sheer, unintended chance—one of those reasons had struck something true.

"If he returns, the first ones in danger are none other than me. The Malfoy family, who betrayed them all and handed everything to Dumbledore—we would be the first to fall! So I am using my life for the sake of my family. Not for you!"

Carina seized Harry's hand. She pressed the wand in his grip against her own chest and screamed. And all the while, Harry was thinking—deeply, quietly, somewhere else entirely.

"Do it! Do it and kill him through me! End every last tragedy with this! So you never have to agonise over it again!"

There she stood—Carina, who had steeled herself for the worst and offered her life.

"Heh. Heh heh."

Harry laughed.

While Carina's brow furrowed, Harry pulled her arm away and raised his wand.

"You tried to trick me. Riddle. You were frightened, weren't you?"

When Voldemort was sealed, his power and knowledge had flowed into Harry. Or so Harry had believed.

But Voldemort's consciousness had remained, and they did not share every memory. If they had shared all of his memories and personality, the Horcrux would have been the first thing Harry thought of.

Yet among Voldemort's memories, everything pertaining to the Horcruxes had been absent.

"I am a Horcrux."

Harry said it quietly, without inflection.

"And to destroy a Horcrux, ordinary methods won't do."

To destroy a Horcrux required a magical means capable of damaging the soul itself. The venom of a Basilisk. The Sword of Gryffindor. Or—

"For a living human—the Killing Curse."

The Killing Curse was exactly what its name promised: a curse that took life. Every person ever struck by it directly had died without exception. No wound, no mark—only a stopped heart.

The reason no external injury could be found was that the spell took nothing but life itself.

The effect of Avada Kedavra was to sever the connection between body and soul.

"Professor Dumbledore told me to take my own life. But did he mean… that I should die?"

"Harry? What are you saying?"

Harry answered Carina's confusion with a smile, and raised his wand. He was still smiling—openly, freely—as he brought it level with his own face.

"Thank you, Carina. You've helped me make up my mind."

A brief farewell. Then he pressed the wand to his temple.

"Harry!"

Carina lunged forward. But Harry's smile was full and unguarded, and his voice rang with something that had not been there before.

"Carina. Trust me. I'm not going to die."

Carina's outstretched hand stopped.

Her face was still uncertain. Her eyes asked the question her voice couldn't form.

Can I really trust you this time?

You're not killing yourself?

Harry nodded—firmly, without hesitation.

"I'll be back in a moment. Wait for me."

He said it the way someone might say it before a short trip, and cast the spell on himself.

"*Avada Kedavra.*"

A flash of green light bloomed near his temple.

—

It was a familiar place.

Every year, arriving at Hogwarts with a heart full of anticipation, he had walked through these doors. The Great Hall stretched wide, four long tables arranged in rows. The tables that should have been crowded with students reaching for food were empty now—save for two.

A chandelier drifted through the air far above, suspended beneath a ceiling so high its end was lost to sight. Along the walls, banners of crimson, green, yellow, and red alternated in sequence. At the high table, chairs as distinctive as the professors who had once occupied them stood waiting.

This was the Great Hall of Hogwarts.

"Snape… Professor Snape cast a spell on me at the end. When he was shielding me."
A banner of crimson and gold silk lay spread across one of the tables. Harry sat on it and spoke.

"Sacrificial Protection. What my mother used for me. Professor Snape had spent his entire life searching for that magic, and only managed to obtain it after he became the Defence Against the Dark Arts professor. I think, in his way, he was rather romantic. Once he had the spell, it seems he intended to use it on me—someone he didn't even particularly like. Perhaps he wanted to remain with me even in death."

"…As foolish as that woman, in the end."

Across from him, at the Slytherin table draped in green and silver, a dark-haired young man rose to his feet. His handsome face was rendered meaningless by the cold contempt with which he regarded Harry.

"He came to me on his knees, begging only for her. Said it was enough to kill the child—just spare the woman, he said. Please, spare the woman…"

"Snape was a bit pathetic about it, yes."

Harry met Riddle's gaze with a quiet laugh.

"But because of that, you couldn't separate me from myself. Immediately after taking my body, you killed Professor Dumbledore. Partly to reduce the enemy's strength, of course—but you also intended to use the Horcrux ritual to tear my soul free, didn't you?"

The moment he had taken the body, he had killed the one who came closest—Dumbledore. Riddle had reasons enough to want Dumbledore dead. But revenge alone was not the whole of it.

Harry's soul had still been present in that body, and Riddle had wanted to eliminate that unstable element.

"But just before you tore my soul free, you remembered the Sacrificial Protection Professor Snape had cast. Sacrificial Protection—the effect gained by offering one's own life—is to repel all that is corrupt and malign. If you had tried to split my soul, it would have reflected back and split yours instead. Isn't that right?"

Riddle's face twisted with displeasure. Harry mocked him, then struck without warning—thrusting his wand forward. The shards of glass scattered from the chandelier became a flock of sharp-edged birds and flew at Riddle.

"And thanks to Professor Dumbledore's magic, I can draw on a portion of your power."

The glittering glass birds struck an invisible wall—a barrier Riddle had conjured. Like birds flying unseen into a pane of glass, they crashed one after another and fell to the floor, dissolving back into shards.

But in that moment, Harry appeared directly before him. He had concealed himself with a Disillusionment Charm and charged alongside the glass birds.

"I survived because of my parents and Sirius, who gave their lives for me—and I'm standing here now! You fell from the most powerful Dark Lord of the age to a parasite living inside my body!"

An enormous explosion erupted from the tip of his wand—powerful enough to shatter the barrier along with it. Riddle staggered from the force of the blast, but he could not retaliate.

Not because Harry's assault was too overwhelming—though it was—but because if he made a mistake and killed Harry, he would die along with him.

"And because of Carina's resolve, I was finally able to defeat you here!"

A black serpent materialised and lunged to coil around Harry. Harry severed it in a single stroke, then shaped its smoke into a dagger and hurled it. Riddle threw himself aside and barely avoided it.

Riddle could subdue Harry, could cause him pain. But the Harry standing before him now was not an opponent he could subdue with ease. Harry pressed the attack relentlessly, with overwhelming advantage.

"Yes! What drove you to this point—that is the result of the love you spent your life searching for!"

"You… insolent…!"

In that instant, Harry felt warmth spread through his fingertips. He looked up. Beyond the boundary of this consciousness, in the real world—a girl inside the trunk was holding his hand.

Harry smiled broadly and reached out. There, hanging upside down and nodding drowsily, was the Sorting Hat.

"Let's finish this. I'm rather busy."

Shing. A clean, bright ring of metal.

The Sword of Gryffindor—capable of destroying even a soul—burst forth with force, blazing with its own light, training that light on Riddle.

Harry levelled the sword. This was nothing like the old days, when its weight had nearly pulled him off his feet. Sword in one hand, wand in the other, Harry called out to Riddle.

"I have a honeymoon to get to."

"No—don't—!"

"It's my honeymoon—who are you to tell me no? Not even my father-in-law gets a say in my honeymoon!"

The Sword of Gryffindor cleaved through the barrier and through Riddle's body in the same stroke. Imbued with Basilisk venom, it cut through the soul itself.

Riddle screamed in agony. White light bled from the great gash across him. Hairline fractures spread across his entire form—and then, in an instant, white radiance consumed him whole.

Harry exhaled slowly and sheathed the Sword of Gryffindor. It was then that he noticed someone seated at the high table.

"Professor Dumbledore. It's been a while."

Dumbledore inclined his head with a kind smile.

"Well done, Harry."

There had been so many thoughts. That Dumbledore had left Harry to die. The final instruction to take his own life. Harry had never let it show, but it had weighed on him—how could it not, to be told by the person you trusted most to end your own existence?

Harry let his shoulders drop and addressed him plainly.

"You can't just tell someone to die out of nowhere. I genuinely thought I had to."

"I'm sorry. But I believed that without a true readiness to die, you would not be able to cast Avada Kedavra. The will to kill oneself… comes from despair."

Dumbledore's beetle-bright eyes rested on Harry, then drifted upward toward Carina.

"And yet, in the end, I failed to trust either of you… If you had not wanted to live, if that girl had not fought so hard to keep you alive, could you have overcome him?"

"Well. It was thanks to you and Professor Snape, in the end."

"You finally call Severus 'Professor.' I corrected you so many times and you never changed."

Harry shrugged at the gentle rebuke. Dumbledore laughed—a soft, hollow sound—and said:

"Do you resent me?"

"I'm not sure. If things had gone badly, perhaps a little. But."

Riddle was gone. His remaining power still resided in Harry. All that was left now was to return to the real world and find Carina.

It was over.

Snape. Dumbledore. And the sacrifices that had preceded them both, stretching back further than Harry could trace—from those closest to him to those he had barely known. The long chain of deaths made on his behalf had finally reached its end.

The chain of death was broken. What remained would be birth.

"Because everyone gave so much for me, I was able to survive. Thank you, truly, Professor Dumbledore. Please pass my thanks to Professor Snape as well."

"I will, Harry. And know that we are grateful to you in equal measure."

"Come now. I only did what I had to do to survive."

"That is not entirely what I meant."

Dumbledore looked toward something with quiet, distant eyes—somewhere that was neither Hogwarts nor the Ministry, but somewhere else entirely.

His voice carried the weight of old regret.

"If the two of us had truly understood each other, been honest with each other, tried hard enough to change each other… we might have walked a different road. What a shame. What a shame…"

The old see the past in the present. They are people who have been standing in it since long before.

Then and now are not the same. The standards, the morality, the sense of justice, the experience—all different. Dumbledore had never trusted himself entirely, had never believed he had the right to govern another person's fate.

And so he had watched. Snape's sacrifice. Harry's suffering. He had known all of it, and had not intervened, had not altered a thing. Only watched.

Fortunately, those he had taught had arrived, in the end, at the right place.

Was that not enough? He had not been happy—but those he left behind would be. That was the duty of those who come after, and the reward of those who came before.

Dumbledore smiled.

"I should be going. Someone is waiting for me."

"Ah. Yes. Of course."

Harry kept glancing back at Dumbledore, as though something about leaving him here didn't sit right. He seemed to be debating whether it was all right to go. Dumbledore's smile turned gently mischievous.

"You have a honeymoon to attend to, don't you?"

"Ah. You heard that."

Harry scratched the back of his head, caught out, then recovered with easy composure.

"I was going to ask you to give a toast. Pity."

"I'm sorry to miss it. But marriage belongs to the living—what can one do? Ask Horace instead. He would give a magnificent toast…"

"If the opportunity arises."

For a brief moment, their eyes met. Harry bowed his head toward Dumbledore.

"Goodbye, Professor."

Dumbledore nodded, unhurried.

"Take care of yourself."

And Harry's consciousness drifted free.

—

Harry opened his eyes slowly.

The first thing he saw was a pair of silver-grey eyes, brimming with something too large to contain. Whatever those wide eyes had been holding, it had overflowed.

Carina's gaze met his. She startled—and moved to wipe her tears away. But Harry tightened his grip on her hand and held it fast.

Between them now, the only remaining obstacle was roughly fifty centimetres of air.

An easy obstacle to overcome.

Harry lifted his head without hesitation. The distance between them—the last obstacle—

was nothing at all.

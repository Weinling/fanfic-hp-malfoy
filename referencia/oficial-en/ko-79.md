Severus Snape had finally obtained the Defence Against the Dark Arts position he had long coveted. The resulting vacancy in Potions was filled by an old professor named Horace Slughorn—appointed personally by Dumbledore.

Carina's first impression of him was that he moved with the unhurried ease of an aged bear, and was every bit as cunning.

From the moment he stepped into the classroom, Slughorn had greeted Harry Potter and Blaise Zabini with conspicuous warmth. The reason was obvious enough. Those two were either the most famous students in the room, or the ones with the most powerful connections.

The reason Carina was not among them was equally simple.

It was Slughorn's way of making clear that he had no particular interest in cultivating ties with the sons and daughters of Death Eaters.

As if to mirror her mood, four cauldrons bubbled and churned at the front of the room. Carina kept her expression neutral and took her seat among the Slytherins. The underground classroom was thick with the strange, mingling scents of the potions.

The smell was something like the familiar warmth of a broom cupboard, or the clean fragrance of a freshly laundered cloak. It was a pleasant, heady scent—sweet as fine wine—yet something about it made Carina uneasy, and she pressed her sleeve against her nose.

Through the drifting haze, Slughorn spoke.

"Now, I've brewed a few of these simply because I thought they'd be of interest. Every one of them is something you'll be expected to produce if you continue on to N.E.W.T. level."

He gestured toward the cauldron sitting directly in front of the Slytherin table. Carina leaned forward to examine the liquid. It looked like plain water.

"Does anyone know what this potion is?"

Colourless and odourless. She knew of one potion that fit the description. But before the name had fully formed in her mind, Hermione—who had always been first to these moments—already had her hand in the air.

"Veritaserum. This colourless, odourless potion forces the drinker to tell the truth with as little as three drops."

"Excellent!"

Slughorn beamed and moved on to the cauldron before the Ravenclaw table, where a mud-like potion simmered slowly.

"This one is rather well known—it's been mentioned in Ministry pamphlets on more than one occasion… Does anyone know what it is?"

"Polyjuice Potion! It transforms you into someone else!"

"Splendid, splendid! And this one?"

Hermione's hand shot up again, and Slughorn's expression shifted to something faintly pained.

"Amortentia! The most powerful love potion in existence!"

"Correct. I begin to feel rather foolish asking. You'd have recognised it by the distinctive mother-of-pearl sheen, I imagine?"

"And by the steam—it rises in a spiral, which is quite unusual!"

Hermione was practically glowing as she continued.

"This potion is known to smell differently to each person, according to what attracts them. For me, right now, it smells like—"

It was a performance. The moment Hermione Granger was added to Slughorn's list of promising students was almost physically irritating—made worse by the fact that Carina herself was not on it.

It seemed she would not be getting on with this old professor. Carina had nearly resigned herself to that conclusion when—

"Shall we get on with the lesson, then?"

"Professor, you haven't told us about that cauldron yet."

"Ho!"
Someone pointed to the final cauldron, the one that shimmered with a golden light. Slughorn drifted toward it with theatrical slowness. He had clearly been waiting for someone to prompt him.

"So I hadn't… That, my dears, is a most fascinating little potion called Felix Felicis. Its effect is—well, I suspect you know, Miss Granger?"

"It's Liquid Luck!"

"Precisely! It brings luck to the drinker—everything goes right, the world seems to sing in your favour! Take this, and for one day you become the luckiest person alive! And—it also happens to be today's prize. Whoever brews the finest potion this lesson will win this small bottle of good fortune!"

Carina was on her feet before she had consciously decided to stand.

Liquid Luck? Twelve hours of fortune, handed to her?

She could not think of anyone in this room who needed it more. For the first time in her life, Carina leaned forward over her desk and fixed her entire attention on the lesson with something close to ferocity. She needed that bottle. She had already staked her life on something that might well be impossible—wanting a little luck on top of that could hardly be counted against her.

She had never once exerted herself for the sake of marks or praise. But in this moment, Carina resolved to brew with everything she had.

"Right, then—everyone turn to page ten of Advanced Potion-Making and we'll attempt the Draught of Living Death. It will be more difficult than anything you've brewed before, but the best result takes home the prize! Begin!"

Scrape.

As if on cue, every student dragged their cauldron forward. Carina did the same. With rather less elegance than usual, she rolled up her sleeves, pulled her hair back, and began measuring ingredients faster than she ever had before.

But.

As the minutes passed, the hope in her chest deflated like a punctured balloon.

Hermione Granger's potion was a bright violet. Not quite as clear as the textbook described, but still several shades better than Carina's murky navy mixture.

And yet even Hermione was not in first place. Harry Potter was stirring a softly rose-tinted potion with an almost careless ease. Even given another hour, no one else in this room would come close to what he had produced.

Effort and talent.

Hermione Granger read every textbook before term began. People reduced her to a single word—*clever*—but that word concealed the invisible hours and relentless work that had forged her. It was a product of discipline, not accident.

And then there was Harry Potter, who had a natural gift for Potions. It had surfaced almost by chance in their first year, when sheer stubbornness—a refusal to give Snape any grounds for criticism—had coaxed something innate into bloom.

Carina had neither.

She had not read ahead. She had no instinctive feel for it. She was capable of executing a method precisely as written, but she could not catch the subtle shifts that advanced potions demanded. If she were being honest, Carina was merely competent at most things.

The realisation settled over her with quiet clarity. She had never once beaten Harry Potter in Potions. Not once.

Slughorn passed by her table just then. On an impulse, Carina spoke.

"Professor—did you happen to know my grandfather? Abraxas Malfoy?"

It was, for Carina, a considerable concession. Slughorn did not even glance at her.

"Ah. A great pity when he passed. Quite unexpected, really. Dragon pox, at his age…"

He murmured it as he walked on. The words she had offered broke apart like a wave against stone, and whatever remained of her composure crumpled quietly.

That was simply how it was. The sixteen-year-old daughter of a Death Eater, with her father locked in Azkaban—there was no particular reason to bother with her.

The gentle world that had once surrounded her, the one that had always been kind, was breaking apart. She did not feel despair, exactly. Not shock.

Only a quiet, settling acknowledgement. Ah. I see.
Time was called. Professor Slughorn moved through the classroom, evaluating each student's work. He did not spare a glance for Carina's cauldron—which was, in a way, a mercy, given the state of it.

"I think we have a clear winner!"

Slughorn announced the inevitable with evident delight.

"Magnificent! Harry, you must have inherited your mother's gift—Lily had an extraordinary instinct for Potions! Here—as promised!"

Harry Potter accepted a small vial filled with golden liquid. He gave a brief nod and slipped it into his pocket.

Carina had fallen short. If she had known Felix Felicis was on offer, she would have practised beforehand and taken it by sheer preparation alone.

Where Hermione won through effort and Harry through talent, Carina had always won through leverage and connections. That was the Malfoy way.

But the older students who had once whispered answers to her before class had gradually graduated. Her father, who had always passed along advance intelligence about professors, was gone. The things that had made Carina Malfoy exceptional were vanishing like mirages, and she was left standing alone in a dry, shadowless wasteland.

She could not afford to sit down and give up. It was her turn, now, to cast the shadow—to shelter what remained of her family beneath it.

She had no particular ability, and obligations far beyond her means. But there was one thing she still possessed that no one else in this room had.

Desperation. The kind that only belongs to someone who has genuinely put their life on the line.

Veritaserum. Polyjuice Potion. And Amortentia.

It was nothing new for Potions professors to brew stocks of their own supplies in advance. It was a professional habit—an occupational instinct, like a squirrel laying in stores for winter.

But no one would expect someone to slip in and steal from them. And aside from Felix Felicis, these were potions that wouldn't be sorely missed if a small amount went missing.

He'll want to show them to other years. Which means they'll likely still be here tonight.

Tonight. Carina would take what she needed—enough to be useful, not enough to be noticed.

She emptied her cauldron, cleaned her tools, and while returning the leftover ingredients to the supply shelf, she used the motion as cover to note the exact position of each cauldron. She was the last one left, finally packing her bag, when—

Someone brushed past her.

Tap.

A small vial of golden liquid dropped into her bag.

Carina spun around. Harry Potter was already near the door. He paused for just a moment on the threshold, and their eyes met—briefly, barely—before he turned and disappeared from view.

Carina stood there, staring.

"What…"

Something hot surged up inside her chest.

He hadn't spoken to her once since sixth year began. Hadn't sought her out. When their eyes had met before, he had always been the first to look away. He had never taken the hand she'd extended—

"Don't confuse me like this…"

Why give her something like this, and leave her standing here in pieces?

She reached into her bag and closed her fingers around the vial. It glowed a brilliant gold, warm as sunlight against her palm.

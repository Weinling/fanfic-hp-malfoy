He never thought he could keep it hidden. Nor had he particularly wanted to. Truth comes in many varieties, and trying to suppress something too enormous will only tear you apart from the inside. If ordinary gossip couldn't be contained, how could anyone stay silent about something on which the peace of the world depended?

The only reason Harry hadn't told Carina everything—

Was simply that he wasn't ready. If she showed fear of him… he thought even he might not survive that.

"Really? It can't be a pleasant story."

"Since when did you get to decide how I feel, Harry? Don't presume to overstep. Whether I laugh it off or find it disagreeable is for me to determine."

But Carina had read his hesitation and pressed him all the harder for it.

Harry burst out laughing. Carina, who had been speaking in earnest, furrowed her brow in displeasure—but he couldn't stop himself.

She had just witnessed his magic, and rather than fear him, she was worried about him. She didn't avert her eyes. She didn't tremble. She leaned her upper body toward him, meeting his gaze head-on with eyes that permitted no lies.

No need for Veritaserum. In front of her, Harry couldn't mix in a single falsehood.

"The fact that you like me—that was also your heart's decision, I suppose."

"…That could change, depending on what you say next."

She didn't deny that she liked him. Harry could hear the worry tucked inside those blunt words. With someone this easy to read, how spectacularly stupid had Voldemort been? The world was full of ways to uncover the truth beyond Legilimency.

It would be all right to tell her. No—if he didn't, she would be more disappointed.

Having made up his mind, Harry took her hand. Holding that small, warm hand, courage welled up in him. It felt as though he could push through anything.

Even if that was nothing more than a fleeting illusion.

"Let's go to the Pensieve. I'll show you the memory."

Carina took Harry's hand and stepped into the memory of that day. It was the moment after Voldemort had been defeated—when everyone was still drunk on the joy of victory. Carina looked at the celebrating crowd, then flinched when she spotted Snape's body crumpled on the floor. It had been a fierce battle, one that had claimed its sacrifices…

Dumbledore flicked his wand, and Snape's body rose gently into the air. He summoned a white sheet and drew it over the face, then extended his hand to Harry, who was sitting on the floor.

At this point, Dumbledore was still very much alive, still casting spells. Then who had killed him? Carina was puzzling over this when—

"*Avada Kedavra.*"

Carina's face went rigid.

She wasn't alone. Everyone in that room within the memory gaped in horror. Even Mad-Eye Moody momentarily lost his grip.

Harry Potter killing Dumbledore? How? Why? The impossible had happened.

All the while, Harry Potter's eyes blazed red as he broke into wild laughter.

"Ha—hahaha! How does it feel, Dumbledore! How does it feel to be killed by the very student you cherished so dearly? Has it knocked some sense into you at last?"

A wicked, contemptible voice poured from Harry's lips. After laughing for a long moment, he looked down at the fallen Dumbledore and scratched his head with an air of mock regret.

"Oh dear. The dead can't speak, can they? What a shame. I did so want to hear the words from his own mouth."

Molly, Moody, Narcissa, Lupin—and Hermione and Ron too. They all stood gaping, utterly at a loss before this transformed Harry. They couldn't process what was happening, couldn't grasp what had occurred.

"Harry!"

Hermione let out a cry of horror.

"Wh—what have you done? You killed Professor Dumbledore—? And with—with an Unforgivable—"

"Hermione. No."

Ron pulled her back, fumbling for his wand. Clumsy as the motion was, he was the first to level it at Harry, and he muttered with a grave expression:

"That's not Harry… Something's different. Someone's controlling him!"

"Controlling? No, Ronald."

Harry smirked and shrugged his shoulders.

"I am Harry Potter. And simultaneously, I am Lord Voldemort. There's no need to distinguish between the two—I have become one with him. Hmm, though I suppose that's confusing. For now, would you call me Riddle? I ought to have some way of defining myself."

"Tom Riddle—! What dark method did you use! How did you take over Harry's body?"

Moody, having pieced together the situation, shouted as he aimed at him.

Harry—no, Riddle—showed not a flicker of tension before the greatest Auror in living memory. He regarded him with nothing more than contempt, as one might regard an insect.

Riddle strolled at his leisure and explained.

"It wasn't exactly something I intended. You wouldn't have known, but… Harry Potter was simply my Horcrux."

Hermione's mouth fell open.

"A—a Horcrux? But you can't use a Horcrux on a living creature—"

"You remember that? Hermione, as expected. You never forget what you've been taught. Though you lack the creativity to devise anything yourself—and there's an error in what you said. It isn't that you can't; it's that the side effects are severe. When you inscribe a soul into a living being, it becomes corrupted by the influence of the soul already residing there."

Like me, for instance.

Riddle possessed Voldemort's power and knowledge, and he twisted Harry's face into a grin as he laughed with evident delight.

"When I set out to create my final Horcrux, I decided to use my destined adversary's life as the material. A Horcrux forged through a meaningful kill would be all the more valuable! By killing Harry Potter—the adversary the prophecy had appointed—I would make my eternity and immortality all the more unassailable! So I completed my preparations for the Horcrux and paid a visit to their home. Ah, or should I say my home? The irony."

Riddle went on with a snicker.

"But that woman—the mother, or whatever she was. Her tiresome counter-charm caused the curse to rebound. Thanks to that, I died—and in that moment, the Horcrux magic activated. Entirely against my intentions, a fragment of my soul latched onto him. Quite the irony, isn't it? My destined adversary becoming my spare life."

Harry Potter as a Horcrux. That meant Voldemort could not be killed before Harry died.

What a cruel irony. Harry and Voldemort were supposed to be adversaries appointed by prophecy—yet one of them was holding the other's life in his hands.

"Harry was a Horcrux… and you already knew?"

"Isn't that obvious? I am the greatest wizard in this world when it comes to Horcruxes. When some child occasionally slips into my mind and peeks around, how could I possibly fail to notice? Surely you don't think I was incapable of Occlumency and simply couldn't block his intrusions? Ha. How little you must have thought of me."

It was then. Moody, whom Harry had designated as an enemy, made a sudden move—he raised his prosthetic leg and brought it down in a stamp, attempting to unleash one of his signature shockwaves. In the instant before the wooden leg met the hard floorboards, Moody went skidding and crashed to the ground with a tremendous clatter. The lower portion of his prosthetic had simply ceased to exist—Riddle had vanished it in that split second.

Moody, sprawled on the floor, snapped his head up—but Riddle flicked his wand, and Moody's eye patch transformed into rope that slammed him face-first into the ground.

"Now, now, Professor. Do try to pick your moment before you barge in."

Moody ground his teeth.

"Riddle—! You were aiming for this from the start!"

"Not particularly. Taking this body offered me no advantage, so I was simply going to kill him."
The wand-work was that of a Dark Lord, but the tone and manner were pure Harry. And so Ron, Hermione, Molly, and Narcissa alike found it impossible to act with any decisiveness.

In truth, even without their hesitation, they could not have brought Riddle down. Riddle now possessed power approaching Voldemort's.

From a foundation of overwhelming force and the ease that came with it, Riddle replied with casual indifference.

"I told you. When a fragment of my soul is inscribed into a living being, it is warped by the soul already present. In a beast, the distortion is relatively minor—but in a human, it becomes so disfigured that the original form is nearly unrecognizable. Look at me now. My personality, my power, my appearance—none of it is the Lord Voldemort of old. Lord Voldemort did not speak with such levity."

Riddle was far too garrulous and cheerful to be called Lord Voldemort. His soul had lived alongside Harry Potter for nearly sixteen years, and in doing so, had come to resemble him.

Because the last remaining soul fragment had undergone that transformation, Riddle had become something distinct from both Voldemort and Harry—something else entirely.

"Even so, a soul is a soul—and when Lord Voldemort died, the remnants of his scattered memories and power were drawn into the last surviving fragment. Thanks to that, I emerged into the world with my full strength intact! A rather disagreeable body, admittedly… but I'm not in a bad mood. I get to see the terror on all your faces! Hahaha!"

The moment those words ended, Lupin roared and raised his wand. Molly and Narcissa joined in, firing off spells. Dozens of flashing streaks of light hurtled toward Riddle.

Riddle met them with a snort and a flick of his wand.

With a single, effortless motion, every spell flying toward Harry was deflected in all directions. Red and violet beams scattered wide, striking the floor and ceiling. Riddle flicked his wand once more, and everyone's wands flew into the air as if by prior arrangement, sticking fast to the ceiling. Simultaneously, an invisible force bore down on them and drove them to their knees.

Beyond disarmament—complete and total incapacitation. Riddle wielded his power with evident pleasure. Those whose limbs were wrenched at wrong angles screamed in pain.

Ron, now empty-handed, cried out in desperation.

"Harry, snap out of it! You can't just hand your body over to something like that!"

Riddle snickered in reply.

"Haha. Do you think he can overpower me and reclaim control?"

"Yeah! You idiot—keep going the way you are and you'll lose Malfoy too! Is that fine with you?"

"Malfoy? Ah. Carina Malfoy. The one who had the audacity to betray me…"

All at once, Riddle's eyes went wide and he looked down at his own body. An indescribable sensation stirred in his chest. Like a limb he had never used suddenly being attached—something cumbersome had sprouted in his emotions, moving him against his will.

"Oh, the body is responding. How curious. Is this what they call love? Fascinating… truly fascinating…"

The unfamiliar sensation was a mystery unto itself, and a source of curiosity. Riddle's eyes gleamed.

Dumbledore had spoken of love time and again. But the original soul of Voldemort had contained no such thing, and so it had always remained an unknown territory. Yet this new soul fragment—suppressed and warped by Harry's soul—had come to know something it was never meant to know.

In a twisted, distorted direction.

His mouth split into a grin that seemed to tear his face apart. It was nothing like Harry's usual expression—a smile of pure, unadulterated cruelty.

"Very well. As a final tribute to Harry Potter, I shall take that woman in this body. I'll sever the tendons in her hands and feet, suppress her mind, and fashion her into a doll made only for you—a decoration for your room. I'll dress her in a beautiful gown, and cast a preservation charm on her radiant hair. I'll stop time around her and preserve her in eternity. She will sit inside an infinite casket and whisper her love to you forever…"

That was the moment. Riddle suddenly clutched his own face. It was not his will—his left hand had moved on its own, reaching up to claw at his own eyes. Riddle jerked his head back sharply. His glasses shattered, shards of glass biting into his hand. Blood welled up and stained one eye red. Through that blood-reddened eye, Harry clenched his teeth and muttered:

"Don't be ridiculous…"

"Harry!"

Ron called out with relief. Harry bit his lip hard enough to draw blood, forcing out each word one by one.

"Carina's charm is in her bite… you don't know the first thing about her…"

But that alone seemed to have pushed Harry to his limit. The moment his voice faded, Riddle's laughter rang out once more.

"Haha! And what exactly can you do about it? You're nothing but a child who hasn't even come of age. All you can do is watch from in there! With Dumbledore dead, there is no one left who can stop me!"

"Not quite yet, I'm afraid."

The voice was Dumbledore's—and it resonated from his own body. Riddle's eyes went wide.

Dumbledore, who had unmistakably been struck by the Killing Curse and killed, was slowly rising, as though time itself were rewinding.

He had been slow to notice, but at some point, the song of a phoenix had begun to fill the air. Fawkes, wreathed in flame, came soaring across the room. The phoenix, blazing red, seemed to breathe strength and courage into the hearts of everyone present.

Fawkes alighted on Dumbledore's shoulder. In that instant, where they touched, a sacred flame began to burn.

Riddle recoiled in shock.

"Dumbledore! How—!"

"Well done, Harry. Hold on."

The phoenix was burning. The final blaze it showed when preparing for death. The flame of life enveloped Dumbledore together with the phoenix.

Dumbledore raised his shoulders. The swirling fire followed his movement.

The island shuddered. From deep beneath this earth, a tremendous surge of magical power erupted upward. A great mystery that the world could scarcely contain—an old man channeled it into his wand and wielded it freely.

The one who had drawn the curtain on three eras.

The one person that man feared above all others.

Albus Dumbledore had the power to do exactly that.

The manor, caught in the crossfire without warning, let out a wail. Its very axis twisted, unable to contain the magic that all the world should have knelt before.

An ancient magic—nameless, and unknowable.

Struck one person directly.

"This is—impossible—!"

"Stay inside, Riddle."

Dumbledore pointed his wand at Riddle—at Harry.

A silver force gathered along the walls, flowing across the floor, converging on its center: Harry. In one concentrated instant, the silver force struck him full on.

Harry's body was hurled upward and came crashing down. An immense power that could have upended the world entire grazed past Harry—and then Harry lay sprawled across the floorboards, motionless.

Ron cried out.

"Harry's dead! Professor Dumbledore got his revenge!"

"Ah… not dead yet…"

Harry staggered to his feet. Ron started toward him, then stopped short, eyeing him with wariness.

"What spell did I use on Malfoy in second year?"

"Slugs… you prat…"
"Hmm. Welcome back, mate."

Harry took Ron's hand and stood. Ron flinched briefly at the gesture—a sense of déjà vu—but this time Harry cast no magic.

Harry steadied himself and looked toward Dumbledore. Dumbledore, who had just unleashed that extraordinary magic, looked profoundly exhausted. The flames wreathing Fawkes had already burned halfway down, hovering now around his chest.

"Professor…?"

"Yes. An explanation is needed… one that everyone can understand…"

Dumbledore conjured a chair with his wand and lowered himself into it with effort. His face was not so much weary as simply drained of any vitality.

Harry asked haltingly.

"You were clearly struck by the Killing Curse—by Riddle—"

"An old wizard tends to keep a secret or two. He was not the only one who prepared for death… I shared my soul with Fawkes, and I am able to draw on a portion of Fawkes's power. One of an old man's many tricks."

"Then you've been resurrected, Professor? Like a Horcrux?"

Dumbledore shook his head with a gentle smile.

"No, this is not resurrection. It is a deferral of death. I am already dead. But just as a phoenix blazes brightest in the moment before it dies, I am stretching out that brief final instant. For now I can move and speak—but when Fawkes's flame is spent, death will come for this body. A true death, from which there is no return."

The faces around him went rigid. But Dumbledore folded his hands as a man with no particular regrets and began to speak.

"Oh, there is no need for concern. Death comes for everyone in time, and old men like me receive its visits more frequently. You need feel no guilt, Harry. If anything, it is I who owe you an apology."

"An apology? I'm the one who killed you—"

"No—Riddle did. And it is my fault as well… Harry. I already knew that you might be a Horcrux."

Harry stared at him, disbelief written across his face.

"You… knew?"

"Yes. As he said—sharing a field of vision across distance is difficult to explain without a sameness of soul. I formed that hypothesis from that point onward. That perhaps he… in attempting to create a Horcrux and failing, had made you an imperfect one."

The first thing that came to Harry was a sense of betrayal.

Dumbledore had known. Voldemort had known. The only one who hadn't known was Harry.

Then regardless of how this fight ended, Harry would—

"Forgive me. It was all due to my own inadequacy. I did not trust you… When I knew you were his Horcrux, I thought that if you learned the truth… you might betray us."

"Betray you? How?"

"Rather than take your own life to kill him, I thought you might instead—go over to his side and beg for your life. Because the greatest thing driving you was a future with the people you loved."

Dumbledore confessed with quiet sorrow.

"That is a fierce will to live… but it can also become a desperate clinging to survival by any means necessary. And your lover, moreover, had been one of Voldemort's most devoted. So I concealed the truth and placed you in the most dangerous position on the battlefield… Even if you were to die, I reasoned that a death as a sacrifice in desperate resistance was preferable to becoming Voldemort's puppet."

His confession contained a terrible truth.

Both Dumbledore and Voldemort had wanted Harry dead. Voldemort had raised his wand to eliminate a failed Horcrux; Dumbledore had sent Harry before Voldemort to eliminate that same failed Horcrux. The leaders of both sides had wanted Harry's death, and Harry had been dancing uselessly between them.

It was simply that the sacrificial lamb had turned around and killed the wolf.

"Severus's choice was beyond my expectations… I never imagined he would sacrifice himself for you, and that you would use that as a foothold to defeat Voldemort. How could I have known. That Severus's noble choice would bring about such a result… No, I cannot diminish it that way. In the end, his wish to keep you alive was fulfilled."

But his sacrifice, too, had been rendered meaningless. Riddle's soul resided within Harry's body—so even with Voldemort defeated, Harry's soul would have remained crushed and unable to emerge.

Harry's death had been ordained from the beginning. Whether he defeated Voldemort, or Voldemort defeated him—either way, only a desperate future awaited Harry.

Hermione, who had grasped the implication a moment too late, said with a face drained of color:

"Wait—then the reason you deliberately sent Harry ahead and entered from the ground floor yourself was all to buy time until Harry could die—"

"I'm sure you have many questions, Miss Granger. But let me finish. I don't have much time left."

Dumbledore gestured toward his shoulder. Fawkes's flame had nearly burned away entirely. When the fire climbing to his collar consumed his head, the life Dumbledore had barely held onto would end as well. Fawkes was now a candle flame. When there was nothing left to burn, even the faint light lingering in Dumbledore's eyes would be extinguished.

With a kind face, Dumbledore delivered a cruel truth.

"I am sorry, Harry. For placing such a harsh fate in your hands. But I will not ask for forgiveness. I did only my best—and that remains true even now. After being struck by the Killing Curse, I used magic at the cost of my fading life to strengthen the protective charm your mother placed on you. Thanks to the protection charm bound in your blood, Riddle will not be able to emerge until you come of age. And so, Harry."

Before your birthday comes next year.

Take your own life.

The flames surrounding Fawkes blazed brighter still. The world spun. In one moment the fire vanished, and Dumbledore's head dropped. Fawkes, who should have lifted his head from the ashes, was nowhere to be seen—gone somewhere unknown.

The screams of those who had glimpsed this wretched truth rang out. Several gazes converged. Harry stood still, wand in hand, before a merciless fate.

The memory—Harry's memory, laden with its desperate truth—was drawing to a close. Carina wanted it to go further. She wanted to hear the story she didn't know, wanted there to be a more hopeful prospect ahead. If it ended here, nothing but a wretched tragedy would continue. There had to be something, something remaining—

But the memory ended here. Silver light swirled, and Harry and Carina were flung back into the headmaster's office.

Only silver instruments that had lost their master and a dust-covered perch were there to greet them. But none of it could help Carina, or Harry. All of it was useless.

The Dumbledore who would have sat here and offered counsel of one kind or another was gone now. No—even if he had been here, she could not have relied on him. Dumbledore had, in the end, intended to sacrifice Harry.

Carina looked at the boy beside her with trembling eyes. The boy who had abruptly become a walking Azkaban, a Riddle's soul sealed within him, smiled with serene composure and whispered:

"Not a pleasant story, is it?"

Liar. It was a pitch-dark story with not a single exit in sight. The news she had woken to was so utterly hopeless that Carina couldn't find a single word to say.

That there was no way to deal with the soul trapped inside—that the prison itself had to be thrown into the furnace. That the result of all that was the death of the boy who had fought at the very front.

But… if that wasn't done, Riddle's soul would break free and plunge the world into darkness. There was no one left to stop him now…

"Still—at the very least, you have no right to say anything to me, Carina. You did something similar, didn't you? Drinking poison in front of me. Do you have any idea how frightened I was? I thought, when you came back, I was going to give you a proper scolding."

To kill Riddle, Harry had to take his own life. It was the exact inverse of when Carina had drunk the poison herself.

The one difference, if there was one, was that Carina had at least had a way out—however underhanded. And there had been powerful wizards to help them.

"Now, I suppose that's off the table too. Let's call it even."

Harry had to face this alone, without anyone's help.

How could the world be so cruel. If there was someone who had written a story like this, that person was truly a heartless, pitiless wretch.

The despair was so complete that even tears wouldn't come. Carina simply wanted to sink to the floor.

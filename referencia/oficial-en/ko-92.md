Not every Slytherin was connected to the Death Eaters. There were half-bloods in Slytherin, and many purebloods who opposed the Death Eaters outright; others privately sympathized with certain ideals but recoiled from the more radical methods.

Yet the image of Slytherin as 'Voldemort's followers' had taken hold because those voices were the loudest and most extreme—so much so that even other Slytherins kept their distance from them.

Carina brought Draco with her to the girls' dormitory. Ordinarily, boys were not permitted inside, but an exception existed for those carried in—as Carina was doing now.

"Sleep with your sister tonight."

"Ugh, it's too cramped."

"You know as well as I do. Being alone right now is dangerous."

Voldemort's death had been too sudden, too abrupt—more than the Death Eaters could simply accept in defeat. Not all of them had been present at the manor, and the traitors embedded within the Ministry still lingered, watching and waiting.

To them, the Malfoy family were worse traitors than the Weasleys.

Carina had played a decisive role in Voldemort's downfall, and Lucius Malfoy had walked free through a plea arrangement. Once Voldemort's greatest patrons and most devoted followers, they were now the ones who had to run.

Carina turned over how to make Draco understand this. Then Draco muttered, his face drawn with misery.

"…It's because we're traitors, isn't it?"

Whether that was fortunate or not, she couldn't say. Carina didn't need to work at making him understand. The perceptive child already grasped the shape of things, at least in broad strokes.

"I heard it from Bennet. That we betrayed Him—that it's our fault He's gone…. Is that true? Are we the bad ones?"

"No, Draco…."

A wave of sadness moved through her, and Carina pulled him close. She held him tightly—sorry that her little brother had been swept up in the affairs of adults, and wanting to steady him against the fear that must be gnawing at him in all of this.

"Ugh, you're hot. You smell. Stop it."

Draco squirmed. Even now, Carina found herself weighing whether to smack him.

She got him washed and tucked into bed. Having a boy sleep in the girls' dormitory was the sort of thing that invited sideways looks, but fortunately the Slytherin girls who shared her room were considerate enough. Daphne Greengrass and Tracey Davis, both in her year, had never liked the Death Eaters to begin with, and so they didn't think particularly ill of Carina for having helped bring about their master's fall.

Of course, that same reason was precisely why they had never been close to Carina either—but right now, that degree of distance suited her just fine.

If anything….

Carina looked toward Crabbe and Goyle with a quiet sadness. They were huddled together, whispering, and when their eyes met hers, they snapped their heads away.

It seemed it would take quite some time before she could sit down and talk with them calmly again.

Carina steadied herself and settled Draco to sleep. He had been grumbling endlessly about the bed design being embarrassing and the mattress being too narrow, but before long he had dropped off completely, dead to the world. As it is with children—terrors when awake, angels the moment they fall asleep. Carina looked down at Draco breathing softly in his sleep.

It couldn't be helped.

She had needed to save her mother. Her father. And in doing so, she had ended up betraying Crabbe and Goyle.

She felt sorry for it, and she regretted the pain it caused—but she did not regret the choice itself. It had been the only option at the time.

Still. If Carina ever found herself with the means to do more—after her family, the next people she would help would be them.

She didn't know what power she had left. But she made that promise to herself.

Whatever the circumstances, there comes a moment when a person must put food into their stomach. Wizards, too, had to burn fuel to keep their bodies moving.

Carina brought Draco down to the Great Hall. She moved through the corridors, lightly ignoring the stares that followed them, and found a corner table to settle at. Voices aimed at the Malfoys carried across the wide expanse of the hall.

Traitor. A girl who gave everything to Harry Potter. A spy all along. None of it was pleasant to hear. Carina angled her body to shield Draco and got on with breakfast.

The attention and the contempt alike were hers to bear. Slytherin regarded her as a traitor; those outside Slytherin watched her with cool, unreadable expressions. Her betrayal had been too secretive, too sudden—she had earned no sympathy from either side.

The secrecy was what had made it possible to defeat Voldemort, so perhaps that was something to be grateful for. Carina spooned bell pepper onto Draco's plate and let her thoughts drift. Whatever those stares meant, right now her task was to feed her brother breakfast and think about what came next.

That was when it happened.

"Make way—Gryffindor's hero coming through!"

"Woo-hoo! Do you lot know you're looking at the Chosen One!"

Harry Potter's entrance was anything but quiet.

Gryffindors flanked him on either side like an honour guard. Students at the tables whistled at the story's hero—the one who had defeated the Dark Lord. Children from the other houses cheered and called his name; some pressed their hands together and gazed at him with open admiration. If they'd had handkerchiefs, they might well have waved them.

That Harry had fought Voldemort was no secret. There had been far too many witnesses that day. Members of the Order of the Phoenix, the Aurors who had supported Dumbledore, even captured Death Eaters—all had given similar accounts. The Minister for Magic and Mad-Eye Moody alike had spoken openly of Harry's heroic deeds, and the Daily Prophet had run the story of that day, based on those testimonies, in every edition since.

The result was that Harry had become, without question, a hero. A true hero who had freed the world from darkness before he was even of age. Everyone was singing his praises.

Ron and Hermione, who knew a somewhat deeper truth, wore expressions that were rather less comfortable.

"Oh, hold on."

In the midst of all that adulation, Harry turned with perfect ease toward the Slytherin table. Dozens of eyes tracked his unhurried steps. Trailing no fewer than a hundred pairs of gazes behind him, Harry walked in a straight line toward Carina.

Carina happened to be in the middle of taking a small bite of jam-spread toast. Harry came up beside her without ceremony.

"Good morning, Carina. What are you eating?"

It was a pointless question. Her plate showed the toast disappearing along the line of her bite marks, and a faint smear of red jam lingered at the corner of her lips. Anyone with half a brain could deduce what she'd been eating.

Carina looked up at him with an expression that said exactly that.

"Hard to say. Open up, Carina."

Harry chose to be an idiot. He bent down toward her from above.

The kiss looked less like an impulse and more like a daily habit—as routine as lifting a spoon to stir soup, something repeated so often it had stopped requiring thought. Even the way he pressed past the slight parting of her lips carried no hesitation, as though he were stepping through his own front door.

Carina startled and drew back slightly, but Harry followed with an uncanny precision that closed the distance instantly. Something soft met something soft. The uninvited intruder moved like a thief, stealing the taste of her.

Mouths fell open across the hall. The only person who missed the entire thing was Draco, who happened to be bent over his plate in fierce concentration, picking out vegetables.

Draco finished removing every last piece of greenery and announced his triumph with great satisfaction.

"Ha! Did you really think I'd just eat this?"

When Draco turned to look at Carina, Harry had already pulled back and was savouring the aftertaste with quiet consideration. He smiled, bright and unbothered.

"Strawberry jam. Good choice."
He left it at that and moved on toward the front of the hall. Draco watched him go with a baffled expression, then looked up at Carina.

"What was that? Did he come all the way over here for jam?"

"Apparently."

Carina dabbed her mouth with a napkin, perfectly composed.

"Draco. Shall we step outside for a moment?"

"Ugh, why."

She coaxed her reluctant brother along, and as she did, she murmured toward the retreating figure of Harry in the distance.

"It's about to get… a little loud in here."

Having made his cowardly hit-and-run, Harry stepped straight up onto the dais. He already had everyone's attention. The moment he moved to the front, the entire Great Hall turned to watch.

Harry cleared his throat lightly.

"Everyone, I have a confession to make. It concerns the secret of that day—the day we defeated him. I imagine you're all curious. How did he disappear? And what happened afterward? I'll tell you now."

It was exactly what everyone had been wondering. They'd read the newspaper accounts over and over, but hearing it directly from the person who had lived it—surely that would be more vivid, more real. Gryffindor led the cheering; other students joined in. Some were already poised to applaud.

With the crowd's full attention secured, Harry spread his arms wide and declared:

"I am Voldemort."

Silence fell.

The students stared, unable to process what Harry had just said, hands half-raised, blinking. A few, apparently deciding this was a bad joke, produced uncertain smiles.

Harry continued as though he had anticipated every one of those reactions.

"Voldemort is dead, but he has not disappeared. Just as he did sixteen years ago! As he died, Voldemort made one last desperate attempt to seize my body. He burrowed into my soul, intending to use it as a vessel for his resurrection. Thanks to Professor Dumbledore's swift intervention, I was fortunate enough to regain my senses—but Voldemort is still inside me, biding his time and waiting for his moment."

The Great Hall erupted.

He Who Must Not Be Named. Dead, they said—but his name still carried fear. And here was Harry Potter not only saying that name aloud, but confessing that Voldemort was living inside him.

Those who had already let their guard down dismissed it as a joke, or looked away with a disbelieving shake of the head.

Harry paid them no attention. It didn't matter how they chose to receive it.

"Right. I think that's clear enough."

Harry fixed his gaze on the Slytherin table. Several students flinched under the cool weight of it.

Voldemort living inside him—under ordinary circumstances, even Slytherin would have laughed that off. But some of them had witnessed it firsthand, and they were frightened. The sheer power Harry Potter had displayed. It had been enough to bring Voldemort immediately to mind. And now that power resided in Harry Potter. How terrifying was that.

"From this point forward, anyone who calls Carina Malfoy a traitor—or anything else along those lines—will be executed. Voldemort and Harry Potter will come for you together. This has been agreed upon with the Voldemort currently residing inside me. So keep your heads down and stay out of trouble."

"Harry! What on earth do you think you're doing!"

Professor Slughorn lurched to his feet, his face drained of colour. He rounded on Harry on behalf of the staff.

"We had all agreed to keep this quiet! If you say it now—! The other students will be terrified of you!"

Harry was everyone's hero. He had faced He Who Must Not Be Named and ultimately taken his life. No one could deny what Harry had endured and sacrificed—even if, for a brief time, his body had been taken from him and used to kill Dumbledore.

And yet Harry was fated to take his own life before the thirty-first of July of next year.

That was why the adults—the Minister for Magic among them—had done everything in their power to cast Harry as a great warrior and a noble sacrifice. It was a kind of consideration: ensuring that whatever time he had left would be spent as a celebrated hero.

But.

"This is what I've decided."

"What!"

That consideration was simply another name for the sacrifice they had imposed on him. The act of making him a hero in advance, on the assumption that he would, of course, die.

Harry, however, had already bitten into the forbidden fruit.

He was not a hero. He had no intention of dying for anyone. The boy who had learned selfishness had decided to live for himself.

"Right! In any case, as the person closest to Voldemort on this earth, I hereby declare on behalf of every Voldemort in existence: just as you have freely used the name Harry Potter, feel free to use the name Lord Voldemort just as freely. Voldemort, Voldie, Lord Vol, Harry 'Voldemort' Potter—whatever you like. Use them all!"

Having unleashed that particular storm, Harry walked out of the Great Hall without a backward glance.

Ron and Hermione exhaled in unison. Students who had heard Harry's words were already converging on them, demanding to know if it was true.

Ron found himself the sudden centre of attention—which was not, in any form, what he had wanted. He complained with mournful eyes.

"That git dumps all the inconvenient work on us. Hermione—honestly, don't you think we're entitled to skip the wedding gift when Harry gets married?"

"Is that really what matters right now, Ron?"

Hermione whispered, her voice unsteady.

"I still don't know…. Of course I don't want Harry to die, but if things go the way Harry's planning now…."

Riddle would resurface, and the fragile peace they had barely managed to win would shatter. Ron understood what she wasn't saying without needing to hear it.

It was a very Hermione response. Unlike Ron.

Ron, who had coasted through school without much forward planning, didn't share her habit of worrying in advance.

Neither Ron nor Hermione wanted Harry to die. Even if someone demanded Harry's death for the sake of the future, neither of them could ever actually carry it out. How could you kill a friend you'd lived alongside for six years?

Well then. Was there any point in forcing yourself to try to do something you couldn't do and didn't want to do?

Ron decided to make his peace with it and simply cheer Harry on.

"Ron. Is what he just said… true?"

Lavender Brown came to him with her hands clasped anxiously, asking the question. Ron nodded without hesitation.

"Yeah. He Who Must Not Be Named…."

He let the phrase trail off for a moment, then changed the name he used.
"…Lord Voldemort has the ability to survive by hiding inside another living creature. This time, that creature is Harry."

Fear of Voldemort was fear of Harry now. Ron wasn't afraid of Harry, and he had no intention of starting.

Saying the name turned out to be easier than he'd expected.

Carina sent Draco back inside the Slytherin common room and let out a short breath, then looked behind her. As she had expected, Harry was following.

She turned with unhurried elegance, folded her arms, and spoke.

"Kissing me wasn't part of the plan."

Harry shrugged.

"If you didn't like it, I won't do it again."

"When did I say that? Don't go making assumptions to dodge my questions, Harry."

Which was to say: the kiss hadn't been entirely unwelcome. As always, this particular person managed to communicate far more than the surface of her words.

Harry moved naturally toward her and drew her into his arms. Carina didn't resist. If anything, she seemed to welcome it—she unfolded her arms and opened them slightly. She pressed her face against his chest and spoke.

"That was too soon."

"What was?"

"…Emphasising your relationship with me. It invites suspicion about your motives."

Carina lifted her head. Those silver-grey eyes, which usually surveyed the world from a position of cool detachment, were now looking up at Harry filled with worry.

"Harry, what you needed to do today was come forward and tell the truth. I'm a footnote. The purpose was to inform everyone of what really happened—and to warn the remaining Death Eaters."

"That's a lie, though."

"If a lie can make the other side lower their guard, I'll tell it a hundred times over."

Beneath the measured tone, concern and something warmer bled through—love for Harry, unmistakable. Dozens of plans made and discarded, all in service of keeping him alive.

Carina turned that same voice on Harry now, with a note of reproach for having disrupted those plans.

"Our opponents are Dumbledore's will and the Ministry of Magic. The most foolish institution in the world is hoping you'll kill yourself. They seem to have relaxed their vigilance because they expect you to end your own life before your birthday next year—but…."

Tap. Her forehead knocked lightly against his chest again. The sound that followed was somewhere between a complaint and a lament, rising from where her face was pressed against him and carrying all the way to his ear.

"If they have even the slightest doubt, they'll move to watch you. Or they might try to deal with you before that. Assassination, an ambush—with Dumbledore gone, they'll be desperate, and there's no telling what methods they'll use. So please, Harry—right now, your safety matters more than I do."

Harry silenced her by covering her mouth—not with his hand, but by tilting her chin up and pressing his lips to hers again. Whatever she had been about to say dissolved, swallowed before it could take shape.

Carina no longer resisted when Harry kissed her. She didn't seem to feel embarrassment or reluctance—she closed her eyes naturally and tilted her head just slightly. When his tongue tapped lightly at her lips, she opened to him as though welcoming someone home.

No words were needed. The raw, unformed feeling that existed before language passed between them directly. Breath, a soft sound, the quiet press of warmth—all of it carried through the body and into the other person.

When they finally parted, Harry lifted his head. Carina's flushed face drew slowly away from his.

"Ha—"

They say comedy is distance and tragedy is proximity. That was wrong. What feels tragic up close only looks absurd from far away. The truly good things only became better the closer you were.

Quickened breath. Lips still slightly damp. Eyes that seemed to want more, cheeks touched with the faintest colour. Harry could not begin to understand the version of himself who had once considered ending his life while this was in front of him.

"Why…?"

Carina had read the refusal in the kiss and asked. Harry brushed her hair back from her face and murmured.

"What if something happened to you? Like that Pansy—what if someone with a grudge tried to curse you?"

"They're stupid, but they don't do things that cost them. They're isolated right now. In the middle of enemy territory, in Hogwarts—they won't attack me with a spell."

"When I first walked into the Slytherin common room, someone was about to do exactly that."

"That was because you appeared out of nowhere and everyone got worked up…. But ignore things like that. There are priorities right now."

"Priorities? How many things could possibly matter more than you?"

"But—"

"You're the one who told me to find a way to stay alive and stay by your side, Carina."

He kissed her briefly again, and spoke against her ear.

"For me to survive, I need you here with me. Whole."

Carina dropped her gaze. He had learned long ago that this was how she showed embarrassment. She used to hide it from him—but now even her quieter moments belonged to him.

She fidgeted slightly, as though something was making her uncomfortable. Harry waited with patient calm, and at last Carina looked up with the expression of someone who had made a firm decision.

"Come to our house over the Easter holiday."

The unexpected invitation made Harry's eyes widen. For a moment her words echoed in his ears, and a rather vivid imagination briefly ran away with him.

Honestly, Harry had been exercising considerable restraint. But he was currently sharing a body with Riddle. Spending a night with Carina and whispering things to her in this state—that wasn't something he was willing to do, and so he had held himself back through any number of opportunities.

But Harry was still a man. If the invitation was this explicit, there was no reason to resist. No reason at all.

Harry steadied his racing pulse and waited for whatever her lips would say next.

"I know you have reservations about my father. But he'll be useful to you, I'm certain of it…."

…So that wasn't what she'd meant. Harry let his shoulders drop with quiet disappointment—then reconsidered.

Harry was currently the being who carried Voldemort within him, and also the person who had brought about Voldemort's downfall. What sort of reaction would Lucius Malfoy have, faced with someone who had been simultaneously his lifeline and his enemy? And what if that same person was now trying to take away the daughter he had raised with such care?

That could be rather entertaining.

"A meeting with the family, then? Wait a moment, Carina—I'll need to have a suit made first."

"…Pardon?"

Carina tilted her head, but Harry's mind was already filling with plans.

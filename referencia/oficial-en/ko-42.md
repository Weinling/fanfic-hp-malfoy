# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 42: Truth

Dumbledore approached slowly.

Sirius felt a particular, uncomfortable variety of guilt at the sight of him. The man he had just bound and thrown aside was Moody—almost certainly a longtime friend of Dumbledore's. Whatever grounds for suspicion had existed, the fact remained that Sirius and Harry had just attacked their mentor's old companion.

Sirius looked back and forth between Moody and Dumbledore with an awkward expression.

Contrary to his fears, Dumbledore did not reproach them.

He neither scolded them for recklessness nor frowned at the excess of force.

Dumbledore simply shook his head and raised his wand.

"Alastor. You still haven't broken that habit of yours."

Dumbledore pointed his wand at Moody. A complex spell—deceptively simple in execution, yet difficult to replicate—dissolved the bindings that had held Moody fast.

Moody came to his senses, pressed a hand to the ground, and hauled himself unsteadily upright. Sirius, standing beside him, deliberated for quite some time before offering an awkward smile and reaching down to help Moody to his feet.

"Thank you... Ever since I had one leg replaced, getting up from anything that isn't a chair has been a damned ordeal..."

Moody spoke with perfect composure, as though nothing of consequence had just occurred between them.

He gave Sirius a companionable pat on the shoulder, then turned to face Dumbledore.

"I'm sorry, Albus. I have nothing to say for myself."

Dumbledore regarded Moody with the look of a man watching a friend who had just been arrested for causing trouble.

"Alastor. I don't object to your methods as such—but this sort of conflict helps no one."

"I wouldn't say that. I was testing their mettle... and what I saw satisfied me well enough."

"They spent far too much to pass your test. Alastor. Not everyone possesses your inexhaustible vigilance."

As he spoke, Dumbledore turned his wand toward Rita Skeeter. The ropes binding her began to lengthen at every point. When the restraints had loosened sufficiently, Rita Skeeter shook herself a few times and the ropes fell away entirely.

The moment she was free, Rita Skeeter sprang to her feet and rounded on Dumbledore.

"Treating a journalist like this! Did you think banning me from Hogwarts and scheming in secret would keep it all hidden? I will expose every last thing you've concealed and make sure the whole world knows!"

"Ah, Skeeter. It seems even a journalist of your calibre couldn't hold onto both money and a sense of mission at once. The moment you lose the job, the real reporter comes back."

Dumbledore replied at his leisure.

"But I think there's a misunderstanding. The reason I issued that ban was because you were prying into private lives and calling it truth. Even a journalist has no right to reduce someone's personal affairs to mere entertainment."

"That was the truth! I simply printed what was there in the Daily Prophet!"

"Then I suppose I'm free to print a truth of my own? That you are, in fact, an unregistered Animagus, and that you've been scouring for stories in the form of a beetle?"

At the mention of that particular matter, Rita Skeeter snapped her mouth shut. It appeared that, for her, truth was only truth when it cost her nothing.

Having silenced Rita Skeeter with admirable economy, Dumbledore turned his gaze at last to Carina and Harry.

"I'm sorry, Harry—and Miss Malfoy. It was my failing that I didn't put a stop to his bad habits before this."

"Bad... habits?"

Dumbledore nodded at Carina's murmur.

"You will have heard what people say about Alastor. Regrettably, even I—his friend—cannot dismiss as mere rumour the stories of him stirring up trouble because he suspects even peace itself... He doubted his allies. He doubted himself. Wary of growing soft in quiet times, he would test those around him in the most outlandish ways. It seems you were both caught up in that particular habit of his."

"A test?"

Harry pointed at Rita Skeeter and raised his voice. Skeeter, who had been reaching for her Quick-Quotes Quill, hastily tucked her notebook back inside her robes.

"So leaving Rita Skeeter to run loose, sharing nothing with us—all of that was a test?"

"I thought of it as an education. Rita Skeeter made a far better teaching aid than any blasted textbook."

Moody replied in his gravelled voice.

"I told you, lesson after lesson, to stay constantly on your guard. But no matter how many times I said it, not one of you could keep up with even half of what I was teaching. And Harry Potter, of all people—the one someone is actively trying to kill—was too busy with his love life to pay attention..."

"How petty. You couldn't stand the sight of Harry Potter, who's supposed to be fighting Voldemort, falling in love."

Harry's tone was sharper than it had ever been.

"I had no idea someone like you would take such a keen interest in the romantic lives of children. I suppose it's people like Mad-Eye Moody who keep that sort of gossip filling the pages of the Daily Prophet."

It was hostile to the point of rudeness. Had a stranger witnessed it, they might have taken Harry and Moody for enemies. The atmosphere had turned that ugly.

Which left only Sirius, caught between them, thoroughly uncomfortable.

He had come running to Hogwarts to help Harry, and had been the one to help subdue Moody. But the man they had subdued turned out to be an ally. He needed to smooth this over—and Harry was still furious.

Sirius offered an uncertain smile and spoke to Harry.

"Harry. Mad-Eye didn't mean any harm by it—why don't we leave it there? I'm sure Professor Moody never intended to drive a wedge between you and Malfoy."

"Ah, but that's where you're wrong, Black. If the opportunity presents itself, I intend to reduce the risk wherever I can."

Moody, however, had no more interest in compromise than Harry did. He trampled the bridge of reconciliation Sirius had laid and pressed on.

"I didn't pay it much mind at first. I know how foolish children that age can be. And I know how quickly that kind of feeling burns out—like a fierce flame consuming its kindling all the faster."

Moody's gaze met Harry's. The magical eye that seemed incapable of losing a staring contest locked onto the green eyes hidden behind those round spectacles.

Moody spoke slowly.

"But tell me, Black—do you know why Potter called you here to attack me?"

"I beg your pardon?"

"I'll grant that I wasn't forthcoming. But Potter read Skeeter's article and immediately suspected me. That was the right instinct—I won't deny there was a leap of logic involved, but the conclusion was correct."

Sirius had received Harry's message and come straight here. He hadn't known the full circumstances, and so he had acted entirely on Harry's word.

Moody had then resisted violently before fleeing, which had led Sirius to assume Harry's suspicions had been correct.

But looking back, there had been more than one or two things that didn't add up.

"Did it ever occur to you to ask how I could be an impostor?"

"Well—I thought perhaps Polyjuice Potion, or the Imperius Curse."

Moody gave a derisive snort.

"Someone drinking Polyjuice and impersonating me? Think about it, Black. You'd need to brew Polyjuice in secret every single day, drink it without anyone noticing, and carry off my mannerisms so perfectly that not one person who knows me—including Albus—would suspect a thing.

Yes, perhaps it's possible. There are dark wizards with extraordinary talents.

Perhaps there exists a wizard who is a masterful potioneer, who could secretly procure months' worth of ingredients—including my hair—without detection, and who could so perfectly imitate me as to fool Albus, Karkaroff, and Barty Crouch, all of whom know my face.

But even granting all of that, verification would be simple enough. One trip to Madam Pomfrey for an antidote, and the matter's settled. Or if you'd thought to check the Marauder's Map, you'd have had your answer even faster.

The Imperius Curse is the more plausible theory—it's a conveniently wicked piece of magic. But even that isn't foolproof. Have you ever heard of an Imperius Test?"

It was one of the countermeasures Moody had mentioned on the first day of class, when he introduced the Unforgivable Curses.

A method for detecting the Imperius Curse. He had mentioned developing and disseminating it during the last war.

At the time, Carina and the other students had dismissed it as the sort of hobby only someone with Moody's level of paranoia would pursue—but Sirius, who had lived through that war, nodded as though it were common knowledge.

"I know of it. You deliberately fail a spell and establish a pattern from the effect."

"Exactly. That method provides a reasonable defence even against the Imperius Curse. Unless you're as skilled as Lucius Malfoy, it's nearly impossible to make someone replicate even the act of deliberately failing a spell. The very first time I encountered Albus, I exchanged an Imperius Test with him before I'd even shaken his hand."

Dumbledore confirmed this with a nod.

"I would have preferred otherwise, but Moody was so insistent. Being levitated upside-down and dropped on one's head is not a pleasant experience. I confess—age does not entirely eliminate one's sense of embarrassment."

Dumbledore conveyed, with characteristic lightness, that Moody had not been under the Imperius Curse.

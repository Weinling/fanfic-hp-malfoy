Diagon Alley.

The street built for wizards alone was filled with wondrous magic and mystery. A ladle stirred itself through a cauldron packed with a mud-coloured potion; each time a bubble burst, iridescent smoke drifted upward on a faint, sweet scent before dissolving into nothing.

A witch seated at the roadside yawned at length. The shops were cluttered beyond measure with magical ingredients and reagents, yet the street itself was cleaner than it had any right to be.

Today, Diagon Alley had no customers.

There were never many wizards across all of Britain, so a quiet day outside the busy season was hardly surprising—but the particular emptiness today was not a matter of imagination.

The Triwizard Tournament at Hogwarts had been drawing people away for days. Ministry employees had poured every effort into ensuring the Tournament concluded without a hitch, and a considerable number had been dispatched to Hogwarts. Beyond them, anyone with even a passing connection to the Ministry had found some excuse to attend and watch.

As a result, Diagon Alley today was quieter than anywhere else in London.

"Which is precisely why we came."

Gringotts, the bank built by goblins, was open for business as always.

An elderly goblin found himself facing a customer he hadn't seen in a very long time. He looked up with a sour expression at the sharp-featured woman standing before him.

"And so."

Lustrous black hair. Heavy-lidded eyes. A witch who had grown somewhat gaunt but still possessed a brilliant, imperious beauty stared down at the goblin with undisguised arrogance.

Bellatrix Lestrange. Imprisoned in Azkaban, she had aged only the very slightest fraction beyond her youth as she pressed the goblin with her demands.

"When can I access my vault?"

"The vault has been unused for the past seventeen years, and locating the key is taking a little time. If you would be so kind as to wait just a moment."

The elderly goblin answered with practiced courtesy. But Bellatrix Lestrange suddenly cried out.

"I am in a hurry! I need to retrieve it this very instant!"

"It will only be a moment. That said, Mrs. Lestrange—I had understood you to be confined in Azkaban."

The elderly goblin ventured the remark as a test. Bellatrix responded with a high, grating laugh.

"Kyahaha! Curious, are you? Shall I tell you? Would you like me to explain, very kindly, exactly how I escaped from that place?"

"...No. That will not be necessary."

Anyone who assumed that goblins, working only in a bank, gathered no intelligence would be making a dangerously costly mistake. Goblins were a people who employed every means available to protect themselves.

They, too, had been reading the ominous signs in their own way, drawing information from every quarter they could reach.

Where gold flows, information follows.

The goblins had obtained certain pieces of intelligence unknown to wizardkind, and so, while Bellatrix's appearance startled them, it did not entirely confound them.

"May I see your identification?"

"Don't hold it long. If you leave so much as a fingerprint on my wand, I may find myself wanting to kill you."

Bellatrix extended her wand with the same imperious, overbearing manner. The elderly goblin received it in both hands without any change of expression and examined it carefully.

Walnut wood. Dragon heartstring. Twelve and three-quarter inches.

Unmistakably the wand of Bellatrix Lestrange.

Beyond that, Bellatrix drew a necklace from inside her robes and dangled it briefly.

"If you still don't believe me, I have the Black family seal as well. But if you allow your grubby hands to touch even this, I will be taking one of those hands with me."

That cruelty, that contempt—fourteen years in Azkaban had done very little to change her.

"That will not be necessary. The Black family seal is unmistakable."

The elderly goblin bowed his head and produced a golden key. He called over a younger goblin and passed it to him.

"Mardnak. Escort Mrs. Lestrange to her vault."

"Understood. This way, if you please."

Bellatrix wheeled around. The two shaggy-haired men who had been standing behind her shuffled nervously after her.

Bellatrix and the two men followed Mardnak. When Mardnak opened the door, a long passage of rough-hewn stone appeared before them.

Before stepping through, Mardnak turned to Bellatrix.

"Mrs. Lestrange, these gentlemen are...?"

"They escaped Azkaban with me."

Bellatrix glanced at them sidelong. One man wore his long hair hanging loose over his face; the other was a shabby-looking fellow with a flat cap pulled low. For just a moment, something like affection flickered in her eyes as she looked at the shabby one.

It passed in an instant. Bellatrix gave a contemptuous sniff.

"We shared a prison, so I agreed to give them a bit of gold. The more worthless a person is, the easier they are to use as a servant..."

"Ah, I see. Will they be entering the vault with you?"

"I promised them as much gold as they could carry in both hands. Greedy creatures..."

Mardnak was seized by a cold, crawling dread.

Ancient magic had been laid upon Gringotts: any gold lifted from another's vault would grow scorching hot to the touch, and simultaneously a Gemino Curse would trigger, burying the thief beneath an avalanche of burning false gold. A thief who helped themselves to something that wasn't theirs could be drowned, crushed beneath a cascade of searing metal.

Bellatrix had planned all of this to make an example of these unsuspecting escapees.

How sadistic. How utterly merciless. Surely the heavens had simply forgotten to include compassion when they made her.
"Understood. Then I shall show you the way..."

Mardnak climbed into the front seat of the cart with a barely perceptible tremor, intending to use the excuse of driving to avoid meeting Bellatrix's eyes for as long as possible.

Bellatrix settled into the back, and the other two crammed themselves into the corners. Once everyone was seated, he set the cart in motion.

Then Bellatrix's expression eased, and she flicked her wand. A soundproofing barrier sprang up between the front and rear seats.

"How was I?"

Bellatrix—or rather, Tonks, who had taken on Bellatrix's appearance—spoke.

The shaggy dark-haired man in the back seat raised his head. Beneath the overgrown hair and concealed face was simply Sirius, who had let his hair grow out.

Sirius Black grimaced.

"Identical. Enough to bring back some very unpleasant memories."

"Don't take it out on me. I didn't exactly want to look like this either."

Tonks muttered.

Tonks, a Metamorphmagus, had taken great care in altering her appearance for today. Bellatrix and Tonks were aunt and niece, and Andromeda had kept a few photographs of her sister, so the disguise had not been especially difficult to achieve.

On top of that, Dumbledore had visited Azkaban and retrieved Bellatrix's wand from the storage room. The face, the wand—she was Bellatrix Lestrange in every particular. It was no wonder the goblins hadn't noticed.

The harder part, by far, had been mimicking that arrogant personality and overbearing manner of speech.

"Having to transform into a murderer and a Death Eater. And one who drove my mother away for marrying my father, no less. I'd just changed into such a lovely look, too..."

Tonks said this and placed a hand on her lower abdomen. The man seated on the other side gave a small start.

Lupin, who had needed no particular disguise to look thoroughly dishevelled, spoke carefully.

"T-Tonks. Are you all right?"

"Of course. My back's a little stiff, that's all."

"I'm sorry. The, er—"

"Sorry for what?"

Lupin gave another small start. Tonks laughed lightly, and a peculiar, awkward silence settled between them.

The cart descended deeper. The vaults of old families—Malfoy, Black, Lestrange—were kept in the lowest reaches. Centuries of accumulated treasure demanded vaults that were correspondingly vast and secure.

Sirius leaned back against his seat and muttered.

"Why did it have to be today, of all days—the day of the Triwizard Tournament? Everyone else is watching the Tournament right now."

"That's exactly the point."

Tonks answered.

"Everyone's eyes are fixed on Hogwarts. Apparently even a few of the goblins who work here went to watch the Tournament—Ludo Bagman made some sort of wager with them, and they wanted to see the outcome with their own eyes. So today, Diagon Alley is deserted, and there are barely any goblins left to guard Gringotts. There won't be a better opportunity than this."

"If it was this straightforward, you didn't need me at all."

Sirius kept grumbling. His real objection to the Gringotts mission was that it had kept him from watching Harry at the Triwizard Tournament.

When his complaints continued, Lupin spoke up.

"Stop complaining to Tonks, Sirius. These are Professor Dumbledore's orders. Same as yours."

"Quite right. So you're a James man after all. Meaning a lover takes precedence over a friend?"

Lupin flinched briefly, then recovered his composure.

"Professor Dumbledore is at Hogwarts. Along with the other professors, Mad-Eye Moody, and Mr. Barty Crouch from the Ministry, all keeping watch. Having you stay at Hogwarts as well, when you can move freely, would be a waste."

"Fine. I'll do my part..."

Sirius muttered. Then, with a sudden flash of mischief, he nudged Lupin.

"Did you enjoy yourself while I was away? Sounds like you made up for lost time."

"Wh-whatever do you mean."

"Whatever? Good lord. Are you going to make me say it myself?"

"Ahem!"

Lupin cleared his throat loudly.

"...It was, somewhat, an accident."

"An accident?"

A sharp jolt.

A truly formidable glare came from the front seat. Tonks was staring back at Lupin with undisguised fury. Lupin swallowed, dropped his gaze, and said:

"Tonks and I... are seeing each other."

"I knew it."

Sirius let out a low whistle.

Once that kind of atmosphere develops between two people alone in a house together, it's already over. Lupin, moreover, had no resistance whatsoever when it came to women. If both parties were willing, things would have moved very quickly indeed.

"Thank you, Remus. Now I have a perfectly good reason never to set foot in that miserable house again. To think that wretched place could become the setting for a friend's honeymoon—Father would have been absolutely furious if he were alive. Ha. That's deeply satisfying."
"Sirius!"

Lupin, who had no desire to continue this line of conversation, changed the subject swiftly.

"What about you? You ought to find someone yourself, one of these days."

"Oh ho. Changing the subject, are we?"

"I don't entirely mean it as a joke. Harry's changed a great deal since he started seeing Miss Malfoy, hasn't he? You could stand to—"

"Leave it. I'm busy enough as it is..."

Sirius trailed off, thinking of Harry and Carina.

"Besides... Harry and the Malfoy girl have broken up. When I think about that, I honestly can't make heads or tails of it..."

"What? Why?"

"That's a story for later—and you'll want to tread very carefully. Harry's on a razor's edge about it. Ask him once more and he might do something rash out of sheer anger."

At that moment, the cart lurched and entered a different section of the tunnels. Tonks, who had been watching ahead, called back in a low voice.

"Everyone stay sharp. I can see the magical waterfall Professor Dumbledore warned us about up ahead. Once we pass through it, all concealment spells will be stripped away."

Lupin and Sirius straightened and gripped their wands. Their faces held a measured tension—and beneath it, something small and bright that the years had buried but not extinguished.

Sirius bared his teeth in a grin.

"James would be gutted. Raiding Gringotts—the kind of thing that goes down in history—and we're doing it without him!"

"My thoughts exactly. There's supposed to be a dragon at the innermost level. Shall we see what on earth it's guarding?"

Even Lupin couldn't suppress the smile that escaped him.

Tonks looked at them both, allowed herself a small smile, then schooled her expression and lifted her chin.

The magical waterfall crashed down over them.

The stands packed with spectators. Cheering that rang from every direction.

In the Quidditch pitch, hedges had grown to twice Harry's height, forming a vast, towering maze. Ludo Bagman, serving as commentator, called out to Harry with eyes full of expectation.

"Hogwarts' Champion, with the highest score—Harry Potter enters the maze first! Armed with nothing but his wand, he must face the countless threats lurking within!"

Harry walked into the maze at a steady, unhurried pace.

"Can the youngest Champion claim the Triwizard Cup?"

A thoroughly pointless question. The Triwizard Cup meant nothing to Harry.

Tension, excitement—both were distant things to him now. Harry gripped his wand and walked on without hesitation.

About a week before the Tournament, Ludo Bagman had brought the Champions to the Quidditch pitch. He had helpfully explained that these hedges would grow into a maze, and that various traps would be placed throughout.

While Harry asked Bagman questions, his eyes had been tracing the pitch, drawing a single line in his mind: an imaginary line connecting the southern entrance where they would begin to the Triwizard Cup waiting in the north.

The maze filling the Quidditch pitch was enormous, and its structure was correspondingly complex. Memorising the entire layout from a brief glance would have been beyond even Hermione.

But a single line, once drawn in the mind, could be held.

Harry followed the line.

"*Reducto.*"

A mountain of stone rubble exploded outward. Several boulders came rolling toward him, but Harry transfigured them into balloons with a simple charm. He left the cluster of balloons drifting skyward and walked on.

The path toward the Cup was strewn with obstacles of this kind. A swamp inhabited by Kappas and Grindylows. A fog that stripped away all sense of direction. Piles of stone heaped high across the path.

Harry worked through each one in turn, drawing on every spell and piece of knowledge he had accumulated.

The smell of grass saturating the air was entirely artificial. Somewhere in the distance, voices seemed to carry—but they were muffled, as though blocked by an invisible wall.

From the stands above, Harry's every move would be visible. But with green-grey walls pressing in on either side, Harry had only what lay directly ahead. He walked forward with the feeling of a pet inside a cage.

Carina would be sitting somewhere up there, watching him make his way through the maze. He had no idea how she was watching. Whether her palms were damp with tension, whether her hands were clasped together in worry, or whether she was looking down at everything with that old expression of hers—faintly amused, faintly contemptuous.

"It doesn't matter."

He would have to face Voldemort regardless. Voldemort would try to kill him, and Harry, for his part, could think of no particular reason why Voldemort ought to remain alive.

But reasons to kill him? Those were more than sufficient. So he would. Without a shred of mercy.

Harry was protected by his mother's magic—an ancient enchantment running through his blood, powerful enough to prevent Voldemort from laying so much as a finger on Harry or his kin until he came of age.

His very existence disrupted the balance between Dumbledore and Voldemort.

The trump card that worked on Voldemort alone. His natural enemy. His fated adversary.

As long as Harry existed, Voldemort would be forced to shrink back. In the time Voldemort had spent in hiding, Dumbledore had already destroyed two Horcruxes and located a third. Before this day was out, his third soul fragment would be destroyed.

After that, all Voldemort would have left were the ragged remnants of a broken army—and his own wretched, diminished life.

"You'd better be ready, Carina. After Voldemort is dead... I wonder how much will be left for your side to pay."

Harry was curious.

When that day came, would the Malfoys still choose the family?

And whether his own feelings would still be what they were now.

Harry was dreaming.

It had to be a dream. Because the corridor that had appeared to him night after night for the past several months—that long, dark corridor leading to the Department of Mysteries—had appeared again.

As it always did in the dream, Harry moved slowly toward the door at the corridor's end. He reached out his hand, and the door swung open before his fingers even touched the handle. He stepped inside, carried by the sensation that everything was falling neatly into place.

Inside the Department of Mysteries, there were many doors. Each one led to a different field of research, it seemed. It should have been disorienting, but Harry chose one without a moment's hesitation and walked through.

Naturally, it was the Hall of Prophecy. The one he had been searching for all along.

The Hall of Prophecy was an extraordinary place—thousands upon thousands of glass orbs arranged in rows, their shelves rising so high they seemed to brush the night sky. A repository of every prophecy ever made, from the remotest antiquity to the present day.

The orbs were as numerous as stars, yet Harry did not let his gaze wander. He walked with absolute certainty to the ninety-seventh row.

Before a dust-covered orb, a long yellow label bore an inscription.

S. P. T to A. P. W. B. D.

Harry Potter.

His prophecy waited there, waiting for its subject to arrive.

Strange, really.

Harry had never been to the Department of Mysteries. He had no memory of ever seeing a prophecy. And yet—was fate truly a real thing? He had found his prophecy as though guided by some invisible hand. It had been almost startlingly easy...

...That can't be right. When has anything ever gone easily for you?

A voice rose from somewhere deep inside him—endlessly twisted, coiled tight with resentment.

It seemed to belong to someone who found fault with everything in the world, and who was therefore displeased by how smoothly things were going.

Harry answered it.

Don't interfere. Everything is going well.

That's exactly what's wrong, Potter. What in your life has ever gone well?

You spent ten years in that damned uncle's house. Then you found out you were a wizard. You were drunk on the idea of being special—but you were 'more' special than you bargained for. Because a murderous lunatic who wanted to rule the world had singled you out specifically to kill.

Because of that, you've nearly lost your life, put everyone around you in danger, and now you've even been jilted.

Think the world is easy? You think this rotten world is smiling at you?

Everything is going well. Harry repeated it like a parrot.

Then the voice shifted slightly.

Let me put it another way. Those people over there—doesn't it bother you? Doesn't it turn your stomach, watching them so pleased with themselves because some cobbled-together plan happened to work out by sheer luck?

It did bother him. Harry felt a sudden, visceral twist in his gut. Watching those people gloating, laughing—he couldn't bear it. He wanted to ruin those smug faces. He couldn't stop himself.

You have been miserable. And you will go on being miserable. Because even the possibility you found has been stolen from you by those wizards.

Pay them back in kind, Potter. Be true to your nature. You are a bitter, twisted, warped little child.

Harry decided to do exactly that.

The Imperius Curse broke.

"Yes. Hand it over to me, Potter..."

When he opened his eyes, Lucius Malfoy was speaking in a voice brimming with triumph. Harry heard it, looked down at the glass orb in his hand, and considered.

If not for that shameless man, Harry and Carina's relationship would have been ten times better than it was. It had started with the diary in second year—turning the school upside down—and then, as it turned out, the diary was a Horcrux, which had proceeded to tighten its grip around their own throats. It was a genuine mystery how someone like Carina had come from someone like him.

Harry wanted to smash the orb over Lucius's head, but the thought of Carina stopped him.

Instead, he hurled it at the floor.

Crash.

The orb shattered into fragments. A faint wisp of smoke seeped out between the shards.

Lucius stood blinking, staring down at the broken glass at Harry's feet with hollow, uncomprehending eyes. From his perspective, Harry hitting him over the head with it would probably have been preferable—at least then there was a chance it might not have broken.

But Harry had already done it.

The world ground to a halt, and within it, only Harry smiled.

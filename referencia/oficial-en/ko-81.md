It seemed the news of Carina attending Slughorn's party had spread throughout the entire school. That was what Carina thought.

Otherwise, Snape—who should have been the last person to hear such gossip—would never have come to find her.

"What exactly are you trying to do, Carina?"

"What am I trying to do? I'm doing my best for the mission."

"Do you think I don't know? You went to see whether you could steal something useful from Professor Slughorn again."

Carina quickly composed her expression, but Snape had already approached with the air of a man who had made up his mind. He pressed her sharply.

"Do you think a Potions master wouldn't notice the quantities of his own brews? Professor Slughorn mentioned that someone appeared to have stolen a potion he'd had simmering. I suggested it was Peeves, but the moment I heard it, I knew it was you."

"You're mistaken. There's no proof I took anything, is there? Or did you read my mind?"

"Occlumency is not infallible, Carina! Even if you can use Occlumency, it's perfectly easy to work out who the thief is! Draco told me. He said you've been slipping out somewhere every night lately. And it started from the very first day of term—the day of Professor Slughorn's class!"

That loose-lipped little brat. Carina clicked her tongue briefly and replied with cool indifference.

"Then go and tell Dumbledore. Report that Carina Malfoy has been stealing from a professor and sneaking out at night, and have me disciplined. That would settle it, wouldn't it?"

"Carina. You know that's not what I want. If you need help, you have only to ask me—so why are you setting your sights on Professor Slughorn's things? If you're caught doing something like that, it will be difficult for both of us!"

"I'm the one who'd be punished. Why would it be difficult for you?"

"Because I made an Unbreakable Vow to your mother. To protect you!"

Even Carina could not help looking at him again at that.

An Unbreakable Vow. It was a merciless magic that could never be broken—and if one party violated it, death was the immediate price. Having made that vow, Snape was bound to protect Carina if he wished to go on living.

But Carina did not let her guard down.

An Unbreakable Vow is not infallible. There was once a sorcerer-king who had distributed Unbreakable Vows freely, hoping to build an army of absolute obedience—and he was struck down by a trusted subordinate who harboured resentment at his unjust commands. No vow had been made against killing him, after all.

Another dark wizard, having learned from that example, bound his followers with vows far more detailed and specific—only to die in his own arrogance, poisoned, with no one to help him. His subordinates had simply watched. They had become puppets incapable of acting without their master's direct order.

Beyond those cases, there were countless others that illustrated the dangers of the Unbreakable Vow. A double-edged sword. A chain that bound both parties. The stronger the magic, the greater the cost—that was an old adage of the wizarding world.

And beyond all that, she had no way of knowing whether Snape's words could simply be trusted.

"I don't need your protection, Professor. So please set your mind at ease and go prepare for your next lesson."

"You have nearly been caught several times already. And you keep pushing your head further in!"

"I haven't been caught, and I won't be. And even if I were discovered, I could talk my way out of it a few times over."

"I am telling you that you don't need to! If you would only accept my help—!"

"Since I won't be accepting Professor Snape's help, I suppose I do need to manage on my own."

Carina's manner was impenetrable—not a needle's width of opening. Snape seemed to finally abandon persuasion, and when he spoke again his voice had dropped to something quieter and more deliberate.

"Don't forget the mission. If you don't kill Harry Potter or Dumbledore, you die. It's a very simple problem, isn't it? Three options, one correct answer. I believe you're a girl who can solve something this straightforward."

"If you are truly a teacher, Professor, then step back and wait for my answer."

Carina spun on her heel and ended the conversation unilaterally, striding away down the corridor. Behind her retreating figure, only Snape's grim warning followed.

"…Bear it in mind. The Dark Mark has no patience. Until you die, it will keep watching you—and it will bite…."

Carina had not refused Snape's help without reason. She still had one last resort.

Felix Felicis.

Ironically, it was the luck potion Harry had given her that had been delaying Carina's resolve.

If she asked Snape for help, she might be given a poison capable of killing someone within a week, along with an opportunity to slip it into a drink. A perfect, prepared chance to take a life—requiring nothing more than a slight tilt of a vial.

If it came to that, there would be no stopping it. She would be pushed and pushed until she killed someone almost by accident, then clamp her trembling hands together by sheer force of will and flee Hogwarts.

So she would wait. Wait until Felix Felicis offered no answer. Only then would it not be too late to go to Snape. Carina steadied herself with that thought, and measured out the moments when she might drink the Felix Felicis by the strictest of standards.

But fate, indifferent as ever, brought a moment that forced her hand.

Professor Slughorn spoke with an air of barely contained urgency.

"Terribly busy today! Sit down quickly, all of you! Now—does anyone know Golpalott's Third Law?"

Golpalott was a wizard who had devoted his entire life to magical poisons and their antidotes. He was a name that came up frequently in Potions lessons. Carina searched her memory for the law before Hermione could answer.

"Golpalott'sThirdLawstatesthattheantidoteforacompoundpoisonmustbemorethanasimplecombinationoftheantidotesforeachofitscomponentparts!"

As always, Carina's recollection came a fraction too late. Hermione had already spoken. Having long since given up on outpacing Hermione, Carina turned her attention to Slughorn's lecture. Today's lesson was on poisons and antidotes.

Poisons and antidotes. The very things Carina might need at any moment…. As she listened, a peculiar sensation crept over her. As though this lesson had been arranged specifically for her—an ominous sense of perfect, terrible fit.

"…you'll need Scarpin's Revelaspell for this. Separate the individual components of this poison, prepare an antidote for each, and then combine them. Now, everyone come up and take one of these vials! See how many component antidotes you can produce before the end of class!"

Her jaw trembled. Her palms were damp and slick. Carina walked slowly to the front, accepted the vial Slughorn held out, and returned to her seat.

Poison.

A poison made by Professor Slughorn had come into Carina's hands with startling ease. A single vial's worth—enough to kill several people. Placed in plain sight before her.

Carina shook her head as though trying to deny reality.

'No. It's probably just something trivial—stomach cramps, bruising, diarrhoea. Surely he wouldn't give students a lethal poison.'

But that was far too dismissive of the N.E.W.T. curriculum. Most of the potions they had studied could produce effects of lethal toxicity if combined incorrectly. At the advanced level, separating compound poisons and preparing antidotes was a core requirement.

With trembling hands, Carina applied Scarpin's Revelaspell and identified the components of the compound poison.

One, two, three…. Thirteen in total. Each one a straightforward poison capable of bringing a person to the brink of death—and together, a simple compound poison that would kill whoever drank it.

At that moment, the snake on Carina's wrist opened its eyes. Hidden beneath her sleeve, invisible to anyone else, but Carina could feel it stirring against her skin. The snake flicked its tongue toward the poison in the vial as though regarding a fine wine—as if urging her to consume it at once.

"Carina. What are you doing?"

A Slytherin student nudged her, and Carina came back to herself. Glancing around, she saw students dragging scales and cauldrons toward them, rushing about in search of ingredients. Carina set to work on the antidote as well, if only to appear engaged in the lesson.

Thirty minutes remained.

It was a failure—or rather, failure had been inevitable. It was impossible, at a student's level and within the time limit, to produce thirteen separate antidotes and combine them correctly using the right catalysts in the right proportions without neutralising one another.

It was not a matter of Carina's ability. Not even Hermione—nor Harry Potter, who possessed an almost preternatural instinct for Potions—had managed to produce a complete antidote.

From across the room, Harry Potter's voice carried over in complaint.

"Thirteen poisons. It takes three hours just to make the antidote for each one individually. This is physically impossible."

"It's probably for grading purposes. Stop complaining and add another ingredient."

"No, no matter what I do, I can only get nine in the time. And every additional one after that requires a staggering amount of catalyst. Honestly, at this point I'd rather just shove in a Bezoar. One universal antidote would be far more practical."

If even Harry Potter was at his wit's end, it meant that producing an antidote on the spot—for whoever drank this—was simply not possible.

Carina looked down at the half-finished antidote in her cauldron. The charred, blackened residue clinging to the bottom seemed to mirror the state of her own heart.

If the antidote had been easy to make, she would have dismissed the poison as useless and ignored it. But the Potions lesson had already proven its worth.

Lethal. Difficult to counter. And now in her hands.

The snake had already shown interest in the poison. If she left without taking it, the snake tattooed on her skin would grow suspicious. It would bite, it would press her, it would report back to its master. And the Dark Lord would show no mercy.

She had to take the poison—to remain faithful to the 'mission' of killing someone. The snake was watching her with wide-open eyes. If she didn't take it, something far worse would press its fangs to her throat.

But she didn't want it. She didn't want to take hold of a weapon. She didn't want to grip an instrument of death and agonise over whom to aim it at.

Cold sweat ran down her jaw. Her pale skin had gone beyond white—it was nearly translucent. Her spasming hands seemed incapable of holding even her wand properly. She steadied them by force of will and thought:

Perhaps now was the moment to summon her luck.

She would need an empty vial to transfer the poison, in any case. If she produced a separate empty vial and was caught in the act of decanting the poison, the consequences would go well beyond a simple detention.

So this was purely about obtaining an empty vial. Not because she hoped some stroke of luck might spare her from having to take the poison.

Deceiving herself—and the snake—Carina reached into her bag and closed her fingers around a small bottle of golden liquid. Felix Felicis was still warm, as though generating its own heat. With sweat-slicked hands she barely managed to keep hold of it, then concealed it in her sleeve and rose from her seat.

"Professor. May I be excused to use the lavatory for a moment?"

"Oh, Miss Malfoy! Be quick about it! There are only ten minutes left!"

Carina gave a brief nod and slipped out of the classroom. She reached a secluded bathroom, checked quickly in both directions, and once she was certain she was alone, drew the bottle from her sleeve.

The luck potion. This draught that Harry Potter had given her—where would it lead her? What fortune would it bring?

There was nothing to do but hope.

Carina drank the Felix Felicis in a single swallow. It was like swallowing light. A warmth that filled her completely passed down her throat and into her stomach, then began to spread through her veins to every part of her body. It was as though a small sun had entered her.

Carina felt vitality surge through her. Her mind was clear as someone waking from a deep, refreshing sleep, and the exhaustion that had threatened to bring her to her knees was gone without a trace. She felt as though she could catch every Snitch in the sky, no matter how many came at once.

Even the fear that had consumed her only moments ago seemed like nothing at all. Why had she worried so much over a problem with an obvious answer?

If she had no choice but to take it, then she would simply take it.

She breathed in deeply and counted to three in her mind. Carina walked back into the classroom and, with perfect naturalness, slipped the vial of poison into her bag and exchanged it for the bottle that had held the Felix Felicis.

Fortunately, the two vials were identical in shape. Once the faint traces of golden liquid inside were gone, no one would be able to tell what the bottle had once contained.

Carina gripped her wand. She had never successfully cast a vanishing spell—or any advanced wordless charm—before, but today she was the luckiest girl alive, and today she could do anything. She focused, levelled her wand at the bottle, and the last traces of golden liquid vanished entirely. Carina allowed herself a small, quiet smile.

"Right, time's up! Let's see what we have! Blaise. What on earth did you make?"

As Slughorn worked his way along the Slytherin table, Carina presented a clean empty vial and a cauldron of blackened residue. Slughorn narrowed his eyes.

"Hmm. Miss Malfoy? What were you attempting?"

"I couldn't see any way to produce the antidote within the time limit, so I tried casting a counter-charm directly on the poison. Something must have gone wrong—the poison simply disappeared."

It was an improvised excuse. But Slughorn stroked his moustache and examined the result with genuine interest.

"Hmmm. The thinking behind it isn't bad at all. Yes—sometimes the simplest approach is the right one. Though that method is only effective against a very limited range of components, so next time, do make sure to separate them thoroughly first."

"Thank you, Professor."

It had been a hasty fabrication, yet the assessment was unexpectedly favourable. Perhaps this particular lesson had called for ingenuity and instinct more than diligence. Carina thought as much, then turned her wand on the cauldron. It came away spotless.

After class, Carina skipped dinner entirely and went straight to the Room of Requirement. She had been thinking that with Felix Felicis still in her system, she might be able to repair the Vanishing Cabinet more easily. She passed the beautiful diadem resting on its bust, turned left past the mountain of stacked books, walked one block further, and arrived at the Vanishing Cabinet. As always, she drew her wand and attempted to repair it.

But it seemed that what was impossible remained impossible even with luck on one's side. After nearly an hour of trying every repair charm she knew, the Vanishing Cabinet remained broken.

Carina gave it a petulant kick. She was half-wondering whether some stray stroke of luck might use the impact to miraculously fix it.

"…Right. Nothing was ever going to be that easy."

Of course, no such miracle occurred. Carina was leaning against the cabinet in resignation when a voice reached her from somewhere nearby.
"Malfoy? Is that you?"

Crabbe and Goyle had arrived, it seemed. Carina had been about to go and meet them when a sudden mischievous impulse took hold—instead of showing herself, she opened the Vanishing Cabinet and hid inside. Her plan was to fling the door open and startle them when they came close enough.

It was entirely unlike Carina. But so what? Carina 'Felix' Malfoy had no patience for petty dignity. Family honour? When the family itself was on the verge of ruin, there was no point in being precious about it.

She was careful, however, to keep the cabinet door from closing completely. If it shut all the way, she would be sucked somewhere inside Hogwarts and experience firsthand exactly why it was called a Vanishing Cabinet.

"Hm? Malfoy's not here."

Crabbe's voice came from beyond the cabinet door. Carina held her breath and waited.

"Didn't you hear something from the direction of the cabinet just now? A bang, like something hitting it."

"Not sure. Maybe we were walking so heavily that the vibration knocked something over?"

"Don't be ridiculous. You're not as heavy as you were at your worst—things don't just fall over from someone walking."

"So you're saying I could have managed it at my worst?! That's awful!"

Carina had to press her sleeve over her mouth to keep from laughing. The two of them circled the area, apparently searching for her. Just as Carina was gauging the right moment to emerge, Goyle's voice—thick with grievance—resonated through the cabinet walls.

"Honestly. She tells us we absolutely have to come find her after every class. And she's not even here yet? Tch. Does she actually think we're her servants or something?"

It was impeccable timing. Carina stopped herself from stepping out. Now that Goyle had begun venting his frustrations about her, it would be deeply awkward for everyone if she opened the cabinet door and walked out.

"I'm not happy about this lately. We're already swamped with remedial lessons—why does she keep summoning us? And she won't even let us touch our wands!"

Goyle continued to grumble, completely unaware that someone was hiding in the Vanishing Cabinet. Crabbe lowered his voice to a whisper.

"W-why are you saying all this, Goyle."

"Am I wrong? Don't ask Snape for help—because she has to do it herself. Fine. But what about us? We're helping Malfoy too, aren't we? Does that count as her own effort? Why? Because we're her subordinates?"

"W-well…."

Had Carina ever asked for their opinions when she was doing something? Had she ever treated them as equals?

Carina turned the question on herself and shook her head. She couldn't even deny that she regarded Crabbe and Goyle as something close to underlings—so how must they feel? She had no right to say anything if they were unhappy.

At least this much was fortunate. That she had been able to confirm their feelings, even in this way. Perhaps this was what Felix Felicis had been guiding her toward….

But the two of them had not finished their conversation—had not finished speaking about Carina.

"Malfoy's just having a hard time. Bear with it a little longer. All right? If this goes well, we'll get that diadem too."

Crabbe did his best to soothe Goyle. Carina pressed her face to the gap in the cabinet door and watched them. Crabbe was smiling his easy, guileless smile; Goyle wore an expression of pointed indifference.

"What's the point of getting something that might be cursed? If she keeps treating us like this, I'm done. If it succeeds, all the credit will go to Malfoy. If it fails, we'll be the ones left holding it. I'm not an idiot."

Goyle was stubborn. And, beyond that, he was logical. Crabbe considered this for a moment, then pressed his hands together over his chest and spoke in a wavering voice.

"I… I don't dislike Malfoy."

"What's gotten into you all of a sudden?"

"W-well. Of course, Malfoy can be a bit much sometimes, and she drags us around however she likes… but she has her honest moments, and at the very least she tries to give us something in return."

"She gives us things because we keep flattering her and it goes to her head."

"No…. I don't think it's quite like that. Do you remember? When we started second year. I was worried because I'd put on so much weight, and you were struggling because your hair had grown out and you couldn't manage it."

Crabbe brought up the old memory in a quiet, matter-of-fact way.

"The presents Malfoy gave us that Christmas, after she'd heard us talking about it. She gave me sweets that wouldn't make me gain weight, and she gave you a hair-glossing potion and a tangle-free comb."

"I remember. And then she was absolutely insufferable about it afterwards."

"Sh-she was, but… all the same, I always thought Malfoy looked after us rather well. If she truly didn't care at all, she wouldn't keep us close, and she wouldn't have thought carefully enough to give us those gifts…."

Crabbe was half right.

When Carina provided someone with something they needed, it was not an expression of affection—it was a skill for building connections. She had given thought to the gifts, yes, but to hear that he still remembered it years later, and spoke of it with such feeling… even from Carina's perspective, it was both touching and faintly embarrassing.

"Compared to that, I don't put nearly that much thought into gifts. I just give whatever looks interesting or fun, or if I can't be bothered, something like a book or socks. Malfoy must spend a tremendous, tremendous amount of time thinking about things, even in ordinary life."

Even so. To have learned this much. To have overheard the genuine feelings of two people she had never truly known.

It was surely the work of luck.

Goyle let out a long, deep sigh and grumbled.

"…This one time only. If she keeps treating us like underlings after this, I really will cut ties with her."

"Hehe. By then things will be better, I'm sure!"

"Let's go find her. If she's been lazing about… tch, I suppose we can let her off for one day."

Crabbe laughed his best cheerful laugh and followed Goyle. By very, very good fortune, neither of them had any idea that someone was hiding inside the Vanishing Cabinet.

Slughorn had been right. The day she drank Felix Felicis had been genuinely lucky. Better still, the potion's effects had not yet fully worn off.

Carina felt a lightness she hadn't known in a long time. So much so that she didn't notice the cabinet door sliding slowly shut. By the time she realised, the thin line of light at the door's edge had been narrowing—and then, with a soft click, it was gone.

"Oh."

In that instant, Carina's body began to be pulled somewhere.

The Vanishing Cabinet—a mysterious object that, when something was placed inside and the door closed, caused that object to reappear somewhere else in Hogwarts. 'Something' included people. That was precisely why Carina had been trying to repair it.

She had never intended to conduct the experiment with her own body.

Carina felt her stomach compress. Her body spun, her hair and robes whipping in a wind from nowhere. She tumbled through a dim, boundless space—and then jammed, hard, somewhere in the middle.

"It moves perfectly well—so what exactly is broken…!"

She stopped mid-sentence as a possibility she had never considered before suddenly occurred to her.

The Vanishing Cabinet could send things. When something was placed inside and disappeared, that was why it was called a Vanishing Cabinet.

In other words, the sending function itself was working correctly.

The problem was that the destination was not the matching cabinet in Borgin and Burkes, but various random locations throughout Hogwarts.

Which meant—

Perhaps what was broken was not the cabinet itself, but this passage.

"*Reparo!*"

Carina did not hesitate. She did not stop to worry whether a misfired spell might injure her, or strand her as a ghost in the space between places.

Because Carina Malfoy was the luckiest girl alive today.

The repair charm struck the dark, boundless passage. She felt something that had been misaligned click back into place. But in the process, the walls that had been holding her fast gave way. Her body was pulled inward again—swirling like water draining from a basin, drawn steadily toward one end of the passage—

And then, with a thud, she hit the ground.

"Eek!"

"Whoa!"

A dizzying sense of weightlessness greeted her. Carina curled in on herself and squeezed her eyes shut. But by good fortune, she did not hit the floor. An unfortunate someone below her had served as a cushion.

The identity of that cushion—the one who had been in entirely the wrong place at the wrong time—was Harry Potter.

"Carina?"

Harry, having been ambushed without warning, had forgotten the pain entirely and was staring up at her with a thoroughly bewildered expression. Carina was no less startled. Of all the places to land, Harry Potter had to be directly beneath her? They hadn't even met privately since the start of term—and running into the person she might have to kill, alone, was the last thing she wanted.

"How did you get here? This is the boys' lavatory, and there was no one here before I arrived."

Harry raised the obvious question. Unable to explain the Vanishing Cabinet, and finding the situation entirely unwelcome, Carina moved to leave at once.

But Harry was faster. One hand caught her arm; the other wrapped around her waist, pulling her close and holding her there. It left him sitting on the bathroom floor, his trousers soaking up the water, but he seemed entirely unbothered. He pulled her toward him and growled.

"What are you doing. Exactly? Apparition is impossible inside Hogwarts—so what did you do to appear out of thin air like that? Don't even think about running,"

Carina answered with silence, but Harry was relentless.

"Don't think about running, Carina. I won't let go easily."

If he kept pressing her like this, he would find the thread. There was only one way to silence him immediately.

Without any warning, Carina pressed her lips to his.

The attack was so sudden that Harry's eyes went wide—he moved to push her away—and then stopped. His hand grasped at empty air. Then changed direction, and caught hold of her head.

It had been nearly a year since they had last kissed. They came together with an urgency that seemed to compensate for all the time apart—fierce and yet entirely natural. Carina's exhale became Harry's inhale; his breath filled her in return.

Breath carries warmth. The heat that had built up inside them, unresolved, only accumulated further between them. They grew more breathless, more desperate for each other.

Carina wrapped both arms around Harry's shoulders. Harry leaned against the bathroom wall and tasted her freely, thoroughly, as she sat above him.

Something Carina would never have done first. Something she would have allowed only under the pretence of being swept along—held back by embarrassment, by propriety, by the weight of circumstances.

But the force of Felix Felicis moved through her body and led her forward. Forgetting dignity and shame alike, Carina followed something rawer and more immediate.

It was not entirely unpleasant. It was not her way—but the unguarded collision of it was unfamiliar and, she had to admit, enjoyable. Enjoyable enough that she almost thought it would be all right to go further, to cross a line she had always kept.

But…. Now was not the time.

"Thank you."

For the Felix Felicis. For giving her something to hold on to. For holding on to her until the very end.

She said it plainly, with genuine feeling, and pushed herself upright using his shoulder. Harry, suddenly deprived of her, reached out and caught nothing.

By the time he had gathered himself, Carina had already reached the bathroom entrance. Harry scrambled to his feet and called after her.

"Wait, Carina! I haven't finished what I was going to say—!"

"If you have something to say, meet me in the Room of Requirement. Saturday night, two days from now, at eleven."

Carina drew one finger across her lips. It was a deliberate gesture, unmistakably so. Her flushed face, her pale fingers trailing across her red mouth—Harry's eyes went wide, and he froze halfway to standing.

Carina smiled—a bright, teasing smile that was almost impish.

"…Come alone."

She whispered it playfully. The distance between them was considerable, yet it reached Harry's ears as though spoken from inches away.

Carina turned at once and walked away from the boys' lavatory. Harry did not follow.

Half-running down the corridor, Carina pressed both hands over her mouth.

It had all been impulsive. The kiss. The promise to meet. Every breath they had shared. Raw emotion, barely filtered through reason.

Whether this was the luck Felix Felicis had given her, or whether it was simply Carina herself—she didn't know.
"Hey, Malfoy."

Just then. Urquhart, the new Slytherin Quidditch captain, came striding toward her with a look of barely contained irritation. By something close to instinct, Carina smoothed her expression, tossed her hair back, and met his gaze.

"What is it?"

"I need to talk to you about your Quidditch practice."

Urquhart got to the point without preamble.

"Your attendance has been lacking lately, and your form hasn't recovered to match. On top of that, more students are beginning to question your ability."

Ah. Quidditch. The sport of wizards, the great festival of Hogwarts. Whatever her own circumstances, Carina knew perfectly well how many students were looking forward to the matches.

But if things went according to plan, she would not be there for that celebration. It was time to begin preparing her farewell to the team.

"It seems my health has been declining lately—my form simply won't come back. I think I'll need to find a replacement."

Urquhart's expression darkened with displeasure.

"That's irresponsible. You know better than anyone that there's no one ready to take your place."

"Actually, there is one person."

Carina spoke as though she had prepared for this.

"I'll pass my Firebolt on to Draco. Try him as Seeker."

"Draco?"

"Yes. He's spent every holiday flying with me from morning to night—his skill shouldn't be too bad."

Perhaps it was the Felix Felicis. The words seemed to come out ahead of her thoughts. The saving grace was that every word appeared to be leading toward the best possible outcome for Carina.

Urquhart had clearly been hoping she would simply commit to practice again, but he was not the sort to force someone who had made up their mind.

After a moment, Urquhart looked away.

"I'll give him a trial. But if he doesn't meet the standard, I'll cut him immediately—your brother or not, Firebolt or not."

"Of course."

"…And if you're struggling, rest. Not just for Quidditch—taking care of yourself matters."

Urquhart walked away. Carina gave a small nod and went on her way.

She needed to hurry. The Felix Felicis was beginning to fade. The warmth that had filled her completely was slowly draining away, and the last ember left in her chest was all the luck she had remaining.

Who would she meet at the end of this final stretch? What encounter would prove to be her last stroke of fortune?

Remarkably, at the end of this path stood someone Carina had not expected at all.

"…Well. Longbottom. I didn't know you were capable of romance. And with…."

Neville had stopped dead in his tracks, like a mouse cornered by a cat. And beside him, wearing a vague, dreamy expression, stood Luna Lovegood—her hand in his.

"A Ravenclaw, no less. Surprising. I didn't know you had that much nerve. I've revised my opinion of you."

"W-what are you doing here, Malfoy? This is right in front of the Ravenclaw common room!"

Neville stepped in front of Luna. While Carina had been occupied elsewhere, he had apparently developed enough courage to shield his own girlfriend.

It was, of course, faintly ridiculous to Carina. She looked past Neville to Luna.

"Luna Lovegood?"

"Mm. That's my name. And yours is Carina Malfoy, isn't it?"

"Mm."

Luna looked directly at Carina. Even in those dreaming eyes, there was a clear and steady resolve to stand by her boyfriend's side.

They protect each other, then. Carina gave a small, dismissive sound.

"Not the wisest choice, I'd say. Of all the people to fall for, you chose Longbottom—who stands at the opposite end of everything Ravenclaw represents."

"That's rude. Neville is very good at Herbology."

"He's good at Herbology and nothing else. Everything else is not just mediocre—it's rather catastrophic."

"Hmm. That's… a little…."

Luna did not deny it. But the look in her eyes when she glanced at Neville still held unmistakable warmth.

"Even so, it's all right. Neville listens when I talk about Crumple-Horned Snorkacks and Stubby Boardman. He doesn't laugh at me or dismiss me."

"That's probably because he's too dim to point out that they don't exist."

"They do exist."

Luna said it with the faintest trace of a pout.

"Neville believes the same as you—that they don't exist. But Neville is very kind. He holds his own opinion and still considers my feelings, and he walks me to my common room every single day. That's why I like him. That's why I'm with him."

"How very devoted."

"Neville says he learned it by watching how Harry treats you."

The unexpected words made Carina stumble. Luna continued in her unhurried, faraway way.

"Harry has been watching you all along, even while you were fighting. Since first year—sometimes when he was eating, he'd glance over, and at the end of that glance there was always you. Neville said when he first realised, he felt betrayed and resented Harry for it. But after watching it go on year after year, he found himself rooting for you both instead."

"L-Luna…."

Neville went red and tugged at Luna's arm. Even Carina felt heat rise to her face and had to look away—yet Luna, who had said it all, seemed entirely unaffected.

Carina fanned her burning cheeks lightly with one hand.

"…I see. In a way, you two are perfectly matched."

"Thank you. You and Harry are—"

"Stop."

Carina cut her off sharply, and Luna laughed—a soft, bubbling sound.

Neville and Luna released each other's hands in front of the bust of Hogwarts founder Rowena Ravenclaw, which stood at the entrance to the Ravenclaw common room.

Watching the couple from the outside gave Carina a strange, new feeling. Had she and Harry been this unbearable to look at? Probably. Thinking of Crabbe and Goyle's perspective, she let out an involuntary sigh.

She was about to look away, unable to bear it—when something caught her eye.

"…Wait. Lovegood."

"Yes?"

It was something she might have walked past without a second glance on any ordinary day.

What Carina had seen was a beautiful diadem—silver, with striking pale blue gemstones.

What sat atop the bust was merely bronze, a rough approximation of the shape.

Even with Carina's sharp eye, she would not have noticed the correspondence without looking carefully—the matching of forms.

But the last warmth left in her chest flared up, and something—a sense of wrongness—snagged her gaze and held it. Carina pointed to the ornament displayed on the bust.

"Do you know what that is?"

"That's Ravenclaw's Diadem. It belonged to Rowena Ravenclaw herself—it's said to grant wisdom and show the path forward to whoever wears it."

Ravenclaw's object.

Carina repeated the phrase silently, and an old memory surfaced.

Harry Potter had mentioned it—after finding Hufflepuff's Cup. He had said that if they could just find one more Ravenclaw artefact, they would have accounted for all the Horcruxes. That once it was destroyed, He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named would hold no more terror.

Oh, Carina breathed—a small, quiet sound.

"If I had that, I might be able to find a clue about the Crumple-Horned Snorkack. But it's been lost for so long. What a shame."

Carina knew exactly where that object was.

Luna would never be able to wear that diadem. It had already become a Horcrux, hidden away in the Room of Requirement.

Hide a tree in a forest. It seemed He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named had thought along the same lines as Carina. Whether it was his arrogance that had left it displayed so openly atop a bust, or whether someone else had placed it there later, she couldn't say.

But one thing was certain—a new Horcrux had been found.

"So this is my luck…."

The last of Felix Felicis blazed up as it spent itself. Feeling the dying flame in her chest, Carina exhaled.

Everything that had happened today had been her luck. Taking Professor Slughorn's poison. Repairing the Vanishing Cabinet. Kissing Harry Potter and arranging to meet him alone. Passing on the Seeker position along with her Firebolt.

And recognising the Horcrux for what it was.

Thanks to the fortune Felix Felicis had given her, Carina now knew clearly what she had to do. And she had obtained the means to do it.

So even as she resented Felix Felicis—she had no choice but to be grateful for it.

"…Malfoy. Are you crying?"

Neville and Luna were staring at her with expressions of profound shock. Neville in particular looked as though he had seen a ghost—or something considerably worse.

Carina noticed a single tear tracing its way down her cheek.

Just one drop. She wiped the trace away with her sleeve in a single motion, then let out a long, slow breath.

"No. I don't cry in front of other people."

The Felix Felicis was gone now. Carina 'Felix' Malfoy—impulsive, emotional, unguarded—no longer existed.

All that remained was ordinary Carina Malfoy, Death Eater, stripped of luck and sentiment alike.

She turned away with composure. With no luck left to her, she would have to navigate what came next by will and ability alone. With a handful of potions, a Vanishing Cabinet, and two good friends.

As she walked away from Neville and Luna, a thought seemed to occur to her, and she left one last word behind.

"The two of you… stay together and be happy. Until the very end."

It was the first kind word she had ever offered Neville—and likely the last. Carina left the young couple behind her and walked alone down the corridor.

Tomorrow would be busy with preparations.

And the night after that—the night she had arranged to meet Harry.

Everything would be decided then.

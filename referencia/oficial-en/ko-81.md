It seemed the news of Carina attending Slughorn's party had spread throughout the entire school. That was what Carina thought.

Otherwise, Snape—who should have been the last person to hear such gossip—would never have come to find her.

"What exactly are you trying to do, Carina?"

"What am I trying to do? I'm doing my best for the mission."

"Do you think I don't know? You went to see whether you could steal something useful from Professor Slughorn again."

Carina quickly composed her expression, but Snape had already approached with the air of a man who had made up his mind. He pressed her sharply.

"Do you think a Potions master wouldn't notice the quantities of his own brews? Professor Slughorn mentioned that someone appeared to have stolen a potion he'd had simmering. I suggested it was Peeves, but the moment I heard it, I knew it was you."

"You're mistaken. There's no proof I took anything, is there? Or did you read my mind?"

"Occlumency is not infallible, Carina! Even if you can use Occlumency, it's perfectly easy to work out who the thief is! Draco told me. He said you've been slipping out somewhere every night lately. And it started from the very first day of term—the day of Professor Slughorn's class!"

That loose-lipped little brat. Carina clicked her tongue briefly and replied with cool indifference.

"Then go and tell Dumbledore. Report that Carina Malfoy has been stealing from a professor and sneaking out at night, and have me disciplined. That would settle it, wouldn't it?"

"Carina. You know that's not what I want. If you need help, you have only to ask me—so why are you setting your sights on Professor Slughorn's things? If you're caught doing something like that, it will be difficult for both of us!"

"I'm the one who'd be punished. Why would it be difficult for you?"

"Because I made an Unbreakable Vow to your mother. To protect you!"

Even Carina could not help looking at him again at that.

An Unbreakable Vow. It was a merciless magic that could never be broken—and if one party violated it, death was the immediate price. Having made that vow, Snape was bound to protect Carina if he wished to go on living.

But Carina did not let her guard down.

An Unbreakable Vow is not infallible. There was once a sorcerer-king who had distributed Unbreakable Vows freely, hoping to build an army of absolute obedience—and he was struck down by a trusted subordinate who harboured resentment at his unjust commands. No vow had been made against killing him, after all.

Another dark wizard, having learned from that example, bound his followers with vows far more detailed and specific—only to die in his own arrogance, poisoned, with no one to help him. His subordinates had simply watched. They had become puppets incapable of acting without their master's direct order.

Beyond those cases, there were countless others that illustrated the dangers of the Unbreakable Vow. A double-edged sword. A chain that bound both parties. The stronger the magic, the greater the cost—that was an old adage of the wizarding world.

And beyond all that, she had no way of knowing whether Snape's words could simply be trusted.

"I don't need your protection, Professor. So please set your mind at ease and go prepare for your next lesson."

"You have nearly been caught several times already. And you keep pushing your head further in!"

"I haven't been caught, and I won't be. And even if I were discovered, I could talk my way out of it a few times over."

"I am telling you that you don't need to! If you would only accept my help—!"

"Since I won't be accepting Professor Snape's help, I suppose I do need to manage on my own."

Carina's manner was impenetrable—not a needle's width of opening. Snape seemed to finally abandon persuasion, and when he spoke again his voice had dropped to something quieter and more deliberate.

"Don't forget the mission. If you don't kill Harry Potter or Dumbledore, you die. It's a very simple problem, isn't it? Three options, one correct answer. I believe you're a girl who can solve something this straightforward."

"If you are truly a teacher, Professor, then step back and wait for my answer."

Carina spun on her heel and ended the conversation unilaterally, striding away down the corridor. Behind her retreating figure, only Snape's grim warning followed.

"…Bear it in mind. The Dark Mark has no patience. Until you die, it will keep watching you—and it will bite…."

Carina had not refused Snape's help without reason. She still had one last resort.

Felix Felicis.

Ironically, it was the luck potion Harry had given her that had been delaying Carina's resolve.

If she asked Snape for help, she might be given a poison capable of killing someone within a week, along with an opportunity to slip it into a drink. A perfect, prepared chance to take a life—requiring nothing more than a slight tilt of a vial.

If it came to that, there would be no stopping it. She would be pushed and pushed until she killed someone almost by accident, then clamp her trembling hands together by sheer force of will and flee Hogwarts.

So she would wait. Wait until Felix Felicis offered no answer. Only then would it not be too late to go to Snape. Carina steadied herself with that thought, and measured out the moments when she might drink the Felix Felicis by the strictest of standards.

But fate, indifferent as ever, brought a moment that forced her hand.

Professor Slughorn spoke with an air of barely contained urgency.

"Terribly busy today! Sit down quickly, all of you! Now—does anyone know Golpalott's Third Law?"

Golpalott was a wizard who had devoted his entire life to magical poisons and their antidotes. He was a name that came up frequently in Potions lessons. Carina searched her memory for the law before Hermione could answer.

"Golpalott'sThirdLawstatesthattheantidoteforacompoundpoisonmustbemorethanasimplecombinationoftheantidotesforeachofitscomponentparts!"

As always, Carina's recollection came a fraction too late. Hermione had already spoken. Having long since given up on outpacing Hermione, Carina turned her attention to Slughorn's lecture. Today's lesson was on poisons and antidotes.

Poisons and antidotes. The very things Carina might need at any moment…. As she listened, a peculiar sensation crept over her. As though this lesson had been arranged specifically for her—an ominous sense of perfect, terrible fit.

"…you'll need Scarpin's Revelaspell for this. Separate the individual components of this poison, prepare an antidote for each, and then combine them. Now, everyone come up and take one of these vials! See how many component antidotes you can produce before the end of class!"

Her jaw trembled. Her palms were damp and slick. Carina walked slowly to the front, accepted the vial Slughorn held out, and returned to her seat.

Poison.

A poison made by Professor Slughorn had come into Carina's hands with startling ease. A single vial's worth—enough to kill several people. Placed in plain sight before her.

Carina shook her head as though trying to deny reality.

'No. It's probably just something trivial—stomach cramps, bruising, diarrhoea. Surely he wouldn't give students a lethal poison.'

But that was far too dismissive of the N.E.W.T. curriculum. Most of the potions they had studied could produce effects of lethal toxicity if combined incorrectly. At the advanced level, separating compound poisons and preparing antidotes was a core requirement.

With trembling hands, Carina applied Scarpin's Revelaspell and identified the components of the compound poison.

One, two, three…. Thirteen in total. Each one a straightforward poison capable of bringing a person to the brink of death—and together, a simple compound poison that would kill whoever drank it.

At that moment, the snake on Carina's wrist opened its eyes. Hidden beneath her sleeve, invisible to anyone else, but Carina could feel it stirring against her skin. The snake flicked its tongue toward the poison in the vial as though regarding a fine wine—as if urging her to consume it at once.

"Carina. What are you doing?"

A Slytherin student nudged her, and Carina came back to herself. Glancing around, she saw students dragging scales and cauldrons toward them, rushing about in search of ingredients. Carina set to work on the antidote as well, if only to appear engaged in the lesson.

Thirty minutes remained.

It was a failure—or rather, failure had been inevitable. It was impossible, at a student's level and within the time limit, to produce thirteen separate antidotes and combine them correctly using the right catalysts in the right proportions without neutralising one another.

It was not a matter of Carina's ability. Not even Hermione—nor Harry Potter, who possessed an almost preternatural instinct for Potions—had managed to produce a complete antidote.

From across the room, Harry Potter's voice carried over in complaint.

"Thirteen poisons. It takes three hours just to make the antidote for each one individually. This is physically impossible."

"It's probably for grading purposes. Stop complaining and add another ingredient."

"No, no matter what I do, I can only get nine in the time. And every additional one after that requires a staggering amount of catalyst. Honestly, at this point I'd rather just shove in a Bezoar. One universal antidote would be far more practical."

If even Harry Potter was at his wit's end, it meant that producing an antidote on the spot—for whoever drank this—was simply not possible.

Carina looked down at the half-finished antidote in her cauldron. The charred, blackened residue clinging to the bottom seemed to mirror the state of her own heart.

If the antidote had been easy to make, she would have dismissed the poison as useless and ignored it. But the Potions lesson had already proven its worth.

Lethal. Difficult to counter. And now in her hands.

The snake had already shown interest in the poison. If she left without taking it, the snake tattooed on her skin would grow suspicious. It would bite, it would press her, it would report back to its master. And the Dark Lord would show no mercy.

She had to take the poison—to remain faithful to the 'mission' of killing someone. The snake was watching her with wide-open eyes. If she didn't take it, something far worse would press its fangs to her throat.

But she didn't want it. She didn't want to take hold of a weapon. She didn't want to grip an instrument of death and agonise over whom to aim it at.

Cold sweat ran down her jaw. Her pale skin had gone beyond white—it was nearly translucent. Her spasming hands seemed incapable of holding even her wand properly. She steadied them by force of will and thought:

Perhaps now was the moment to summon her luck.

She would need an empty vial to transfer the poison, in any case. If she produced a separate empty vial and was caught in the act of decanting the poison, the consequences would go well beyond a simple detention.

So this was purely about obtaining an empty vial. Not because she hoped some stroke of luck might spare her from having to take the poison.

Deceiving herself—and the snake—Carina reached into her bag and closed her fingers around a small bottle of golden liquid. Felix Felicis was still warm, as though generating its own heat. With sweat-slicked hands she barely managed to keep hold of it, then concealed it in her sleeve and rose from her seat.

"Professor. May I be excused to use the lavatory for a moment?"

"Oh, Miss Malfoy! Be quick about it! There are only ten minutes left!"

Carina gave a brief nod and slipped out of the classroom. She reached a secluded bathroom, checked quickly in both directions, and once she was certain she was alone, drew the bottle from her sleeve.

The luck potion. This draught that Harry Potter had given her—where would it lead her? What fortune would it bring?

There was nothing to do but hope.

Carina drank the Felix Felicis in a single swallow. It was like swallowing light. A warmth that filled her completely passed down her throat and into her stomach, then began to spread through her veins to every part of her body. It was as though a small sun had entered her.

Carina felt vitality surge through her. Her mind was clear as someone waking from a deep, refreshing sleep, and the exhaustion that had threatened to bring her to her knees was gone without a trace. She felt as though she could catch every Snitch in the sky, no matter how many came at once.

Even the fear that had consumed her only moments ago seemed like nothing at all. Why had she worried so much over a problem with an obvious answer?

If she had no choice but to take it, then she would simply take it.

She breathed in deeply and counted to three in her mind. Carina walked back into the classroom and, with perfect naturalness, slipped the vial of poison into her bag and exchanged it for the bottle that had held the Felix Felicis.

Fortunately, the two vials were identical in shape. Once the faint traces of golden liquid inside were gone, no one would be able to tell what the bottle had once contained.

Carina gripped her wand. She had never successfully cast a vanishing spell—or any advanced wordless charm—before, but today she was the luckiest girl alive, and today she could do anything. She focused, levelled her wand at the bottle, and the last traces of golden liquid vanished entirely. Carina allowed herself a small, quiet smile.

"Right, time's up! Let's see what we have! Blaise. What on earth did you make?"

As Slughorn worked his way along the Slytherin table, Carina presented a clean empty vial and a cauldron of blackened residue. Slughorn narrowed his eyes.

"Hmm. Miss Malfoy? What were you attempting?"

"I couldn't see any way to produce the antidote within the time limit, so I tried casting a counter-charm directly on the poison. Something must have gone wrong—the poison simply disappeared."

It was an improvised excuse. But Slughorn stroked his moustache and examined the result with genuine interest.

"Hmmm. The thinking behind it isn't bad at all. Yes—sometimes the simplest approach is the right one. Though that method is only effective against a very limited range of components, so next time, do make sure to separate them thoroughly first."

"Thank you, Professor."

It had been a hasty fabrication, yet the assessment was unexpectedly favourable. Perhaps this particular lesson had called for ingenuity and instinct more than diligence. Carina thought as much, then turned her wand on the cauldron. It came away spotless.

After class, Carina skipped dinner entirely and went straight to the Room of Requirement. She had been thinking that with Felix Felicis still in her system, she might be able to repair the Vanishing Cabinet more easily. She passed the beautiful diadem resting on its bust, turned left past the mountain of stacked books, walked one block further, and arrived at the Vanishing Cabinet. As always, she drew her wand and attempted to repair it.

But it seemed that what was impossible remained impossible even with luck on one's side. After nearly an hour of trying every repair charm she knew, the Vanishing Cabinet remained broken.

Carina gave it a petulant kick. She was half-wondering whether some stray stroke of luck might use the impact to miraculously fix it.

"…Right. Nothing was ever going to be that easy."

Of course, no such miracle occurred. Carina was leaning against the cabinet in resignation when a voice reached her from somewhere nearby.

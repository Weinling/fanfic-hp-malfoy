# Crítica de escenas rehechas: romance, amistad y mesa del pub

*7 de noviembre de 2026. Crítico Opus, a ciegas. Lectura de los ocho capítulos enteros en su estado actual, `cap-36` como referencia de voz y grep de continuidad. Números de archivo, no de lector. No he leído guiones, fichas, manuales ni handoff.*

## 1. Veredicto

La tanda mejora el libro: todo gana a lo que había salvo un pasaje, y el 59 (el buckle) y el 80 (la ceja) son lo mejor. El romance ya se vive: hay nervios, ganas de quedarse y torpeza física, y Lyra toma la iniciativa en todas las escenas.
El fallo es de sistema. Lyra manda y Harry obedece en casi todos los pasajes: en el texto añadido, Harry pide (“I could go”, “Stay a bit”, “I didn’t mean stop”) y el narrador cierra con “He stopped trying.”, “He held still.”, “He did.”, “He stopped.”. Queda demasiado educado.
El 49 se pasa de explicar la posesividad justo después de ganársela: la muñeca repetida traduce el “Mine”.
El único cambio que no sé si gana es la coda del papel del 59: rebaja “For sitting.” y tiene un error físico. Se arregla, no hace falta quitarla.
La mesa del pub funciona y no frena, pero se olvida de lo más importante: Goyle lleva seis meses intentando que Lyra coma, y aquí Lyra come y nadie reacciona.

## 3. Respuesta a las preguntas

**1. ¿Mejora?** Sí, salvo la coda del papel, que queda en empate. En el 45 se pierde “not at all sensible” y la duda de quién cruzó el suelo, pero sale ganando: la iniciativa de Lyra importa más.

**2. ¿Se vive?** Sí. Lo que más lejos llega es el 59 y el 49. Se queda corto en dos sitios: la mano izquierda del 45 (se planta y no se paga) y la falta de ningún gesto de Harry que no sea respuesta. La altura tenía un problema que no se veía en cada capítulo por separado: ver incoherencia 1.

**3. Harry, Lyra, posesividad.** Lyra conserva la iniciativa siempre y se entiende lo que quiere. Harry es demasiado considerado por acumulación más que en un punto concreto: en todo lo añadido no inicia un solo contacto. Sus cuatro intervenciones son peticiones. “He meant it” (45) y “I want to know which bit that was” (70) son los dos puntos que más lo notan. En la posesividad, “She wanted the fists.” es perfecto y ambiguo; lo que viene después lo explica. Los puños de Harry y “She could feel that he was not going to be the one who let go” se sostienen solos.

**4. La mesa del pub.** Goyle y Crabbe tienen vida propia: Goyle probando pasteles “to be sure”, Crabbe apuntándolo y Goyle discutiendo el asiento contable. Crabbe no le dirige la palabra a Lyra en ningún momento, y la resolución es muy buena: enseña la cifra sin enseñarla. No frena: son unas 300 palabras que dan sustancia a “It was a good day”, que antes no tenía nada detrás. También hacen que “It had heard about the marble” y “half a mince pie” paguen algo. Riesgo: repite la forma de la escena del horno (Lyra hace una pregunta de verdad con voz sin defensas). Se corrige quitando el aviso. El “said no” de Goyle está bien: no roba nada al “No” de 85:157, porque ese “No” ya tiene dos precedentes (78:93, 84:119).

## 5. Tics y ecos (medido)

- **Remate “That + reacción”:** cero en lo añadido. **“she found”:** dos, heredados. **“It was”:** bajo en lo añadido; el 85 tiene 34 en total, pero casi todo es de lo que no se ha tocado.
- **“as if”:** tres de los cinco del 49 están en lo añadido, en 30 líneas. Las correcciones quitan dos.
- **“for some time / for a while / some time”:** 49 y 59 tienen cinco cada uno; el límite de la rúbrica es dos. Lo nuevo aporta dos al 59.
- **Harry obedece, en frase corta:** “He stopped trying.” (45:247), “He held still.” (49:143), “He stopped.” (49:157), “did it properly.” (49:171), “He did.” (59:147), “He stayed facing the right way.” (59:167). Cada una es buena; seis en cuatro escenas son un patrón.
- **Risa que “got out”:** 49:149 se suma a 59:109, 70:91, 78:155 y 85:41. Es la ruta que señala la lista de patrones. El 49 se cambia en una palabra.
- **“crooked”:** 45:225, 45:251, 46:179, 49:137, 49:149, tres escenas de romance seguidas con las gafas torcidas. En el 49 se quita una.
- **“He had grown”:** 45:227 y 49:137 hacen el mismo chiste con “which”. El segundo reconoce al primero (“again”): vale.
- **“folded himself up”:** 59:157 y 59:243, en el mismo capítulo.
- **“squared it to the edge with two fingers”:** 59:289 es el gesto de Granger (49:191, 51:17). Harry no debería tenerlo.
- **“with great attention”:** 45:167 y 45:235.
- **“flat pale ear”:** 85:41 y 85:57.
- **Muñeca:** 49:123 y 49:163.

## 6. Correcciones listas para aplicar

Por orden de importancia.

**1. 49:163. La posesividad sin traducir.**
Actual: `It was extremely well-mannered, and she hated it. She wanted the fists. She had not known that until they were gone, and she stood there as outraged as if somebody had picked up her doll.`
Propuesto: `It was extremely well-mannered, and she hated it. She wanted the fists.`

**2. 45:239. Harry, menos noble.** No sabemos si es cortesía o amenaza, y así debe quedar.
Actual: `He meant it. She saw that. He would say good night in that even, pleasant voice,`
Propuesto: `He would, too. He would say good night in that even, pleasant voice,`

**3. 59:279-289. La coda del papel.** Resuelve el guardafuegos, enlaza con 60:7, quita el gesto de Granger y devuelve la salida cómica con la orden de su declaración: “Get off my chair”.
Actual (279, inicio): `It was some time before anybody remembered the paper. It had got between them,`
Propuesto: `Nobody remembered the paper until it crackled. It had got between them,`
Actual (289): `He leaned down and laid it on the fender, a long way from the fire, and squared it to the edge with two fingers. She could read the date from the chair. She did not tell him to move it.`
Propuesto:
```
He folded it in four, very slowly, along none of the creases it had already got, and put it in his back pocket, and sat on it.

“That’s another ten,” said Lyra.

She did not tell him to get off the chair.
```

**4. 85:73. Goyle ve comer a Lyra, sin decirlo.**
Actual: `Lyra took up her fork. The pie broke very neatly down the middle, and she ate one half while they disagreed about`
Propuesto: `Lyra took up her fork. The pie broke very neatly down the middle. Nobody looked. Goyle was attending to her own plate, with great care, and breathing through her nose; and Lyra ate one half while they disagreed about`

**5. 85:65-71. La lógica del mármol.**
Actual:
```
“And if it doesn’t?”

“Then we get the marble.”

Crabbe looked at her.

“Then we keep it,” said Goyle, with her mouth full. “For a bit. I know.”
```
Propuesto:
```
“And if it goes up twice?”

“Then we get the marble.”

Crabbe looked at her.

“Then we get the marble *later*,” said Goyle, with her mouth full. “I know.”
```

**6. 45:249. Pagar la mano izquierda.** Se planta en 45:227 y es lo único que falta en la escena.
Actual: `She only knew that she had gone up on her toes for it, and that he kept his right hand in his pocket the whole time,`
Propuesto: `She only knew that she had gone up on her toes for it, and that his left hand had made up its mind at last, somewhere behind her elbow, and that he kept the right one in his pocket the whole time,`

**7. 49:137. Altura distinta y frase comprensible.** Harry también se mueve. Sin puntillas.
Actual: `He had grown again, which was inconsiderate. She had to go up on her toes, and she arrived crooked, with his nose cold against her cheek and her own hands not at all steady, and she kissed him anyway, as if she had been meaning to all day, which she had. His arms went round her. One of them got it wrong and had to go back for another try; and for once there was nobody standing far enough away to look superior.`
Propuesto: `He had grown again, which was inconsiderate, and he had to come down the last of the way by himself. He came. They arrived badly, with his nose cold against her cheek and her own hands not at all steady, and she kissed him anyway, as if she had been meaning to all day, which she had. His arms went round her. One of them got it wrong and had to go back for another try, and she let that pass; she was in no position to look superior.`

**8. 70:121. Harry quiere, en vez de atender.** “Which bit did you like?” se queda; lo que cambia es la justificación, y queda más egoísta.
Actual: `“You had a face on, at the door. I want to know which bit that was.”`
Propuesto: `“You had a face on, at the door. I want that one again.”`

**9. 59:167. Un gesto que sea de Harry.** Él toma algo sin pedirlo.
Actual: `She left her hand where it was, in his ridiculous hair, for want of anywhere better, and the log caught along one side.`
Propuesto: `She left her hand where it was, in his ridiculous hair, for want of anywhere better. He let the whole weight of his head go back against it, and shut his eyes, as if that had been the arrangement from the start. The log caught along one side.`

**10. 49:141-143. No gastar el “Hold still” de 59:273.** Quita además un “as if”.
Actual: `He had been told that before. He held still. She kept one hand in the jumper and felt for the pin with the other, while the glasses hung off one ear, and he stood under her fingers with his eyes on her face, as if the pin were the last thing holding up the castle.`
Propuesto: `He held still. She kept one hand in the jumper and felt for the pin with the other, while the glasses hung off one ear, and he stood under her fingers with his eyes on her face.`

**11. 85:57-61. Que la voz quede para el “Bacon”.**
Actual (57): `They had a whole way of doing that. Lyra watched the pencil go back behind the flat pale ear. She had been going to say something about how one approached a landlord. She had a very good voice for approaching landlords.`
Propuesto: `Lyra watched the pencil go back behind the ear. She had been going to say something about how one approached a landlady. She had a very good voice for landladies.`
Actual (61, inicio): `It wasn’t the voice. Crabbe looked at her`
Propuesto: `Crabbe looked at her`

**12. 70:131. Tenedor y pluma.**
Actual: `She took the quill back, and went up the page,`
Propuesto: `She turned the quill the right way round, and went up the page,`
Actual: `He had stopped eating. He looked at her the whole time;`
Propuesto: `He had not picked up the fork. He looked at her the whole time;`

**13. 59:137-147. Buckle y bolsa.**
Actual (137): `It was an extremely slow buckle, and she could have had him through the door in half the time, if that had been what she wanted. She had been getting people through doors since she was eleven.`
Propuesto: `It was an extremely slow buckle.`
Actual (143): `“Have I got some more?”`
Propuesto: `“Is there more?”`
Actual (145): `You may take your hand out of the bag.`
Propuesto: `You may take your hand off the buckle.`
Actual (147): `She had been on the same one for some time.`
Propuesto: `She had been on the same one since the buckle.`

**14. 49:179. Párrafo que se enreda.**
Actual: `She had the rest of the sixth floor to walk and a book to write up, and she had plenty of time to think of both of them, and of several other things, and they could all wait.`
Propuesto: `She had the rest of the sixth floor to walk and a book to write up. They could wait.`

**15. Ecos de una palabra.**
- 59:157: `and folded himself up on the floor beside her` → `and let himself down on the floor beside her`
- 45:235: `He looked at her badge, with great attention.` → `He looked at her badge. It seemed to be giving him some difficulty.`
- 49:149: `It got out very small,` → `It came out very small,`
- 85:43: `before Lyra had finished shaking out her cloak` → `before Lyra was through the door`

## 7. Lo que no hay que tocar

- **45:** “She had had eleven days of that door.”, “He stopped trying.”, “neither of them seemed to know whose job that was”, “in a voice she had not heard at breakfast”.
- **46:** el cambio entero.
- **49:** la pelea de la costura, “You’re holding me like a tea tray”, “She wanted the fists.”, el dedo sobre el corazón y “the handsomest thing that anybody had ever failed to say to her”. Aunque tiene algo de aforismo, se lo ha ganado.
- **59:** “She took him by the top of the head”, “She could hear it perfectly well.”, “The goblins have gone to bed.”, el cierre “nobody gave it a mark”.
- **64:** el recorte. La comparación con el perro está en la lista de prohibidas, pero es anterior al cambio y, en el punto de vista de Harry, funciona: no la tocaría.
- **70:** “It’s the same squiggle.”, “Everybody else was still doing two questions.”, “there was no room left on the paper”.
- **80:** la ceja, entera. No aclarar si “the wrong one” es despiste o payasada.
- **85:** “and she didn’t push the book across”, “Four times. I wanted to be sure.”, “That doesn’t come out of the marble.”, “You’ve put it under Lunch, Ness.”, y el “said no” de Goyle.

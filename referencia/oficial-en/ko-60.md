The next day, and the day after that. Harry walked into the room without a word and wrote with the same pen.

With every line he wrote, the wounds cut deeper. At some point they stopped healing between sessions, bleeding freely enough to drip.

But Harry had no intention of saying anything Umbridge might enjoy. The only words he ever spoke in her office were "Hello" and "Goodbye." In the silence between, the two of them held their ground like opponents in a duel, neither willing to yield a syllable.

By Thursday, the wounds from the night before hadn't healed by morning. Harry showed no sign of pain. He wrote with almost cheerful energy—cursive once, print once, deliberately sloppy a third time—methodically adding to the tally carved into his skin.

"Very well. Give me your hand, Mr. Potter."

Harry offered it without protest. Umbridge shook her head.

"Hmm. It seems you still haven't learned your lesson. Very well—that will do for today."

"Do I have to come back tomorrow?"

"Of course. One more session ought to drive the lesson a little deeper, don't you think?"

"But tomorrow you said we'd have independent study because of your Ministry visit. With you away, how could someone as deficient in guidance as myself possibly manage detention alone?"

Umbridge smiled broadly.

"That kind of clever scheming tells me we have quite a way to go yet. Don't worry, Mr. Potter. I've arranged for someone to supervise in my absence."

"Oh dear. I do wonder about the qualifications of such a person. What sort of extraordinary individual could possibly stand in for Professor Umbridge? I can't even begin to imagine."

"Don't worry. This person is quite authorised to administer your detention."

Could she be bringing Snape? Unlikely. Snape would never waste his precious time watching a student copy lines—not even for Harry Potter. He had too much contempt for that kind of errand work.

Well. Tomorrow would tell. If it came to it, he could always overturn the room and walk out.

"Goodbye, Professor Umbridge."

Harry said it as pleasantly as he could manage and made his way back to Gryffindor Tower.

The time lost to Umbridge's detention meant every remaining hour had to go toward homework. He barely finished his essays by two in the morning, and the next day he dragged himself to the Great Hall feeling like something scraped off the castle floor.

The one mercy was that Defence Against the Dark Arts was independent study today, which meant he could work on other assignments. He managed to finish practising his Vanishing Spells just before the period ended.

On his way to Umbridge's office, Angelina came charging at him, furious. She demanded to know whether he had any sense of responsibility as Seeker, and told him he might as well skip detention entirely.

But that was a last resort. He needed to keep that option in reserve for the day Umbridge decided to hold the Quidditch matches themselves hostage.

"Is it really that hard to just say you're sorry once? Potter, if you want to keep your place as Seeker, you'd better be working twice as hard as everyone else at practice!"

Angelina snapped. Harry did not say then find another Seeker. Suggesting he might give up the position would send her into a fury that no apology could contain.

He managed to escape only after promising Angelina that if the supervisor turned out to be a Slytherin student, he would hit them with a Confundus Charm and walk out.

Harry trudged toward Umbridge's office.

Truth be told, he was exhausted. He'd fought Dementors over the summer, sat through a Ministry tribunal, and now spent every evening in what amounted to torture disguised as detention. No one—not even Hermione—could have kept up with a schedule like his.

But the world was not particularly gentle, and there weren't many people with the luxury of offering comfort. Harry pushed open the office door, tired and irritable.

"Excuse me."

He shoved it open and stepped inside. As always, the room greeted him with its aggressively girlish décor—all lace and china cats and the particular shade of pink that made his eyes ache. And waiting for him inside was—

A green cardigan. Bright platinum hair.

"You're late, Potter."

Carina Malfoy, prefect badge pinned neatly to her chest.

Harry had never expected to feel anything resembling pleasure in Umbridge's office, so he stopped and assessed the situation with cool precision. He cast a quiet detection spell around the room, checking whether Umbridge had left some enchantment behind, watching Carina carefully.

Nothing. The detection spell found no trace of magic. The person before him was unquestionably Carina—the same self-assured expression, the same faint scent that drifted toward him whenever she was near. Exactly as he'd always known her.

"What's this? Did you come to see me?"

"Professor Umbridge asked me to supervise your detention."

Carina tapped the desk twice. Beneath its lace-trimmed cloth sat a single sheet of paper and the familiar pen.

She smirked.

"You must have made quite an impression, Potter."

"I tend to be harsh with unpleasant women."

Harry said it flatly and sat down. Carina settled herself on the corner of the desk.

Lace and frills on every surface. Carina watching him from close enough to touch.

Even if he couldn't reach out to her—not here, not now—Harry found himself wondering, with genuine bewilderment, whether this was some kind of gift Umbridge had accidentally arranged.

"She doesn't know about us."

Carina said it quietly.

"Even so, don't think about slacking off, Potter. I'm here to supervise your detention. If you don't copy those lines properly, I'll report it to Professor Umbridge."

"Then I'd better work especially hard today. That way she might arrange this again."

"...Let me be clear, Potter."

Carina kept her voice as cold as she could.

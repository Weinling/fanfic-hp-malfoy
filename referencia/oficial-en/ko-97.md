Draco had thrown himself headlong into Seeker practice. It was a matter of pride, and of proving himself.

After his father's release from prison, the family had found its footing again—but within Slytherin, the looks directed at the Malfoy name had soured. Friends who used to spend time with him had begun drifting away, and certain rough older students had taken to shouldering past him deliberately.

If he could catch the Snitch in the next Quidditch match, everyone would see him differently. That hunger was what drove Draco through every practice session.

"Hmm. His agility isn't bad, I'll give him that."

Harry tossed a small ball to Draco. Draco reached for it, only to fumble as it glanced off his thumb, both hands flailing uselessly after it.

Harry watched and sighed.

"The ability to judge the direction of a small object, extend your arm, and close your hand around it. What do you call that? Accuracy? Hand-eye coordination? Whatever it is—it's dire."

"That's because you threw it without warning!"

Draco hurled the ball straight back at Harry. Harry's arm shot out and snatched it cleanly from the air. The same ball that had ricocheted off Draco's hand like two magnets repelling each other flew into Harry's palm as though drawn there by the opposite pole.

Harry began tossing it up and catching it, over and over.

"Hmm. Why is that, I wonder."

"Because I haven't practised! I'd get better with practice too!"

"Practising like this would only make things worse. I've never once let a Snitch slip through my fingers, and I've never trained for it."

"Stop showing off! I'll beat you next time, just you wait!"

"Yes, yes… But why, though? Is it because you're too tense?"

Harry scratched his chin, then opened both palms and held them out.

"Try punching my hands."

"What? Punch them? That'll hurt."

"As if those little fists of yours could hurt me. You could hit me in the face with them and I'd be fine."

"Brace yourself—I'm going to make it hurt!"

Draco advanced with his fists windmilling through the air. The way he clenched and unclenched them with that blazing glare was practically an announcement to the world: I am about to throw a punch. He tensed every muscle in his body, let out a tremendous battle cry, and swung.

"Hyah!"

Thwp.

A thoroughly unimpressive sound for such an elaborate build-up. Harry nodded, having identified the problem.

"You're putting too much force into everything. From your toes to your fingertips—you're absolutely rigid."

"Ugh! Being strong is a good thing!"

"You need to let some of it go. Here."

Harry tapped Draco lightly with his wand. Draco reflexively raised his arms to block—and then, inexplicably, felt all the tension drain out of them. Both arms dropped to his sides like wet rope.

Harry pocketed his wand.

"I've loosened your arms and legs a little with the Jelly-Legs Jinx. Seemed like that was about the level of loosening you needed."

"Wh—what do you think you're doing, casting spells on me without—"

"Seeing is believing. Here, catch."

Harry threw the ball. Draco scrambled to grab it with his rubbery, uncooperative limbs. But the arms under the Jelly-Legs Jinx refused to obey. The ball was already nearly upon him while his hands lagged hopelessly behind.

Draco protested.

"You've jinxed my arms—how am I supposed to catch anything—"

Slip.

"…What?"

Draco's eyes went wide. His limbs had refused to do what he told them—and yet, somehow, he'd caught the ball. Effortlessly, at that.

He was still staring at it in his hand when Harry's voice reached him.

"It's because your arm is slow, so you're receiving it much later in its arc. Up until now you've been lunging forward to meet the ball, haven't you? The longer and slower you track it, the easier it is to predict the trajectory."

"It didn't bounce off this time either."

"Think of a rubber balloon. When it's fully inflated, everything bounces off it. Your hand was exactly like that. Let the air out, and things stick—see? Keeping your arms and shoulders relaxed is what matters. You need to leave room to move. If you hold something in reserve, you have something left to adjust with when something unexpected happens."

"Oh…"

For just a moment, Draco looked at Harry with something approaching admiration. Harry basked in it shamelessly, showing off in front of him with every trick he could think of.

"Want to see something fun? Ta-da—Snitch juggling!"

"Whoa! How do you do that?"

"Simple. You imagine you have three hands. One ball in each real hand, and you treat the air as an invisible third hand holding the third. Every time you release one, you catch another—yap, yap, yap."

"Ohhh!"

"Stop teaching Draco nonsense."

Carina came striding across from the direction of the manor. Harry caught all the Snitches in one clean sweep and turned to face her. Draco was still marvelling at the trick when Carina fixed him with a pointed look.

"Draco. Don't be dazzled by flashy nonsense—focus on practising catching a single Snitch properly. What you need is precision with one. Getting distracted by circus tricks is nothing but a waste of time."

Harry shrugged.

"Well, take it from someone who knows. Your sister was distracted by my tricks once too."

"Don't be absurd. I was never distracted by your acrobatics."

She had merely regarded Harry with contempt for performing them to court attention. She had realised later that he'd only ever done it when she was watching—but that was beside the point. Carina dismissed the memory and held out a broomstick to Draco.

"You've inherited my Firebolt, which means you have an obligation to lead Slytherin to victory. You're still a long way behind me, but your potential isn't bad—so practise harder."

"What? A long way behind you?"

Draco bristled. Carina gave a short, contemptuous laugh and placed her hand on top of his head.
"The fact that you bristle at such a minor provocation is precisely what I mean by 'a long way behind.' Flying a broomstick isn't everything—keep that in mind."

"That's rich, coming from someone who got kicked off the Quidditch team!"

"Excuse me?"

A sibling's jab always finds the exact right nerve. A vein flickered briefly at Carina's temple. She reined herself in and said, with quiet emphasis:

"I stepped down of my own accord because I had other things to attend to. I was not kicked off."

"Hmph. If you'd had real talent, you'd have kept playing Seeker no matter how busy you were."

"Why, you little—"

Hadn't she just said that bristling at minor provocations was a sign of being a long way behind? Harry thought it, but possessed enough self-restraint not to say it aloud. His more reckless moments tended to make people forget it, but Harry was actually quite careful with his words around friends.

"I could still outfly you even after stepping down!"

"Only because of the broomstick."

Harry stepped between the bickering siblings and inserted himself into the gap.

"Are you two really going to settle this with words? Don't you need this?"

He held up a Snitch. Draco and Carina both glanced at it—then turned to glare at each other, slow smiles spreading across their faces.

"Get on the Firebolt, Draco. I'll show you the difference in class."

"Just don't blame the broomstick afterwards, sister."

They snarled at each other and mounted their brooms. Harry grinned and raised the Snitch.

"Right then—I'll referee. Ready… go!"

A sibling rivalry for the ages. The legendary duel that played out at least three times a day commenced. Both of them flew at each other with everything they had.

The last night at Malfoy Manor. Harry had bathed in the wide tub, pulled on a dressing gown, and thrown himself onto the enormous bed. The soft duvet and mattress wrapped around his back like a warm embrace. Moonlight poured through the square window, casting the room in a pale, diffuse glow. In the deep quiet of a manor that seemed to have fallen entirely asleep, Harry lay there and turned over everything he'd done these past weeks.

He'd found a fair number of magical instruments and spells—but nothing decisive. At best, a few pieces of equipment that might help restrain him. Even those were things he'd forced himself to bring along despite knowing they were barely worth the effort.

No real results. But it had been good. Living in a manor as beautiful as a painting, setting every worry aside, spending his days playing with broomsticks and balls. Even when he'd shut himself in the basement full of Dark objects and torn at his hair in frustration, happiness had filled him to the brim.

And every time it did, an uncomfortable question surfaced in his mind.

Could an Unbreakable Vow truly sustain this dream of a life? Would it really be a perfect solution? Or was he, in trying to save his own life—in reaching for his own happiness—simply pushing every threat onto everyone else?

The Death Eaters. If he swore off magic and one of them decided to retaliate—

Or if a situation arose that required Voldemort's power—

After compromise upon compromise, would he still be able to hold to his principles when the moment came?

The answer, surely, would fall from the sky. No matter how deeply he thought, he couldn't find a satisfying answer in dark, damp earth. It offered only the dirty, tenacious compromise of a kudzu root pulled from the soil.

No matter how hard he turned it over, no answer came.

To escape from the vast despair into tomorrow, Harry closed his eyes.

That was when it happened.

Knock. Knock.

Two short raps at the door. Harry sprang upright and crossed to it. As he drew close enough, a voice came from the other side.

"It's me."

"I know."

There was no one else at Malfoy Manor who would come looking for Harry's room. Even setting that aside, connecting the neat, precise knock to Carina required no logic at all. Harry opened the door promptly and welcomed her in.

"What brings you here at this hour? I'm not a Werewolf, but I do get dangerous when the moon is out, so you should be careful about—"

He was speaking lightly, teasing—and then he saw her, and his mouth shut.

She was standing there in nothing but a thin chemise, fingertips toying with the damp ends of her hair. She must have just stepped out of the bath; a cool, dewy fragrance clung to her, like hyacinths that had drunk in rain—the kind of melancholy scent that belonged to a wet flower garden on a rainy night.

The flush on her cheeks was probably from a long soak in hot water. While Harry stood there at a loss for words, Carina said, with her usual cool composure:

"I wanted to discuss… our plan going forward. We go back to Hogwarts tomorrow."

"…Right. Come in."

Harry managed to find his voice and looked around briefly for somewhere to sit. But Carina had already walked straight in and settled herself, with complete naturalness, on the edge of the bed.

Harry paused—then slowly turned and moved toward the door, which was still slightly ajar. He closed it without a sound, and, just in case, locked it as well. Then he came back and sat down beside her on the edge of the bed.

Carina took a moment to gather her thoughts, then spoke.

"…When the term ends—"

She pushed her hair back over her shoulder. The fragrance hidden among the pale platinum strands scattered into the air. It was different from his—the shampoo in each room must have been different. Unless Carina had put on perfume separately.

Harry swallowed and said:

"Ends—?"

"First, let's make sure we sit our exams properly. There's a limit to what we can investigate while we're at school anyway."

"Exams? Now, of all times?"

Voldemort was gone. Dumbledore and Snape were dead. Everything felt unmoored. Even the professors seemed drained of something. At school, the students had all but abandoned their studies—gathering in twos and threes to whisper about recent events. And she was telling him to prepare for exams.

Harry raised an eyebrow. Carina nodded.

"Exams matter. The marks you earn now will determine what you're able to do afterwards."

"Afterwards…? What does it matter. Best case, I lose all my magic."

Harry muttered it quietly. Carina's eyes sharpened and she turned to face him sharply.

"You need to change that attitude! Why are you carrying on as though you're about to die?!"

She leaned forward, pressing into his space. That made things difficult for Harry. The neckline of the chemise dipped in a way that threatened to reveal something, and the warm scent of her skin was filling the air, drawing closer with every passing second. The large eyes looking up at him from below were trying to look angry and still managing to be lovely.
Sensing that if this continued there would be no coming back from it, Harry averted his gaze and answered.

"It's not about dying—it's about necessity. I'm either going to die or lose my magic. So why bother."

"You are going to live. You'll survive somehow and things will be all right. You are not going to die!"

"But—"

"There is no 'but'! You have a way to survive!"

Harry smiled, a little bitterly. He knew what Carina wanted to say. He knew what she felt.

But the way she was saying it—what she was asking of him—was something he simply could not accept.

Carina pressed him harder.

"Putting it off and putting it off is fine. That's the next-best option. But surely surrendering to Voldemort and keeping your life is better than the worst outcome—dying…! Why won't you even consider it? If you just submit to him, you could survive somehow!"

Harry smiled, calm and distant. They might fight over this again and again, any number of times. But Carina's argument would remain the same, and so would his answer.

He took a moment, then said, evenly:

"Sirius died to save me."

"Yes!"

"And that's not all. My father. My mother. They gave their lives for me when I was a baby who couldn't even remember it."

"Then you have all the more reason to live! You have to survive by any means necessary! So why—why are you out there looking for Unbreakable Vows and instruments that put your life at risk?!"

"But would they have wanted me to survive by surrendering to the darkness? The very darkness that took their lives—would submitting to it be the outcome they wanted?"

There was nothing Carina could say to that. At the time of Harry's parents' deaths, the Malfoy family had been Death Eaters. And Sirius Black had died caught in his father's scheme.

Carina, too, had played a part in those deaths. Harry's refusal to blame her didn't give her the right to argue otherwise.

Harry thought of the dead, and murmured, with a hollow sort of sadness:

"Snape. Professor Dumbledore. It was the same with them. Whatever their individual reasons, they threw their lives away trying to protect me."

"…Ignore that. They only ever saw you as a weapon to fight him with."

"Perhaps. But if they staked their lives on it in the end—wasn't that a sacrifice? And wasn't that desire—to defeat Voldemort—so great that it drove them to it? Can I… disregard that will?"

The answer was already decided. Harry gave a small shake of his head.

He had always held his head up and smiled without flinching. When something hurt, he shook it off quickly and got back on his feet. He had disguised himself behind a show of strength, and bluffed to bring his enemies down.

A girl he had met when he first entered the wizarding world. Because he had never forgotten her advice, he had been able to gain so much.

But that didn't mean Harry was always strong.

In places Carina couldn't see, he had cried. He had bitten his lip until it bled. When Sirius died, he had wept openly as he buried him in the cemetery at Godric's Hollow. When Dumbledore died, he had sat in the headmaster's office for an entire day, staring at nothing.

He simply didn't show it. Everyone bleeds. Everyone carries wounds.

"If I surrender, their sacrifices become meaningless. With no one left to stop him, there will only be more victims. And I… I don't want to burn any longer on someone else's life as fuel."

Quietly, without force. Harry said it to Carina.

Carina could not bring herself to say anything. She worked her mouth for a long moment, and in the end found no words that would do. She turned her head sharply away, as though sulking, and murmured:

"Coward."

Accepting one's own death rather than face the deaths of others—was that courage and goodness, or was it simply running away?

And in the meantime, refusing to let anything deeper take root—refusing, out of fear that it would only make leaving harder—refusing any closeness that might become a reason to stay. Was that a firm resolve, or was it weakness?

"Coward…"

Carina covered her eyes with her arm and lay back. The bed swayed, barely. The same bed that had groaned and protested when Harry threw himself onto it received Carina's weight gently, folding around her without complaint.

The moonlight through the window fell across her. The pale, almost luminous skin was not something a single thin chemise could conceal. A faint flush showed through the delicate fabric. Unencumbered by anything beneath, the soft curves of her body pressed against the thin cloth above.

Carina seemed either unaware of how she looked, or entirely indifferent to it. She kept her arm over her eyes and said:

"If you die, I'll die too."

"Don't say things like that, even as a joke, Carina."

"Does it look like I'm joking?"

Her hand was covering her face, so he couldn't see. Not that it mattered—Harry was already finding it difficult to look away from Carina lying there, framed by the bed like a painting. A single layer of fabric so thin it seemed it might tear at a touch, so sheer that even the faint moonlight passed through it. The way it simply rested against her made the outline of her body more arresting than bare skin would have been.

Something traced the corner of his mouth. Harry quickly wiped it away and said, as casually as he could manage:

"Carina. I don't think you realise—your clothes—"

"By whatever means necessary. I won't let you die."

Her voice cut across his. Her body was trembling, faintly but unmistakably—as though she might shatter if no one held her together.

"Whatever it takes…"

He had been deliberately keeping his eyes away from anything that might make him want to live. Afraid that if he let himself, he would find himself utterly unable to die. He had been careful, on purpose. Guarded.

The Unbreakable Vow. A constraint purchased with a life. Even that made Carina shudder—but to Harry, it had seemed like the only answer.

She had tried to stop him, but Harry, who gave way on almost everything else, had been immovable on this. He had been willing to stake his own life for the sake of lives already lost.

So.

"Whatever it takes…"

Transparent tears fell.

He couldn't help it. Harry slowly bent forward to wipe them away. Soft curves grazed the edge of his vision. There was nothing angular about her—no hard lines anywhere. A slender silhouette rose in a breathtaking arc, reached its peak, and curved back down. The neckline of the chemise revealed her collarbone. Absurd as it was, he found himself thinking that even her bones must be soft. He followed the line of her slender throat down to the small face where the tears were falling.

He moved her arm away from her face and looked into eyes as deep and wide as a lake. Beauty must love curves, he thought—there was no other explanation for why Carina could be this soft.

This too would become something he couldn't let go of. But some tests arrive in a form that cannot be avoided. For Harry, Carina was that kind of inevitable assault. He hovered above her and slowly lowered his head.

The chemise was far too thin.

Harry was dreaming.

It had to be a dream. Because the corridor that had appeared to him night after night for the past several months—that long, dark corridor leading to the Department of Mysteries—had appeared again.

As it always did in the dream, Harry moved slowly toward the door at the corridor's end. He reached out his hand, and the door swung open before his fingers even touched the handle. He stepped inside, carried by the sensation that everything was falling neatly into place.

Inside the Department of Mysteries, there were many doors. Each one led to a different field of research, it seemed. It should have been disorienting, but Harry chose one without a moment's hesitation and walked through.

Naturally, it was the Hall of Prophecy. The one he had been searching for all along.

The Hall of Prophecy was an extraordinary place—thousands upon thousands of glass orbs arranged in rows, their shelves rising so high they seemed to brush the night sky. A repository of every prophecy ever made, from the remotest antiquity to the present day.

The orbs were as numerous as stars, yet Harry did not let his gaze wander. He walked with absolute certainty to the ninety-seventh row.

Before a dust-covered orb, a long yellow label bore an inscription.

S. P. T to A. P. W. B. D.

Harry Potter.

His prophecy waited there, waiting for its subject to arrive.

Strange, really.

Harry had never been to the Department of Mysteries. He had no memory of ever seeing a prophecy. And yet—was fate truly a real thing? He had found his prophecy as though guided by some invisible hand. It had been almost startlingly easy...

...That can't be right. When has anything ever gone easily for you?

A voice rose from somewhere deep inside him—endlessly twisted, coiled tight with resentment.

It seemed to belong to someone who found fault with everything in the world, and who was therefore displeased by how smoothly things were going.

Harry answered it.

Don't interfere. Everything is going well.

That's exactly what's wrong, Potter. What in your life has ever gone well?

You spent ten years in that damned uncle's house. Then you found out you were a wizard. You were drunk on the idea of being special—but you were 'more' special than you bargained for. Because a murderous lunatic who wanted to rule the world had singled you out specifically to kill.

Because of that, you've nearly lost your life, put everyone around you in danger, and now you've even been jilted.

Think the world is easy? You think this rotten world is smiling at you?

Everything is going well. Harry repeated it like a parrot.

Then the voice shifted slightly.

Let me put it another way. Those people over there—doesn't it bother you? Doesn't it turn your stomach, watching them so pleased with themselves because some cobbled-together plan happened to work out by sheer luck?

It did bother him. Harry felt a sudden, visceral twist in his gut. Watching those people gloating, laughing—he couldn't bear it. He wanted to ruin those smug faces. He couldn't stop himself.

You have been miserable. And you will go on being miserable. Because even the possibility you found has been stolen from you by those wizards.

Pay them back in kind, Potter. Be true to your nature. You are a bitter, twisted, warped little child.

Harry decided to do exactly that.

The Imperius Curse broke.

"Yes. Hand it over to me, Potter..."

When he opened his eyes, Lucius Malfoy was speaking in a voice brimming with triumph. Harry heard it, looked down at the glass orb in his hand, and considered.

If not for that shameless man, Harry and Carina's relationship would have been ten times better than it was. It had started with the diary in second year—turning the school upside down—and then, as it turned out, the diary was a Horcrux, which had proceeded to tighten its grip around their own throats. It was a genuine mystery how someone like Carina had come from someone like him.

Harry wanted to smash the orb over Lucius's head, but the thought of Carina stopped him.

Instead, he hurled it at the floor.

Crash.

The orb shattered into fragments. A faint wisp of smoke seeped out between the shards.

Lucius stood blinking, staring down at the broken glass at Harry's feet with hollow, uncomprehending eyes. From his perspective, Harry hitting him over the head with it would probably have been preferable—at least then there was a chance it might not have broken.

But Harry had already done it.

The world ground to a halt, and within it, only Harry smiled.
It was a magnificent sight. Harry doubled over laughing. Lucius's dumbfounded face. Bellatrix's expression of pure horror. Karkaroff reaching out uselessly with one hand. Bartemius dropping to his knees in despair, trying to gather up the pieces of glass.

This was, without question, the most satisfying moment of the year. Next time I need a Patronus, this is the memory, Harry thought, cackling.

"—You—! Crucio!"

Harry sucked in a sharp breath. A searing agony seized him—as though someone had reached into his skull and set his brain alight. He opened his mouth wide and rolled across the floor. Shards of broken glass drove themselves into his body.

But the sight of their devastated faces was so unbearably funny. Even as he writhed in pain, Harry laughed like a madman.

"Lucius! What on earth are you doing?! That was your one contribution to this entire operation!"

"Y-yes, my grip on the spell weakened for a moment when I reached for the prophecy! Even I couldn't have anticipated—"

"Does that matter?! What do we do now? The Dark Lord will be furious! We were supposed to bring him the prophecy—!"

Bellatrix clawed at her own face and wailed. Then she turned abruptly to stare at empty air and began to sob.

"Forgive me, Master—I did everything right, please forgive me—"

Voldemort, absent from the room, could not answer. Harry filled the silence instead, still snickering.

"Ha—hahaha! It's all right, I forgive you! Your face was absolutely priceless!"

Bloodied from rolling across the broken glass, apparently incapable of feeling pain, Harry laughed and laughed. The Death Eaters, who had always been the ones to bring chaos and fury to others, were experiencing it from the other side tonight. Lucius and Bellatrix stared blankly at the ruined orb. Karkaroff looked at Harry—at this boy whose spirit had not broken despite everything—with something approaching revulsion.

"...It seems you have no regard for your own life."

Bartemius raised his wand toward Harry. His face, always expressionless, now held something that could only be called rage.

"The only reason you've been kept alive until now is because we needed the prophecy, Potter. But you've just destroyed the reason we were keeping you."

"Is that so? Then in place of the broken prophecy, allow me to make one of my own!"

Shards of glass were embedded in his skin. Blood ran freely enough to blur his vision.

And yet Harry's eyes were still alive. As though he felt neither pain nor fear, he fixed them with the same undiminished hostility.

"You are all going to die miserably—at the hands of an enraged Voldemort! Every sacrifice you've ever made for him will mean nothing, and he will kill every last one of you! That is my prophecy, so make sure you pass it along!"

Even Bartemius found the spectacle faintly unsettling. Five years in Azkaban, then ten years as a puppet under his own father's Imperius Curse. Through all of it, Bartemius had shed his emotions one by one. Only a blind, absolute reverence for his master remained to move him.

But now, watching Harry Potter—bloodied, screaming, unbroken—Bartemius felt something he had not felt since the first time he had stood before Voldemort.

If Harry had been given just a little more time. Or if Voldemort had never existed... Harry might have become something that could stand alongside the Dark Lord himself.

Bartemius shook his head. As long as the Dark Lord lived, and as long as Harry remained his enemy, that potential would be crushed before it could ever fully bloom.

He tightened his grip on his wand to finish Harry off.

But the moment of hesitation had cost him too long.

"*Stupefy*!"

A jet of red light burst into the room. The others ducked aside, but Lucius, who happened to have his back turned, took the Stunning Spell squarely between the shoulder blades and crashed to the floor.

Under covering fire from a volley of wands, Sirius came sprinting toward Harry.

"Harry, are you all right!"

The Order of the Phoenix. They had arrived.

Sirius used to fall into brooding whenever he sat still in Grimmauld Place.

He had joined the Order of the Phoenix straight out of Hogwarts and fought against the Death Eaters. Then, when he learned that Voldemort had set his sights on the Potters, he had risked his life to draw the enemy away—to protect his friends.

But the price of trusting someone he should never have trusted was the loss of his dearest friends, his honour, and twelve years of his life. Unable to give testimony against Peter Pettigrew, he had been buried under a mountain of false charges and thrown into Azkaban.

The years of imprisonment occupied a significant portion of his life, yet they were like a strip of blank film—something that could be cut out and discarded without loss. There were no memories in that time, no feelings. Sirius had simply grown older, hollow.

After the escape, things had been tolerable enough. Harry, who looked so much like James, was just as full of life; Lupin was still the same principled, slightly stiff man he had always been. The cheerful Weasley children had breathed some warmth into the bleak rooms of Grimmauld Place.

He had even tried his hand at teaching. When he was laughing and mucking about with the students, he almost felt like one of them again.

But no matter how close he was to the students, he was not a child. And he had never quite managed to become a responsible adult either.

Sirius was something caught between the two—neither child nor grown man.

Even if he saw James in Harry, Harry was not James. With James, they could argue until it came to blows, and the next morning they would take one look at each other's bruises and burst out laughing, throwing their arms around each other's shoulders—wearing those bruises like badges in the margins of a memory.
With Harry, that could never happen. He was Harry's guardian. He had to protect Harry.

However close they were, however much Harry resembled James—it could never be the same.

He liked the students, enjoyed their company. But every time he thought about having something to teach them, a heavy stone seemed to press down on his chest. He had been so daunted by it that he had even felt a grudging flicker of admiration for Snape, who had been doing the job for nearly a decade.

In the end, under the cover of being a temporary instructor, he had let the lessons slide into something half-improvised and half-abandoned.

He was neither child nor adult, neither student nor teacher. A pureblood who had turned his back on his own family.

He thought he had navigated a long and difficult road on his own terms. But when he occasionally looked back, he saw himself there—drifting, belonging nowhere.

Still. One thing remained that was genuinely adult, genuinely a duty, genuinely his role.

Sirius Black was Harry Potter's guardian.

When Ron had appeared through the fireplace and told him that Harry had gone to the Ministry to report Voldemort's return, Sirius moved faster than he had at any other moment in his entire life.

"*Avada Kedavra*!"

Green light—the colour of murder—flared. Sirius spun aside and let the curse slide past him.

Bellatrix Lestrange. His most despised cousin. The living embodiment of everything rotten in the Black family. Sirius was locked in combat with her.

The Death Eaters' elite were not to be underestimated. Dark curses came at them from angles no one could have predicted.

On top of that, other Death Eaters who had been stationed throughout the Ministry came flooding in. The Order of the Phoenix, who had rushed inside to reach Harry, regrouped and fought their way out of the Hall of Prophecy with him.

Spells tore through the air from every direction. Wizards fell one by one. Light flashed, explosions rang out, and screams echoed through the Department of Mysteries.

On the whole, the Order was outmatched. But then unexpected reinforcements arrived.

The Aurors who had stormed Hogwarts had retreated back to the Ministry. And hard on their heels came the furious Hogwarts students who had been chasing Umbridge—who now came pouring in after them.

"The Ministry is invalid! Invalid!"

The agitated mob marched through the corridors, firing spells in every direction. When they heard the noise from the Department of Mysteries, they had inevitably found their way there.

Sirius shouted urgently at the sight of them.

"Get the children out! This is no place for you!"

He was terrified that the Death Eaters might attack the students or take them hostage.

But the Death Eaters were no less thrown off than Sirius. The Hogwarts students were numerous, and even wild, undirected spells from that many wands were nothing to dismiss.

There was also the matter of facing children who were the same age as their own sons and daughters—which gave them pause. In the end, they began to give ground, however slightly.

Chaos. Pandemonium. An untimely brawl erupted in the depths of the Ministry. Screams and noise, spells flying without direction.

And then Sirius saw it. In the moment his attention had been pulled toward the students, Bellatrix had turned her wand on Harry, who lay fallen on the ground.

Sirius raised his wand quickly, but a Disarming Spell from somewhere knocked it from his hand. He had no time to look for who had cast it.

He had to protect Harry.

Sirius ran with nothing but his body. His limbs felt sluggish, as though he had blundered into a Impediment Jinx. He pushed himself forward, willing his body faster, and threw himself between Bellatrix and Harry before she could finish the incantation.

"*Avada Kedavra*!"

The green light came toward him slowly. Just barely—Sirius's body was faster. The Killing Curse meant for Harry struck Sirius squarely in the chest instead.

The unsparing spell that delivered death to all equally.

For a brief moment, all sound vanished. Sirius's body fell to the ground without resistance. It was as though someone were calling his name in a whisper. Something beyond the Veil had sensed his death and was reaching out a hand...

In the instant before his eyes closed, Sirius saw Harry's eyes—wide open, staring at him. Harry's face held shock and grief in equal measure.

Sirius tried to reassure him. But he could not move even his fingers. He tried to speak, but not only did no sound come—his lips would not move at all.

He tried to do something, anything. But it was no longer his body to command.

Ah. So this is death.

Sirius understood it, belatedly. But it was not as frightening as he had expected. Unlike what people said, there was no flash of his life before his eyes.

That was surely because Sirius had fulfilled his duty.

Sirius Black had been Harry Potter's guardian.

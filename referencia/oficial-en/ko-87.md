Who Dumbledore trusted most was impossible to say.

But the person with whom Dumbledore shared the most secrets was, without question, Severus Snape.

"That child was born to be Voldemort's adversary."

The wand's master had appeared. Dumbledore had said as much after reading the letter Ollivander sent. Snape's face went rigid.

"Why? Because their wand cores are the same? For that reason alone, the child must face him?"

"How surprising. I thought you disliked the boy."

"I do. Probably more than anyone else in this school. But that's entirely beside the point!"

"Voldemort will return someday. And when that time comes, Harry's life will be in danger. If the fight cannot be avoided, shouldn't we prepare for it?"

Dumbledore continued his explanation, unhurried.

Voldemort used a wand made with a feather from the phoenix Fawkes. By coincidence, the wand Harry had chosen possessed the very same core.

And though it was not widely known, there was a phenomenon that occurred when two wands sharing the same core clashed. A resonance arose between the magical sources contained within them; the two became connected, reflecting back the spells that had previously been cast. Between them stretched an echo of all the magic that had passed before.

Priori Incantatem.

The Reverse Spell Effect.

Dumbledore continued.

"The moment the wands connect, their duel becomes something entirely independent of power. In a state of perfect equality, the only thing that decides the outcome is will. In my estimation, Harry is more than capable of defeating him. Harry possesses a formidable will."

"You could face him yourself, Professor Dumbledore."

"Oh, Voldemort will never fight on my terms. He would attack in ways I could never choose to employ. With my methods so constrained, victory would be difficult to secure. But with Harry, the positions would be reversed. Voldemort tried to kill him and found himself bound by any number of restrictions…."

Yet Severus's expression remained troubled. Dumbledore studied his face and asked,

"You seem uneasy. Snape—what did the boy say to you?"

His face contorted without restraint.

Since Lily's death—since he had lost her, who had been the same as the entire world to him—he had believed nothing could wound him again. There was nothing left to take.

It took ten years to understand how arrogant that belief had been. The sins of the past had returned in the shape of her son, tearing open old wounds.

Snape spoke as though forcing the words out through a mouthful of blades.

"…He told me that I killed his parents."

It could not be seen, but blood was flowing from his mouth. Blood that was thick and black, more than ten years old—blood he had thought long since dried.

He had seen glimpses of James in Harry and let himself be petty because of it. He had openly disparaged Harry's potions, taken the Slytherin students' side with undisguised partiality. In ways that even Snape himself could only describe as childish.

And yet Harry had not bent. He had lifted his chin higher and fixed Snape with those defiant eyes. Lily had died and still left behind her pale green eyes in the world. Every time those eyes watched him, Snape was reminded that the past was a binding he could never discard or escape.

The fruit of the love between Lily and James Potter.

What a dreadful resonance that was.

Harry was his sin, and the executioner sent to pass judgment upon him. He was also a creature of chaos—the tender first love of his school days fused with a waking nightmare. Harry, interrogating him for his mother's death, confronting him with his father's face—he was a catastrophe beyond managing, something one could only be swept away by.

Snape spoke, his voice unsteady.

"It would have been better… to send the boy to a good wizarding family."

"So that your guilt might be lessened?"

Dumbledore smiled and shook his head.

"Severus. You must not ease your guilt that way. If you had done so and Harry had grown into an arrogant, self-absorbed child, you would have had reason to despise him—which might have suited you—but… I'm sorry to say, you have not even the right to that."

Snape started to say something and stopped. He had wanted to ask whether Harry should instead have been sent to those dreadful Muggles who would clearly have mistreated him—but it was not Snape, of all people, who had any standing to raise that question.

Because it was he who had caused the deaths of Lily Potter and James Potter.

Snape despised Harry. Harry despised Snape. That they bore each other no goodwill whatsoever was a fact anyone who had spent even a single day at Hogwarts could have told you.

Snape knew Harry's nature. A reckless child who craved attention. Blood told, as they said—he was no different from James in any meaningful way.

The constant quarrels with the Slytherin students were pure James as well. The one thing that could be said in Harry's favour, if anything, was that unlike James he did not shy away from fights he had no chance of winning. From Snape's position—obliged, however reluctantly, to protect the boy—that only made things more troublesome. But regardless.

In Snape's eyes, Harry fought to win popularity.

Particularly when Harry quarrelled with Carina Malfoy—the very embodiment of Slytherin—Snape would sneer at Harry's pettiness and let his contempt show freely.

And yet, at some point, Snape found himself understanding something with quiet, almost effortless clarity. Perhaps because he had experienced it himself once, he recognised it a little sooner than most.

Harry was in love with Carina. Even when they were fighting, he could not conceal his affection for her.

It was not even amusing. Carina Malfoy—representative of Slytherin, a pureblood supremacist, daughter of a Death Eater family—the very distillation of wizarding elitism. How could she possibly be suited to Harry Potter, the boy who had defeated the Dark Lord?

Snape had scoffed, dismissed it, declared it impossible. The likelihood of Lily and Snape ever having been together was considerably greater. At least Lily and Snape had known each other as childhood friends before Hogwarts.

No matter how hard Harry tried—different houses, completely opposing positions. How could it ever come to anything…

They came together in the end. The emotions Snape felt in that moment could not be conveyed in an entire book.

One thing was certain: that book would be a contradictory thing, with different words written on the front and back of a single page.

Lily, whom he loved. James, whom he hated. The child they had made together.

Gryffindor and Slytherin, set against each other.

Harry Potter, who had crossed with ease what Snape had never been able to overcome—and the envy and jealousy with which Snape watched him do it.

As he observed a relationship buffeted by one difficulty after another, Snape felt a base, shameful satisfaction—and yet, in some corner of his heart, a contradictory hope swelled: that they would surmount it all and find their way to each other.

Every single thing Harry did became a regret that tore through Snape's chest.

If he had been as handsome as Harry, or had devoted himself to self-improvement,

if he had been good at Quidditch,

if he had been honest and unguarded,
if he had worn his affection openly instead of hiding it in shame,

if he had stayed by her side instead of keeping company with Death Eaters—

would anything have been different?

No matter how long he turned it over, no answer came. The only certainty was that Snape had not made the best choices, and that even his efforts had fallen short.

Then one day. Carina received orders to kill either Dumbledore or Harry Potter. It was a cruel assignment—and yet, perhaps Snape had been waiting for precisely that moment.

Lives were at stake. Could they truly love each other more than life itself?

And if… if it seemed Carina was going to fail… would you complete what she had set out to do in her place?

If witnessing that was the price of admission, then surely his own life was a fair enough ticket. Snape had sworn he would do it.

Snape opened his eyes slowly. His body would barely move. The aftermath of brutal torture had left him in ruins.

Just as Dumbledore had foreseen, the snake had been a Horcrux. Infiltrate the manor, neutralise the protective enchantments, kill the snake. Snape had carried out the final mission Dumbledore had entrusted to him.

But there was still one thing left for him to do.

Snape had to protect Lily's son. Even at the cost of his life.

It was a vow that superseded any mission—an absolute oath he had made to himself.

"Fools…."

Beyond the wreckage, he could see Harry and Lupin barely holding off Voldemort's spells. Voldemort swept his wand toward them.

"Did you think destroying all my Horcruxes meant you had defeated me? No. No. A Horcrux is merely a tool—one that can be made again…. How utterly laughable."

No—they were not even holding him off. They were being tossed about by spells Voldemort cast with casual ease.

Glass shattered, and shards tore through the air like a storm. Harry and Lupin shielded each other with defensive spells and managed a single retaliatory curse between them; Voldemort deflected it without effort or conjured a silver shield to absorb it.

Priori Incantatem required the spells to collide directly. But Voldemort had no intention of killing Harry outright. He only wished to toy with him until the very edge of death—to demonstrate the gulf between them.

Because of that, Harry and Lupin were forced to survive one near-death moment after another without ever being able to play their trump card.

The assault paused. Voldemort lowered his wand and stood at ease, while Harry and Lupin could not even summon the nerve to counterattack, gasping for breath.

Voldemort surveyed the room with flat, black eyes.

"Is this all?"

The emotion in his face and voice was one of profound disappointment. Snape, collapsed and helpless. Harry and Lupin, barely able to keep themselves upright. Like a potter regarding a failed piece, Voldemort looked down at them with something like dejection.

"Dumbledore always told me. Love—it is the most powerful force in the world, and it would bring fear to its knees. He kept saying it, over and over, all the years he taught at Hogwarts…. But what is this? Is the essence of love to throw one's life away against an unstoppable catastrophe, one by one?"

The expression of someone who had anticipated a magnificent gift and found it wanting. That was precisely the look Voldemort wore as he regarded those who had come to kill him.

"Your parents fought me to the very end…. I gave them the courtesy of advising them to step aside if they wished to live, and still they persisted in irritating me. Yes—I will grant that they succeeded, in their way. I returned, but at least Harry Potter survived in the meantime…. Yet what meaning does that carry? When the end is death regardless."

"Ha—confident, aren't you?"

Even in ruins, Harry's fighting spirit had not dimmed. He swayed on his feet but kept his chin raised as he spoke.

"Go on, then—kill me. Stop talking and do it! The word of someone who's tried to kill me how many times and failed every single time doesn't carry much weight, does it? You call yourself the Dark Lord, but you can't even manage a proper murder, can you?"

It was something Snape had always known: Harry Potter was a genius at provoking his enemies. It was the sort of taunt that would not have been surprising had it driven even Voldemort to hurl the Killing Curse in a fit of rage.

But whether from caprice or something else, Voldemort kept his expression blank and merely flicked his wand. In that instant, the marble floor surged upward and coiled around Harry's arms and legs. It happened in a heartbeat. With a sharp crack of breaking bone, Harry's wand fell to the ground.

No scream followed. The stone that had erupted from the floor had sealed Harry's mouth as well.

"Harry!"

Lupin raised his wand in haste, but Voldemort levelled his own and an unnamed curse crossed the space between them and struck him. Lupin's body folded in half and was flung into a corner like refuse. He went limp—unconscious.

Having dealt with them all, Voldemort turned his gaze to Snape.

"I had no opportunity to ask before…. Why did you betray me, Severus? I cannot fathom it…."

That he had spent some time in a fury over Nagini's death and only now found the leisure to ask—'no opportunity.' Snape let out a hollow laugh and exhaled a ragged breath.

"You… would not understand even if I told you…."

"Since when did you decide that? I merely wish to hear it."

"Then… read it."

Snape released his Occlumency. The solid walls that had enclosed his mind collapsed in an instant, laying bare the dim and lightless past he carried.

Among Snape's memories, certain ones shone with particular brilliance. The moments he had spent with Lily stood out. And then, at a certain point, those radiant memories lost their colour. In a world drained to black and white, Snape was receiving the news of Lily's death.

The world's most accomplished Legilimens read those memories. Then he murmured, as though something had gone out of him,

"…You as well? That thing called love? Was that why you asked me to spare that woman?"

"Yes…."

"And so—you remained at my side, waiting for your chance, in order to avenge her?"

"No. I bear no particular resentment toward you…. It is all my own responsibility. Whom would I blame…."

"Then what is your reason for taking up your wand and rising now?"

Snape gave a small, quiet laugh. It was likely the first time he had smiled since Lily died.

Blood continued to flow without pause from the wound where Nagini had bitten him, and his tortured body was screaming with pain.

But Severus Snape met Voldemort's gaze directly.

"…I am already dead. My brightest years, my happy memories—everything was taken by you… no, by myself. What remains is guilt. What must be done is atonement."

"That woman is already dead. Saving her child will not bring her back."

"I told you. There is nothing left for me. And a person who has already died… can do anything."

The wand was heavy. He raised it nonetheless.
His opponent was powerful. The overwhelming force that Snape had once dreamed of—the kind that could compel anything from anyone. The Dark Lord's immense magic pressed down on him.

The only hope of defeating him was trapped inside stone. Without even a wand. If Voldemort chose to act on that intention, the ember of hope would be snuffed out by the gale.

Of course. To do that, he would first have to kill Snape.

"Even if that means standing against you."

"If that is the whole of 'love,' I may find myself disappointed."

A massive shockwave erupted from Voldemort's body. An omnidirectional assault that shattered everything in its path with overwhelming force. The only way to resist it was to wrap one's entire body in a defensive charm.

But the moment he spent even one move on defence, he could no longer stop Voldemort. He would only drown, swept away by the seamless, unbroken current of magic.

Smooth, beautiful, and devastating. The power that had once blinded Snape.

And so.

"*Avada Kedavra!*"

Snape abandoned defence and attacked. While the shockwave tore through his body, the green light at the tip of his wand sought out the gap in Voldemort's guard. The killing curse that no defensive magic could stop.

Voldemort conjured his silver shield and blocked it. The Killing Curse struck the shield without effect, producing only a deep, resonant toll—like a gong. In the meantime, Snape's body, reduced to wreckage, rolled to a stop at Harry's feet.

Using the time he had bought, Snape turned the stone binding Harry soft and yielding. Harry wrenched himself free with violent effort.

"Pitiable…. To sacrifice a hard-won opportunity merely to free Harry Potter from his bonds…. I cannot begin to understand what that is… I find it simply pitiable now."

The moment Harry was free, he shouted.

"If everyone else understands it and you alone don't—doesn't that make you the strange one? Maybe the reason you hate Muggles is self-loathing. Muggles don't know magic, but you don't know love—and even Muggles know what that is!"

It was a remarkably well-timed provocation. Voldemort appeared to have lost interest entirely; he looked as though he had no intention of leaving either Snape or Harry alive.

"Idle bickering…. One need only test it directly. If love prevents your death… then it exists. If you die, then love is nothing more than an illusion."

Voldemort raised his wand and aimed it at Harry. Murderous intent swelled like something monstrous. This time, truly, he would use the Killing Curse—he would finally be rid of the insufferable Harry Potter. If Harry could meet it with a spell of his own and trigger Priori Incantatem—

"*Avada Kedavra.*"

But Voldemort's curse came too fast. For Harry, with a broken arm, to retrieve his wand and speak an incantation—the Killing Curse was already too close.

The instant Snape understood that, he spread both arms wide and pulled Harry against him.

Harry's eyes went wide. He wore the expression of someone who had received something they had never, in any world, expected from this particular person.

That was fair. Snape had always been cruel to Harry—had acted with cold calculation, even to the point of using Carina.

Snape had driven Carina Malfoy—that pure, good-hearted girl—into sacrificing herself. Harry, who had always been acutely sensitive to anything concerning Carina's safety, had understood that immediately.

But Carina had not been the only one to stake her life.

If Carina had been unable to withstand the coercion and had tried to kill Harry Potter—what would have become of Snape, who had sworn to help her?

Snape had been obligated to protect Lily's child. It was an absolute oath he had made to himself, one that stood above even the Unbreakable Vow.

If the poisoned cup had gone to Harry—if Carina had tried to kill him—Snape would have saved Harry, killed Carina, and then forfeited his own life as the price of breaking his vow. Snape, too, had placed his life on the table.

Fortunately, Carina had chosen to sacrifice herself and stand against the Dark Lord.

And so Snape would spend his life on this, as well.

"I…."

Snape held Harry close and looked into his pale green eyes with an expression of genuine, unguarded pain. Every other feature was stamped with James—but those eyes, hatefully, were Lily's.

"…I despise you."

Holding those eyes that made him feel endlessly, hopelessly foolish.

Snape felt the Killing Curse meet his back.

Snape's most radiant years. Before things had gone wrong with Lily—what might have been his last chance.

Lily had tilted her head to one side.

'Half-blood? Why? Isn't that brilliant?'

'No. They think anyone who isn't pureblood is inferior….'

'I don't believe that for a second. And it's not just because I'm Muggle-born! But Sev, you're proof—and there are so many other half-bloods who are extraordinary!'

Young Snape had listened to that and smiled like an idiot before saying, in a voice that barely carried,

'I… I think you're extraordinary too, even though you're Muggle-born….'

'Thank you! The fact that you can say something like that already makes you ten times better than the other purebloods! Gryffindors, Slytherins—all those purebloods are so arrogant, I can't stand any of them!'

She had clearly meant James and Sirius, the troublemakers of Gryffindor. The mere fact that Lily found them disagreeable was enough to make Snape feel something like salvation.

Lily, having thoroughly vented her feelings, seemed to remember something.

'Sev, your mother's maiden name was Prince, wasn't it?'

'Er….'

'So there are two brilliant things about you. Half-blood, and a prince!'

Lily clapped her hands.

'Right, I've decided. Sev, you don't have a nickname yet, do you? From today, your nickname is the Half-Blood Prince! I'll call you that every day so you can be proud of it. Two brilliant things means you're twice as brilliant!'

'Half-Blood Prince? Who on earth would use a nickname like that….'

Snape grumbled, and Lily smiled like the sun and said,

'You and me! Isn't that enough?'

It was enough.

It had been, without question.

Brring, brring—.

The phone rang. Carina picked up her smartphone, pressed the answer button, and brought it to her ear. She swept her platinum-blonde hair back so it wouldn't get in the way, gave a small, deliberate cough, and spoke.

"Hello?"

[Ahem, ahem. Yes, good day, ma'am. This is Lloyds Bank calling. We're contacting you regarding an issue that has arisen with your account.]

A rough, unfamiliar male voice came through the line.

Lloyds Bank—that was where Carina had recently opened an account.

Harry had explained it to her: Muggles received only numbers written on a piece of paper called a bankbook. There were no vaults assigned to each family.

When she'd asked why, he'd told her it was because Muggles didn't possess treasures or enchanted objects. Carina had understood immediately. If the only currency in the world were a single Galleon, there would be no need to distinguish yours from mine. They're all the same Galleon, after all.

In any case, Carina turned her attention back to the voice coming through the phone.

"What sort of issue?"

[Ahem. We at Lloyds Bank have observed suspicious activity on the cards of several customers. It appears, ah, that your card may have been cloned.]

"Cloned?"

Carina clicked her tongue.

"This is precisely the problem with Muggles. How does one even manufacture a card that can be cloned?"

[I beg your—pardon?]

"It would be unthinkable at Gringotts. Then again, they verify with a wand there. I suppose goblins managing things is rather more competent than Muggles."

[I—I'm terribly, sorry?]

The man stumbled over his apology. Muggle banks, it seemed, couldn't even manage their staff properly. Carina clicked her tongue again and continued.

"Very well. What am I supposed to do? Should I withdraw everything from the account?"

[Please read us your card number.]

"My card number?"

[Yes. If you would kindly read us the number printed on your card, we'll take care of everything from our end. Heh heh.]

A Muggle with not a single redeeming quality, right down to that witless laugh. Carina muttered irritably and picked up her card.

"You claim to have detected the problem from your own bank, yet you don't even know the card number. Utterly incompetent."

[Er...]

"Never mind. Can you at least write this down?"

[Of course!]

There was a scrambling sound from the other end of the line. Had they been face to face, one look at Carina's withering expression would have rendered him speechless entirely. Making no effort to conceal the contempt in her voice, Carina read out the number on the back of the card.

When she'd finished, she replied in a tone that made clear she found the whole affair tiresome.

"Done. Sort it out quickly."

Click. The line went dead without so much as a goodbye. Carina set the phone down and returned to what she'd been doing.

"Carina, I'm back!"

Just then, Harry came through the hotel room door. Carina, who had been wearing a thoroughly sour expression, brightened at once and greeted him as though nothing had happened.

"Harry. Just now, I—mmph."

The rest of Carina's sentence was swallowed by Harry's mouth. With complete ease, he slipped an arm around her shoulder and walked her backwards—using nothing but lips and tongue—until they were nearly at the bed.

The two of them forgot whatever they'd meant to say and lost themselves in each other. The mood had ripened to perfection, and Harry was just reaching for the hem of her thin shirt when—

Something surfaced in Carina's mind.

"There was a phone call just now."

"What? You only got that number last week. Who from?"

"Some man. He said my card had been cloned by the bank."

"Oh?"

Harry listened as Carina recounted the whole thing, then nodded slowly.

"That's vishing."

"Vishing?"

Harry explained. It was a scheme where someone impersonated a bank or a trusted contact, extracted financial information, and drained the account. People who didn't know better fell for it all the time.

Carina, having heard the explanation, could no longer contain herself.

"That man—he tried to deceive me?"

"Easy, Carina. Let's just freeze the card first, all right? There'll be some loss, but there's a spending limit, so they can't take everything—"

"Out of my way! I'm calling him back!"

Carina shoved Harry aside and marched toward the phone. Harry clicked his tongue in disappointment, cast a longing glance at the bed, then stood and followed after her.

"Carina. That number probably won't go through. So just let it—"

"Hello! You—how dare you deceive me?!"

"Wait, it's ringing? How?"

While Harry stared in disbelief, the man on the other end burst into laughter.

[Well, well. It's the mark from before, isn't it? Ha! I'm making excellent use of that card!]

"You picked the wrong person."

[Oh, I picked exactly right! Got myself a fool who thought something was off and still read out every digit!]

"A fool?"

Carina exhaled softly—a short, quiet breath—and pushed her hair back, her voice dropping to something quietly ominous.

"Listen carefully. I am going to find you. And when I do, I will make you pay for this."

[What, are you Liam Neeson now? Ha. How exactly are you going to find me? You think the police are going to mobilise for some ordinary person's complaint?]

"No. Someone else will find you for me. We happen to have exactly the right person for this sort of thing."

The corner of Carina's mouth curved upward. It was a smile she hadn't worn in a very long time—the one she used to put on when she was about to make someone's life thoroughly unpleasant.

"Voldemort, specifically."

[Vol... demort? Who on earth is that?]

"Found you."

The man must have felt something cold pass through him, because he hung up. But it was already too late. He had spoken that name aloud.

Carina was on her feet in an instant, pulling on her clothes. Harry clicked his tongue wistfully and looked at the bed, then stood and followed her.

"That spell on his name... you never lifted it?"

"It's useful to have around."

She answered offhandedly, tugging on a pair of skinny jeans and a loose hoodie, then picked up her wand. Harry shrugged back into his jacket and went after her.

Vishing could be a professional operation, but just as often it was ordinary people acting on impulse. No particular expertise was required, and without the enthusiastic cooperation of law enforcement, perpetrators were rarely caught.

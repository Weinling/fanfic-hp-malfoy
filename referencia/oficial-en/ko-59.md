Hagrid had vanished from Hogwarts.

When a three-metre giant who had always been there to greet students simply disappeared, it couldn't go unnoticed. Many students wondered where he'd gone—whether they'd been fond of him or not.

Harry, of course, knew exactly where Hagrid was.

Over the summer, Hagrid had left for Europe on Dumbledore's orders, tasked with making contact with the giants. The aim was to negotiate a peace of sorts—to keep them from entering into an alliance with Voldemort.

Knowing this, Harry wasn't surprised when Professor Grubbly-Plank took over Care of Magical Creatures in Hagrid's absence.

"Today's lesson is on Bowtruckles. They live exclusively in trees whose wood is used for wand-making. They are highly magical and significant creatures, and you'll need to learn how to interact with them properly."

"Harry, pay attention! These things come up constantly on O.W.L.s—they're important!"

But the more Professor Grubbly-Plank conducted proper lessons—and the better the reviews she received—the harder it became not to feel a pang of something like regret.

"Why did Hagrid spend all of last year teaching us about Blast-Ended Skrewts instead of Bowtruckles and other creatures?"

"He simply thought we'd enjoy them. And we never complained to his face because we didn't want to hurt his feelings."

Hermione replied with characteristic composure. Harry muttered, his expression faintly mournful.

"If Blast-Ended Skrewts had come up on the O.W.L.s, at least you would've enjoyed studying them, Hermione."

"I very much doubt that. I'm genuinely fond of Hagrid, but that doesn't mean I want his favourite creatures appearing on our exams."

Hermione sketched a Bowtruckle on her parchment as efficiently as possible, then added footnotes and meticulously catalogued its characteristics. The small illustration paired with twice as much written description made it look like a page from a textbook.

"Harry—never mind that. What are you going to do about Malfoy?"

Hermione said it as though in passing. Harry turned to look at Carina with an expression even more subdued than before.

Surrounded by her Slytherin companions, Carina was currently facing down Ron. She wore a smile of effortless superiority, her tone imperious as she spoke down to him.

"I heard from my father. Minister Fudge intends to have unqualified professors removed from Hogwarts. The first to go will be—well, it's rather obvious, isn't it?"

"Shut up! Hagrid is not unqualified!"

"Proving that would require a fair evaluation. But until last year, he was bringing dangerous hybrid creatures into class. He has no concept of risk. If he got his hands on a Basilisk egg, he'd probably coddle it until it hatched and then use it for a lesson."

The argument was sound, and the facts were ones even Ron couldn't flatly deny. Even the Gryffindors who were broadly sympathetic to Hagrid found themselves without a ready answer.

Having pressed her point home, Carina added a more pointed barb beneath the surface.

"Perhaps it's the giant blood. His thinking simply isn't like that of ordinary people. Shouldn't someone like that be kept separate from the rest?"

"I said shut up!"

Ron snarled. Professor Grubbly-Plank came striding over.

"Have you all finished your observations of the Bowtruckles? Hurry along! They're patient creatures, but waste their time and make them uncomfortable, and they'll remember it forever!"

Carina and the Slytherins turned away with smiles of quiet satisfaction. Pansy Parkinson fell into step behind Carina, tossing a parting sneer at the still-fuming Ron.

Hermione turned to Harry.

"You ought to decide where you stand, Harry."

As she said it, her gaze moved between Harry and Carina with something like sympathy for both.

"Malfoy seems to have already made her choice."

Harry said nothing.

Just then, the door to the nearby greenhouse swung open and a line of fourth-years filed out, Ginny among them. She spotted Harry and came running over with a bright smile.

"Harry!"

Sirius stood before the fireplace and tossed in a handful of Floo Powder. The flames roared to life in brilliant green. Without a moment's hesitation, he thrust his head into the fire and called out.

"12 Grimmauld Place!"

Much to Snape's misfortune, the fire did not see fit to burn Sirius's face. His head traveled through the Floo Network and emerged in the fireplace of a wizarding manor.

12 Grimmauld Place was the Black family estate, owned by Sirius. The Black family had long since struck Sirius from their family tree, but a series of unfortunate incidents had left him the last surviving member of the line—and the rightful owner of the house.

Sirius himself had no great fondness for the inheritance, but a wizarding manor protected by powerful enchantments was an extraordinarily useful thing, and Dumbledore had made the building the headquarters of the Order of the Phoenix.

Not that there were more than two people in the house at the moment.

Sirius's face appeared in the fireplace at 12 Grimmauld Place. As arranged, Lupin was waiting by the hearth.

"Remus! Good to see you!"

"I'm well enough. Never mind me—what about Harry? Is he safe?"

"Safe might be a stretch, but one thing's certain: the Triwizard Tournament hasn't managed to hurt him. Harry took the top score in both the first and second tasks with absolutely brilliant thinking!"

"That is remarkable. I always knew he was talented, but to distinguish himself so thoroughly—"

"And do you know what he did in the second task? He flew his broomstick straight into Snape's office and walked out with Gillyweed. You should have seen Snape's face. It was a sight to behold."

Sirius delivered this with enormous satisfaction, but Lupin, who had spent the better part of last year in Snape's company, could not quite bring himself to laugh.

"Doing something like that. The Snape I saw last year didn't seem the sort to tolerate it at all. You may need to protect Harry from him."

"Well, what can Snape do about it? As long as Mad-Eye's there, he won't dare put a foot wrong."

The two exchanged the usual small talk. Sirius spoke mostly of Harry—what he'd gotten up to, how much he resembled James.

Watching him carry on, he was every inch the proud parent. The only difference was that he felt no shame whatsoever about Harry's misdeeds. The more dangerous the prank, the more proudly he recounted it. Lupin couldn't decide whether Sirius simply lacked the instincts of a godfather, or whether being a godfather was precisely what gave him license to speak that way.

Perhaps Sirius hadn't changed at all, and Lupin had simply become too dull an adult.

Lupin smiled faintly and got to the point.

"Right. You said Moody's back from his assignment? When will he be returning to the house?"

"Probably not before the Tournament ends."

"What? Why?"

Sirius looked genuinely puzzled by Lupin's urgency.

"Because he still hasn't identified whoever's been targeting Harry. Sorry, but I'll have to leave the next mission's preparations entirely in your hands. I'm counting on you."

"Wait. You mean I have to stay in this house? Alone with Tonks?"

"What's the problem with—oh."

The meaning clicked, and Sirius's face broke into the roguish grin of his Hogwarts days—charming and conspiratorial in equal measure.

"Ha. What are you on about? I know you. You're not the type to make a move on a woman just because you happen to be in the same house. If you were, you'd have dated half the girls at Hogwarts. What are you so worried about? Have you gone and fallen for Tonks?"

"That's absurd. The age difference alone—"

"How much of a difference can there really be?"

"Tonks is your cousin's daughter. She was four years old when I graduated from Hogwarts."

"She's an adult now. Age, background, family, being a werewolf—you've always let those things weigh on you far too much."

Sirius shrugged. Lupin couldn't see the gesture through the flames, but the sentiment came through perfectly clearly.

He wasn't the least bit troubled by Lupin's concerns. He was simply enjoying himself.

"Besides, if you did want to pursue Tonks, I wouldn't stop you for a second. Turning into a beast once a month is a rather thin reason to keep women at arm's length. You ought to know you're remarkably decent, even by ordinary standards. Even when you've transformed, you—"

"Sirius."
"Heh, only joking. Oh, and naturally, if you're both so inclined, feel free to use the bed. Assuming you don't mind one that's several hundred years old."

"Sirius!"

As far as Sirius could tell, Lupin was rather taken with Tonks. If he weren't, he'd have treated her the way he'd treated women at Hogwarts—with the detached courtesy of someone tending to a rare creature he expected never to encounter again.

Sirius resolved to summon the house-elf to Hogwarts, as a belated gift to his friend's long-overdue spring. Two people alone together would serve better than three—even if the third was a house-elf.

"I'm trusting you with this because I trust you. Unless Andromeda's given you strict orders not to be left alone with her daughter?"

"...No, nothing like that."

"Then don't overthink it. You're both adults, aren't you?"

"Even so, she's still young."

"You're still going on about that."

Sirius spoke with the easy detachment of a man watching someone else's house burn.

"Regardless, keep preparing for the Gringotts job. To get into the Lestrange vault, we'll need Tonks—and her abilities as a Metamorphmagus."

A Metamorphmagus was a witch or wizard born with the innate ability to alter their physical appearance at will. Tonks had been able to change her hair and features freely since birth. That gift for transformation had been central to her becoming the youngest Auror on record.

It would be just as central to this particular act of theft.

Tonks—niece of Bellatrix Lestrange, and a Metamorphmagus—was the linchpin of the entire operation.

"Right, then. I'll be off. Enjoy yourselves. Though don't push her too far—we need her in one piece for Gringotts."

"Get out, Sirius."

The teasing grin didn't leave Sirius's face until he vanished entirely. The green flames crackled and died, and the fireplace returned to its ordinary red glow.

Lupin exhaled slowly.

Sirius had gotten one thing wrong.

Remus Lupin was a penniless man without a steady job, a werewolf who became a beast with every full moon.

He knew his own circumstances better than anyone. The thought of becoming someone's partner had never once crossed his mind—not seriously. It would only make things harder for the other person.

He had always believed that. He had always acted accordingly. And yet—

"Remus?"

Tonks appeared from the far end of the room. She must have just bathed; the ends of her light brown hair were slightly damp, and she came into the kitchen wrapped in a white dressing gown.

When they'd first met, she'd had spiky pink hair and the unmistakable look of someone fresh out of school. But over the course of their shared missions, Tonks had been changing—gradually, almost imperceptibly, using her Metamorphmagus abilities.

Lupin had assumed it was simply a hobby of hers. That was what he'd thought, until a few days ago.

"Who were you talking to?"

"Sirius. An old friend of mine—and the owner of this house. We're imposing on his hospitality, so it seemed right to let him know."

"Ah."

Tonks gave a small sound and settled into the seat across from him. The dressing gown was a size too large and covered her with only the barest margin, though she didn't appear to notice.

Or perhaps she was pretending not to.

"I only have vague memories of him from when I was very young, but when he was in Azkaban, my mother used to say it often. That he couldn't have betrayed the Potters. That there had to be some misunderstanding."

"That's a relief. Saves me the trouble of explaining."

Tonks opened her mouth with a trace of unease.

"...He's not coming back to the house, is he?"
"No. He said he'd be staying at Hogwarts a while longer. The Tournament isn't over yet, so—"

Lupin caught himself mid-sentence and looked at her.

Tonks's eyes lit up with something unmistakably like hope. Whatever was happening at Hogwarts was clearly of far less interest to her than the fact that Sirius would not be returning to the house.

More precisely—she seemed quite pleased by the fact that she and Lupin were alone here.

Over the months they'd spent investigating the Lestrange family together, Tonks had been changing, little by little. The pink hair had gradually shifted to a warm light brown. The face that had once struck him as almost uncomfortably young had softened into something he could no longer find any reason to object to.

Months of shared work. The offhand remarks Lupin had let slip without thinking. The small clues he'd scattered without meaning to.

Tonks had gathered them, one by one, and assembled them into a single ideal—then changed herself toward it, so slowly that Lupin hadn't noticed until it was done.

Without quite knowing when it had happened, Tonks had become exactly what he'd always imagined.

Lupin could not bring himself to deny it.

The woman of his dreams sat across from him in nothing but a dressing gown, and with unhurried elegance, produced a bottle of wine and two glasses.

"We ought to celebrate. Even the busiest Auror takes a day off after finishing a mission."

"Ah, well—"

He couldn't push her away, either. Tonks had never once said a word about her feelings aloud.

In a situation like this, how was he supposed to say: I've noticed you've changed yourself into my ideal type, which suggests you have feelings for me—I'm sorry, but I have no intention of being with anyone.

If he'd been capable of that, he wouldn't have been alone all these years.

There was nothing for it. Lupin set aside the noise in his head and picked up his glass.

"I'm tired, so just a quick toast."

Tonks smiled with her eyes.

Lupin noticed the small flutter in his chest—and, as he always did in moments like this, turned his thoughts deliberately toward the beast inside him.

The creature that surfaced with every full moon howled through him, trampling whatever warmth had begun to stir. As if someone like you could ever be with anyone. It mocked him.

The feeling subsided. It always did. The moment he remembered what he was—a werewolf—whatever small hope had begun to swell would collapse like a punctured balloon.

Lupin raised his glass with professional composure. Whatever Tonks might feel, the months of working together had built a genuine warmth between them, and there was no shortage of things to talk about.

The trouble they'd run into at the Lestrange estate. The meeting with Andromeda. How they were going to get inside Gringotts.

They refilled their glasses and kept talking.

"...I don't love the idea of having to become that woman... but there's no other way... oh—"

Tonks spilled her wine.

It ran from her lips in a thin red line, tracing the curve of her throat before disappearing into the hollow of her chest. The alcohol evaporated quickly, leaving only a faint stain against pale skin before vanishing entirely.

"I've made a mess of myself..."

Tonks said it with a look of mild distress. But beneath that look, unmistakably, was something else—something bright and intent.

You idiot. What are you waiting for.

The beast inside Lupin stirred and whispered, in the voice of a beast.

A Metamorphmagus.

Because they could change their appearance at will, they sometimes lost track of who they truly were.

That was why a Metamorphmagus sought a partner. Their appearance would settle into the shape of that person's ideal. In finding their one match, they finally became complete.

Nymphadora Tonks had, at last, become wholly herself.

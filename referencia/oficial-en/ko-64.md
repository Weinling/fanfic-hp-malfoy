Cedric Diggory, Hogwarts' proudly elected Head Boy, knocked on Umbridge's office door. From within came a simpering voice: "Come in." Cedric pushed the door open—firmly, but not rudely.

"Professor! This is completely unjust!"

"Mr. Diggory. A greeting first, if you please."

Cedric caught himself at her correction and offered one.

"I apologize, Professor Umbridge. I got ahead of myself."

"A greeting."

"Good afternoon, Professor Umbridge."

"Very good, Mr. Diggory."

Umbridge's face, as she received his greeting, was the picture of contentment. On the wall behind her desk, her certificate of appointment as Hogwarts High Inquisitor hung in a rose-adorned frame as though it were a trophy. She tapped the brim of her new black hat—glossy and self-important, clearly purchased to mark the occasion—and received Cedric with an air of exaggerated warmth.

"Now then, Mr. Diggory. What brings you here?"

"It's about Harry Potter's Quidditch situation."

Cedric kept his voice measured.

"Quidditch is an inter-house competition. It's a matter of honour and pride for everyone involved. And yet I'm told you've revoked Harry's eligibility to play—along with the Weasley twins."

"That's correct."

"I'd ask you to reconsider. Harry was wrong to strike Miss Malfoy, I won't argue that—but stripping three players of their eligibility over it is an excessive response. In practical terms, you're telling Gryffindor they have no season this year."

Umbridge listened to the end, then smiled and tapped her desk.

"You and Mr. Potter were rivals, weren't you, Mr. Diggory?"

"Rivals would be generous. I was on the losing end, consistently."

"You faced each other in the Triwizard Tournament as well."

"There was a significant age gap. And Igor Karkaroff deliberately assigned him the most punishing tasks. The conditions were stacked against him."

The smile on Umbridge's face flickered with a trace of displeasure.

"Are you suggesting, Mr. Diggory, that Igor Karkaroff was involved in some sort of conspiracy?"

"There's no other way to account for the irregularities in last year's Tournament."

Cedric pressed on, oblivious to the way her expression was cooling.

"Harry faced a Hungarian Horntail. He was sent to the bottom of the lake without having mastered Advanced Transfiguration. In the final task, he encountered twice the obstacles I did."

"And?"

"And—what do you mean, 'and'? Harry never wanted to be a champion in the first place. As Professor Moody said, someone must have placed a Confundus Charm on the Goblet of Fire and entered his name. That person was clearly—"

"Mr. Diggory." Umbridge's voice remained pleasant. "The Ministry conducted a full investigation and closed the matter. It is not the place of a student—however well-intentioned—to second-guess those findings. What you're implying constitutes a challenge to the authority of the Department of Magical Law Enforcement, the Minister himself, and—I might add—to me, as Hogwarts High Inquisitor."

Cedric fell silent. Umbridge, visibly gratified by the weight of her own authority, laced her fingers together and continued.

"Let me be plain, Mr. Diggory. Harry Potter is a boy who has let his fame go to his head. He used some underhanded method to cross the Age Line, entered his name in the Goblet of Fire without authorisation, and then, when his own abilities proved insufficient to win, invented a convenient villain to blame for his failure."

"That's not—"

"Do not interrupt me, Mr. Diggory."

The smile never left her face, but her tone made clear that no rebuttal would be entertained.

"He lost because he wasn't good enough. Rather than accept that, he invents a Karkaroff conspiracy to make himself look more sympathetic. In reality, he is simply a troubled child. And the most effective way to manage a troubled child is to deny him the spotlight he craves."

"I can't believe that. Harry is—"

"Mr. Diggory."

Umbridge smiled at him with an intimacy that made his skin crawl.

"I have great expectations for you. I've thought from the beginning that a Head Boy who is both reasonable and kind is exactly what this school needs."

"Thank you."

"But part of a Head Boy's responsibility is to hold his peers accountable. Those in positions of authority must be consistent in both reward and consequence."

"I'm sorry?"

Cedric had never thought of himself as being above his fellow students by virtue of his title. And the idea of punishing Harry had never once crossed his mind.

He didn't understand her meaning immediately.

"What I'm asking of you, Mr. Diggory, is to administer appropriate discipline. Young people need guidance to keep them from going astray. You've been too lenient to provide it—which is why I had to step in. The fact that matters have reached this point is, in part, a failure of your oversight."

"Then—"

"Denied. And Mr. Diggory—if you have any ambitions of joining the Ministry after graduation, you would do well to understand that true leadership sometimes means guiding a friend back to the right path, even when it's uncomfortable."

Umbridge was asking him to help her persecute Harry.

Cedric was slower than most to reach that conclusion. He had trusted people—trusted the Ministry—in the way that only a Hufflepuff truly could. The depth of the betrayal he felt now was proportionate to that trust.

He stood, his expression gone rigid.

"Good afternoon, Professor Umbridge."

He left without another word, his strides long and deliberate.

Umbridge's smile remained in place.

Cedric went directly to Harry.

"I'm sorry, Harry. I couldn't get through to her."

"Why are you apologising? Everyone already knows Umbridge is a deaf toad."

The bluntness drew a rueful smile from Cedric.

He didn't want to admit it, but adults were pettier than he'd given them credit for—petty enough to drag students into their quarrels.

He made his decision, and turned to address Harry and the group he'd assembled.

"Everyone, hear me out. I've reached my own conclusion."

Cedric Diggory was Head Boy. His duty was to the students.

The students. Not the school, not the rules—those were for professors and headmasters to uphold. As the students' representative, Cedric's allegiance had always been, and would always be, to them.

Cedric was diligent and decent. He also felt a deep sense of obligation to deliver victories to the people who fell under his care.

He clenched his fist.

"I'm authorising the formation of a Defence Against the Dark Arts study group—Dumbledore's Army. And I'll be joining it myself."

"You heard him."

Harry turned, and the response was immediate: most of the Gryffindors and Hufflepuffs, along with a handful of Ravenclaws, erupted in enthusiastic cheering.
These were students who had already been let down by Umbridge's abysmal teaching. Whatever slander was being levelled at Harry from certain quarters, Hufflepuff and Gryffindor stood firmly behind him—and they were all thoroughly fed up with her classes.

Hogwarts students were, sadly, accustomed to half-hearted instruction. But that didn't make their anxiety about O.W.L.s any less acute.

Do you honestly think you'll pass your O.W.L.s sitting through Umbridge's lessons? Are you prepared to cast a spell in the exam hall that you've never actually practised?

No... what do we do?

That's exactly what the Defence Against the Dark Arts practical club is for. D.A.! Sign up now!

Harry had recruited by targeting their anxiety. In the blink of an eye, the sign-up list had swelled to thirty names.

With Cedric secured, Harry moved forward without hesitation.

"Right. From now on, we'll meet regularly to practise the spells covered in Defence Against the Dark Arts. I've already scouted a location—I'll let everyone know at the next meeting."

"Um... do we need permission from Professor Umbridge?"

Marietta Edgecombe, pressed close to Cho Chang's side, glanced nervously around the room.

"Defence Against the Dark Arts is her subject, isn't it? Is it really all right to do this without her approval?"

"Of course it is!"

Harry answered without missing a beat.

"Why would Professor Umbridge object to students studying her own subject on their own time? Do you think a teacher would actually resent students for wanting to learn?"

"I... no."

"Neither do I! If someone like that existed, they'd have no business calling themselves a teacher!"

The implication—that Umbridge had no business calling herself a teacher—was not lost on several students, who stifled laughter.

"And we'll be meeting in secret, so Umbridge won't have the faintest idea what we're doing."

With that, Harry distributed a Galleon to each person present.

"These are Enchanted Galleons. They're the tokens of our club—only members carry them. When I set the date for our next meeting, the serial number on your Galleon will change to show it."

Harry demonstrated. The Galleons in everyone's hands grew warm and began to shift. The students looked back and forth between Harry and the coins, wide-eyed.

Terry Boot stared at his in astonishment.

"This is N.E.W.T.-level Transfiguration! Harry, you can do this?"

"Hermione made them."

"Ah."

Of course. He turned the coin over in his fingers, curiosity undimmed.

"One more thing—don't get any ideas about spending these. They're charmed fakes. Lose yours and you'll be buying a replacement for one real Galleon. Now—dismissed!"

The students pocketed their Enchanted Galleons and dispersed in small clusters. Most of them looked more excited than anxious; the idea of belonging to a secret society had clearly appealed to something in them.

Once the room had cleared, Cedric approached Harry.

"Do you think this will actually help?"

"Without a doubt. Thank you, Cedric."

Harry extended his hand with a broad smile, and Cedric clasped it, smiling back.

Yielding the Triwizard Cup had been the right call. Cedric was a good person—popular, well-liked, and willing to take on thankless work without complaint.

Getting this much out of someone with a small favour and a mild sense of obligation. Quite the bargain.

Harry smiled inwardly and resolved to keep this kind, obliging—well, friend—close for a long time to come.

Cedric seemed to remember something and asked,

"By the way, why did you name it Dumbledore's Army, Harry? Anyone who hears it is going to find it suspicious."

Harry answered as though the answer were obvious.

"That was deliberate. If things go sideways, I can always invoke Professor Dumbledore's name as cover."

"Oh."

The logic was so unexpected that Cedric's jaw dropped.

Harry left the rest to Cedric and headed for the library.

Things were about to get considerably busier. He needed to select the defensive spells he'd teach the others, and practise them himself beforehand. Tedious work—but if he was going to push back against Umbridge's escalating interference, he needed a power base.

Fortunately, or unfortunately, the loss of Quidditch practice had freed up a great deal of time. He had people to lean on—Hermione, Fred, George. Harry resolved to spend every hour Umbridge had inadvertently given him on dismantling her.

Start with Stunning Spells? No—getting Stunned can leave people feeling off. Better to begin with a basic Impediment Jinx and a Shield Charm...

Then someone lunged at him from behind.

Harry caught the presence half a beat early and spun around. The moment he'd sensed something off, his mouth had already begun forming the incantation.

"*Stupefy—*!"

He saw Draco's face and wrenched his wand aside at the last second. The red jet of light scorched past Draco's ear.

A beat behind, Draco levelled his wand and completed his own spell.

"*Tarantallegra!*"

Harry's legs erupted into frantic, uncontrollable movement. Draco crowed in triumph.

"How do you like that, Harry Potter!"

"*Expelliarmus.*"

"Ah—!"

The Disarming Charm struck. Draco's wand flew from his hand and sailed toward Harry. Harry cancelled the jinx on his legs, then strolled toward Draco, twirling the hawthorn wand idly.

"Never let your guard down. Landing Tarantallegra doesn't mean I've lost my wand."

"You—! That's cheating!"

"Cheating? You ambushed me from behind. That's ten times worse. Now—what's your grievance this time? Let's hear it."

Harry approached at a leisurely pace. Draco's fists clenched.

"...You want to hear my grievance? You have the nerve to ask that?"

"I asked, didn't I?"

Draco's fury boiled over.

"How can you stand there like nothing happened! You hit my sister!"

"Ah."

Harry went quiet.
So. This impulsive boy had witnessed Harry strike Carina, and had come after him in a rage.

How much does he adore her? Harry scratched the back of his head and looked down at Draco.

He should be grateful it stopped at a slap. And his idea of revenge is Tarantallegra*. Unbelievable.*

Oh, do shut up. He's rather sweet.

Harry suppressed the thought and spoke.

"So you came to avenge her? You, personally?"

"Hmph. I'm not the only one! I'm just the opening act. Father will make you pay—properly, and without mercy!"

Draco trembled with indignation, but managed to curl his lip.

"You lost your Seeker position too, didn't you? And this isn't over. Father visits the Ministry every single day to lodge formal complaints. Before long, Dumbledore will be nothing but a figurehead—a headmaster in name only! And when that day comes, you're finished!"

"How, exactly?"

"How? Well, that's—"

"What are you planning? Tripping Jinxes from behind? Putting a toad in my bag? Do you honestly think that sort of thing is going to make my life difficult?"

Draco's expression faltered. He hadn't actually thought through the specifics.

Harry continued.

"After all that effort, the best your father managed was getting me banned from Quidditch. How are you going to finish me? Force me to be expelled? And then what—it's over?"

"W-well—"

"Or what—a duel?"

Harry tossed Draco's wand back to him. Draco reached for it reflexively, fumbled, and dropped it. By the time he'd scrambled to pick it up, Harry already had his own wand trained on him.

Hic. Draco looked at the wand pointed at him and began to hiccup. Harry spoke to the trembling boy before him.

"You're not ready for that. And you haven't touched your wand once in Defence Against the Dark Arts this year, have you? If I used a Dark Spell, what would you do?"

"Ugh..."

Draco clamped his mouth shut, overwhelmed. But he hadn't lost his spirit entirely—he was still glaring at Harry with undisguised resentment.

He was genuinely furious. He had no idea that Carina had engineered the whole thing.

The devotion was almost touching. On impulse, Harry reached into his pocket and produced one of the fake Galleons.

"Here. A gift. Draco."

Harry flicked it toward him. Draco dropped it again. Harry watched him scramble after the coin as it rolled away and muttered that Draco had clearly no future as a Seeker.

When Draco had retrieved it, Harry addressed him with as much gravity as he could muster.

"If you want real power... when that Galleon grows warm, come and find me on the date marked in the serial number."

He left it at that and walked away. Draco stared after him, expression blank.

Back in the common room, Harry explained everything to Hermione. Predictably, she was furious.

"You gave that to little Malfoy? What were you thinking?"

It had been an impulse, more than anything. Harry answered in a small voice.

"He just... seemed rather sweet about it—"

"*Sweet?* What if he runs straight to Umbridge and tells her everything?"

"Don't worry. Kids that age don't usually go running to teachers. I say that as someone who went off to protect the Philosopher's Stone with only his classmates at age eleven."

"That's because you were the one doing it!"

Harry winced and stepped back. Hermione set down a half-finished fake Galleon with a sharp clatter.

"Go and get it back right now!"

"You can't take back a gift—it's undignified—"

"Your dignity? That's your concern? I made those Galleons to keep us secure, and you handed one to a Slytherin? What are we supposed to do now? I didn't sleep for days making those!"

It was Ron who rescued Harry from Hermione's tirade. He rubbed his nose and offered a placid smile.

"Now, now, Hermione. Harry had his reasons."

Shortly after Harry had struck Carina, he and Ron had reconciled. Ron had been deeply moved by the idea that Harry had slapped his ex-girlfriend in defence of a friend. As a result, the Ron of recent weeks was the most generously disposed toward Harry that he'd been in years.

True to form, Ron took Harry's side now.

"He thought it through, didn't he, Harry?"

"Somewhat."

Draco had told Harry that Lucius was incensed—that he was visiting the Ministry daily to lodge furious complaints. His behaviour was that of a father outraged on behalf of his beloved daughter.

But if Harry's read was correct—if Carina had provoked the Weasleys precisely to give Lucius that pretext—then Lucius had enlisted Carina's help to manufacture a reason for his visits.

Which meant the Ministry visits themselves were the point.

Harry's expression sobered.

"It seems Lucius Malfoy has been treating the Ministry like his second home. He appears to have arranged a convenient excuse to visit as often as he likes without raising suspicion. Ron—ask your father to look into Lucius Malfoy. He may already be aware, but it's worth making sure."

Ron nodded. Hermione caught something in Harry's tone and spoke carefully.

"...Harry. Did you get that information by manipulating Draco Malfoy?"

"That wasn't my intention. He volunteered it."

It had been an unnecessary thing to say, and not particularly significant information. But because Draco had said it, Harry now had a clearer picture of where Lucius was directing his efforts.

In the end, Harry had benefited from Draco.

And so he'd wanted to give something back to this guileless boy—if Draco came to the club, he'd at least learn the minimum spells needed to protect himself.

"You really are something..."

Hermione exhaled and let it go. Harry said, with an air of mild unconcern,

"Besides, if Draco does tell on us, we'll just reorganise—a bit of a nuisance, but manageable. We haven't even properly started yet."

Hermione stared at a point somewhere in the middle distance.

"The Galleons... I worked so hard on them..."

"Thank you, Hermione. You're my best friend in the world."

"Easy for you to say..."

Harry offered a sheepish smile by way of apology. Hermione sighed once more, and nodded.

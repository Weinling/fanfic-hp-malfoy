# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 42: Truth

Dumbledore approached slowly.

Sirius felt a particular, uncomfortable variety of guilt at the sight of him. The man he had just bound and thrown aside was Moody—almost certainly a longtime friend of Dumbledore's. Whatever grounds for suspicion had existed, the fact remained that Sirius and Harry had just attacked their mentor's old companion.

Sirius looked back and forth between Moody and Dumbledore with an awkward expression.

Contrary to his fears, Dumbledore did not reproach them.

He neither scolded them for recklessness nor frowned at the excess of force.

Dumbledore simply shook his head and raised his wand.

"Alastor. You still haven't broken that habit of yours."

Dumbledore pointed his wand at Moody. A complex spell—deceptively simple in execution, yet difficult to replicate—dissolved the bindings that had held Moody fast.

Moody came to his senses, pressed a hand to the ground, and hauled himself unsteadily upright. Sirius, standing beside him, deliberated for quite some time before offering an awkward smile and reaching down to help Moody to his feet.

"Thank you... Ever since I had one leg replaced, getting up from anything that isn't a chair has been a damned ordeal..."

Moody spoke with perfect composure, as though nothing of consequence had just occurred between them.

He gave Sirius a companionable pat on the shoulder, then turned to face Dumbledore.

"I'm sorry, Albus. I have nothing to say for myself."

Dumbledore regarded Moody with the look of a man watching a friend who had just been arrested for causing trouble.

"Alastor. I don't object to your methods as such—but this sort of conflict helps no one."

"I wouldn't say that. I was testing their mettle... and what I saw satisfied me well enough."

"They spent far too much to pass your test. Alastor. Not everyone possesses your inexhaustible vigilance."

As he spoke, Dumbledore turned his wand toward Rita Skeeter. The ropes binding her began to lengthen at every point. When the restraints had loosened sufficiently, Rita Skeeter shook herself a few times and the ropes fell away entirely.

The moment she was free, Rita Skeeter sprang to her feet and rounded on Dumbledore.

"Treating a journalist like this! Did you think banning me from Hogwarts and scheming in secret would keep it all hidden? I will expose every last thing you've concealed and make sure the whole world knows!"

"Ah, Skeeter. It seems even a journalist of your calibre couldn't hold onto both money and a sense of mission at once. The moment you lose the job, the real reporter comes back."

Dumbledore replied at his leisure.

"But I think there's a misunderstanding. The reason I issued that ban was because you were prying into private lives and calling it truth. Even a journalist has no right to reduce someone's personal affairs to mere entertainment."

"That was the truth! I simply printed what was there in the Daily Prophet!"

"Then I suppose I'm free to print a truth of my own? That you are, in fact, an unregistered Animagus, and that you've been scouring for stories in the form of a beetle?"

At the mention of that particular matter, Rita Skeeter snapped her mouth shut. It appeared that, for her, truth was only truth when it cost her nothing.

Having silenced Rita Skeeter with admirable economy, Dumbledore turned his gaze at last to Carina and Harry.

"I'm sorry, Harry—and Miss Malfoy. It was my failing that I didn't put a stop to his bad habits before this."

"Bad... habits?"

Dumbledore nodded at Carina's murmur.

"You will have heard what people say about Alastor. Regrettably, even I—his friend—cannot dismiss as mere rumour the stories of him stirring up trouble because he suspects even peace itself... He doubted his allies. He doubted himself. Wary of growing soft in quiet times, he would test those around him in the most outlandish ways. It seems you were both caught up in that particular habit of his."

"A test?"

Harry pointed at Rita Skeeter and raised his voice. Skeeter, who had been reaching for her Quick-Quotes Quill, hastily tucked her notebook back inside her robes.

"So leaving Rita Skeeter to run loose, sharing nothing with us—all of that was a test?"

"I thought of it as an education. Rita Skeeter made a far better teaching aid than any blasted textbook."

Moody replied in his gravelled voice.

"I told you, lesson after lesson, to stay constantly on your guard. But no matter how many times I said it, not one of you could keep up with even half of what I was teaching. And Harry Potter, of all people—the one someone is actively trying to kill—was too busy with his love life to pay attention..."

"How petty. You couldn't stand the sight of Harry Potter, who's supposed to be fighting Voldemort, falling in love."

Harry's tone was sharper than it had ever been.

"I had no idea someone like you would take such a keen interest in the romantic lives of children. I suppose it's people like Mad-Eye Moody who keep that sort of gossip filling the pages of the Daily Prophet."

It was hostile to the point of rudeness. Had a stranger witnessed it, they might have taken Harry and Moody for enemies. The atmosphere had turned that ugly.

Which left only Sirius, caught between them, thoroughly uncomfortable.

He had come running to Hogwarts to help Harry, and had been the one to help subdue Moody. But the man they had subdued turned out to be an ally. He needed to smooth this over—and Harry was still furious.

Sirius offered an uncertain smile and spoke to Harry.

"Harry. Mad-Eye didn't mean any harm by it—why don't we leave it there? I'm sure Professor Moody never intended to drive a wedge between you and Malfoy."

"Ah, but that's where you're wrong, Black. If the opportunity presents itself, I intend to reduce the risk wherever I can."

Moody, however, had no more interest in compromise than Harry did. He trampled the bridge of reconciliation Sirius had laid and pressed on.

"I didn't pay it much mind at first. I know how foolish children that age can be. And I know how quickly that kind of feeling burns out—like a fierce flame consuming its kindling all the faster."

Moody's gaze met Harry's. The magical eye that seemed incapable of losing a staring contest locked onto the green eyes hidden behind those round spectacles.

Moody spoke slowly.

"But tell me, Black—do you know why Potter called you here to attack me?"

"I beg your pardon?"

"I'll grant that I wasn't forthcoming. But Potter read Skeeter's article and immediately suspected me. That was the right instinct—I won't deny there was a leap of logic involved, but the conclusion was correct."

Sirius had received Harry's message and come straight here. He hadn't known the full circumstances, and so he had acted entirely on Harry's word.

Moody had then resisted violently before fleeing, which had led Sirius to assume Harry's suspicions had been correct.

But looking back, there had been more than one or two things that didn't add up.

"Did it ever occur to you to ask how I could be an impostor?"

"Well—I thought perhaps Polyjuice Potion, or the Imperius Curse."

Moody gave a derisive snort.

"Someone drinking Polyjuice and impersonating me? Think about it, Black. You'd need to brew Polyjuice in secret every single day, drink it without anyone noticing, and carry off my mannerisms so perfectly that not one person who knows me—including Albus—would suspect a thing.

Yes, perhaps it's possible. There are dark wizards with extraordinary talents.

Perhaps there exists a wizard who is a masterful potioneer, who could secretly procure months' worth of ingredients—including my hair—without detection, and who could so perfectly imitate me as to fool Albus, Karkaroff, and Barty Crouch, all of whom know my face.

But even granting all of that, verification would be simple enough. One trip to Madam Pomfrey for an antidote, and the matter's settled. Or if you'd thought to check the Marauder's Map, you'd have had your answer even faster.

The Imperius Curse is the more plausible theory—it's a conveniently wicked piece of magic. But even that isn't foolproof. Have you ever heard of an Imperius Test?"

It was one of the countermeasures Moody had mentioned on the first day of class, when he introduced the Unforgivable Curses.

A method for detecting the Imperius Curse. He had mentioned developing and disseminating it during the last war.

At the time, Carina and the other students had dismissed it as the sort of hobby only someone with Moody's level of paranoia would pursue—but Sirius, who had lived through that war, nodded as though it were common knowledge.

"I know of it. You deliberately fail a spell and establish a pattern from the effect."

"Exactly. That method provides a reasonable defence even against the Imperius Curse. Unless you're as skilled as Lucius Malfoy, it's nearly impossible to make someone replicate even the act of deliberately failing a spell. The very first time I encountered Albus, I exchanged an Imperius Test with him before I'd even shaken his hand."

Dumbledore confirmed this with a nod.

"I would have preferred otherwise, but Moody was so insistent. Being levitated upside-down and dropped on one's head is not a pleasant experience. I confess—age does not entirely eliminate one's sense of embarrassment."

Dumbledore conveyed, with characteristic lightness, that Moody had not been under the Imperius Curse.

Moody continued.
"Impersonating someone is an extraordinarily difficult undertaking... It's easy to let your guard down precisely because of that, but once you've decided to be suspicious, confirmation isn't hard to come by. Yet Potter chose to attack me outright. Going so far as to bring you in from a distance to help."

Sirius appeared shaken by the realisation that his godson had used him. He looked at Harry once, then asked in an unsteady voice.

"...Why?"

"Simple. Because I was a thorn in Potter's side. My presence was an obstacle between him and Malfoy..."

Sirius's gaze moved to Carina. A girl raised as befitted the daughter of Narcissa and Lucius Malfoy—as though anything sordid or beneath her had never been permitted to touch her.

She was his godson's lover. And simultaneously, the daughter of his enemy.

Even Sirius had expressed concern when he first learned of Harry and Carina's relationship.

Still, Sirius was a romantic in the best and worst senses of the word, and rather than standing in Harry's way, he had cheered him on wholeheartedly.

But not everyone was like him. Alastor Moody, in particular.

"I know Lucius Malfoy, and I've kept a close eye on his daughter, Carina Malfoy. I'll confess it now—I was considering extracting information from her... Of course, she's a student, so I had no intention of harming her. But what kind of fool ignores an enemy's most cherished daughter sitting right beside him? The same applied to the other Death Eaters' children, naturally..."

From the very first day of class, Moody had provoked the Slytherin students by bringing up the Unforgivable Curses.

Carina had refused to swallow the insult and protested—only to suffer the humiliation of being transfigured into a ferret by Moody's magic.

Had Harry not arrived at precisely that moment, it would have remained a deeply frightening memory.

...Had Harry not appeared at precisely that moment. Carina turned the thought over in silence.

"I have no complaint with the fact that Potter suspected me. If you have reason to doubt someone, striking first is a perfectly sound strategy. When you came for me, I was startled—but inwardly, I was rather proud of Potter. What I cannot overlook is his motive.

Potter's attack had nothing to do with my identity. He didn't care whether I was an Auror or a Death Eater. He simply found me inconvenient. Because, whether by design or accident, my handling of Skeeter had nearly damaged his relationship with Malfoy..."

Dumbledore had been away on business and would have returned before the ball. Harry could have confirmed the truth simply by waiting a few days.

But Harry had called for Sirius, ignored his misgivings, and pressed ahead with the attack.

To disarm Moody before the ball and force a confrontation.

"You weren't planning to harm me, were you? You intended to subdue me and demand answers. Your miscalculation was that I resisted violently without offering a single word of explanation. Isn't that right, Potter?"

Sirius looked at Harry's face. He asked in a trembling voice, as though he could barely believe it.

"Is—is that true, Harry? You only...?"

But Harry was not looking at Sirius. He was looking at Carina, his expression more unsettled than she had ever seen it.

Carina Malfoy stood with her lips pressed shut, listening to the words that passed around her.

The vague unease she had been carrying for so long was slowly taking shape.

It wasn't that a student's romance had become a news story. It was that Harry and Carina's romance had become one. The relationship between the Chosen One and the daughter of the Malfoy family made for rather compelling reading.

The reason her father kept sending her letters. The reason she had felt that creeping anxiety even in the midst of happiness.

Carina had known, really.

She had known all along that this sweet, happy world—this soft, warm, cloud-like world she had been living in—was, like a cloud, smoke that could vanish at any moment.

"Carina?"

Harry's voice cut through to her. Even now, even here, the warmth in it shook something in her chest.

But that warmth was a drug that made you forget reality.

Carina took one step back from Harry.

"...I'll be going now."

"Wait. Carina."

Harry's hand caught her. But Carina pushed it away—gently, but without hesitation.

"Don't. Even if you hold on... I'm still going."

The joined hands came apart. Carina turned her back on all of them—Dumbledore, Moody, Sirius, and Harry.

She walked away from them at a very slow pace. No one—not even Harry—could stop her.

It had always been strange, really, that Carina had been here among them, among the Order of the Phoenix, at all.

Harry's outstretched hand fell back to his side. He watched her go with a hollow expression, following her retreating figure with his eyes until she disappeared entirely.

At last, she was gone.

Harry spoke.

"...Why can't you just leave her alone?"

The atmosphere shifted. Harry, who moments ago had been venting his anger with a flushed face, let out a long, slow breath—as though every emotion he had shown until now had been a lie.

This was the future Harry had least wanted. And at the same time, the future he had always known was coming.

The daughter of a family that had served Voldemort. The Boy Who Lived who had defeated him.

Malfoy, who would serve Voldemort when he returned. Harry, who would have to stake his life against Voldemort.

Realistically, they could not be entangled with each other.

"The Triwizard Tournament, held in the name of unity—a shambles from the start. The professor brought in to protect me has been busy turning Carina into a ferret."

But Hogwarts was a school of magic, and strange things happened there with some frequency. From the moment Harry and Carina had found each other, they had been under a spell.

As the old headmaster was always proclaiming, love was a truly mysterious and peculiar kind of magic. Together, time vanished as though a Time-Turner had been wound backwards. Every worry and fear receded into the background, and in their own world, just the two of them, they had been happy.

Carina and Harry both. They had savoured every moment of it, genuinely.

"A tabloid journalist digs through someone's love life looking for a story. Someone else decides it's better that way and lets her run loose. And then they call it a test. A test."

But reality was crueller than magic. Harry had come to understand that the beautiful, radiant backdrop was nothing more than a set built on a broken stage.

Voldemort was gone, but what he had left behind still moved freely through the wizarding world, and the world was slowly preparing for his return.

It had been less than a year since they had confirmed what they felt for each other. But reality had come for them in many forms.

And the reality that wore Mad-Eye Moody's face had been the most direct of all.

"You're a fool, Mad-Eye."

Harry pushed his hair back from his forehead. His fine features twisted.

"You're as dreadful as Aunt Petunia. When something grates on your nerves, you won't rest until everyone around you is just as agitated as you are. You spread anxiety in every direction like some wretched conspiracy theorist, preaching chaos wherever you go. Thank you ever so much, Mad-Eye. Because of dangers that haven't even arrived yet, you've managed to make Carina reconsider everything between us."

Moody replied curtly.

"It would have come eventually."

"I knew that too. I simply didn't expect it to be delivered by someone who calls himself my ally."

Harry enunciated each word with deliberate precision.

Sirius looked between them. From what Harry was saying, it seemed he had genuinely disliked Mad-Eye Moody from the start.

"Harry, so the reason you attacked Mad-Eye was..."

"Yes. He was in the way. I wanted him out of the picture before the ball."

Sirius's jaw dropped at Harry's admission.

Harry removed his glasses and rubbed his face with a dry hand. Without the round frames to soften it, his gaze was cold and unfiltered as it settled on Moody.

"Mad-Eye. I've said it more than once. Carina is my girlfriend—leave her alone. But you didn't."

"I never agreed to that."

"When Dumbledore told you to stop investigating Snape, you spent your time investigating him anyway."
"It was necessary. You weren't exactly displeased about it."

"Whether I liked it or not, the fact that it was useless doesn't change."

Tap. The glasses swung from his fingers. Harry staring down Moody without his spectacles looked like an entirely different person.

No one could have said that a warm smile suited him in this moment. The cold expression and the faint contempt at the corner of his mouth seemed made for him.

"Comparing her to Snape is absurd, in any case. Unlike Snape, Carina has done nothing wrong. The fact that her parents were Death Eaters and the fact that she and I are close are entirely separate matters. The past is the past."

"The past? Did you say the past, Potter?"

Moody twisted his lips and contorted the skin around his eye into something that might have been a smile. Then, abruptly, the smile died, and he shouted in a voice that made the air feel dangerous.

"As long as I draw breath, no one gets to call it the past!"

Moody tapped his eye patch sharply with his wand. The magical eye moved with more ferocity than ever, expressing a fury that words could not.

"Look at this eye. I still remember. It was during the confrontation with Fynents and Ekalto. I had barely subdued Fynents when I turned and saw a bolt of blue flame coming straight for my left eye. I couldn't close it. If I had, Ekalto's Killing Curse would have found my chest. I watched dark magic carve out my eye and cast a Stunning Spell at the same time. I managed to subdue them both. It was sheer luck that I recovered the eye afterwards. Lucky enough—it let me have a better magical eye made."

Clack. This time it was the leg. His prosthetic struck the ground with a deeply unpleasant sound.

"This leg? Death Eaters caught in an Auror trap set a booby trap as they fled. The idea was to sever one of ours and use it to bind the rest. By some miracle, the experience of losing my eye had taught me to cast a binding spell even as my foot was blown to pieces. I dragged myself along the ground like a worm without letting go of my wand, and I arrested five of them... Unfortunately, I didn't recover my foot that time. The largest piece was my big toe."

And finally, Moody thrust his face close to Harry's. Every time he contorted his expression, the scars pulled apart.

"These scars are my proof. Some might call it obsessive paranoia. But I can see the ceiling with my eyes closed. When the spell wears off, the prosthetic slips on the floor. Every time I look in a mirror, the scars— all of this has carried forward from that day. And it isn't only me. Everyone who lost family to them remembers. Thirteen years. Thirteen years is not enough. I am telling you that thirteen years is far too short a time to forget hatred and pain!"

Who could call his suffering a lie? Who could hold a staring contest with those eyes?

He was the evidence of pain, a monster born of suspicion. He had earned his scars by failing to suspect, and he had kept his life by suspecting. Everyone called him an eccentric, a madman—and yet no one could dismiss him. Because Mad-Eye Moody was an Auror who had endured a lifetime of suffering.

But Harry did not offer him the respect the others did.

"I'm sorry, but—"

Harry Potter replied without particular weight.

"Thirteen years is my entire life. And it's Carina's entire life as well."

The boy who had lost his parents and grown up in a Muggle household. The Chosen One who had defeated Voldemort and yet spent ten years clawing through abuse—he murmured coldly.

"I don't know whether it's enough time to forget. But it's more than enough time for their children to grow up. I'm not my parents. And Carina is not hers."

If Harry had never met Carina in Diagon Alley, he would have spent years as a withdrawn, beaten-down child.

If Carina had never warned him about the diary, it would have taken far longer to find the Chamber of Secrets.

If Carina hadn't been there when the Dementor attacked, he might never have summoned a Patronus.

Three years. A short span compared to Moody's. But long enough for an enemy to become love.

"Don't pass your hatred down to me. I have no intention of carrying it."

Harry said it coldly and turned his back. Moody clamped his mouth shut and bored into Harry's retreating figure as though he could see straight through it.

Sirius, who had been shifting anxiously, called out to Harry's back.

"Harry!"

Harry stopped walking and turned only his head to look back. A kind of benign cynicism, a gentle contempt, settled at the corner of his mouth.

"Don't worry, Sirius. There was a bit of a scene, but I'm still Voldemort's enemy. I won't rest until I've sent him to whatever corner of hell he belongs in."

Not a vow. Not a promise. Simply a statement of fact, delivered without inflection.

Only the reason behind it was different from what everyone assumed. Harry walked forward as he spoke.

"Because I can't be with Carina until he's been sent back where he came from."

Moody's hand had gone white from gripping too hard.

Moody left without another word. The only reason Sirius remained was that he hadn't yet decided what to do.

And so Sirius turned to the one person he trusted for counsel.

"Professor Dumbledore. Why didn't you stop them from the beginning?"

"Sirius. It takes me an age just to decide which pyjamas to wear of an evening. What business do I have judging anything? I can only watch."

"They're going to keep fighting."

"That too I can only watch. If I intervened, would Moody abandon his convictions? Or would Harry abandon his love?"

Dumbledore remained as serene and unhurried as ever. Sirius, fraying at the edges, pressed on.

"Dumbledore. I still don't understand your approach. With your abilities, couldn't you have prevented all of this before it happened? Why did you let it go on?"

Dumbledore did not answer immediately. He smoothed his silver beard and gazed into the distance.

"Thirteen years, you said. Sirius, you spent that time in Azkaban."

"Twelve years, to be precise—though adding one more doesn't quite make it a boast."

"Was it a long time?"

Sirius couldn't fathom what he was driving at. He answered with some reluctance.

"Long and painful... but I'm not sure. Those years held no meaning for me. It was as though someone had taken a knife and cut that time clean out of my life."

"Yes. No matter how large and fine a chest of drawers may be, what you put inside it matters just as much."

Sirius found himself contemplating the length of thirteen years anew.

Thirteen years had been Moody's absence from the world. Thirteen years had been Sirius's Azkaban.

In that same span of time, his godson had grown from an infant into a young man—and had fallen in love with the daughter of his enemy, who had grown alongside him.

...And for Dumbledore, who had lived through two great wars, it may have been nothing more than a brief interlude. A period of preparation for the next.

"Moody has spent his entire life fighting dark wizards. He was arresting them from his youth, and when Voldemort appeared, his abilities shone brightest of all... For Moody, it was an age of both suffering and glory.

But age inevitably leaves you behind. The values you held most dear become relics of history, and the common sense you spent a decade learning is overturned in an instant. Children born into a world that has moved past your own time are a terror beyond description. When I invited Moody to teach, I hoped he might come to understand that..."

That seemed like rather a large failure, then. Moody was a cornerstone of the Order of the Phoenix. The fact that he was already at odds with Harry was, by any measure, a cause for concern.

Sirius looked at Dumbledore with something like reproach—but Dumbledore chuckled warmly and replied.

"Well? Isn't it going rather well?"

Even Sirius could only shake his head at that.

Dumbledore smiled to himself, then turned.

"And you, Skeeter? Quite an interesting story, wouldn't you say?"

There was Rita Skeeter, holding her breath, scribbling furiously in her notebook.

Rita Skeeter had been writing without pause, using both her Quick-Quotes Quill and her own pen simultaneously, when she belatedly registered the eyes of Dumbledore and Sirius upon her and gave a forced cough.

"Ahem, ahem. Well. It was all rather embarrassing, I must say. Somewhere in the middle I stopped being sure whether I was writing an article or a romance novel."

Rita Skeeter fanned herself with her notebook in a show of composure. Sirius's eyes moved to the notebook.

Skeeter clutched it to her chest with a frightened look.

"You're not thinking of taking my notebook, are you? That would be an infringement of press freedom!"

"Skeeter. As I have said on more than one occasion—even a journalist has no right to reduce someone's private life to mere entertainment."

Skeeter hugged the notebook tighter and stepped back. Dumbledore smiled with a hint of mischief.

"A novel, however, is another matter entirely. If you are a novelist rather than a journalist, I have no particular interest in confiscating a notebook full of material. You'll find it difficult to place articles in the Daily Prophet from here on in any case—this might be a good moment to consider a change of profession."

It didn't take her long to make up her mind. Rita Skeeter nodded, again and again.

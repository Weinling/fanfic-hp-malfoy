Harry informed Professor McGonagall that he intended to leave Hogwarts over the Easter holiday. Professor McGonagall replied with a face worn to the bone.

"Harry, as you know…."

"Someone will be watching me while I'm away?"

"That's right. There's no helping it. You're still a minor, which means you can't use magic outside Hogwarts—and in that gap, remnants of his followers might try to attack you. And…."

"I know. More than that, you're worried about what's inside me. So who's the one doing the watching?"

"Lupin and Moody volunteered, but the Minister refused. His reasoning was that a Werewolf and Mad-Eye would only get in the way of your holiday. Instead, he's assigned Kingsley Shacklebolt as your escort."

Kingsley was an Auror with the Ministry of Magic—thorough, meticulous, and trusted implicitly by the Minister.

It seemed the Minister of Magic was wary of the fact that Moody and Lupin had personal ties to Harry. Sending one of his most trusted Aurors to keep watch over him made that plain enough.

"So go, and take care of yourself."

But there was something the Minister of Magic didn't know.

Kingsley Shacklebolt was a member of the Order of the Phoenix, and over the course of this holiday, he would extend every courtesy to Harry that he possibly could.

With the Easter holiday approaching, Harry decided to steal away for a brief excursion. On a free day just before the break, Harry slipped out of Hogwarts by a particular means.

The Vanishing Cabinet had remained at the seaside manor all this time. The one used to infiltrate Harry and his forces had simply been left behind once everyone who'd gone through it returned the same way. It looked like an ordinary wardrobe, and Harry had pushed it flush against a wall as a rudimentary disguise—so when the Ministry's cleanup detail swept through, they'd found nothing worth noting and moved on.

Leaving such a valuable object unused would be a crime. In this particular philosophy, Harry took after his father. It was a violation of school rules, but Harry gave it no thought whatsoever and used the Vanishing Cabinet to slip free of Hogwarts.

"Well. It's been a while."

The moment he stepped through, the air changed. From the texture of the floorboards underfoot to the faint, salt-edged smell of the sea that hung through the manor—it all made the fact of being outside Hogwarts feel real. The furniture was arranged much as he remembered. There was no reason it would have changed, really. It seemed no one had bothered to come back here after the war ended.

Harry turned on his heel and shut the empty cabinet door. He counted to three in his head, then opened it again. As if by sleight of hand, a girl with beautiful platinum hair stood inside, arms folded neatly before her.

"Welcome, my lady."

Harry stepped back and bowed deeply from the waist. He extended one hand in a long, sweeping gesture, offering his escort so the lady might step down in comfort.

Carina reached out with elegant grace and took his hand.

"Gladly."

Having arrived at the manor, Carina looked around with eyes soft with memory.

"Ah, this place…. When I was eight, I told Father the sea smelled fishy and put him in an awkward spot…."

"So that's why your father offered it to Voldemort as a gift."

"…So it seems. Looking back on it now, it wasn't so bad."

Carina swept her gaze slowly around the room, her eyes touched with longing. The wide bed, the modest wardrobe, the old mirror sitting atop the dressing table. The room had the feel of a place stripped down to only what was strictly necessary to call it a room at all.

Of everything in it, only the mirror showed signs of particular care—and Carina had no difficulty guessing why.

"When you came here in my body, you must have checked the mirror."

"That's right. If the transformation had broken, it would've shown a hairy man wearing a woman's chemise. Even if the Death Eaters hadn't known it was Harry Potter, they'd have cast Avada Kedavra on the spot."

"That's not—wait. Chemise?"

Carina, who had been laughing quietly, startled and took a defensive step back. As Harry tilted his head, she glanced down at herself and wrapped her arms around her body.

"…You changed clothes? In my body?"

"Ah, is that what you mean?"

Harry replied with easy amusement.

"It's fine. I didn't touch anything."

"You—you still looked!"

"That was unavoidable. To change clothes, I at least needed to know where things were on my body—or rather, your body."

"That's—well, yes, but—"

Carina's expression remained sour, as though something still didn't sit right with her. Harry smiled at her.

"More to the point, I've already decided that touching requires permission."

"You haven't decided anything!"

"Hmm? I thought that was rather a sensible thing to say just now…. Obviously you ask before putting your hands on someone else. Or what—do you prefer it without asking?"

The flush of embarrassment that coloured her face was so vivid it was almost unfair to look at. Enough to make one wonder whether she was teasing him on purpose. She had a singular talent for making him want to reach out and touch her.

Harry waved a hand to reassure her.

"Don't worry too much. Looking down at my own body from that angle felt strange—too uncanny to be enjoyable. The outside perspective is definitely better."

"Hmph. What would you know about it. You've never actually seen a woman's body."

"Oh? Even if that's true, saying it like that stings a little…."

Harry stopped mid-sentence and closed his mouth. He studied the girl who had just provoked him. Carina still had her arms wrapped around herself defensively, and for some reason she couldn't quite meet his gaze—her eyes sliding just slightly away.

Harry swallowed. His instincts, it seemed, were fairly reliable.

…Or she was sharp enough to use even his instincts against him.

"Huu…."

Harry slowly looked around the room.

The Aurors had already swept through the manor. Carina was its rightful owner. No one was coming. There happened to be a thin nightgown in the wardrobe, and the bed was neatly made.

If they rested here—just briefly, just for a little while—no one would ever know. They could have quite a pleasant time of it.

But.

Occlumency.

Not yet.

He called the lake to mind. He fixed his gaze on the still, cold landscape of his inner world. He reminded himself of the dark creature lurking somewhere beneath the surface.

The hunger for life—the release of desire—didn't end once it was spent. It would only return as a greater, more consuming attachment, and it would come for him.

And he had no intention of sharing this moment with Voldemort, of all things, and handing him a weakness.

Having held the temptation fully at bay, Harry stepped toward Carina—who had, rather presumptuously, been the one doing the provoking. She must have been tense, because she flinched more sharply than usual.

Harry slipped an arm around her waist and moved to sweep her off balance—then didn't. Instead, his cloak swirled as he raised his other hand.

"As it happens, that's rather convenient. Where we're going next is connected to exactly that."

"Wh—what?"

She must have been bracing herself for something, because the sound that escaped Carina's lips was entirely undignified. Harry smirked and pulled her firmly into his arms.

"Right then. I'm going to Apparate. Hold on tight."

Harry gripped her waist and turned on the spot. With the familiar sensation of the world pressing in from all sides, Harry and Carina vanished.

They reappeared in a dim alley somewhere in London. The Apparition was remarkably clean—Carina found her footing almost immediately.

Beyond the mouth of the alley, cars rumbled past. The noise of the busy city centre seeped into every corner, filling even this deserted lane, reminding them that it was still part of the city.

Harry straightened his cloak and spoke.

"How are you feeling?"

Carina replied with a flat expression.

"…When did you learn to Apparate? You certainly haven't received your licence yet."

"Since when have I needed a licence to walk the school corridors at night?"

"We're outside Hogwarts. Is it all right to use magic here?"

"Yes. Among his knowledge, there's also how to use magic without the Ministry detecting it."

Harry gave the brief explanation and led the way out of the narrow alley. It was too tight for two people side by side, so he angled himself sideways to push through and clear the path.

From behind him came a small, muttered sound.
"…Coward."

Harry turned around. Carina was following him with her lips pressed firmly shut.

"What? What did you just say, Carina?"

"It's narrow and filthy. I said the dust is getting on my clothes and it's irritating, so hurry up and take me somewhere decent, you idiot."

His girl was in a thoroughly disagreeable mood. Harry smirked and headed toward the place he had in mind.

Regent Street, in the heart of London. The bustling thoroughfare—a twenty-minute drive from King's Cross toward the University of London—was thick with people in the middle of the day. Harry brought Carina to a stop in front of a large department store.

The building was finished entirely in timber, opulent and old-fashioned, with several entrances through which dozens of people flowed in and out. Carina looked at it and frowned.

"It's a Muggle shop."

"A department store. Have you been to one before?"

"The largest ones, a few times. But only to look—I've never actually bought anything. There's nothing worth purchasing in a Muggle shop."

"Today we're buying clothes here."

"Clothes? Here?"

Carina repeated the question with an expression of distinct reluctance. She seemed particularly put off by the crowd pressing in from all sides.

"You can get clothes in Diagon Alley. Do we really need to buy clothes with no magical value whatsoever—not even a proper robe?"

Why wizards were so attached to robes, Harry still had absolutely no idea. But pointing that out to Carina would accomplish nothing, so instead he brushed her platinum hair back and murmured,

"Because you'd look beautiful in Muggle clothes."

"Hmph. Even so. Muggle clothes won't suit me."

"Of course not. No outfit could draw the eye more than you already do. I only hope that among the millions of things in there, we might find at least one worth choosing."

"Flattery."

Even as she said it, her manner softened slightly—her mood lifting just a fraction. Carina glanced at him sideways and asked,

"Do you have Muggle money?"

The fact that she was asking about what came after choosing something meant she'd at least decided to go inside. Harry took out his wallet and showed her the notes.

"Of course. Did you think I'd come unprepared and embarrass my lady?"

"How did you get it?"

"The Potter family vault has Muggle currency and jewellery as well. The Potters were always rather open-minded, as you know. I helped myself to a bit of the inheritance."

"Converting family wealth into Muggle money. What a waste."

"Only if you don't spend it well. Don't you think?"

Harry felt no particular regret about disposing of the family fortune as he pleased. He was going to die anyway, and it wasn't as though he'd manage to spend it all before then—nor was there anyone to leave it to.

Besides, the thing Rita Skeeter had been preparing was nearly ready to come out. Harry murmured to himself,

"And the royalties should be coming in before long."

"What?"

"Ah, don't worry about it for now. I'm planning to release it in the wizarding world soon enough…. Shall we go in?"

Harry and Carina strode into the department store.

The building had looked stately from the outside, and the interior matched—finished in aged hardwood throughout. The structure followed Tudor-era design, open and unbroken across its floors, so that the glass ceiling was visible from the ground level and the whole space could be taken in at a glance, like stepping into a grand hall. Several enormous glass chandeliers hung from the ceiling, some trailing so low they nearly grazed the ground floor.

Even Carina, who had been frowning at the crowd outside, seemed to grant the interior a grudging pass. She looked around with the ease of someone who'd been there before and said,

"The whole shop is laid out in the open. So then—what did we come for?"

"Actually," Harry said, with complete honesty, "I've wanted to see you in skinny jeans and a hoodie for quite some time."

Carina didn't catch his meaning immediately and tilted her head.

"Right. Put it on."

"Wow. This suits you too."

As expected, Carina was beautiful in whatever she wore. That held true even accounting for the scowl currently on her face. Harry noticed that the ordinary people browsing the department store—Muggles, yes—were losing their gazes to Carina as well. It made him proud and irritated in equal measure. He wanted to show her off to everyone, and he wanted to hide her away and keep her entirely to himself.

Carina looked down at her outfit and furrowed her brow.

"Tch. I genuinely don't understand what this is supposed to be. Having to squeeze in and out of clothes in that tiny little room."

Carina in skinny jeans and a hoodie, twisting a strand of hair with an air of dissatisfaction, radiated a kind of rebellious appeal. It was pleasant to observe—but unfortunately, Harry was her companion. In the interest of his companion's comfort, Harry raised his wand toward the fitting room and gave it a flick.

"*Capacious Extremis.*"

The Undetectable Extension Charm. A spell that expanded the interior of a given space—demanding considerable effort even from a skilled witch or wizard. Harry had dispatched it with a single incantation, and he gestured toward the result with quiet pride. Carina stared at him with an expression of pure disbelief.

"You cast an Extension Charm that easily."

Probably a fragment of Voldemort's power—but still. Carina peered inside the fitting room. What had barely fit one person now had room enough to dance in. She said, flatly,

"Using magic outside school. Applying an Extension Charm to an unlicensed object. How many International Statute of Secrecy violations are you racking up?"

"It's fine. I'll undo it before we leave."

"The number of laws broken in a single day. At this rate, you'd have nothing to say for yourself if they sent you to Azkaban."

"It's not a crime if you don't get caught. Fortunately, I happen to have the ability to ensure that. Might as well enjoy it."

If the Death Eaters had known he was using Voldemort's power for something like this, they would have been apoplectic. But Harry had decided to enjoy these small, ordinary things to the fullest.

"How does it feel? If it's something that's touched your body, I'd buy every piece and keep them all—but if you'd rather not have your wardrobe filled that way, I won't insist."

"Whatever you like."

She let her displeasure show freely, but didn't actually refuse. Harry watched Carina disappear into the fitting room and tilted his head.

Something was clearly bothering her—that much was certain—but she wasn't expressing it beyond a certain point, which made it difficult to gauge.

At first he'd assumed it was the Muggle shop, the Muggle clothes. But that didn't seem to be it. As for a lack of compliments—she'd been blushing and bristling at his excessive praise, so that wasn't it either.

Then what was the problem? Harry was deep in thought when—

"Harry, wait. What is this."

The door opened a crack, and Carina's disgruntled voice came from inside. Harry cast Muffliato and a few other concealment charms, then stepped close to the fitting room door.

Sure enough. Carina was complaining again.

"The Extension Charm is too much. The clothes rail on the other side is too far away now. I have to take three whole steps just to hang something up."

Now she was grumbling that it was too spacious. She seemed born specifically to find fault with things. What on earth did it mean? She, who would ordinarily storm out or say exactly what she thought when something displeased her, was instead needling him—as if testing his patience, and nothing more.

Wait. Patience?

His body understood before his mind did. Harry opened the door and slipped inside.

Carina, who had been muttering to herself, startled at the uninvited entrance. Or perhaps she startled because the audience she'd been inviting had actually arrived.

Harry smiled and closed the door behind him. A metallic click. There was a lock, naturally—it was a fitting room. Harry found it, turned it carefully, and looked back into the room.

"Ah, right. If the inside was too empty, I should have filled it rather than left you to fuss about it. I was slow to catch on. Sorry."

Carina, who hadn't changed yet and was still in the skinny jeans and oversized hoodie, gripped the trailing cuff of her sleeve tightly and looked up at Harry with silver-grey eyes. Expectant or apprehensive—furious at the presumption or flushed because things had gone exactly as she'd wanted—Harry couldn't tell at a glance.

He placed his hands on her shoulders. She trembled slightly but didn't pull away. Through the thick fabric he could feel how slight she was. He took hold of her shoulders and turned her—half a rotation—until she was facing the full-length mirror at the back of the fitting room.

"The clothes too—I put you in something a size too big, calling it an oversized fit."

In the mirror: Carina in clothes one size too large, and Harry standing behind her, arms loosely encircling her. The Harry in the mirror moved his hands with unhurried ease.

His hands drifted downward. Carina's small frame gave a startled shiver. Harry smiled pleasantly and didn't stop. His hands passed her sternum, moved toward her lower abdomen. Then, as his fingers brushed near her navel, the girl in the mirror seized that hand with sudden urgency. Harry stilled.

A brief silence. But Carina only held on—she didn't push the hand away. She gripped it with force but no direction, simply holding it fast. More, or stop—or two contradictory impulses locked in opposition. Her small hand trembled.

Harry whispered against her ear.

"See? You're not ready yet either."
"Mm—"

The girl flinched as though struck somewhere tender. Harry smirked, and instead of going further, slipped his hand into the front pocket of the hoodie. The trembling stopped.

Their hands layered together in the same pocket, Harry drew her gently against him and said,

"Stop playing games. You're not used to this—it shows."

Carina pulled the hood up and bowed her head. Her face was hidden, but Harry felt, somehow, that he knew exactly what expression she was wearing. Shame and embarrassment—embarrassment in several different senses of the word. To name that feeling precisely would be an unkindness to Carina.

She had been walking a dangerous line—from just now, or perhaps from even before that. She who might be worried about something, and at the same time might be hoping for it.

Not now—but as if to promise a someday, he held her close.

Harry said quietly,

"Let's wear that when we go to see your father."

"What?"

Carina looked at him as though he'd said something completely unhinged. Ah, there it is. Say something stupid and she comes right back to herself. Harry smiled awkwardly.

"You want to go home in those Muggle clothes?"

"Don't you think it would be rather a shock?"

"It would be an attack. Father might actually collapse."

"Well, that might not be so bad…. Ow."

A sharp sting from inside the hoodie pocket. Carina had pinched the back of his hand. As punishment, he retaliated by teasing her hand in the hidden pocket where no one could see, and they passed a little while like that.

They needed to be back before the evening meal, so Harry and Carina Apparated back to the seaside manor where the cabinet waited.

At that moment. Harry's senses caught something—a foreign magic, wrong in a way he felt before he understood. He yanked Carina toward him instantly, drew his wand, and swept it like lightning. A crack—something snapping. Then the sounds of shattering, bursting, a concealment breaking apart in rapid succession.

A human shadow dropped from the air.

Harry's eyes went wide and he swept his wand again and again. Invisible force hammered outward in waves. Bang, bang, bang. One by one, the shields surrounding the figure shattered. When the last one broke—

A gaunt-faced blond man was revealed inside. A young man who had been a Death Eater, one of Voldemort's most devoted.

"Wait—"

The man thrust out a pale hand with desperate urgency. Harry ignored the gesture and raised his wand with a ferocious expression. Invisible force seized the man and hauled him into the air.

He'd been subdued without managing any real resistance. Breathless, he spoke.

"That—that power… as I thought. It belongs to the Dark Lord. Ah. I found it. I've finally found it…."

The man muttering like a man possessed was someone Carina knew well. Her voice shook as she spoke his name.

"Bartemius Crouch Junior…."

The rough-haired blond man, Bartemius, answered with a face that showed nothing.

"It has been a while…, Carina Malfoy. I always believed you were the most promising candidate to become the Dark Lord's most devoted servant. But."

Bartemius looked at Harry and Carina standing side by side and managed a strained smile.

"You've gone beyond servant—you've become his consort. As expected, my predictions were not wrong…. Gk—!"

A brutal force closed around his throat. Bartemius's face went scarlet as the air was cut off. He thrashed in agony, but Harry's expression only grew more terrible, his wand extended without mercy.

Then Carina's hand covered Harry's.

Harry looked at her face. Carina's expression was rigid, set like stone, and she gave a small, slow shake of her head. Harry clicked his tongue and withdrew his wand.

With a dull thud, Bartemius dropped to the floor. He caught himself on his hands and coughed in pain.

"How did you know I had his power? What possessed you to show yourself in front of me?"

"I had to… confirm…."

"The price is death."

"There is something… you also… need to confirm, is there not…?"

Harry's eyes narrowed. He was right—Harry needed to know how Bartemius had found him, and whether there were other remnants still at large. Harry exhaled slowly.

Despite having just had his life threatened, Bartemius rose to his feet with eyes that held no particular emotion, watching Harry and Carina.

Harry's voice dropped low.

"How did you find this place? How did you track my movements? How did you escape Azkaban?"

"If you know the route of your own assault, this is the most obvious place to suspect. I've been watching it continuously. I was never taken to Azkaban in the first place. The moment the Dark Lord descended into your body, Moody's binding spell broke for just an instant. I used that opening to cast a Confundus and slip away."

"Bloody hell, the Ministry can't do a single thing right…."

Harry finished speaking and looked at Carina. She had her cold mask in place, watching Bartemius in silence. The teasing, testing quality she'd had just minutes ago—measuring Harry's patience, probing his limits—was gone entirely. There was only Carina Malfoy, her mask drawn tight against a threat.

On this good day, this day that had felt almost like magic, the ending had come in the worst possible form. Violent fury and hatred wrapped around Harry. He ground his teeth and muttered,

"Right. So I have something to do. Before my birthday arrives, I need to hunt down every last one of them and kill them."

"…That would be your will as well."

"My will."

"Your will, permitted by his."

Bang—Bartemius was thrown aside before he could react. He struck the wall hard and slid down it with a pained groan.

But what lived in Bartemius's eyes was fanaticism.

Whatever Harry did, it was a frenzied faith in something dwelling inside him that drove the man—nothing else.

"And I will give you one warning."

Bartemius stood again. With eyes as flat as if the pain belonged to someone else, he spoke to Harry and Carina.

"I was not the only one who escaped that day. Igor Karkaroff fled the moment he sensed the Dark Lord had fallen. Bellatrix Lestrange escaped at much the same time as I did. The Aurors are pursuing them, but they will never be caught."

"How generous. You're warning me?"

"Yes. I have no doubt whatsoever that the Dark Lord will return. I confirmed it with my own eyes—now all that remains is to wait and believe. But the others—I cannot say what they will do. They carry great hatred and a sense of betrayal…. Directed at none other than the girl beside you, and her family."

The arm around Carina tightened. Even so, the faint trembling of her body would not stop.

That they would go after her rather than him, even now. Harry's fury blazed openly as he raised the hand holding his wand.

"I've heard you intend to kill yourself, Harry Potter."

Facing Harry's rage without flinching, Bartemius continued to speak in the same flat, factual tone.

"If you mean to protect your girl, you'll need to keep that power intact until the very end. Otherwise, his followers will reduce the traitors to ash."

"…Is that supposed to be a threat?"

Harry's fury shook the manor. Stone and pillar and ceiling trembled with unease—as if groaning at the catastrophe to come. A power so immense it put fear even into Bartemius's expressionless eyes came into being.

Red light flared in green irises. Harry raised his heavy wand and murmured with quiet menace,

"Right. I have my work cut out for me. Before my birthday comes—find every last one of them and kill them."

"…That too would be your will."

An enormous magic assembled itself around a single purpose: one man's death. Bartemius faced power of staggering magnitude and did not look away. He spread his arms wide, as if in worship of the force itself—as if welcoming it.

But.

The power was never unleashed.

Carina wrapped her arms around Harry's. The soft pressure against his left arm startled him, and he pulled his wand back sharply. Carina looked up at him steadily and shook her head, slow and deliberate.

Understanding her meaning, Harry looked back at her—almost pleading. They needed to end this now, he said with his eyes. Leave that man alive and he becomes a problem later.

But Carina would not allow it. Harry shut his eyes and let the magic die. The tremendous force that had been enough to shake the seaside manor to its foundations vanished in an instant.

Harry and Carina turned their backs on Bartemius and stepped into the Vanishing Cabinet.

The Vanishing Cabinet that had stood in the Room of Requirement was destroyed completely. Beyond any possibility of repair.

# Traducción inglesa oficial — coreano 103: To Hogwarts

Just the two of them. In the Rolls-Royce that moved by magic, and on the Hogwarts Express departing from King's Cross Station. Harry and Carina moved together, and alone.

When he'd left Hogwarts, Harry had been surrounded by Aurors—so the changed circumstances left him puzzled.

"Strange. Why isn't anyone following us?"

An escort exists for one of two reasons: because the subject is in danger, or because the subject is the danger.

Harry carried Voldemort's power within him, which meant the likelihood of his being harmed was essentially nil. But if something went wrong, Voldemort could seize Harry's body. From the Ministry's perspective, that was a far more alarming prospect than Harry being attacked. So they had assigned Aurors to shadow him under the guise of protection—surveillance dressed up as security. Harry had the ability to shake them off, of course, but his own unease about his power had led him to tolerate it.

And yet.

"They made such a fuss at the start of the holidays, and now they've abandoned me entirely."

Harry glanced around, brow furrowed.

The Rolls-Royce was one thing—a tail could follow at a distance without being noticed. But on the Hogwarts Express, someone could easily have taken a compartment nearby. This long, long train held only two passengers: Carina and Harry.

"Would you like something to eat?"

The trolley witch, of course, being the exception. Harry fished out a Galleon and bought two Chocolate Frogs and a pair of Chewable Ice Juices. The tinkling of the trolley faded down the corridor. Harry watched it trundle toward the rear of the train and muttered to himself.

"She's selling sweets to a train with two passengers. I wonder if that's profitable."

"She's as much a part of Hogwarts as the castle itself. Best not to think too hard about it."

"It's just odd. The Aurors who should be here aren't, and the trolley witch who doesn't need to be here is."

"The Aurors are occupied. They won't have time to spare for you—not with Karkaroff to interrogate."

Carina unwrapped a Chocolate Frog and held it out to Harry. He took it without thinking, then caught himself.

"Wait. Karkaroff? How did they catch him?"

Carina adopted an expression of perfect innocence.

"Some particularly skilled Auror must have made the arrest."

"That's impossible. This is our Ministry we're talking about. Who could possibly catch Karkaroff after he fled abroad?"

"Mad-Eye Moody, perhaps?"

"Professor Moody is busy hunting down the remnants here in Britain. Karkaroff ran to the Continent—I can't imagine he'd have shown himself so easily…."

Harry frowned, turning it over in his mind. Leaving him to his thoughts, Carina drew out the wizard card from inside the Chocolate Frog wrapper.

Albus Dumbledore.

Former Headmaster of Hogwarts.

Until barely a year ago considered the greatest wizard of the age, Dumbledore is particularly celebrated for his defeat of the Dark wizard Gellert Grindelwald in 1945, his discovery of the twelve uses of dragon's blood, and his alchemical research conducted alongside his partner Nicolas Flamel. Even in his advanced years, he fell heroically in battle against He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named.

It was more than enough to redirect Harry's attention. Carina held the card out to him—Dumbledore's painted likeness moving within its frame.

"Harry. Look at this."

"Professor Dumbledore…."

A flicker of grief crossed Harry's face. He read the inscription and murmured, a bitter edge to his voice.

"They were quick about it. He hasn't even been gone that long."

There was a faint wistfulness in his tone. Carina glanced at the card with studied indifference.

"It doesn't mention the other thing, does it? That he advised his own student to take his life."

"Carina."

"Then again, I suppose Chocolate Frog cards don't tend to include the unflattering details."

Harry spoke with quiet firmness.

"Carina. There were exceptional circumstances, and you know it."

"Exceptional circumstances or not—the fact remains that he did it."

She still harbored a grudge against Dumbledore. Particularly after confronting the memory of him telling Harry to end his own life—after that, Carina had made no effort to conceal her contempt. The reason wasn't difficult to guess. Harry felt a complicated warmth at it, gratitude tangled with something like exasperation.

"Let's leave that alone. We need to think about what's ahead."

"Hmph."
Carina turned her head away primly. Harry smiled to himself and pressed one of the Chewable Ice Juices to her lips. The bright yellow ice tapped lightly against them, and Carina, as though she couldn't help it, parted her lips and let it in.

Harry watched her chew in silence for a moment, then returned to the original subject.

"About Karkaroff, though."

Carina's expression crumpled with visible displeasure.

"If Karkaroff has been moving, the others probably have too. I'm not particularly worried about myself—but you should be careful, Carina."

The ice crunched sharply between her teeth. Harry spoke quickly, before she could swallow.

"I've placed a protective charm on you—one that makes it difficult for Death Eaters to attack you directly. If anyone tries, the Dark Mark will burn hot enough to stop them. Karkaroff in particular, with that metal arm of his, is probably the least dangerous opponent you could face. A silver arm is a Dark Mark in its own right—it will answer to your commands, or mine, before it answers to his. But the others…. Bellatrix Lestrange, for instance. I can't guarantee the same holds for her."

A swallow. The ice was gone. Carina replied with a look of distinct irritation.

"And when, exactly, did you put a protective charm on me without asking?"

"I'm sorry for that. But I needed some minimum assurance of your safety. More importantly—Carina."

"No."

She cut him off before he could finish.

"You're thinking of going to find the Death Eaters before your birthday, aren't you."

"It's genuinely necessary."

"No."

"Carina—"

"If you're truly worried about me, then stay by my side and protect me yourself."

And so the conversation returned, as it always did, to the same impasse. Harry had been prepared to die rather than yield to Voldemort—but Carina would not allow it. The greater problem was that Harry himself had lost the certainty he'd need to go through with it without her consent.

If every negotiation failed, and the moment of choice arrived—if Carina were standing beside him, urging him to live—

A month ago, perhaps. But now…

"It's all right, Harry. There's a last resort."

As though she'd read straight through him, her voice came soft against his ear.

"There's a way to separate his soul from yours. If you're willing to commit to it fully."

"What?"

Harry turned. Carina was watching him with a quietly knowing smile at the corner of her mouth.

"What method?"

"That's not what matters right now, is it? You'll find out once we're at Hogwarts."

"What? What kind of method—"

Harry shook his head slowly.

"Then just tell me this much. Who told you? You didn't do something dangerous, did you?"

She had gone to the prison where Grindelwald was held, and extracted the answer from her directly. In the process she had encountered Karkaroff—and rather than the reverse, had subdued him and returned to Britain with him in tow.

The British Ministry had been overjoyed at the news of Karkaroff's capture. They had previously used the press to pin the blame for Voldemort's resurrection squarely on him, making him the convenient scapegoat for everything. But while other Death Eaters had been arrested, Karkaroff had slipped away—leaving the Ministry in a deeply awkward position. Then Carina had not only found him, but brought him back.

The Ministry was grateful. Grateful enough to grant a small favor: a few days' delay in resuming surveillance of Harry Potter.

This was the sort of story that might sound rather alarming to Harry's ears. Carina was still carefully choosing her words when—

"You did it. Something dangerous."

But they knew each other too well for that. Harry's expression hardened as he looked up at her.

"You've been steering around certain topics since earlier. You're hiding something."

"Am I? Without proof, there's not much I can say to a suspicion."

"I can find out exactly what happened if I choose to. Tell me now, Carina."

Faced with Harry's barely contained anger, Carina rose lightly from her seat. She took a few quick steps toward the compartment door, slipped through it, and pulled it shut behind her.

She stood behind the door like a shield, peering at Harry through the small glass panel. Her smile was deliberate provocation.
"I'd rather not."

Harry stared at her, incredulous.

"Stop acting like a child and come back in here."

"A child? Hmm. Like Draco, you mean?" She pressed a finger to her cheek as though considering something, then breathed a slow breath against the glass. A pale fog bloomed across it. Through the haze, Carina smiled brightly and wrote with one finger:

Catch me.

Then she spun on her heel and ran.

Harry let out a short, helpless laugh. He could see exactly what she was doing—buying time, nothing more. But there were moments when you could read someone's intentions perfectly and still find yourself powerless to resist them. Carina in full flight, playful and alive, had a way of dismantling his reason that was both simple and absolute.

He exhaled slowly and stood up.

The Hogwarts Express had an end. No matter how far she ran, she would eventually be caught—and Carina knew that perfectly well. She'd issued the challenge knowing it. Which made it all the more insufferable, and made him want to catch her all the more.

"You won't get off lightly when I find you."

Harry shoved the compartment door open. The sliding panel struck its frame with a crack that rang through the entire train.

A train used by two people alone. No one to worry about. Harry moved methodically from compartment to compartment, checking each one carefully, hunting for wherever she was hiding.

—

Hagrid was waiting at Hogsmeade Station. Harry and Carina climbed into a Thestral-drawn carriage hand in hand.

Even as the carriage rolled forward, Hagrid kept dabbing at his eyes with a handkerchief.

"…How has it come to this!"

Every time the half-giant sniffled, the carriage lurched. Harry, seated behind him, tried to offer some comfort.

"It's all right. It's not as though I'm going off to die, exactly."

"But they might seal you away if it comes to it! That mustn't happen. We've lost too many good people already. Professor Dumbledore, Professor Snape…. There can't be any more grief. There just can't!"

Harry said nothing. He managed a crooked, uncertain smile.

Hogwarts received them with its usual grandeur. The castle in the holidays was too quiet—every place that should have been full of noise held only stillness, as though it had ceased to be a school and become something else entirely.

Filch, who had nowhere else to be, was still there—as unwelcoming as ever. He stood glaring at Harry with an expression of profound displeasure.

"The little brats have arrived…. Come to torment me even during my only holiday…."

"Harry isn't here for that, Filch!"

Hagrid's voice boomed through the entrance hall.

"He's here for something more important than you could imagine! And since it's not term time, you've no business watching over Harry or Miss Malfoy. Leave them be and see to your own affairs!"

"Hmph. What are they to me? I'll catch anyone who breaks the rules, no matter who they are…."

Filch muttered darkly and shuffled away. Hagrid snorted, watching his retreating back with a glare.

"I'm sorry about that. I wanted things to be as comfortable as possible for you both."

"It's fine, really."

Harry shrugged.

"Filch being here doesn't stop us going out at night, does it? Or entering restricted areas? It doesn't matter. I've been breaking Hogwarts rules since first year."

"I'm not sure what to do with that, Harry. I am technically a professor, after all…."

"Don't worry. Carina and I won't have any particular reason to leave our dormitory at night anyway."

Hagrid nodded, reassured.

"Right, of course. It's not term time, and there's nothing you'd need to do in secret."

"Ha. Exactly. Well then—we'll head up to the dormitory."

"Off you go. It's been a long journey—get some rest. If you need anything, call for a house-elf."

Harry took Carina's hand and they walked together toward the Gryffindor tower. Hagrid watched them go with a fond expression—then paused, mildly puzzled as to why they were heading to the dormitory together.

Well. He must be walking her there.

Hagrid didn't dwell on it and turned toward his hut. The Ministry officials would be arriving before long, and he needed to go out and meet them.

Creak.

The gates of Nurmengard swung shut. Carina glanced back once, then slowly turned to face forward.

The method Grindelwald had described was almost insultingly simple. A Horcrux—use the very soul fragment that had tormented them, but in reverse. Cleave that soul and decant it into another vessel.

It was the kind of conclusion that made her wonder why no one had thought of it sooner. So simple, in theory.

No. Simple isn't quite the right word.

The ritual for creating a Horcrux was difficult and intricate—but not impossible, given sufficient preparation.

Killing someone requires no grand apparatus. You can raise a wand and speak the Killing Curse. You can drive a hand's-length dagger into a heart. You can lace a drink with poison, or lure someone to the edge of a cliff and push. Murder is within anyone's reach, provided they have the resolve to cross that line.

Horcruxes were no different. The only real obstacle was obtaining the forbidden knowledge. Once you had it, only one thing remained.

The will to take another life in order to preserve your own.

Carina walked out of Nurmengard with dark, unreadable eyes. Wolfram was waiting for her, leaning against the wall.

"Did you meet the witch?"

Carina nodded.

"What did you discuss?"

"That Dumbledore is dead. A few pleasantries. That sort of thing."

"That's all?"

Wolfram pressed her, clearly fishing for more. Carina ignored him and scanned her surroundings. The only structure on this desolate clifftop was the prison tower itself—no cover, no concealment anywhere outside Nurmengard's walls.

But Carina had sharp eyes. Sharp enough to notice a boulder positioned with suspicious unnaturalness—large enough, she noted, to conceal a person. And that boulder had not been there when she went inside.

Wolfram's expression hardened.

"…How did you know?"

"I might ask you the same thing. Your speech patterns and accent are completely different. Why would you assume I wouldn't notice?"

"Fair enough. I was too hasty."

Wolfram's lips twisted into something unpleasant.

An instant later, both of them drew their wands simultaneously. Carina snapped the incantation out like a whip.

"*Stupefy!*"

Wolfram swung his right arm in a wide arc. There was a sound like metal striking metal, and the red bolt ricocheted away into the distance. He advanced, wand raised, closing the gap with long, deliberate strides.

A Shield Charm? No—the spell had made contact with his arm before bouncing off. Where it had struck, a flash of silver showed beneath the surface, like gilt scraped away to reveal the metal beneath.

A silver right arm, gifted by the Dark Lord. A fighting style that used it as a shield.

Carina recognized him the moment the pieces fell into place.

"Igor Karkaroff!"

"You really are quick, aren't you…!"

Karkaroff dispelled his disguise. The unkempt hair and ragged beard told the story of a man who had been running for a very long time. None of which made him any less dangerous. Carina fired spell after spell, but each one was deflected by the silver arm. With that arm as his vanguard and his left hand moving with a life of its own, Karkaroff pressed forward like a battering ram.

He was nearly on top of her when he shouted.

"*Expelliarmus!*"

"*Prote—*"

Her Shield Charm was still forming when Karkaroff lunged. The silver arm punched straight through it and seized her by the collar. The impact tore the wand from her hand.

"Aah!"

He drove her back into the wall of Nurmengard. The stone slammed into her spine, and a cry escaped her lips.

Karkaroff's voice dropped to something low and menacing.

"Grindelwald. Yes. That was an option I hadn't considered—going to a Dark wizard from the previous generation. My mistake. I never imagined you'd seek out someone like her."

Pinned against the wall, Carina glared at him with undisguised contempt.

"How did you know I'd come here?"

"…Lucius should never have let his daughter leave Britain. I may be hunted by the British Ministry, but my sources abroad are still very much intact. But that's beside the point."

Slam. Karkaroff shoved her harder into the stone. Pain flared through her chest, and Carina's expression tightened.

He leaned in closer, his voice dropping further.

"The information you got from Grindelwald. You're going to tell me everything."

Even with his hand at her throat, Carina answered him with crisp precision.

"And what exactly do you intend to do with it?"

"What do you think? Resurrect the Dark Lord! He will return. By my hand, no less!"

"How devoted of you. It's a pity you couldn't manage that devotion from the start—*aah!*"

The silver arm crushed down on her. She cried out at the searing pain in her throat. Karkaroff snarled.

"You! This is your fault, Malfoy! If not for you—if you hadn't led me to Bartemius, I would never have ended up like this!"

Carina answered through the pain, her voice steady.

"Did I hold a wand to your head? Did I threaten you? Did I tell you that you'd die if you didn't return to Voldemort's side? You came to me because you were frightened. You were already unraveling, and my fear was the last push you needed."

"I didn't think Dumbledore would win! He was too old! And the Dark Lord had conquered death itself! Who could have imagined his defeat?"

It was a wretched, cowardly sight. He had made his own choices—and now he wanted to lay the blame at her feet.

If Carina had deceived him, she might have felt a flicker of guilt. But at the time, she had been his ally, however reluctantly. She could not understand his anger.

"But Dumbledore is dead now, isn't he? The Dark Lord lives on—inside Harry Potter! Once he takes full possession of Potter's body, no one will be able to stop him!"

One thing was certain: Karkaroff was their enemy.

He finished his raving and raised his wand.

"Now. If you won't speak willingly, I'll use the Cruciatus Curse. You will tell me everything Grindelwald told you. Every last word."

She would die before she said a word.

A Horcrux was a method of splitting the soul. If Harry used it, Voldemort's soul could be separated from his—but there was no guarantee that Voldemort, once he seized full control of Harry's body, wouldn't use the same method himself. She could not breathe a word of the Horcrux to Karkaroff. If she did, the remnants of the Death Eaters still in hiding might find new hope and come crawling out of the dark.

But how long could she hold out? Even if she endured the Cruciatus, she was carrying Veritaserum. If Karkaroff found it—

Carina clenched her jaw and forced her eyes upward. And then, almost involuntarily, her gaze fell on the silver arm gripping her collar.

"The silver… arm…"
The surface of that smooth, metallic limb bore the faint trace of a Dark Mark. A serpent watching her. Voldemort had used it to monitor her—to alert him if she betrayed him, and to strike her down if she did.

Had Voldemort placed a similar constraint on Karkaroff, who had his own history of betrayal?

If so—could she use it?

"…Your master is Harry Potter. The Dark Lord's power resides in Harry now!"

It might be a waste of breath. But that was no reason not to try.

Carina spoke to the silver arm with the desperate conviction of someone clutching at straws.

"And I am Harry's lover. We are bound to each other—deeply. So follow me. It is not him you should obey. It is me!"

"What on earth are you—"

"I am giving you a command! Attack your host. Now!"

Karkaroff's face twisted in bewilderment. And then—

The silver arm opened its hand.

Carina staggered upright, rubbing her throat, and watched. The silver arm seemed to hesitate between them—between Carina and Karkaroff—and then, slowly, rotated its wrist.

"What—"

Karkaroff felt it before he understood it. He scrambled to wrench the arm away from himself, but it was already moving. The silver arm lunged and closed around his throat. His eyes went wide.

"*Gkh—!*"

He clawed at it with his other hand, but the metal would not yield. The only answer was the cold, hollow clang of flesh against silver. The color drained from his face as the air left him.

"H—help—"

He reached toward Carina with a desperate, outstretched hand. Carina watched from where she stood, her back against the wall, and did nothing.

It did not take long. Karkaroff crumpled to the ground. His eyes had rolled back—he was completely unconscious.

Carina confirmed it, then addressed the silver arm.

"Enough."

No further explanation was needed. The arm obeyed her, for reasons she couldn't entirely explain. Karkaroff lay collapsed on the ground, but the silver arm remained upright, waiting for her next command.

Carina smoothed the wrinkles from her clothes.

"Stay still. If Karkaroff wakes, knock him out again."

The silver arm acknowledged her with a small flex of the wrist. Carina raised her wand and crossed to the unnaturally placed boulder she had noticed earlier, dispelling the concealment charm layered over it. Inside, the real Wolfram lay unconscious.

He was alive, at least. Carina knelt to wake him, then looked back at the crumpled figure of Karkaroff.

A Horcrux. A life required to create one. A cowardly Death Eater who had conveniently arrived.

The way everything had clicked into place made her uneasy. If this had been a puzzle, she might have felt a clean satisfaction. But this was real, and all she felt was the unsettling sense of something scripted—as though she were a puppet in someone else's play.

And yet.

If the ending of this play was one where everyone survived—

There was no reason to deviate from the script.

Carina returned to Britain.

She brought Karkaroff with her—the man who had sought to resurrect He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named and plunge Britain into chaos—in chains.

—

A car pulled up in front of Number Four, Privet Drive.

The vehicle was so quiet that Vernon's cheap saloon, parked nearby, might as well have been invisible. And yet the neighbors gathered as though a festival had come to Little Whinging.

A Rolls-Royce was not a common sight in this part of the world. As the enormous, gleaming car turned toward the Dursleys' house, the eyes of every gossip-hungry housewife on the street lit up like those of predators scenting prey.

Who owned that car? Why had it come to see the Dursleys?

The whispers were still building when the door finally opened.

Several young men who had followed the car in a daze felt something close to breathlessness. Light seemed to spill from the back seat. A woman stepped out—the kind of beauty rarely seen even in magazines. She wore jeans and a hoodie, and she walked toward the Dursley house with a radiance that made the Rolls-Royce behind her an afterthought. The young men had already forgotten it existed.

She was far too extraordinary a visitor for a place like Privet Drive. In the quiet of Little Whinging, she looked like something cut from another world entirely and pasted into this one.

Regardless. Carina Malfoy walked on, unhurried, at her own pace. She was beautiful even pressing the doorbell. When the door opened a moment later, she smiled pleasantly.

"Good afternoon, Mrs. Dursley. And Mr. Dursley."

—

Vernon Dursley noticed the car before he noticed the person. He was loath to admit it, but this visitor's automobile was every bit as extraordinary as her appearance. The Rolls-Royce—a car that was said to choose its owners as much as the other way around—had a way of establishing hierarchies without a word being spoken.

The moment Vernon registered that the Spirit of Ecstasy on the bonnet was sculpted in platinum, the social order between himself and Carina Malfoy was settled beyond dispute.

"…I had assumed all wizards were eccentrics."

Carina smiled serenely.

"Surely you don't think all of them live the way those ones do? They're considered eccentric even within the wizarding world. Most of us are rather more refined."

"Ha! Well, I suppose not every last one of them could be that rude and disruptive! Birds of a feather, I always say—no wonder that boy collects such peculiar friends!"

Setting aside the obvious bias in Vernon's assessment, there was no denying that Harry did have a tendency to befriend people without much discrimination. Carina said nothing and took a small sip of the juice Petunia had set out. Undissolved sugar floating in a watery, flavorless drink. One expected a certain lack of refinement from a Muggle household of this sort, but the thought of Harry having grown up drinking this made something tighten in her chest.

What Carina did not know—not yet—was that even this had not always been permitted to him.

Then came the sound of heavy footsteps from upstairs. Harry, bags in hand, came thundering down the stairs.

Vernon called out in irritation.

"Boy! We have a guest—could you not manage to—"

He didn't finish. Harry had walked straight into the sitting room and kissed Carina before he'd even set his bags down.

Vernon inhaled sharply.

What made it worse—considerably worse—was that the owner of the Rolls-Royce kissed him back.

The boy who had lived in a cupboard, and the woman who owned a Rolls-Royce. The image was like watching an absurdist play. Vernon, who had already formed a firm impression of Carina as someone of immense consequence, felt as though he were witnessing a beggar wed a princess—and the shock of it left him entirely speechless.

The kiss was brief for Harry and interminable for Vernon. When it ended, Harry looked into her eyes.

"Nothing happened while I was here?"

"Of course not."

"I'll hold you to that if you're lying."

"Oh, do try."

Carina ended the exchange with a dismissive sound and removed his hands from her waist, settling back into her seat. She took Harry's hand in hers and looked down at Vernon—who was still opening and closing his mouth like a fish—with cool, unhurried disdain.
"My apologies. It's been a while. I got carried away. Now—where were we?"

"Er—ah—"

"Nothing important, then. Well, if we're finished, might I have a brief look around the house? I've always wanted to see where Harry grew up."

Without waiting for Vernon's answer, Carina rose, one hand on Harry's hand and the other on his shoulder. She let her gaze travel slowly across the sitting room and kitchen, her eyes narrowing slightly.

"Hmm. A bit worn, but livable enough, I suppose. Is that your chair, Harry?"

She pointed to a large, overstuffed sofa that jutted out prominently. Harry gave a quiet laugh and shook his head.

"No, that's my cousin Dudley's."

"There are only three chairs. So where's yours?"

"There isn't one."

The implication was not difficult to parse. The Dursleys had never thought to provide Harry with a seat of his own. Carina turned her head slowly and fixed Vernon with a look of undisguised contempt.

"Mr. Dursley. Is your family truly so impoverished that you couldn't manage to buy a chair for your nephew?"

The well-used kitchen. The neatly arranged plates and cutlery.

And nowhere in it, any place for Harry.

Carina spoke with quiet, controlled fury.

"Or is this simply a Muggle custom? Giving a child nothing at all?"

Once, when Mr. Weasley had used the word 'Muggle,' Vernon had reacted as though it were a slur—as though it were mocking the Dursleys for being 'normal.' But now, for reasons he couldn't quite articulate, the word in Carina's mouth sounded like something else entirely. It sounded the way an aristocrat might speak of a lower class—with contempt built into the very syllable.

The authority radiating from that Rolls-Royce pressed down on Vernon like a physical weight. It was worse than being called to account by the most powerful employer he'd ever worked for. He found himself making excuses before he'd consciously decided to.

"W—we fed the boy. Every meal."

Harry added, with a quiet snicker:

"About three days a week."

Carina's fury reached a new altitude. Her eyes were cold and full of contempt; her mouth curved into something that was not quite a smile.

"Is this normal for Muggles? No shame, no decency. Treating food and a roof as though they were acts of charity owed to a blood relation."

"Not all Muggles are like this."

"What does that matter? The Muggles in the house where you lived were like this."

Carina's fingers drifted toward her wand. She was visibly weighing whether to draw it. Harry noticed immediately and pulled her out of the sitting room before she could make a decision.

"Easy. It's all right. We're not coming back here."

"Then there's no reason we can't demolish this wretched little house before we go, is there?"

"There's every reason. Calm down, Carina."

"…I can't calm down. Help me calm down."

At her request, Harry wrapped his arms around her. The shoulders trembling with anger slowly dropped. Then Carina looked past him at something on the wall and said:

"Are they keeping something in that cupboard?"

"Why do you ask?"

Carina pointed to the cupboard beneath the stairs. The door had a padlock, a small slot near the top, and a wide, flat gap at the bottom—just wide enough to slide a plate through.

"There. That opening at the bottom. For feeding whatever's inside. Do they keep a puma in there?"

"Ah. That. That's where I slept when I was small."

The shoulders trembled harder. Harry kept one arm around her and used the other hand to catch her wrist. If he hadn't, Carina would have had her wand out and the house in rubble.

Trapped in Harry's arms, Carina fought against him.

"How dare they—Muggles! Common Muggles, doing something like this and expecting to walk away unscathed?!"

"All right, all right. You're worked up again. Breathe."

"How am I supposed to breathe?! They stuffed their nephew in a cupboard and still expected to be treated with respect? Fine. I'll give them exactly what they deserve. I'll show them personally what it means to reap what you sow—"

"I sleep in a proper room upstairs now. I got too tall for the cupboard."

This did not appear to help matters. Carina struggled harder, her voice rising.

"Let go of me! If you don't let go right now, I will come back later and bury this house in the ground!"

Harry glanced toward the sitting room. Fortunately, Vernon had had the sense to take Petunia somewhere out of sight. He had managed to hold his ground against the eccentric wizards who'd visited before, but the aristocratic young witch who'd arrived in a Rolls-Royce had apparently broken something in him entirely.

Harry held Carina for a long time. Eventually, her voice came out quieter, and slightly unsteady.

"…You should forget this place as quickly as you can, Harry. Your room is going to be incomparably larger."

"I like small spaces. More chances for us to touch."

Harry said it with perfect nonchalance. From somewhere against his chest came a reluctant, pleased sound. Carina smiled briefly, then murmured into his shirt:

"Even in a large room… we can still touch often enough."

She finished the sentence and pushed him away. Then she turned and walked toward the front door.

"Come on, Harry. I don't want to spend another second in this place."

Harry felt the same. He grabbed his bags and followed her out. The front door opened, and they stepped out onto Privet Drive together.

A boy who had been admiring the car's ornaments scrambled back in alarm. Carina pointed her wand at the Rolls-Royce and the door swung open on its own. A few onlookers gasped. None of them suspected magic—to them, a Rolls-Royce was already indistinguishable from it.

Harry paused and looked back.

The cramped, worn house where he had spent seventeen years. The low fence, easy to peer over. The obsessively maintained garden and garage. The house that had always felt too small, but only for him.

After today, he would not return. Whether things went well or badly—he would not come back here.

And yet he felt no grief at leaving. No pull of nostalgia. His heart had already gone somewhere else.

"What are you doing?"

Carina's voice came from the back seat. Harry smiled and turned away. There was no point searching for meaning in a place that held no memories worth keeping. His heart was already inside that Rolls-Royce, where she was beckoning him with one hand.

He loaded his trunk and settled in beside her. The door closed. The car moved off on its own, heading for King's Cross.

—

Until his birthday arrived, Harry would stay at Hogwarts.

It was the final compromise, hammered out through tense negotiations with the Ministry of Magic. The Ministry did not want Harry moving freely through the world. And Harry, for his part, had no desire to be entirely unbound. In the end, Hogwarts—empty for the summer—had been chosen as his last cage.

Throughout that time, Carina would remain at his side.

Until the final moment, she would whisper to him of reasons to want to live.

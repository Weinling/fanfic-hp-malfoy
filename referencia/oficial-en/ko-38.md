Hermione spoke with a worried frown.

"Harry, are you really not going to prepare at all?"

"Nope. I have no desire to go to the bottom of a lake."

Harry seemed thoroughly unenthusiastic about the second task of the Triwizard Tournament. He propped his feet up on the desk and yawned extravagantly.

"Someone out there might be looking for a chance to attack me, and I'm supposed to go wandering around the lake floor? That's basically strolling around with a sign that says 'please kill me.' If I use the Bubble-Head Charm to breathe, then a simple Diffindo aimed at the bubble becomes a killing curse. If I get ambushed down there, the lake becomes my grave. And no one would ever know whether I drowned by accident or was murdered."

"Well, aren't you impressive. You're finally listening to the warnings I gave you last year, the year before, and the year before that."

Hermione said it with a dry edge. Harry shrugged and replied.

"Back then, the other side was going to come looking for me whether I moved or not, so I had to go out and intercept them. But there's no need for that now. It's not as though I need the prize money or the glory. More than anything, I want to live a long life. I barely have time to be in a relationship as it is."

Muttering that, Harry tossed a piece of walnut pie into his mouth.

Just then, Neville came through the dormitory common room door. He glanced around, spotted Harry, and trudged over with all the energy of a man walking to his own execution.

"Sorry, Harry... I couldn't get the Gillyweed."

"Why are you apologising? I said I wasn't going in anyway."

"Still... I made such a big deal about having a solution, and then I couldn't find it..."

"It's fine, it's fine. Just knowing something like that exists is enough of a result."

Harry tried to reassure him, but Neville looked so thoroughly crestfallen that no comfort seemed likely to reach him.

"Professor Sprout said she doesn't have any Gillyweed... I looked into ordering it, but apparently it can only be harvested in summer... And on top of that, if you don't keep it in a special potion, it dries out within a few days above ground and becomes useless."

"So it was never really meant to be kept on hand. Who would even have any?"

"...Professor Sprout said Professor Snape probably would."

"Snape?"

Harry's face twisted. And then he understood why Neville had been so apologetic. The thing Neville feared most in the world was Snape, and he simply could not bring himself to go to the man and ask for Gillyweed.

Harry felt the same way. He had no desire to go to Snape with his hat in his hand.

"If it's there, getting it would be impossible short of duelling Snape into the floor and ransacking his office. Snape would make a deal with the devil himself if it meant making my life miserable."

Neville said anxiously.

"H-Harry! You can't attack a professor!"

"I know. I have no intention of picking a fight I'd lose."

"...You say that like you've actually tried it before."

Harry leaned back into the sofa and yawned lazily.

"Well, that settles it. I'm not doing it. I'm not going in. I'm going to live forever in joy and happiness..."

"Hermione!"

Fred and George came striding toward Hermione.

"Have you done something wrong? Professor McGonagall was looking for you."

"She looked absolutely furious, for whatever reason."

"Professor McGonagall? Why? Was there a mistake in the basic Transfiguration essay from last lesson?"

Hermione leapt to her feet in a panic. Harry looked at her with an expression of mild disbelief.

"Hermione, even if it is Professor McGonagall, she wouldn't summon you in the middle of the night over a few essay errors."

"Harry. Basic Transfiguration is extremely important. It changes not just the appearance but the function entirely, so even a small mistake can cause serious problems!"

"How many things we learn here aren't dangerous?"

"Regardless, I'm going. If I'm not back by ten, don't wait up!"

With that, Hermione left the dormitory.

And she did not return even after ten o'clock had passed.

Harry spent the morning of the task searching for Carina. His plan had been to make a great show of complaining before heading to the lake, and collect some sympathy and encouragement in return.

But Carina was nowhere to be found.

If today was the task, she would at least come out to watch—but her shimmering platinum hair was nowhere to be seen.

"Now, all of our Champions are assembled and waiting for the second task. The Champions must... retrieve what they have lost, within one hour!"

Hermione, Cho Chang, and Carina had vanished. The one thing they had in common was that each had been a Champion's partner at the Yule Ball.

The requirement that Champions bring a partner to the ball—that too had probably been designed to identify the hostages.

What elaborate staging. Harry smiled through gritted teeth.

"What they have lost lies below! We trust they have already worked out what that is. On the count of three, we begin!"

Harry had made absolutely no preparations.

He had a rough grasp of the Bubble-Head Charm, but it was far from perfect. His original plan had been to enter the water, pretend the charm had failed, and come back out.

He had no desire to go down to the bottom of that lake—a place where no one could know what anyone might do. He needed to return to the surface, where Carina was.

But Carina was not on the surface. She was waiting for him below.

Then he had to go. Somehow.

"On three! One, two, three!"

"*Accio* Firebolt!"

Whatever the other Champions were doing, he had no time to care.

Far in the distance, the Firebolt cut through the air. Harry swung onto it in one fluid motion and flew toward Hogwarts.

Watching Harry suddenly bolt in the opposite direction, Ludo Bagman shouted after him.

"Harry Potter—where are you flying? That's Hogwarts, not the lake! Oh, wait. That window—"

Harry did not slow down. He aimed straight for the castle wall—toward the large window that faced the lake—and as he hurtled toward it, he raised his wand and cried out.

"*Reducto!*"

The explosion reached them even from that distance. The shattered window burst apart in all directions, and chunks of masonry tumbled down below. Harry drove through the hole he had made, broomstick and all.

While everyone stood speechless at Harry's erratic behaviour, Ron suddenly realised something and shouted.

"That's Snape's office!"

Harry had smashed through the window of Snape's office and broken in. Several people turned to look at Snape with faces drained of colour.

Having just witnessed his own office explode in real time, Snape wore an expression that suggested he would very much like to throttle Harry on the spot.

Meanwhile, Harry was already ransacking Snape's office. He rummaged through a cabinet, muttering to himself.

"He's got an obscene amount of ingredients. He might actually be the wealthiest person in Hogwarts."

Neville had described Gillyweed as looking like slippery aquatic weeds—a magical Mediterranean water plant that, when chewed, caused gills and webbing to grow. Apparently it had been popular among island-dwelling wizards who wished to meet merpeople.

After turning the cabinet completely upside down, Harry finally found the Gillyweed. He shoved it straight into his mouth, climbed back onto his broomstick, and prepared to leave.

Just before departing, he glanced back for a moment. Snape's office looked as though a storm had torn through it.

Still, since he had benefited from the man's stores, it seemed only polite to say goodbye. Harry expressed his gratitude to Snape.

"Thanks for the loan."

And then the Firebolt shot forward like a comet. The backdraft sent parchment and inkbottles scattering across the desk, but that was none of Harry's concern.

Harry chewed the Gillyweed steadily as he flew toward the lake.

"*Impervius.*"

He cast the waterproofing charm once each on his glasses, his robes, and his broomstick, then circled above the lake. He took one deep breath, and accelerated toward the water in the manner of a Wronski Feint.

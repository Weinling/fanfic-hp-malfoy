After the Christmas Ball, Professor Moody was nowhere to be seen. Professor Dumbledore informed the students:

'Professor Moody is away on an extended assignment of a sensitive nature. In the meantime, someone capable and promising will serve as temporary Defence Against the Dark Arts instructor.'

No one missed the way Snape's face twitched with barely contained anticipation.

'Now then—until Professor Moody's return, your temporary Defence Against the Dark Arts professor will be... Padfoot!'

That expression did not last long before it collapsed entirely.

Clean-shaven and impeccably dressed, Sirius was the sort of man who belonged in a fashion spread. Most of Hogwarts had no idea that this striking figure was the same Sirius Black who had escaped Azkaban the previous year. Those who did know also knew he had already been pardoned—and so they simply smiled.

Snape, of course, did not smile at all.

Where Professor Moody's lessons had been professional and intense, Padfoot's were wildly popular. He was handsome, irreverent, and effortlessly charming. His instruction lacked Moody's technical rigour, but he wove humour into every spell until even the most bewildering concepts became clear. Fred and George, who had been so deeply impressed by Mad-Eye Moody, fell completely under Padfoot's spell—particularly once they learned he had helped create the Marauder's Map, after which they treated him with something approaching reverence. Padfoot, for his part, moved among the students as though he were one of them.

Snape regarded Padfoot with undisguised contempt, but no one found that strange. It was common knowledge that Snape coveted the Defence Against the Dark Arts post, and the contrast between cheerful Padfoot and brooding Snape was as stark as oil and water.

Carina Malfoy, however, noticed that the tension between them ran deeper than that.

"Is something going on between Professor Snape and Sirius Black?"

"Hm? Why do you ask?"

Harry looked at her. Carina spoke with an uncharacteristic note of concern.

"Professor Snape seems genuinely unsettled. He clearly can't stand that Sirius Black has been made a professor."

"So my plan worked."

"What?"

"I suggested it. Sirius wanted to stay at Hogwarts to protect me—I told him he might as well take on the temporary Defence post while he was at it."

Harry said it with the expression of a boy who had just pulled off a particularly satisfying prank. Evidently, he still couldn't bear to see Snape get anything he wanted.

"Snape's been in a state since day one. Leave it alone."

"'In a state.' You really do say whatever you like about him, Harry. He's Head of Slytherin House. Show some respect."

"Respect? For someone like that?"

"Show it anyway. You'd be annoyed if I spoke badly about Professor McGonagall, wouldn't you?"

Harry let out a low groan at the argument he couldn't counter. He answered with a look of profound reluctance.

"...Professor Snape and Sirius were in the same year at Hogwarts."

"Were they? Professor Snape was in Slytherin, and Sirius Black was—"

"Gryffindor. Apparently they've been at each other's throats since then. It's... quite something."

"They fought a lot?"

"Well, 'they'... and 'fought'... are both terms that leave a bit of room for debate."

Harry trailed off with a look of mild discomfort and shrugged.

"Not really my concern, though. Sirius is my godfather and my father was my father—does that make it my problem? That's between the two of them. What's strange is that he takes it out on me. Don't you think?"

"That part, at least, is fair. Your own conduct aside."

"I genuinely don't understand why he keeps coming after me."

Harry shrugged again, looking entirely sincere in his bafflement. Carina stared at him.

"Before you ask that question, Harry, perhaps try reflecting on your own behaviour."

"I don't retaliate unless I'm provoked first. You know that. Honestly, Snape fired the first shot."

Carina had nothing to say to that. She had been among those who had piled on when Snape spent their first year making Harry's life miserable. Dumbledore's intervention had eventually put a stop to it—but the fact remained that Carina had played her part in shaping who Harry had become.

"Same goes for you. I always return what I'm given."

"I—what exactly did I give you?"

"Something rather memorable, the first time we met. Whether you remember it or not."

He said it, then pressed his lips to her hair. Carina's face went scarlet. It was as though his lips were a switch—the moment they made contact, her body stopped obeying her entirely.

She steadied her breathing and spoke.

"The first time we met—Diagon Alley? What did I do?"

"So you do remember."

"How could I forget—"

Harry's face drew closer. Carina, momentarily lost in the memory, caught herself just in time and shoved him away with both hands.

"You've gotten awfully bold lately! Did you really think the mood was just going to fall into your lap like that?"

"Tch. What a waste."

"You've been taking me far too lightly, Harry. If you want anything from me, you'd better earn the atmosphere first."

"Your Highness. Your command is my solemn duty."

Carina shot him a withering look. He still walked that infuriating line—and the moment she showed the slightest displeasure, he pivoted to flattery with uncanny precision. How had it come to this? She looked away and changed the subject.

"Smooth talker. Right—have you found Professor Moody yet?"

Harry's expression darkened. It was not a topic he particularly wanted to raise in front of her.

"We have so little time together as it is. Does it have to be this?"

"Tell me. I need to not be worried."

"In that case, I suppose I have no choice. The short answer is: not yet."

Harry walked her through the recent developments. Since his escape, Moody had left no trace whatsoever, and Professor Dumbledore had maintained his silence. He also mentioned that Sirius would continue teaching Defence Against the Dark Arts until the real Moody returned, then added quietly:

"What I can't work out is this—if he wanted to attack me, he should have come to the bottom of the lake. So what was he actually after?"

"What he was actually after..."

Carina murmured, her voice going flat. Harry read her expression and asked:

"Does something come to mind?"

"No. Nothing. And that's exactly what's strange. That Moody—what was he trying to do?"

"Oh, the usual, I'd imagine. Kill me, like so many others."
"If that were all, he could have cursed you during the lesson on the Unforgivable Curses... There's no consistency to his actions. But what if they were all in service of something larger? What if this is more dangerous than we've—"

Carina's shoulders trembled. Harry watched her face and her body for a moment, then wrapped his arm around her. He waited until the trembling stilled before he spoke.

"It's all right. Hogwarts is safe."

Carina swallowed. Harry held her close and murmured:

"Sirius is here. Professor Dumbledore is here. Durmstrang and Beauxbatons are here, and everyone has their eyes on this place. There is nowhere safer right now."

"I... know that."

"You're here. And I'm here."

"Yes..."

"I won't let anything happen to you. And you won't let anything happen to me."

Having steadied her, Harry brought his face close to hers with perfect ease. A brief kiss passed between them—barely more than a shared breath, a moment of confirmation. He stroked her hair. When she frowned at him, he smiled and got to his feet.

"That kiss—I'm letting it go this once. Next time, put in more effort."

"Noted. See you tomorrow."

Carina returned to the dormitory after parting from Harry. His words had done little to settle her. The unease hadn't lifted—some formless, persistent dread that refused to be reasoned away.

She sat down and read the letter waiting for her. The one her father, Lucius Malfoy, had sent that morning.

[Dearest Carina. You were always a clever and perceptive child, though never one who simply did as she was told. You have always known how to meet the expectations of others—on your own terms.

Your father knows how gifted you are, how sharp. Whatever I might say in a letter, you will disregard it if you choose to, and I have no interest in wasting the effort.

I have no intention of interfering greatly in your school life. One ought to make a few mistakes in one's youth—that is how one learns. Were I to try to stop you by force, I would only breed resentment.

You know me as well as I know you.

Carina. And so your father trusts you. When the moment comes that leaves no other choice, I believe you will make the decision that befits who you are.

When everything becomes clear, I trust you will understand.

With concern. Your Father.]

Carina folded the letter. Beneath the worry Lucius had written for her, there was a stern warning—not a threat designed to pressure her, but a warning that if she did not change course, the entire family could be placed in danger.

"Why... why is it coming to this?"

She had been happy. Genuinely, quietly happy.

She was openly seeing a handsome, devoted boyfriend. The Triwizard Tournament, despite all her fears, was proceeding without disaster. Having been taken as a hostage in the second task, Carina was no longer a peripheral figure—she had become a protagonist in her own right, distinct from the Tournament itself. Everyone envied her, everyone wanted to know what had happened. She would answer their questions at her own pace, and then Harry would appear to meet her, and she would walk through the crowd toward him, and he would see her and smile.

It was spring.

Wildflowers bloomed along the lakeshore. Warm sunlight lay across the grounds. On fine days they walked together; when assignments piled up they went to the library together. Harry had woven himself into the ordinary fabric of her days, and his presence alone seemed to add a colour that hadn't been there before.

Inside the castle, everything was peaceful. But the letters from the outside world painted a picture that was anxious and dark. Bertha Jorkins's disappearance still occupied a column in the newspaper, and once the Triwizard coverage ended, there was nothing but grim rumour—someone causing trouble here, someone causing trouble there.

Hogwarts felt like another world entirely. Carina knew it could not stay that way forever.

"...Karkaroff. He's the one I can get information from right now."

She made up her mind to visit him for the first time in a while.

"Ah, Miss Malfoy! Do come in!"

Karkaroff greeted her with a broad smile, but his face told a different story—his beard had grown unkempt, his cheeks had hollowed as though he'd lost weight rapidly. He bared his yellowed teeth and spoke.

"Has Lucius been in touch? Is there a message for me?"

Carina studied him. Was this the face of a man who felt safe? She kept her expression composed.

"My father suggested I consult you. He said the situation is deteriorating, and that I should seek your counsel."

"Lucius said that?"

It had been months since Lucius had actually made that suggestion, but Carina held his gaze without flinching.

"Yes. He said this is precisely the moment when your wisdom is needed."

"So he feels it too? This mark—growing darker by the day?"

Karkaroff clutched his own wrist as he spoke.

Carina caught the scent of something and pressed carefully.

"I don't know the details. Father doesn't explain much in letters—someone might intercept them."

"Of course. So he's being watched too...!"

"Too? Does that mean you are as well?"

Just as she had anticipated, Karkaroff began to talk without being asked.

"Yes. That damned Mad-Eye Moody—he's been intercepting my letters! I haven't been able to get a proper message out to anyone. Trapped here in Hogwarts, rotting away, unable to do a thing!"

"Trapped?"

"I can't simply walk out! I have judging duties! And Moody watches me openly—! Damn it all. If this isn't a prison, what is it?"

"Wait. Mad-Eye Moody?"

Carina looked at him, genuinely confused.

Mad-Eye Moody? The fake Moody—hadn't Sirius and Harry driven him off? He should be gone.

"Professor Moody isn't at Hogwarts anymore. How could he be intercepting your letters?"

"Not here? He is absolutely here. Right now, watching me."

Karkaroff's expression was too earnest, too desperate for performance.

Deception requires composure. Lies are a skill. A man who has nothing left cannot sustain them. He had no reserves left to waste on lying to her.

"I know it. He's pretending to be away on assignment—waiting for me to make a move...! He knows. He knows that when the Dark Mark grows dark enough, I'll have to act!"

"What is the Dark Mark?"

"This!"

Karkaroff shoved up his sleeve and pointed to the black mark on his wrist. Carina's breath caught.

A skull. A serpent curling from the mouth like a tongue. The same Dark Mark she had once seen in the Daily Prophet stared back at her.
"That's... the symbol of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named?"

"Yes! The Dark Lord! The mark he used to summon his Death Eaters. And it's been growing darker! Do you understand what that means?"

Voldemort's return.

Carina swallowed the answer before it reached her lips. Karkaroff wasn't waiting for one.

"It means the Dark Lord's power is returning! That he will be able to call us to him—at any moment!"

Carina kept her voice carefully level.

"But He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named is gone."

"He's coming back! To reclaim his throne—! To kill Harry Potter!"

Karkaroff stopped and looked at her. His face held something unnameable—fear and hope tangled together in a way that had no clean expression.

"Come to think of it—you're his sweetheart, aren't you, Miss Malfoy?"

Was he trying to corner her?

Absurd. Carina was a student, but she was not the sort to be outmanoeuvred by a cornered animal. She steadied herself. She let the agitation drain away, lifted her chin, and met Karkaroff's eyes directly. A smile settled at the corner of her mouth—cool, contemptuous, aristocratic.

"A worthwhile one, I'd say. Handsome, famous, talented—and above all, close to Dumbledore. Wouldn't you say that's a rather more valuable connection than most, Karkaroff?"

"...Potter seems quite fond of you."

"I am Carina Malfoy. Who wouldn't be?"

She let out a soft, deliberate laugh.

"You'd agree, wouldn't you? You're rather fond of me yourself."

"I—yes. Yes, I am."

The calculation drained from Karkaroff's face, leaving behind the same haggard, servile expression as before.

"I only... I worry. Lucius won't look kindly on you spending time with Harry Potter..."

"Don't trouble yourself. Things are what they are for now. It's not as though I've married him—we're simply seeing each other for a while."

Carina covered her mouth with a small, light laugh. The implication was clear: don't read too much into children's games.

Then, without warning, the laughter stopped.

Carina's voice turned cool.

"If you're going to act, now would be the time. The opportunity won't come again... That's the only advice I can offer."

"Th—thank you, Miss Malfoy. I'll use it—I'll use it to find out what's happening! And if you hear anything from Lucius, please, let me know!"

"Of course."

Carina rose with a gracious smile. Karkaroff saw her out with excessive brightness.

The Sturmwind, Durmstrang's ship, was a different world from Karkaroff's quarters—full of noise and life. The Durmstrang students had surrendered entirely to Hogwarts's spring. They gossiped about Viktor Krum and Hermione, and seemed to have caught the same cross-school romance fever as their Champion. Carina smiled at the ones who tried to speak to her as she made her way off the ship.

The moment her feet touched the ground, the smile vanished.

"...He's coming back?"

Why now? How? And to what end?

The Moody who had been targeting Harry was gone. Dumbledore and the Ministry were still standing. The Death Eaters were lying low across the world.

So how was it possible?

"Why does everything keep getting more unsettling..."

Carina was composing herself as she walked along the lakeshore when a beetle began circling her. Already on edge, she waved it away. The beetle dodged her hand with an agility that had no business belonging to an insect.

"What on earth—"

She flicked her hand in irritation, but the beetle persisted, orbiting her with stubborn purpose. Out of patience, Carina drew her wand. The beetle seemed to take fright at last and veered away—but Carina was past the point of mercy.

She was raising her wand to cast when—

"*Expelliarmus!*"

Her wand flew from her hand, spinning through the air.

Carina spun around in alarm. And then her alarm deepened considerably.

Standing there was someone she had never expected to see again.

Professor Moody.

"Finally caught you..."

Clunk. Clunk.

She hadn't heard that sound in nearly two months. The wooden leg knocked against the ground with its unpleasant, irregular rhythm, and the magical eye—the one that saw through everything—rolled in its socket, fixed on her.

The beetle flew toward Moody. Then, abruptly, it transformed—into a sharp-faced old woman in spectacles, dressed with a forced attempt at style that only made her look more dishevelled. Carina recognised her immediately. Rita Skeeter. The old witch who wrote the gossip columns.

She looked at Carina with eyes full of venom.

"Malfoy! Because of your lot, I was thrown out of the Daily Prophet even though I wrote the truth! You wretched girl—you'll pay for this!"

"An Animagus...?"

A witch who could transform into an animal.

Rita Skeeter was an unregistered Animagus.

Everything made sense now. How she had spied on Harry and Carina. How she had written all those articles.

"Stay where you are!"

But that was a secondary concern. Carina stepped back, fear rising in her chest.

She had always found him unsettling. A former Auror and Defence Against the Dark Arts professor who operated outside the bounds of common sense—rude, bizarre, and inexplicably hostile toward her. Someone she had no means of handling. And from the moment Harry had begun to suspect him, he had become something else entirely: a source of dread, a presence that could shatter ordinary life without warning.

"It's been a while, Malfoy."

He spoke in that rough, grinding voice, levelling two wands at her. Disarmed, Carina flinched and stepped back.

Alastor Moody smiled—the smile of a man who had won—and said:

"Now then. Why don't you tell me everything you and Karkaroff discussed?"

"I've been looking forward to Defense Against the Dark Arts class so much lately."

Barnette Crabbe said cheerfully.

"Mad-Eye Moody going on leave was the best Christmas gift ever! Because of that, we got to meet such a young, handsome professor—Padfoot!"

"I'll grant you young and handsome, but I still don't understand why you're so fond of that layabout."

Carina gave her a reproving look. Sirius Black was, as Crabbe said, young and handsome, and he did have a sense of humor—but if she was being honest, his teaching ability fell short of even Professor Lupin's.

He carried on as though he were a student himself, and rather than imparting knowledge, he tended to share tips for pranks and tricks for doing well on exams.

Carina agreed that he was an improvement over Mad-Eye Moody, but she didn't particularly care for him. Even accounting for the fact that he was Harry's godfather.

"His flippant manner and his casual attitude toward lessons don't sit well with me. On top of that, he makes no secret of the fact that he'd rather not be teaching at all."

"Oh, come on. A face like that solves most of those problems. Sure, he's a bit prickly, but still."

"A bit? He may not be as bad as Professor Moody, but he doesn't like Slytherins."

"To put it another way, he doesn't seem particularly fond of Slytherins, but he's nowhere near as bad as Moody! Malfoy, would you actually prefer Padfoot to treat us as badly as Moody did?"

"...No, that's not what I'm saying."

But Carina knew why Sirius didn't openly discriminate against Slytherins. It was because of her.

Carina was Harry's girlfriend, and Sirius cared about that considerably.

Though he was a Black—a pureblood family—and seemed to harbor a deep-seated contempt for Slytherin, that contempt never quite surfaced, presumably out of consideration for his godson's girlfriend. The irony was that this ambivalence read as 'brooding charm' to the girls in the class.

Of course, now that Moody was back, things would probably change. Carina gave a quiet snort.

"I wonder if that's still the case now."

"Hmm?"

There's something that exists between people in a relationship. A kind of unspoken understanding that passes between them through atmosphere and glances alone, without a single word being said.

Since that day, Harry and Carina had not met even once.

Moody was real. Whoever had put Harry's name in the Goblet of Fire was still out there, watching them from the shadows.

The situation surrounding them had reverted to what it was before Christmas.

That Harry and Carina's relationship had not reverted to what it was before Christmas was due, simply, to lingering feeling.

"Brace yourself, Crabbe. There may be some rather devastating news in store for you today."

"What's that supposed to mean? You're scaring me. When you say it like that, it really feels like something's coming..."

Crabbe grumbled as she pushed open the door to the Defense Against the Dark Arts classroom. Then immediately closed it again.

She turned to look at Carina with the stiff, mechanical motion of a broken doll.

"M-Malfoy. H-how did you know."

"Crabbe! Closing that door won't do you any good!"

A gravelly voice rang out from inside the classroom. Crabbe let out a small whimper and gripped the door handle tight.

"That's right. I'm back from leave. My eye is still very much in working order, I'm afraid. I can see right through that door, so stop clutching the handle and get in here!"

Crabbe opened the door with trembling hands and swallowed hard as she looked toward the front of the room. There sat Mad-Eye Moody, wearing an expression of profound displeasure.

The Slytherin students sat in their seats looking as though the world had ended. They exchanged glances with one another, silently lamenting when Moody had returned and why Dumbledore had seen fit to send him away for a mere three months.

Moody's magical eye couldn't read minds—but no magical eye was needed to read the thoughts written plainly on their faces.

"None of you seem particularly pleased to see me back... Padfoot must have done quite a decent job, it seems?"

Crabbe, oblivious to the danger, began nodding before catching herself and slapping a hand over her own mouth. Moody observed the reaction and turned his head, raising his voice.

"Yes. He's pureblood like the rest of you, with a charming face and a free-spirited nature... You may have already grown attached to him."

When Moody put it into plain words, quite a few students gave small, reluctant nods. Several of them were clearly hoping that this confirmation might shock Moody enough to send him back on leave.

But when Moody slammed his fist down on the desk, those soap-bubble hopes burst in an instant.

Moody barked fiercely.

"What you feel about it is no concern of mine! But you still haven't grasped the fundamental point of my class... I have told you, repeatedly, to remain vigilant! Did any of you think to look into who Padfoot was, or how he came to be teaching Defense Against the Dark Arts in my place? Were you really willing to learn from someone whose identity you didn't even know?"

No one had thought to investigate Padfoot. They had all been too busy celebrating Moody's absence, and the one student who might have taken the initiative—Carina—already knew who Sirius was.

While everyone sat in silence, Moody produced a copy of last year's Daily Prophet. On the front page, a gaunt, dark-haired man with a beard glared out with wild eyes.

Around this time last year, the news of Sirius Black's escape had dominated the front page week after week, so everyone recognized the man in the photograph. Beyond that, a few sharp-eyed students noticed that he bore a striking resemblance to Padfoot.

After a moment of simple deduction, they arrived at one conclusion.

"W-wait... Sirius Black?"

"Padfoot's true identity was a murderer?"

"Hold on. Wasn't Sirius Black his most devoted follower? Didn't he betray Harry Potter's parents?"

"What's going on? How did this happen?"

Murderer or professor. Ally or enemy.

The Slytherin students floundered, unable to reach any firm conclusion. Truth and fiction were so thoroughly tangled together that it was impossible to tell one from the other.

Moody waited until the confusion had spread sufficiently, and when the chatter began to die down, he called out sharply.

"What a spectacle. What a spectacle! You spent three months in the company of a wanted criminal without even knowing it. If he'd had a mind to, he could have killed half of you!"

Confusion and shame, fear and anger. A range of emotions played out across the students' faces.

Had it not been for the formidable presence of Mad-Eye Moody pressing down on them, it all would have erupted then and there—that was how unsettled they were.

"He appeared on the front page of the Daily Prophet as a wanted criminal less than a year ago! And you couldn't recognize him just because he'd put on decent clothes and had a shave. You wouldn't even need a disguise to slip in among you lot... just change your outfit and you'd never be spotted! And you call yourselves Slytherins?"

"We only accepted him because we were told he was a professor appointed by Hogwarts!"

Gray Goyle shot back indignantly.

"It's Hogwarts' job to appoint trustworthy professors!"

"Don't leave your judgment to others! Hogwarts? Last year it was a werewolf, the year before that an imbecile, and before that... After four years of being taken in like this, have you learned nothing? A Flobberworm has better learning capacity than you lot! If you want to have a sharp tongue, you ought to at least have sharper instincts than everyone else!"

But even the spirited Goyle was nothing more than a child in front of Mad-Eye. No matter how pointed her words, the fact remained that she too had failed to see through Sirius's identity.

Having silenced Goyle, Moody raised a finger.

"Of course, Sirius Black is not a murderer. He was falsely accused! His name was cleared at the end of last year and the warrant for his arrest was cancelled... But not one of you tried to find out who Padfoot was, or why he'd been imprisoned on false charges in the first place! Complacent—utterly complacent! You wouldn't even need an Imperius Curse or Polyjuice Potion to infiltrate your ranks... just act friendly, and you'll open your hearts all on your own!"

"B-but we were barely born when all that happened..."

Crabbe ventured haltingly.

It was an argument that missed Moody's point by some distance, but for some reason Moody fell silent and fixed Crabbe with a long stare.

Crabbe, frightened as she was, pressed on in her own defense.

"Sirius Black was imprisoned about twelve years ago... We were just children then, we didn't know anything. Everything we know about Sirius Black, everything about what happened—we only learned it from the Daily Prophet last year. How were we supposed to know the full story? How were we supposed to recognize his face?"

"...So it has nothing to do with you whatsoever?"

"Th-that's not what I mean. It's just that the past is something we never lived through. It came to us like a story from a fairy tale..."

Crabbe trailed off uncertainly. It was the kind of excuse a child makes to protect herself.

But it seemed to land on Moody with unexpected weight.

For just a moment, Carina had the impression that Moody had suddenly grown very old.

His actual age must have been considerable. He had been a friend of Dumbledore's, and even when he retired over a decade ago, he had already been among the most senior Aurors of his generation.

But Moody's ferocity and his razor-edged manner made him seem vigorous—young, almost, or at least gave that impression. Perhaps he was not a man sustained by life at all, but by the venom that had accumulated inside him over the years.

"Akin to a fairy tale, you say... something distant and long ago..."

But when something drained that venom from him, what remained was only a wounded, aging, retired Auror.

Moody spoke in a tired voice.
